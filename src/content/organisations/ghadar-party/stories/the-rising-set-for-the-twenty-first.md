In the autumn of 1914, ships began to arrive in Indian ports carrying men who had been away for years. They had come from the American coast and from the Philippines. Their newspaper had told them to go home.

Among them were a boy of eighteen from a village near Ludhiana and an engineering student from Poona. Their names were Kartar Singh Sarabha and Vishnu Ganesh Pingle.

## Coming home

In October 1914, Kartar Singh reached Calcutta by way of Colombo on a ship called the *Salamin*. With him were Pingle and another young man, Satyen Sen. Sohan Singh Bhakna sailed on a different ship, the *Namsang*.

<aside class="note"><b>Two dates</b>: one page says Kartar Singh and Pingle reached Calcutta in October 1914. The page on Pingle says November. Both agree that they sailed in the second half of October.</aside>

The police were waiting for some of them. On 13 October 1914, Sohan Singh Bhakna was arrested in Calcutta. He was held first at Ludhiana and later at Multan jail. He would not be free again for years.

Others slipped through. Kartar Singh and Pingle carried a letter from the Bengal revolutionary Jatin Mukherjee. They took it to Rash Behari Bose in Banaras. Bose, who had escaped after the Delhi bomb of 1912, took charge of the plan.

## The meetings

Ghadar men met at Ladhouwal, near Ludhiana. They agreed to raise money by robbing rich households. Two of them, Waryam Singh and Bhai Ram Rakha, died in a bomb blast during one such raid.

Rash Behari Bose arrived in Amritsar on 25 January 1915. On 12 February a meeting fixed the day of the uprising: 21 February 1915.

The plan was to win over Indian soldiers. The cavalry and infantry at Lahore, Ferozepur and Rawalpindi were to rise together. Plans also named Mian Mir, Dacca, Benares and Jubbulpore. In the 23rd Cavalry, soldiers were to take their weapons at roll call. A mutiny in the 26th Punjab was to be the signal. Then the rebels would march on Delhi and Lahore.

<aside class="note"><b>Mutiny</b>: when soldiers refuse their officers' orders and turn on them.</aside>

## The man in the room

A man named Kirpal Singh had come back from America. He was a cousin of Balwant Singh, a trooper in the 23rd Cavalry. Kirpal Singh went to a meeting at Rash Behari's headquarters in Lahore on 15 February. Pingle and others were there.

He then told the police.

## The date changes

The leaders feared that they had been discovered. They moved the date forward to 19 February. It was too late. On 19 February the police made their arrests. The government took the arms away from the Indian soldiers it did not trust. It declared martial law in Punjab.

Rash Behari Bose escaped from Lahore. He reached Japan in May 1915. His story is told on this site as well.

On 15 February, soldiers of the 5th Light Infantry at Singapore had mutinied. It was put down in three days. One page links this to the Ghadar plan. A historian, Hew Strachan, argues it was a separate event.

## Help from Berlin

The plot had friends far away. The war had made Germany an enemy of Britain. In Berlin, Indian exiles formed a committee. Virendranath Chattopadhyaya, Bhupendranath Datta and Har Dayal were among its leaders. The German Foreign Office gave them money and arranged to ship arms. Two ships, the *Annie Larsen* and the *Maverick*, were supposed to carry the weapons. Both attempts failed.

<aside class="note"><b>Committee</b>: here, a group of exiles in Berlin who worked for India's freedom with German help.</aside>

## Arrests

Kartar Singh got out of Lahore, but he was arrested in Benares. Another account says he went toward Afghanistan and came back on 2 March to work among soldiers at a place called Chak No. 5. There a soldier, Risaldar Ganda Singh, had him arrested.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: one page says Kartar Singh was arrested at Benares. Another says at Chak No. 5. The sources do not say which is right.</aside>

Pingle had been drawn into the nationalist movement as a schoolboy in Poona in 1905. Ten years later he was still working for it.

On the night of 23 March 1915, Vishnu Ganesh Pingle was found in the lines of the 12th Cavalry at Meerut. He had ten bombs with him.

## The trials

Special courts under a new law, the Defence of India Act of 1915, tried the Ghadar men in Lahore. One page counts 291 people. It says 42 were sentenced to death, 114 were sent away for life, 93 were given shorter terms, and 42 were freed.

<aside class="note"><b>Numbers</b>: another page gives 290 accused. One page says 42 were sentenced to death and hanged. Another only says 42 were sentenced to death. Bhakna's death sentence was changed to life in prison. The pages do not say how many were hanged in all.</aside>

Kartar Singh was held in the jail at Lahore. He is said to have tried to cut through the bars of his window with tools hidden under an earthen pitcher, but the guards found them. One page says that he gained fourteen pounds in jail.

Kartar Singh took full responsibility for what he had done. The judge asked him to change his statement. He refused. He would not appeal.

On 16 November 1915, Kartar Singh Sarabha and Vishnu Ganesh Pingle were hanged in Lahore Central Jail. Kartar Singh was nineteen. Pingle was twenty-seven.

<aside class="note"><b>The date</b>: one page gives the date as 16 November 1915. Another gives 17 November for the hanging of the Lahore accused.</aside>

Sohan Singh Bhakna was sentenced to death at first. The sentence was changed to life in prison. He reached the Andaman Islands on 10 December 1915 and went on hunger strike there several times. He was freed in July 1930, after about sixteen years. He lived until 1968.

## Trial in America

The trouble did not stop in India. In 1917 a trial opened in San Francisco. A federal grand jury had named 105 Indians, Germans and Americans. The case was called the Hindu–German Conspiracy Trial. It lasted about five months. In the end, 29 were found guilty, 15 of them Indians and 14 of them Germans. At that time it was the longest and most expensive trial the country had known.

## Why it matters

The rising never happened. But it frightened the government. A page on the Ghadar plot says it was one reason for the Defence of India Act and the Rowlatt Acts. Years later Bhagat Singh kept a photograph of Kartar Singh Sarabha in his pocket.
