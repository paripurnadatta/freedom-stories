Most people in the Ghadar story are men. Gulab Kaur is a woman whose name has come down to us.

## From Bakshiwala to Manila

Gulab Kaur was born around 1890 in the village of Bakshiwala, in Sangrur district of Punjab. Her family was poor. She was married young, to a man named Mann Singh.

The two of them hoped to go to America. On the way they stopped in Manila, in the Philippines.

In Manila she went to talks given by the Ghadar Party. She was inspired by three of its members, Baba Hafiz Abdullah, Baba Banta Singh and Baba Harnam Singh. She gave speeches, handed out leaflets and helped the Manila branch to grow. One page says she guarded the party's printing press.

## The disguise

One page says that she carried a press pass and posed as a journalist, so that she could give out weapons. The page gives no source for this. Treat it as a story told about her, not as a proven fact.

## The bangles

Another story says that she took off her bangles in front of men who wanted to hold back. She is supposed to have said that the women would fight in their place. The page that tells it does not name any source. Treat it as a story.

<aside class="note"><b>Legend</b>: a story passed down over many years. It may be partly true, but nobody has proved it.</aside>

## Going home

About fifty Ghadar men and women left the Philippines to go back to India. They sailed on the ship *Korea*, and changed at Singapore to the *Tosha Maru*. Gulab Kaur was with them. Her husband went on to America. She stayed in India.

In Punjab she worked in the villages of Kapurthala, Hoshiarpur and Jalandhar. She asked people to join an armed revolt.

## Prison

She was arrested in Lahore for "seditious acts". She was held in prison. One page says she was sentenced to two years. Another says she was tortured for around two years. Neither page gives the date of her arrest or release.

<aside class="note"><b>Sedition</b>: words or acts that stir people up against the government.</aside>

## When did she die?

One page says she died in 1941 of an illness. Another page gives 1941 in one place and 1925 in another. The sources do not agree.

## Why it matters

For Gulab Kaur we have only a few lines, some uncertain, from a few places. But they show that a village woman made the same choices as the men around her. A Punjabi book about her, *Gadar Di Dhee Gulaab Kaur*, was published in 2014.
