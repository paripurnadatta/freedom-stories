This is a story about a killing. It is told here plainly, because it is part of what happened in 1914, and because people still disagree about it.

## A granthi in Fraser Mills

Mewa Singh was born around 1881 in the village of Lopoke, in Amritsar district. In 1906 he went to British Columbia in Canada. He worked at the Fraser Mills in Coquitlam. He helped raise money for Vancouver's first gurdwara. He took the Khalsa vows and became a granthi, a reader of the Sikh scriptures. He also joined the Ghadar Party.

<aside class="note"><b>Granthi</b>: a person who reads aloud from the Sikh holy book and looks after the gurdwara.</aside>

## The summer of 1914

In July 1914, the *Komagata Maru* was waiting in Vancouver harbour. Mewa Singh was stopped at the United States border after a trip to buy guns. The guns were reported to be for the ship's passengers. He was held on a weapons charge and let go on 7 August after paying a fine of $50.

He said that an immigration inspector named William Hopkinson pressed him to give a statement against other Sikhs. Hopkinson was not satisfied with what he said.

On 5 September, an informer named Bela Singh shot two Sikhs inside the Vancouver gurdwara. One of them was its president. Bela Singh said that he had acted in self-defence. The pages do not say whether this was true.

## 21 October 1914

Bela Singh was to be tried at the courthouse. Mewa Singh was outside the courtroom. He shot William Hopkinson, who died. Then he did not resist when the police took him.

He pleaded guilty. The trial lasted about an hour and forty minutes. In court he said that he had acted to defend the honour of his religion after the shooting in the gurdwara.

His lawyer, Edward Montague Woods, called no witnesses, as Mewa Singh asked. Later the lawyer appealed for a lighter sentence but failed. In Canada the punishment for murder was death.

## The end

Mewa Singh was hanged at the jail in New Westminster on 11 January 1915. About 400 Sikhs gathered outside. His body was cremated at the Fraser Mills.

## Two ways to see it

Many Sikhs remember him as a martyr and call him "Bhai". His death is marked each year. A school in Lopoke was rebuilt and renamed for him in 2019. Some people have asked Canada to pardon him. They say the killing was a political act against racism. Others point out that a man was killed, and that under Canadian law it was murder.

Officials at the time believed there was a bigger plot behind it. They could not prove it. Charges against several members of the Shore Committee, which had helped the ship's passengers, were dropped for lack of evidence.

## Why it matters

A man was shot dead, and another man gave his life for it. Neither death is something to cheer. But the story shows how tense Vancouver was in 1914. A ship full of people had waited in the harbour for two months and had not been allowed to land.
