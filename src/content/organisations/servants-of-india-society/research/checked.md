# Checked ledger: Servants of India Society

From the shared `four-freedom-organisations-dossier.pdf` (another AI's research). Checked against
sources opened in this session (23 October 2026).

## What held up
- Founded by Gokhale in Pune on 12 June 1905, along with G. K. Devadhar, A. V. Patwardhan and
  N. A. Dravid (the society's own "Who We Are" page; Wikipedia adds Surendra Nath Banerjee to the
  co-founder list and says Gokhale left the Deccan Education Society to form it).
- Members had to take seven obligatory vows (INFLIBNET e-text, quoted in the dossier; the e-book
  domain itself would not open here, so this fact is carried from the dossier's direct quote and
  is used carefully).
- A training rhythm of four months' study in the Poona Home and six months' practical work;
  a trainee's salary of thirty rupees a month, fifty after the five years (Akshara digitised
  Gokhale interview; the same source says twenty-six members in total and an annual expense of
  twenty to forty thousand rupees raised mostly by Gokhale himself).
- Branches: Madras in 1910, Nagpur and Bombay in 1911, Allahabad in 1913; mobile libraries in
  Allahabad, Madras and Bombay; the Elementary Education League (the society's "Branches and
  Centers" page).
- During the 1912 Gujarat fodder famine, ten members and six volunteers worked for ten months
  (Akshara source).
- A contemporary account describes the aim as "Self-Government within the Empire" (Akshara).
- The Gokhale Institute of Politics and Economics followed in 1930 (Wikipedia).

## What could not be checked
- The INFLIBNET e-text (the "seven vows" source) and the Gokhale Institute digital repository
  would not open from this environment; the vow details rest on the dossier's direct quote, and
  the stories say "the rules say" rather than listing all seven, since I could not open the full
  vow text.
- A university digital-edition record (Jane Addams / Ramapo) contradicts itself: its date field
  says 1905-06-12 but its prose says founded in 1915. The society's own record supports 1905;
  the 1915 figure is not used anywhere.

## What was left out

  society's own pages, but the branches page did not repeat them, so they are mentioned only with
  their sources named.
- No invented portrait dates; the Gokhale 1909 portrait licence page (Commons) could not be
  opened (a 404 on the file name), so the portrait is not placed and a painting prompt stands in
