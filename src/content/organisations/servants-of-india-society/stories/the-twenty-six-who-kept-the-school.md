The school did not grow fast. It did not have one thousand members in its first year, the way a big city school might have had.

It had, at its highest, twenty-six members, in one year. Twenty-six people, in the whole of India, who had gone through the school's five years of training, and who were, in that year, working for the village people.

<aside class="note"><b>Word</b>: A <i>member</i> of the school is a person who has finished the school's five years of training, and who is, in that year, working for the village people. A <i>student</i> is a person who is still inside the five years.</aside>

The twenty-six did not all live in the same city. Some of them lived in Pune, where the school's first home was. Some of them lived in Madras, where the school had opened a branch in 1910. Some of them lived in Nagpur, and in Bombay, where the school had opened two more branches in 1911. Some of them lived in Allahabad, where the school had opened its last branch, in 1913.

## What the twenty-six did

The twenty-six did not all do the same work. The ones in the cities ran the school's quiet work in that city: the small hospital, the night classes for the mill workers, the league for the small children.

The ones who went to the villages did not sit in the city. They went to the village, and they stayed there, for a year at a time. They did not come home for the five years. They did not ask for a bigger pay. They asked for one small thing, from the village people: a page, each year, that said what the village had done for itself.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: the school's first record book lists the twenty-six by their names, and by the city they worked in. A later record lists the same twenty-six, but by the branch they belonged to. The two lists do not fight. They just put the same names under different headings.</aside>

## What came after the twenty-six

In the years after 1913, the school's number did not grow to one hundred, or two hundred. It stayed, for a while, close to the twenty-six.

But the twenty-six did something that a group of one hundred might not have done. They did not stop when the work was hard. They did not stop when the village people did not pay the school back. They did not stop when the government did not give them a bigger fund.

They kept the same quiet work going, in five cities, for the years the school was open. And that is what the school's first book called "service."

## Why it matters

The school did not become the biggest group in the country. It did not have the most members, and it did not have the most money.

It had the first twenty-six who worked the same quiet work, for five years at a time, in five different cities. And it kept them working, in the years after, until the school's work went on, in the same small way, to the people who had no other help.
