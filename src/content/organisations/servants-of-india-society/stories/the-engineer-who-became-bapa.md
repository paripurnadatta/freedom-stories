Amritlal Vithaldas Thakkar was born on 29 November 1869 in Bhavnagar, in Gujarat. His family was not rich. His father taught him to be kind to others.

Amritlal studied well. In 1886 he finished school and won a scholarship. In 1890 he qualified as an engineer at the engineering college in Pune.

## A working engineer

He worked as an engineer in Porbandar. He then went abroad to help build the first railway track in Uganda, in East Africa. Later he was the chief engineer in the state of Sangli. After that he joined the Bombay Municipality, the city's own council.

Then something happened in Bombay that changed his life.

## The colonies of the sweepers

In Bombay the city's waste was cleared by workers called scavengers. They did the dirtiest work in the city. Thakkar saw for the first time where they lived. Wikipedia says the places were filthy, and that he was shocked.

He made up his mind. He would spend the rest of his life helping these people.

<aside class="note"><b>Scavenger</b>: a person whose job was to clear away human waste and rubbish. Many were treated as "untouchables", a cruel custom that put some people far below others.</aside>

## Joining the Society

In 1914 he left his job. He joined the Servants of India Society, which Gokhale had begun nine years before. The Society's members gave their lives to public work and lived on very little money.

In the Society he worked for the rights of people then called "untouchables", and for tribal peoples.

## Many places, many schools

Thakkar travelled very widely. While the Constitution was being drawn up, he went to study how people lived in many places. Wikipedia lists forests in Assam, villages in Bengal, the Bhil areas of Gujarat, parts of Saurashtra, areas in Madras and Travancore, and the foothills of the Himalayas.

In 1922 he started the Bhil Seva Mandal, an organisation to help the Bhil people. In 1932 he became general secretary of the Harijan Sevak Sangh, a group Gandhi started to serve the people then called "untouchables".

<aside class="note"><b>Harijan</b>: the name Gandhi gave to people then called "untouchables". Many people in these communities have since preferred other names, such as Dalit.</aside>

Wikipedia says he used the word *adivasi*, "original inhabitant", for tribal people in the 1930s. The word is still used today.

## "Bapa"

Gandhi began to call him *Bapa*, which means father. In 1939 Gandhi called him "Father of Harijans" in one of his appeals. Everyone then knew him as Thakkar Bapa. When India's Constitution was being written, he headed a sub-committee of the Constituent Assembly that looked at the needs of tribal and other areas.

He died on 20 January 1951. The government of India issued a stamp in his honour in 1969.

## What lives on

The Society still has places that carry his name. The Society's Chennai branch runs the Thakkar Bapa Gurukulam at Nirgasimund, a school for the Toda and Kota tribal peoples. In Odisha the Society runs the Thakkar Bapa Ashram at Rayagada, which was set up in 1939. It has a tailoring centre and a typing school.

Later he became one of the leaders of the Servants of India Society itself.

## Why it matters

Thakkar's story shows what the Society was for. A man gave up his job as an engineer. He chose a life on very little money, in order to help people nobody else was helping.
