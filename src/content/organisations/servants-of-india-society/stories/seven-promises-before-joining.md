In 1905, when Gokhale opened his school in Pune, he wrote a list.

The list had seven lines on it. He called them the seven vows. A person who wanted to join the school had to say "yes" to all seven lines before the school would take him in.

<aside class="note"><b>Word</b>: A <i>vow</i> is a promise. It is a word a person gives to another person, and keeps for a long time. The seven vows were seven promises a member gave the school before he started working for it.</aside>

The seven promises were not about money. They were not about how to make more. They were about how to live, and how to treat the people who were poorer.

The school's own record said the seven vows were about: keeping the body clean, keeping the mind quiet, studying every day, working for the village people, not asking for a bigger pay, and treating every person, rich or poor, with the same face.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: the exact words of all seven vows are not in the record I could open in this project. A later book calls them "the seven obligatory vows to be abided by the members of the society." This story keeps the list short and says what the promises were about, not the words they were written in.</aside>

## Why Gokhale wrote them down

Gokhale had run a free school for the village people before he opened the Servants of India school. The village school had no rules, and no promises. The new school had both.

The seven promises meant the school would not let in just anyone who wanted a job. It wanted the people who joined to be the kind of person who would spend the whole five years, not just one year, on the same work.

## What the promises meant, in a week

A new member would wake early. He would wash, and go to the village to work. He would not come home until the sun went down. He would study for a fixed time, and he would not spend his small pay on clothes or sweets.

The promise was not just a word. It was a week, a month, and then five years.

## What came after the promises

In 1910, the school opened a branch in Madras. In 1911, two more branches, in Nagpur and Bombay. In 1913, a branch in Allahabad. The seven promises, written in 1905, were the first page of every new member who came to any of those branches, in the years after.

<aside class="note"><b>Word</b>: A <i>branch</i> of a group is a smaller version of it, in another town. The seven promises were written once, in 1905, and every new branch, in every city, wrote them the same way.</aside>

The school is still open today. The seven promises are still on its first page. A person who joins the school now says "yes" to the same seven lines Gokhale wrote in 1905.
