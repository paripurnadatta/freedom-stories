Gopal Krishna Gokhale died on 19 February 1915. He was forty-eight.

Two things from his last days are remembered. One is a wish for the Congress, which had split. The other is a plea to the members of the Society he had built.

## The day the Congress broke

In 1907 the Congress met in Surat. Two groups were there. One followed Gokhale. They wanted to work with the government and ask for reforms by lawful means. The other followed Bal Gangadhar Tilak, who wanted protest and boycott.

The two sides could not agree on who should be president. They fought over it, and the meeting turned into a scuffle.

Wikipedia says that when people ran towards Tilak on the platform, Gokhale went and stood next to him, to protect him. The session ended. The Congress had split into two parts.

<aside class="note"><b>Moderates and extremists</b>: These were the names given then to the two groups. The moderates, like Gokhale, asked politely. The extremists, like Tilak, pressed harder.</aside>

## Friends who disagreed

Gokhale and Tilak had walked a similar path. Both went to Elphinstone College. Both taught mathematics. Both belonged to the Deccan Education Society. Yet they disagreed about nearly everything that mattered in politics.

Even so, each respected the other. Wikipedia says that the two leaders had "great respect for each other's patriotism, intelligence, work and sacrifice".

Soon after the Surat meeting Tilak was arrested for sedition and sent to prison in Mandalay. Gokhale was in England at the time.

## Mending the Congress

After the split Gokhale worried about the future of the Congress. He thought the two groups ought to be brought together. For this he asked the advice of Annie Besant.

On his deathbed he told a friend, S. S. Setlur, that he wished to see the Congress united. Wikipedia says that he "reportedly" said this. After Gokhale died, Tilak wrote a warm tribute to him in his newspaper, *Kesari*.

## A plea to the workers

India Together tells of something else he said near the end. He told the members of his Society: "Don't waste your time in writing a biography or setting up a statue, but pour your whole soul into the service of India. Then only shall you be counted among her true and faithful servants."

This comes from a modern article. It does not say where the words were first written down.

## After Gokhale

The Society lost much of its strength after he died. V. S. Srinivasa Sastri became its head. It lived on, and still exists today.

Gandhi had come home from South Africa only weeks before, and Gokhale had wanted him to join the Society. When the news came, according to the mkgandhi.org biography, Gandhi mourned by going barefoot for a year.

## Why it matters

Gokhale had spent his life asking, again and again, for lawful change. He did not live to see India free. His last wishes were not for himself. He wanted the Congress to mend, and his workers to go on serving. A statue of him stands at Churchgate in Mumbai. But he had asked for work, not statues.
