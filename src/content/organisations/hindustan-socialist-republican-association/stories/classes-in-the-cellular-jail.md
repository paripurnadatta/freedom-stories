On 7 October 1930, a court in Lahore sentenced several young men to transportation for life. Bejoy Kumar Sinha and Shiv Verma were among those sentenced to transportation for life. Jaidev Kapoor was also sent to the island prison. Transportation meant being sent across the sea to a prison on the Andaman Islands, the place people called Kala Pani, the black water.

## Before the island

Sinha and Verma were first held at the jail in Rajahmundry, in Andhra Pradesh. Verma learned there that Chandrashekhar Azad, Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev and Rajguru were dead.

Kapoor was separated from his comrades before the journey. He asked to see Bhagat Singh and the others one last time. One account tells what happened next. Bhagat Singh gave Kapoor his new shoes. He said that the police would take them anyway. Kapoor kept the shoes as a souvenir.

<aside class="note"><b>A story told later</b>: the shoes come from one page about Kapoor's life. No second page repeats it, so it is told here as a remembered story.</aside>

## Hunger and then books

In 1933 Sinha was taken to the Cellular Jail. The prisoners there went on hunger strike to protest the way they were treated. Mahavir Singh, a young man from Etah district who had helped Bhagat Singh in Lahore, died on 17 May 1933. He was twenty-eight. Two others, Mohit Moitra and Manakrishna Nabadas, died during the strike as well. You can read about those weeks in the story of Batukeshwar Dutt.

<aside class="note"><b>Two dates</b>: one page says Sinha was sent to the Andamans in June 1933. The story of Batukeshwar Dutt, from other sources, has him on the ship that arrived on 23 January 1933. The sources do not agree.</aside>

In the end the officials agreed to some of the demands. The prisoners got soap, beds and food they could eat. They were allowed to study, to have books, and to speak with each other.

## The school

Sinha and Verma taught classes. They taught materialism, political science, world history, the state of the colonies and Indian society. Verma taught alongside two other prisoners, Satish Pakrashi and Bhupal Bose.

<aside class="note"><b>Materialism</b>: the idea that the world is made of matter and can be understood by study, and not by magic or fate.</aside>

So the prison became a school. The men who taught there had come to the island as prisoners of the government.

## The last strike

In 1937 there was another hunger strike. It lasted thirty-six days, and Shiv Verma and Hare Krishna Konar led it. The prisoners demanded to be sent back to the mainland. By 18 January 1938 the Andaman jail had been emptied of its political prisoners.

## What came after

Verma was brought back to India in September 1937 and freed in 1946. Sinha was brought back to the mainland in 1937 and released in 1938. He was arrested again in 1941 and held until 1945. In all he spent more than seventeen years in prison. Kapoor was freed after sixteen years, a couple of years before independence.

After 1947 all three joined the Communist Party of India. Sinha wrote a book about his time in the jail. It is called *In Andamans, the Indian Bastille*. Verma wrote a memoir, *Sansmritiyan*, and gathered papers about the revolutionaries.

## Why it matters

Most stories about the revolutionaries end on the gallows. These three lived on. They show what happened to the ones who did not die young, and what they did with the years: they studied, taught and wrote it all down.
