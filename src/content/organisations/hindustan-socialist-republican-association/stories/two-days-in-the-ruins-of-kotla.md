The Feroz Shah Kotla is an old fort in Delhi. In 1928 it lay in ruins. On 8 and 9 September 1928, revolutionaries from different parts of India met there. Their party was in trouble.

## A party with its leaders gone

Three years earlier, the Hindustan Republican Association, the HRA, had held up a train at Kakori. In December 1927 four of its leaders were hanged. Many others were in jail. 
Chandrashekhar Azad was still free. So were a number of younger men who had been waiting for their chance. Among them were Bhagat Singh and Sukhdev from Punjab, and Bhagwati Charan Vohra from Lahore. Shiv Verma and Jaidev Kapoor came from Kanpur. Bejoy Kumar Sinha, also called Vijay Kumar Sinha, was there too.

## Paying for the train ticket

Meetings cost money. To pay for this one, Sinha sold a return train ticket that belonged to a comrade, Phanindranath.

Sinha also spoke first. He said that the party "should acquire the form of an All-India one", and that its authority should be spread among many people. In plain words, power should be spread among many hands.

## What they decided

The meeting formed a Central Committee. Shiv Verma was one of its members, and he became the organiser for the United Provinces. Bhagwati Charan Vohra became the propaganda secretary. That meant he was in charge of spreading the party's ideas by writing and printing. He also prepared the party's manifesto. Sukhdev was made secretary of the Punjab branch.

The party was to have two wings, one for running the organisation and one for armed action. Jaidev Kapoor was given a place in the military wing.

Then came the change of name. The Hindustan Republican Association became the Hindustan Socialist Republican Association, the HSRA. One book says it was done "to give it a socialistic and secular colour". Another account says the new name was probably due largely to Bhagat Singh's influence. Chandrashekhar Azad led the party.

<aside class="note"><b>Socialist</b>: someone who believes that a country's wealth and land should be shared fairly, and that the government should make sure of it, not leave it to rich owners. <b>Secular</b>: not tied to any one religion.</aside>

## Rules the party made for itself

Two more decisions were taken, at Bhagat Singh's request.

First, the party would stop robbing wealthy people. The men tried for Kakori had been hurt by evidence that such people gave. So the party gave up that kind of robbery.

Second, the party decided to bomb members of the Simon Commission. This was the British group that had been sent to India without a single Indian member.

The party also agreed to work with the Communist International, the Moscow-based body of communist parties. Sinha had already been chosen in 1927 to go to Moscow to ask for help from the Soviets.

<aside class="note"><b>One source</b>: These last decisions come from a single page, which says they were taken at Bhagat Singh's request. The other pages on the Kotla meeting do not list them.</aside>

## A new way of speaking

The ideas in the new party's papers were different from the HRA's. Its later writings spoke of a revolution made by the masses, the working people, not by a few brave men. They spoke of the "dictatorship of the proletariat", a rule by workers, and of removing the "parasites from the seat of political power".

<aside class="note"><b>Proletariat</b>: working people who have no land or business of their own. <b>Parasite</b>: here, someone who lives off other people's work.</aside>

## After Kotla

The work began quickly. The party's first bomb-making classes began in Calcutta, with Jatin Das as the teacher. Shiv Verma and Jaidev Kapoor learned bomb-making in Agra in November 1928. Sinha and Bhagat Singh were given the task of building trust among revolutionaries from different states.

Events moved fast. On 30 October 1928 the Simon Commission came to Lahore. Lala Lajpat Rai led a protest and was hurt in a police lathi charge. He died on 17 November. On 17 December 1928 Rajguru and Bhagat Singh shot a police officer, John Saunders, in Lahore. It was a case of mistaken identity. The group had meant to shoot another officer.

In April 1929 Bhagat Singh and Batukeshwar Dutt threw two bombs in the Central Assembly in Delhi, and gave themselves up.

## Why it matters

The ruins of Kotla gave India's revolutionaries a new shape. The old HRA had been run by a few leaders, and it broke when they were taken. The HSRA tried to share power among many people, and to speak about the lives of poor people as well as about the British.

It did not last. Within three years Vohra, Azad, Bhagat Singh and Sukhdev were dead, and others were in jail. But the rules they wrote in September 1928 explain much of what they did next.
