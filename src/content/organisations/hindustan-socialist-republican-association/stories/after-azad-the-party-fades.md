A party can be broken in one blow. It can also wear away slowly. The Hindustan Socialist Republican Association did both.

## The verdict

On 7 October 1930, the court in the Lahore Conspiracy Case gave its judgment. Bejoy Kumar Sinha and others were sentenced to transportation for life. The party had already lost Bhagwati Charan Vohra, who died in an accident with a bomb on 28 May 1930.

## Alfred Park

Chandrashekhar Azad was the one leader who had never been caught. On 27 February 1931, Allahabad police found him in Alfred Park. There was a gunfight. Azad died there. The pages that tell this story say he shot himself rather than be taken. You can read more about Azad's last day elsewhere on this site.

A few weeks later, on 23 March 1931, Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev and Rajguru were hanged.

## A try at Meerut

Some members tried to start again. In December 1930 they gathered at Meerut to revive the HSRA. The attempt failed. In 1931 two of the men behind it, Yashpal and Daryao Singh, were arrested.

After Azad's death there was nobody left to lead the whole party. Small groups in different regions went on alone. Some of them threw bombs at British officials without any plan from the centre. One account says these regional groups carried on until 1935.

## The end of the party

When did the HSRA end? Pages disagree. One says that it faded after the failure at Meerut and the deaths of 1931. One page says the HSRA ended in 1940, when Udham Singh was hanged in London, because he was running a branch there. Others make no such claim.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: The date 1940 is given by only one page, and the other pages do not repeat it. A safe way to say it is this: the united party ended in 1931, and small groups went on for some years.</aside>

## The prisoners go on

Many of the party's members lived on in jail. Some were in the Andaman Islands. Others were in the jails of the mainland. When they came out, several of them joined the Communist Party.

## Why it matters

The HSRA lasted less than three years under its new name. It left behind a few papers, a few famous trials and a handful of survivors who wrote it down. Its members believed that a free India should be fairer, too. Whether they were right to use bombs is a question people still argue about. That question is the subject of another story on this site.
