Two brothers in Gujarat both dreamed of studying law in England. One of them had saved up enough money. He sent for his passport and his tickets.

They came by post. But the postman gave the packet to the other brother.

## The wrong brother

The brothers were Vallabhbhai and Vithalbhai Patel. Vallabhbhai was the younger one. He had saved the money and ordered the papers. The envelope was addressed to "V. J. Patel, Pleader". Both brothers shared those initials. So the postman handed it to Vithalbhai.

Vithalbhai said that he would go. If an elder brother followed his younger brother to England, he argued, people would talk. Vallabhbhai agreed. He even paid for Vithalbhai's stay.

Vithalbhai had to be sent secretly. In their community it was forbidden to cross the sea.

In London he joined the Middle Temple, to train as a barrister. The course took thirty-six months. He finished it in thirty, and he came first in his class. In 1913 he came back and became a well-known barrister in Bombay and Ahmedabad.

Vallabhbhai Patel went on to become Sardar Patel, one of the great leaders of the freedom movement.

## Not a Gandhi man

Vithalbhai joined the Congress. But he never quite accepted Gandhi's way of thinking or Gandhi's leadership.

When Gandhi stopped the movement after Chauri Chaura, Vithalbhai went with Chittaranjan Das and Motilal Nehru to form the Swaraj Party. Its plan was to go into the councils and work against the British from inside.

Meanwhile his brother Vallabhbhai stood on the other side, with the No-Changers, who wanted to keep the boycott.

## A chair for an Indian

In 1924 Vithalbhai was elected to the Central Legislative Assembly. In 1925 he became its president. This is a job like a Speaker's. The person in the chair keeps order, and decides who may speak. Before him the chair had been held by Sir Frederick Whyte. Vithalbhai was the first elected president, and the first Indian.

GKToday says the election was on 24 August 1925. The government had its own candidate, T. Rangachariar. Vithalbhai defeated him.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: The Wikipedia page on the Swaraj Party says Vithalbhai became president after the 1923 election. His own Wikipedia page says he was elected to the Assembly in 1924 and became president in 1925. GKToday agrees with 1925.</aside>

## Fair to all

Once he was in the chair, Vithalbhai put in place many rules that still exist today. He set the way the Assembly's business was to be done. In 1928 he created a separate office for the Assembly, so that it no longer depended on the government's own offices. He also made it a custom that the president takes no side in a debate. He may use his casting vote only to keep things as they are.

<aside class="note"><b>Casting vote</b>: the extra vote that a chairman may use when the votes for and against are equal.</aside>

## The last journey

Later Vithalbhai travelled in America and Europe. The mayors of New York, Boston, Chicago and other cities received him. He spoke for half an hour to each house of the California legislature.

He grew ill in Europe. As his last act in politics he signed a statement written by Subhas Chandra Bose. It called for a harder form of non-cooperation and for the Congress to be rebuilt. It said it would be unfair to expect Gandhi to change his lifelong beliefs.

Vithalbhai died in Geneva on 22 October 1933. His body was brought to Bombay by ship. More than three lakh people were there when he was cremated.

After his death a dispute arose about his money. A paper said that three-quarters of it should go to Bose, for India's cause in other countries. His brother Vallabhbhai raised questions about the paper. The case went to court. After more than a year the court said the money must go to the family. Vallabhbhai then gave it to a memorial trust in Vithalbhai's name.

## Why it matters

The two Patel brothers took opposite sides in the argument over council entry. Yet both gave their lives to the same cause. A postman's mistake sent Vithalbhai to England to train as a lawyer. Years later he sat in the chair of the Assembly.
