The party did not end the way it had started. It did not end at a big meeting, where the people who had built it in Gaya sat down and voted to stop.

It ended with a slow change, in the years after 1928. The people who had started it were gone. Das had died in 1925. And in the years after, the party's own members started to leave, one at a time, and go back to the Congress, the bigger party that had started the whole fight.

The party's name went quiet, in the years after 1928, not because of one big meeting, and not because of one vote. It went quiet because the work it had set out to do, in the room, was not going the way the people who had built it had hoped. And because the people who had built it, in Gaya in 1922, were gone, or gone quiet, in the years after.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: one account says the party was still working in 1928, and that it was "deeply disillusioned" by then, because its own members were leaving, and the work it had set out to do was not going the way it had hoped. The exact year the party's name stopped being used is not settled in every record, so this story keeps the fact in the main text and the uncertainty in the note.</aside>

The party's work did not stop when its name went quiet. The people who had carried it, in the council and in the bigger party, kept working. And the idea it had carried into the council, that India's own people should make its own laws, stayed in the record, even after the name was gone.

<aside class="note"><b>Word</b>: To <i>dissolve</i> a party means to stop using its name, and to let its members go back to the bigger party it had broken from. The Swaraj Party did not have one day that was the end. It had a few years, in which its members went back to the Congress, one at a time, and the name went quiet.</aside>

The party's work had been done in a room, in the capital, for a few years. And when the room went quiet, the work did not stop. It just moved, into the bigger party that had started the whole fight, and into the people who had carried it there.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: one account says the party dissolved quietly into the Congress by 1930, and that its Madras branch merged back into the Congress in 1934. The exact year the all-India party's name stopped being used is not settled in every record, so this story keeps the fact in the main text and the uncertainty in the note.</aside>

The party had been built in Gaya, in the last week of 1922. And it had gone quiet, in the years after 1928, without one big meeting that was its end. The two men who had built it, Das and Motilal Nehru, had carried its name between two rooms, in two cities, for the years it lasted.
