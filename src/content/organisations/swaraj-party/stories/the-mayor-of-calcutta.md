The Swaraj Party was famous for fighting the government inside the councils. In Calcutta it did something else too. It took over the running of the city.

## A city with a council

Calcutta had a corporation, a council for looking after the city, for a very long time. Its job was to keep roads, water and drains in order. Some members were chosen by the people who paid the city's taxes. Others were named by the government.

In 1923 a new law, the Calcutta Municipal Act, changed this. It was prepared under Surendranath Banerjee, who was then the minister in charge of local government in Bengal. The new law said that a mayor would be elected every year. It also gave women the right to vote in the city. The council was to have a mayor, a deputy mayor, five aldermen and ninety councillors. The daily work of the city would be run by a Chief Executive Officer.

<aside class="note"><b>Mayor</b>: the head of a city's council. <b>Councillor</b>: a person elected to sit on the council.</aside>

## The first mayor

In 1924 the Swarajists won the election to the Corporation. Chittaranjan Das became the first mayor of Calcutta under the new law. The council chose him again for the next year.

Das gave the post of Chief Executive Officer to Subhas Chandra Bose. Another Swarajist, Birendranath Sasmal, had much more experience. Das chose Bose anyway. Das was Bose's political teacher, and the two had been arrested together in December 1921.

Huseyn Shaheed Suhrawardy, a trade union leader who had studied law at Oxford, had joined the Swaraj Party in 1923. He became the deputy mayor in 1924.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: Banglapedia lists the first mayor, deputy mayor and chief executive officer as Das, Bose and Suhrawardy, in that order. Wikipedia says Suhrawardy was the deputy mayor and Bose the chief executive officer.</aside>

## A gazette for the city

In 1924 Das also started the *Municipal Gazette*, the official paper of the Calcutta Corporation. The year before, he had started a weekly paper called *Forward*. It spoke for the Swaraj Party.

Das was a poet as well as a lawyer. He published books of poems called *Malancha* and *Mala*, and in 1913 *Sagar Sangeet*, the "Songs of the Sea". He also started a monthly magazine named *Narayana*.

## What he gave away

Das had been one of the richest lawyers in Bengal. A few years before he died, he gave his house and the land around it to the nation. He wanted it used to help women. In 1950 the Chittaranjan Cancer Hospital opened on that ground. It has since become the Chittaranjan National Cancer Institute in Kolkata.

## His name on the map

Today Das's name is all over India. The wide road that joins north and central Kolkata, once called Central Avenue, is now Chittaranjan Avenue. A town in West Bengal is called Chittaranjan, and it is famous for its locomotive works. In Delhi there is a neighbourhood called Chittaranjan Park, where many Bengali families live.

## Why it matters

In the councils the Swarajists said no. In Calcutta they had to run something. They had to choose a team and keep a big city working. It was a short try, because Das died in 1925. But his name is still on the city.
