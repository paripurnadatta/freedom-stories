In 1923, a new party went to the polls for the first time. It won forty-five seats in the Central Legislative Assembly, the body that made laws for the whole country under the British.

<aside class="note"><b>Word</b>: The <i>Central Legislative Assembly</i> was one of two houses in the council that made laws for India after 1920. It was the lower house, and it had more seats than the other one.</aside>

Out of those forty-five seats, the party elected one of its own men as its leader in the council. His name was Vithalbhai Patel. He was not the president of the party. He was the president of the council, the man who sat in the chair, called the order, and kept the debates moving.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: one account says Vithalbhai Patel became the party's president in the Central Legislative Assembly after the 1923 election. The exact details of how the council's leadership was chosen are not settled in every record, so this story keeps the fact in the main text and the uncertainty in the note.</aside>

Vithalbhai Patel did not use the seat to ask for more power for himself. He used it to speak for the people who had no seat at all. Those were the farmers, the village people, the people who were not in the room but whose laws were being made in it.

The party's rule, when it sat in the council, was not to ask the British to give them more. It was to use the council's own tools. It would vote against the moves that hurt the people. And it would speak for them, one law at a time.

<aside class="note"><b>Word</b>: To <i>obstruct</i> in a council means to use the rules of the room to slow down or stop the government's moves. The Swaraj Party's way of working was to obstruct, not to protest from outside the room.</aside>

The party's two leaders worked from two different places. Das stayed in Bengal, where he had been working for years. Motilal Nehru stayed in the capital, and led the party's day-to-day work in the council. And Vithalbhai Patel, in his seat at the front of the council, carried the name of the party, and spoke for the people it was built to represent.

The work was quiet, and it was slow. And it was done in the room, not outside it.

<aside class="note"><b>Word</b>: To <i>call the order</i>, in a council, means to be the person who decides which question is answered next, and who keeps the debate moving from one to the next. Vithalbhai Patel was the one who called the order, in the council, in the years after 1923.</aside>

The party had not built the council. It had gone into the council the British had built, and it had used the council's own tools, to speak for the people who were not in the room. And that was the work it had set out to do, in the years after 1923.
