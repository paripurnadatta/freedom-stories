In February 1924 Gandhi came out of prison. He had spent two of his six years inside. He was ill, and had been operated on for appendicitis.

He found his followers divided. The Swarajists had gone into the councils. The No-Changers wanted to keep to the old programme. The No-Changers hoped Gandhi would back them.

## A surprise for the No-Changers

Gandhi did not do what they expected. He did not throw his weight behind the No-Changers. Instead he did everything he could to stop the Congress from splitting.

He made friendly gestures to the Swarajists, and let them take the lead on the political stage. The Viceroy wrote home about it. "Gandhi is now attached to the tail of Das and Nehru," he said. He added that Das and Nehru tried their utmost to make Gandhi think that he was "one of the heads, if not the head".

<aside class="note"><b>Viceroy</b>: the British ruler of India, who was sent from London.</aside>

## What the Swarajists asked for

The Swarajists wanted more places in the Congress's own offices. They also wanted to end the rule that Congress workers must spin khadi and do social service before they could hold office. Gandhi's supporters, such as Vallabhbhai Patel, Jawaharlal Nehru and Rajendra Prasad, did not like this.

Gandhi eased the rules about spinning. He gave some Swarajists important posts in the Congress. He also asked the Congress to stand by those Swarajists who sat in the councils, so that they would not be left alone in front of the British.

In December 1924 the Congress met at Belgaum. It was the only Congress meeting that Gandhi ever chaired. There the compromise was made official. The Swaraj Party was accepted as the Congress's own wing for the councils.

## To the hills

In May 1925 Das was ill. He went to Darjeeling in the hills to rest. He stayed at a house called "Step Aside", which belonged to Sir N. N. Sircar.

Gandhi went to see him and stayed with him for some days.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: The Wikipedia page on Das says the photograph shows Das and Gandhi on the Hill Cart Road. The Commons page for the file says that Annie Besant is also in the group.</aside>

Later Gandhi wrote that he left much more behind in Darjeeling than he had ever thought he would. "There was no end of my affection for Deshbandhu and my warm feeling for such a great soul," he wrote.

## The end

Chittaranjan Das died at Darjeeling on 16 June 1925. His funeral procession in Calcutta was led by Gandhi.

Gandhi spoke about him. "Deshbandhu was one of the greatest of men... He dreamed... and talked of freedom of India and of nothing else... His heart knew no difference between Hindus and Muslims and I should like to tell Englishmen, too, that he bore no ill-will to them."

## After Das

With Das gone, the Swarajists lost the leader who held them together in Bengal. They began to split over whether to take government posts. Within a year Motilal Nehru and his group walked out of the Assembly.

## Why it matters

Gandhi and Das had disagreed about the councils. Gandhi did not try to beat the Swarajists. He helped to keep them inside the Congress. When Das died, Gandhi led the funeral procession.
