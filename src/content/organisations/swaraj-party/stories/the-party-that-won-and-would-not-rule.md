The Swaraj Party had two great leaders. In June 1925 one of them, Chittaranjan Das, died.

After that the party began to come apart.

## Two ways to go on

Some of the party's leaders wanted to use the system they had entered. They were N. C. Kelkar, M. R. Jayakar, B. S. Moonje and Madan Mohan Malaviya. They were called the Responsivists. They said Swarajists should accept government posts and the offices of ministers. In this way, they argued, they could protect the interests of Hindus and win some concessions from the administration.

Motilal Nehru and his followers said no. They wanted to go on blocking the government in the councils. In March 1926, when the government refused to consider reform of the constitution, they walked out of the Assembly.

<aside class="note"><b>Minister</b>: a person who runs one department of a government. Under the 1919 law, Indian ministers were given some departments, such as education and health, in the provinces.</aside>

## A hard year

Then came the elections of 1926. Quarrels between Hindus and Muslims were growing sharper in the north. The party's own members were now divided. The Swarajists lost many seats in the Punjab and the United Provinces.

One place was different. In Madras the party still had real strength.

## Madras

The Swarajya Party of Madras Province was started in 1923. Its leaders were S. Satyamurti and S. Srinivasa Iyengar. It stood in every provincial election between 1923 and 1934. The only exception was 1930, when the Civil Disobedience Movement was on.

In 1926 it won more seats than any other party. In 1934 it did so again. Yet each time it refused to form a government.

Why? Under the 1919 law, the provinces were run under a plan called dyarchy. Ministers got only some departments, and the Governor kept the rest. The Swarajists did not believe in it. They would not take office under it.

<aside class="note"><b>Dyarchy</b>: "rule by two". Power in a province was split between elected ministers and officers chosen by the Governor.</aside>

## The end

By 1928 the party had been badly hurt. Many members had left, and others had quarrelled. A biography of Gandhi calls it "deeply disillusioned".

That year the Simon Commission came to India. It had no Indian member, and Indians of all parties were angry. The Congress drew up a plan for a constitution of its own. It was led by Motilal Nehru. Old political divisions were forgotten.

After the Lahore Congress of December 1929 declared that India's goal was complete independence, the Swarajists left the legislatures to join the Civil Disobedience Movement.

In 1934 the Madras party joined the All India Swarajya Party. That party merged with the Congress when it stood in the elections of 1935.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: Wikipedia says the party was set up on 1 January 1923 and ended in 1935. Its page also says the party was "defunct" between 1929 and 1937, as its members "quietly dissolved" into the Congress. GKToday says that the Swarajists walked out of the legislatures in 1930. The party had no single last day.</aside>

## Did it work?

How much did the Swaraj Party win? Its record is read in two ways.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: Wikipedia says that, apart from some heated debates and standoffs, the party's main aim of obstructing British rule failed. GKToday gives a longer list of gains. It says the Swarajists forced the Viceroy to use special powers to pass budgets, and that their pressure led to an inquiry into dyarchy.</aside>

## Why it matters

The Swaraj Party began as a quarrel inside the Congress. It ended by joining the Congress again. Its story asks a question that is still worth asking: is it better to stay out and refuse, or to go in and fight?
