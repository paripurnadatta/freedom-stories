In the Bengal Legislative Council a large group of members were Muslims. Chittaranjan Das saw a problem.

His plan was to block the government by voting against it. But he could not do that alone. He believed the Swarajists could not succeed without the whole-hearted help of the Muslim members. He had to win their trust.

## Unity first

Das believed in friendship between Hindus and Muslims. He spoke for unity and harmony between the communities. The times made this hard. The Hindu–Muslim unity of the non-cooperation days had become a memory. There were riots in several towns, and trust had given way to distrust.

Das tried to mend it in Bengal. He made an agreement with the Muslims of the province. People called it the Bengal Pact.

## The pact

On 16 December 1923 the Swaraj Party's group in the Council accepted its terms. The Muslims of Bengal welcomed it warmly.

Many Hindu leaders in Bengal did not. Middle-class Hindus, led by Surendranath Banerjea and Bipin Chandra Pal, fought it hard. They feared that the pact would weaken the Hindus' influence. They said that Das had given away the rights of Hindus. Even some moderate Hindu leaders thought he had gone too far in trying to win Muslim trust.

<aside class="note"><b>Pact</b>: an agreement between two sides, often written down and signed.</aside>

## Kakinada

That month the Congress met at Kakinada, in the south. At that time the town was called Cocanada. The Congress rejected the pact.

The Muslims of Bengal were bitterly disappointed. They felt it was the worst mistake in the Congress's history, and that it hurt the cause of Hindu–Muslim unity.

Das would not back down. He declared that they could take the Bengal Pact out of the resolutions, but they could not take Bengal out of the Congress. "Is Bengal untouchable?" he asked.

## Sirajganj

Das did not give up on the pact. In June 1924 the Bengal Provincial Congress Conference at Sirajganj accepted its terms. So Bengal's own Congress stood by it, even though the national Congress had said no.

## What came after

Das died in June 1925. Maulana Abul Kalam Azad said afterwards that, had Das lived, he would have changed the mood of the country. Azad also said that after Das died, some of his followers attacked his position, and that his declaration was set aside. In Azad's view, the Muslims of Bengal then moved away from the Congress, and the first seed of Partition was sown.

That is Azad's opinion, and others may see it differently. Suhrawardy, one of the Muslim leaders who had joined Das, turned after his death to Muslim nationalism and the Muslim League.

## Why it matters

The pact shows how hard it was to build trust between the two communities in those years. Das tried, and some of his own side were angry with him. His critics feared that Hindus would lose out. The Muslims of Bengal welcomed his offer. When the Congress said no, they saw it as a great mistake. People still argue about who was right.
