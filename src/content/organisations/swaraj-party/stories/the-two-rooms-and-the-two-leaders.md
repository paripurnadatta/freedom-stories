The party had two men who led it. They did not sit in the same room.

Das was in Bengal, where he had been working for years, and where the party's first meetings had been held. Motilal Nehru was in the capital, where the Central Legislative Assembly sat, and where the party's day-to-day work was done.

<aside class="note"><b>Word</b>: A <i>secretariat</i> is the small office where a party's day-to-day work is done, the room where letters are answered, and the next meeting is planned, and the money is kept.</aside>

The two men did not meet often. They wrote to each other. And they kept the party's name between them, in two different rooms, in two different cities.

One account says Motilal Nehru kept the party's office, in the capital, with only a few people working in it. The office was small, and it was not in the capital's biggest building. It was in a house called Anand Bhawan, and the account says he ran the party's day-to-day work from there, with a small team.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: one account says Motilal Nehru kept the party's secretariat at Anand Bhawan, and that it was a small office, with only a few people working in it. The exact details of the office, and of how many people worked there, are not settled in every record, so this story keeps the fact in the main text and the uncertainty in the note.</aside>

The party did not have a big building. It had two men, in two rooms, who kept its name moving.

Das's room in Bengal was the party's voice in the province. And Motilal Nehru's room in the capital was the party's voice in the council. The two rooms, and the two men between them, kept the party's name alive, even when the two men did not meet.

The party's work, in the years after 1923, was not done in one room. It was done in two rooms, in two cities, by two men who did not sit at the same table. And that was the way the party stayed one party, through the years it lasted: not by meeting in one place, but by keeping its name moving, in two places, at the same time.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: one account says the party's work, in the years after 1923, was split between the two rooms, in Bengal and in the capital, and that the two men kept the party's name between them, by writing to each other. The exact details of how often they wrote, and what they wrote about, are not settled in every record, so this story keeps the fact in the main text and the uncertainty in the note.</aside>
