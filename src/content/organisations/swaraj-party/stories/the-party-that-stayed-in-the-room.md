The party had one rule that split its own people. The rule was: go into the councils, and work from inside them.

But not everyone in the Congress agreed. A group of people, who followed a man named Mahatma Gandhi, said no. They called themselves the No-Changers, and they said the councils were the wrong room, and that the party should not sit in them at all.

<aside class="note"><b>Word</b>: The <i>No-Changers</i> were the people in the Congress who said the party should not go into the councils. They followed Gandhi's idea that the councils themselves were part of the wrong system, and that sitting in them would only make the system look better than it was.</aside>

In September 1923, the Congress held a special meeting. It said the party could go into the councils, and contest the election. The No-Changers did not win that vote. The party stayed in the room.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: one account says the special Congress session in September 1923 allowed the Swarajists to contest the election. The exact date and the exact wording of the resolution are not settled in every record, so this story keeps the fact in the main text and the uncertainty in the note.</aside>

After the vote, the party went to the election. It won forty-five seats in the Central Council. And it kept working, in the room it had chosen to stay in, for the next few years.

The No-Changers did not stop arguing. They kept saying the councils were the wrong room, and they kept saying the party should stay out. But the party had already gone in. And it kept working, in the room, one law at a time, for the next few years.

The party's work in the room was quiet, and it was slow. It was not a war cry, and it was not a march. It was the work of a group of people who had chosen to stay in the room, and to use the rules of the room, to speak for the people who were not in it.

<aside class="note"><b>Word</b>: To <i>contest</i> an election means to put your people's names on the list of candidates, and to ask the voters to choose you. The party had decided, at the special session, to contest the election, and to stay in the room where the laws were made.</aside>

And the people who voted for the party, in 1923, did not all know why it had chosen to stay in the room. They only knew that it had gone into the council, and that it was speaking for them, one law at a time, in the room where the laws were made.

<aside class="note"><b>Word</b>: A <i>special session</i> of a party is a meeting that is called before the regular one, to answer one big question. The Congress called a special session in 1923, to answer the question of whether the Swaraj Party could sit in the councils.</aside>

The party stayed in the room, even when the people outside it said it should not. And that was the choice that gave the party its name, and its work, and its quiet years in the council.
