On 4 February 1922 a crowd of two or three thousand people marched towards a market at Chauri Chaura, a small town in the Gorakhpur district. They were part of the non-cooperation movement that Mahatma Gandhi led. They had come to protest against high food prices and the sale of liquor.

The police were sent to stop them. They fired on the crowd, and three people were killed. The crowd pushed the police back into their station. The crowd then set the building on fire. About twenty-two policemen died inside it.

## Gandhi stops the movement

Gandhi was shocked. He felt that the blame was partly his. He had called people to protest, he thought, without teaching them enough about non-violence.

On 12 February, eight days after Chauri Chaura, he stopped the movement all over India. He also fasted for five days.

Many Congress workers could not believe it. Jawaharlal Nehru and most of the workers were in jail when the news came. They felt the decision was hasty and wrong.

A few months later Gandhi was arrested too. He was sentenced to six years in prison. The movement had stopped, and its leader was in a cell.

<aside class="note"><b>Non-cooperation</b>: a way of protesting without violence. People refused to work with the government. They left government schools and jobs, and stopped buying foreign cloth.</aside>

## A man who said it was a mistake

Chittaranjan Das was a lawyer from Calcutta. People called him Deshbandhu, which means "friend of the country". He had given up his well-paid law work to join the movement, and he had been arrested in December 1921.

His wife, Basanti Devi, had joined in too. That year she led a small group of volunteers who sold khadi, hand-spun cloth, on the streets of Calcutta. The police arrested her, though they let her go by midnight. Her arrest gave the protest new strength. On 10 December 1921 the police arrested Das and Subhas Bose.

Das thought that stopping the movement was a serious mistake. Banglapedia says it left political workers badly discouraged. Das did not want to sit and sulk. He had a plan.

## The plan

At that time the British government ran legislative councils. These were small parliaments. Some of the members were chosen by Indian voters, though only a small part of the people could vote. The councils had little real power.

The Congress had decided to stay away from them. Gandhi and most Congress leaders said the councils were rigged. Many members were not elected at all. They were friends of the government, and the councils only approved what the Viceroy wanted.

Das said: let us go in. Once inside, Congress members would vote against the government every time. Banglapedia gives his plan in these words: "uniform, continuous and consistent obstruction". The government would not be able to pass its laws or its budgets in peace. A biography of Gandhi puts the aim in these words: "to carry the fight into the enemy's camp".

<aside class="note"><b>Obstruction</b>: blocking something. In a council, it means voting down the government's plans and making speeches against them, so that its work cannot go on smoothly.</aside>

## Gaya

In December 1922 the Congress held its yearly meeting at Gaya, in Bihar. Das was the president of that meeting. He brought forward his plan for entering the councils.

Others in the Congress did not agree. Vallabhbhai Patel, C. Rajagopalachari and many more wanted to keep to the old programme. Because they wished no change, people called them the No-Changers. They believed that the boycott of the councils was part of the very idea of non-cooperation. To go in, they felt, was to take part in the government's own game.

Das lost the vote. He gave up the presidency of the Congress.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: The exact numbers of the vote at Gaya are left out, because no source we could open gives them. All the sources agree on the result: the plan to enter the councils was defeated.</aside>

## A new party

Das did not leave the Congress. Instead he started a party inside it. At the end of December 1922 he, Motilal Nehru and N. C. Kelkar formed the Congress-Khilafat Swarajya Party. It is better known as the Swaraj Party. Das was its president. Motilal Nehru was one of its secretaries.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: Wikipedia gives the party's birthday as 1 January 1923. In another place the same page says it was formed in December 1922, and Banglapedia says Das started it after the Gaya meeting. So the party was born around the turn of the year.</aside>

Others joined them. Huseyn Shaheed Suhrawardy, Birendranath Sasmal and a young man called Subhas Chandra Bose came from Bengal. Vithalbhai Patel, the older brother of Vallabhbhai Patel, joined too.

<aside class="note"><b>Swaraj</b>: self-rule. It means a country governed by its own people.</aside>

## Both sides stay

The Swarajists and the No-Changers argued fiercely. But they remembered what had happened at Surat in 1907, when the Congress broke in two. Neither side wanted that again. So they agreed to stay in the same Congress and work in their own ways. Wikipedia says that there was no basic difference between them. Both wanted swaraj. They disagreed about the road.

In May 1923 the All India Congress Committee met in Bombay. Das won a majority for council entry. Ninety-six members voted for it, and seventy-one against.

In September 1923 a special meeting of the Congress was held in Delhi, and Abul Kalam Azad was in the chair. It decided that Swarajists could stand for election and still remain Congress members.

## Two months

The elections were in November 1923. That left the Swarajists barely two months to campaign.

They won well. The government's own Central Legislative Assembly in Delhi now had a solid group of Swarajist members. The party won a good number of seats in the provincial councils too. In the Central Provinces it had a majority. Motilal Nehru would lead it in the Assembly, and Das would lead it in the Bengal council.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: Wikipedia says the Swarajists won 45 seats out of 145 in the councils. GKToday says they won 42 of the 101 elected seats in the Central Legislative Assembly. The 1919 law gave the Assembly 145 members, 104 of them elected. The sources count in different ways. Both agree that the Swarajists won a large block.</aside>

## Why it matters

The Swarajists and the No-Changers did not agree, and each side had a fair point. The No-Changers said the councils were rigged and entering them was a trap. The Swarajists said that staying out left Indians with no voice in the places where laws were made.

Both groups kept their eyes on the same goal. That is why the Congress held together. A lost vote at Gaya had not ended it. It had given India two ways of working for freedom at the same time.
