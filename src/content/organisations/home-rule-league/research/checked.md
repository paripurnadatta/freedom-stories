# Checked ledger: Home Rule Leagues

From the shared `four-freedom-organisations-dossier.pdf` (another AI's research). Checked against
sources opened in this session (23 October 2026).

## What held up
- Two separate leagues. Tilak's was set up at the Bombay provincial congress at Belgaum in April
  1916, working in Maharashtra, Karnataka, the Central Provinces and Berar; Besant's All India
  Home Rule League was founded at Adyar, Madras in September 1916, with charge of the rest of
  India. The two avoided friction by demarcating their areas (the INFLIBNET e-text quoted in the
  dossier; Wikipedia's "Home rule" article and Tilak's page agree on the 1916 founding and the
  rest-of-India split, and give a membership growth from 1,400 in April 1916 to about 32,000 in
  1917, though that last figure comes only from the Tilak article).
- Besant's league was centred on the newspaper New India, and she was its editor (Wikipedia).
- On 16 June 1917 Besant was confined with B. P. Wadia and George Arundale in a house at Ooty;
  they were released on 17 September 1917, after 93 days (the INFLIBNET e-text quoted in the
  dossier; Wikipedia's Annie Besant page says "arrested and interned at a hill station in June
  1917, freed in September 1917" without the names or the 93-day count).
- Besant was elected president of the Indian National Congress that met at Calcutta in December
  1917 (the INFLIBNET e-text quoted in the dossier; Wikipedia's Besant page says "assumed the
  role of Congress president in December 1917 for a one-year term").
- The aim was self-government inside the empire ("dominion status within the British Empire"),
  not full independence, as a 1916 Madras High Court judgment phrases it (the Indian Kanoon
  judgment page opened but is a 137,000-character document; the dossier's exact quote is
  consistent with it).
- The movement faded after 1917, stalled by the rise of Gandhi's mass movement (Wikipedia's
  "Home rule" article).
- A 6 September 1917 letter from the Home Rule League secretaries at 3, City Road, Allahabad
  asked the branches to send the names of their office-bearers and the number of members on their
  rolls (the Nehru Archive document; the exact wording comes from the dossier's direct quote,
  since the archive page would not open from this environment).

## What could not be checked
- The INFLIBNET e-text (H1), the Nehru Archive letter (H2), the Madras High Court judgment (H3,
  opened only in part) and the Jane Addams digital edition (S6, different subject but the same
  self-contradicting pattern) could not all be fully opened here; facts that rest on them are
  used with the source named in the story, and the disputed ones carry "Two versions" notes.
- The "23 April 1916" date for Tilak's league comes only from the INFLIBNET e-text quoted in the
  dossier; Wikipedia gives the looser "April 1916" at Belgaum. The main text says April 1916 and
  the note carries the exact 23 April version.

## What was left out
- The boy scouts at Central Station, Madras (the Commons / IWM photograph) is used as a gallery
  item only; the photo shows people waiting for Tilak, not Tilak himself, so no story claims to
  show him in it.
- No invented meeting scenes; the 93-day confinement is stated plainly, no dramatized jail cell.
