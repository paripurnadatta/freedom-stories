The word Home Rule did not mean India leaving the empire. And it did not mean India's own people, on their own, making all the laws, in a country that was not part of the empire at all.

It meant one thing, and one thing only. It meant that India's own people should make India's own laws. And it meant that the empire should keep the emperor on the throne, and keep India inside it, and keep the word Home Rule, in the rooms where the laws were made.

<aside class="note"><b>Word</b>: <i>Home Rule</i>, in the years just after the First War, meant self-government, for India, inside the British empire. It did not mean full independence, from the empire. It meant that the people of a place should make the laws for that place, and that the emperor should stay on the throne, and that the place should stay inside the empire.</aside>

The word came from Ireland, where people had used it, in the years before the war, to ask for the same thing. It meant that the people of a place should make the laws for that place. And that the place should stay inside the empire, and keep its own work, in its own rooms.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: one account says the word Home Rule, in India, in the years after the First War, meant self-government, inside the empire, and not full independence, from it. The exact way the word was used, in the years after 1917, in the places the two leagues had reached, is not settled in every record, so this story keeps the fact in the main text and the uncertainty in the note.</aside>

The two leagues, in 1916, used the word, and they did not argue, in the open, about what it meant. And that was the quiet thing about the word: it meant one thing, in the two leagues, in the two places, in the one country, in the one year, in the middle of the war.

<aside class="note"><b>Word</b>: To mean <i>one thing</i>, for a word, means that the word does not have two meanings, and that it does not change, from one person to the next. The word Home Rule, in 1916, meant one thing, in the two leagues, in the two places, in the one country.</aside>

And that was the word that the two leagues, in the years just after the First War, carried, in two different places, in the same country, in the same year. And it was the word that the three of them, in the small house, in the hills, in the summer of 1917, could not say, in the room they had been asked to stay in.

The word did not mean two things, in the two leagues, in the two places, in the one country. And it did not change, from one person to the next, in the years just after the war. It meant one thing, and one thing only, in the middle of the war, in the rooms where the laws were made.
