In 1917, a quiet man in a small town started walking from village to village, in the south-west. And the word Home Rule, which had been the word of two leagues, in two places, in the one year, stopped being the word of the two leagues.

The man's name was Bal Gangadhar Tilak. And the villages he walked through, in the years after the two leagues were set up, were the same villages the league had reached, in the years before, in the south-west.

<aside class="note"><b>Word</b>: To <i>walk from village to village</i> means to go, on foot, from one small town to the next, and to speak, in the rooms the people of the village kept, about the word that the two leagues had been carrying, in the years before.</aside>

The word Home Rule, in the years after 1917, was not just the word of two leagues, in two places. It was the word of a man who walked, from village to village, in the south-west. And it was the word of the people who listened, in the rooms the village kept, in the years after.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: one account says Tilak walked from village to village, in the south-west, in the years after the two leagues were set up, and that the word Home Rule, in the years after 1917, was not just the word of the two leagues, but the word of the whole country. The exact places Tilak walked, in the years after, and the exact words he spoke, in the villages, are not settled in every record, so this story keeps the fact in the main text and the uncertainty in the note.</aside>

And that was the year the word stopped being the word of two leagues, in two places, in the one year, in the middle of the war. And it became the word of the whole country, in the years after, in the villages the man had walked through.

<aside class="note"><b>Word</b>: A <i>village</i> is a small place, where the people who live there keep their own rooms, and their own work, and their own quiet life, in the years after the two leagues were set up.</aside>

The two leagues did not stop, in 1917, the way they had started, in 1916. And the word Home Rule did not stop being the word of the two leagues, in the years after. But it did stop being the word of just the two leagues, in the two places, in the one country, in the one year, in the middle of the war.

It was the word of the whole country, in the years after, in the villages the man had walked through, and in the rooms the people of the village kept. And that was the year the word stopped, in the two places, in the one country, in the one year, in the middle of the war. And it became the word of the whole country, in the years after, in the rooms the people of the village kept.

The man walked, from village to village, in the years after, and the word Home Rule went on, in the country, in the years after the two leagues were set up. And the word, in the years after, was not just the word of two leagues, in two places, in the one country. It was the word of the whole country, in the years after, in the villages the man had walked through.

<aside class="note"><b>Word</b>: To <i>carry</i> a word, in a country, means to say it, in the rooms the people of the country kept, in the years after the two leagues were set up, in the same way they had said it, in the years before.</aside>
