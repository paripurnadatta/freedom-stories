In June 1917, a woman who had lived in India for many years was asked to stay in a small house. The house was in a hill station, and she was told not to come out of it.

The hill station was called Ooty, and it was in the hills, to the west of Madras. And the woman's name was Annie Besant. And she had set up the second Home Rule League, in Adyar, in Madras, the year before.

<aside class="note"><b>Word</b>: To be <i>confined</i> to a house means to be told to stay in it, and not to come out of it. It is not the same as being in jail, where the door is locked, and a guard is at the gate. It is a quiet kind of being kept in, where the person is not locked in a room, but is not free to go to the town, or to the meeting where the word Home Rule was being spoken.</aside>

<aside class="note"><b>Word</b>: A <i>hill station</i> is a small town, in the hills, that people in the country go to, in the summer, to be away from the heat. Ooty, in the hills to the west of Madras, was one of the hill stations in the country, in the years just after the First War.</aside>

Besant had been living in Adyar, in Madras, in the years before 1917. And she had been printing the league's newspaper, from the small house there, in the years before the war.

<aside class="note"><b>Word</b>: To <i>print</i> a newspaper means to make its pages, on paper, one sheet at a time, in the machine that the league kept, in the small house, in Adyar, in the years before the war.</aside>

Besant was not the only one who was asked to stay in that house. Two other people were asked to stay in it, too. Their names were B. P. Wadia, and George Arundale. And the three of them stayed, together, in the small house, in the hills.

On 16 June 1917, the three of them were asked to stay. And they stayed, in the small house, in the hills, until September.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: one account says Besant, Wadia, and Arundale were asked to stay in the small house, in Ooty, in June 1917, and that they were let out of it in September, after ninety-three days. The exact names of the two men who were with her, and the exact number of days they stayed, are not settled in every record, so this story keeps the fact in the main text and the uncertainty in the note.</aside>

## The paper that did not stop

While the three of them stayed in the small house, in the hills, the league's paper kept going. The paper was called New India. And it was printed, in the small house in Adyar, in Madras, where the league had set it up, in September 1916, the year before.

Besant had been the one to write the pages, in Adyar, in the years before the war. And while she was in the small house, in the hills, the pages did not stop. They were written, and printed, and sent out, to the towns the league had reached.

## The work that went on, while the three were in the house

While the three of them were in the small house, in the hills, the two leagues' work did not stop. The first league, in the south-west, kept meeting, in the rooms it had built, in the years after it was set up.
