The second league, the one set up in Adyar, in Madras, had a newspaper. And the woman who had built it sat at its desk, in Adyar, in the years before the war.

The newspaper's name was New India. And Besant edited it, in the years before the war, from the small house in Adyar where the league's work was done.

<aside class="note"><b>Word</b>: To <i>edit</i> a newspaper means to be the person who decides what goes into it, and how it is put together, and when it goes to the press. Besant was the one who decided what went into New India, in the years before the war.</aside>

When the three of them were in the house, in the hills, in the summer of 1917, the pages did not stop. And the word Home Rule, in the pages, in the years after the war, was not just the word of the league in Adyar.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: one account says the league's newspaper, New India, was edited by Besant, from Adyar, in the years before the war. The exact details of how the newspaper was run, while the three of them were in the house, in the hills, are not settled in every record, so this story keeps the fact in the main text and the uncertainty in the note.</aside>

The newspaper did not just carry the word Home Rule. It carried the work of the league, in the towns it had reached, and in the places the word had gone, in the years before the war.

<aside class="note"><b>Word</b>: To <i>carry</i> the word, in a newspaper, means to print it, on the pages, one sheet at a time, in the machine that the league kept, in the small house, in Adyar, in the years before the war.</aside>

And it carried the quiet thing the three of them had not been able to do, in the small house, in the hills, in the middle of the summer: the work of the league, in the room it was built to be in.

<aside class="note"><b>Word</b>: A <i>press</i> is the machine that prints a newspaper's pages, on paper, one sheet at a time. The league's press, in Adyar, in the years before the war, was the place where New India's pages were made, and where the word Home Rule was printed, on the paper, and sent out, to the towns the league had reached.</aside>
