In September 1917, a letter went out from the league's small office, in a city in the north. The office was at a place called City Road, and it was the place where the league's work was kept, in the years after the two leagues were set up.

The letter asked the branches to send the names of their office-bearers. And it asked for the number of people on their rolls.

<aside class="note"><b>Word</b>: An <i>office-bearer</i> is the person who keeps the records, and who runs the day-to-day work, of a small group. The league's letter, in September 1917, asked the branches to send the names of their office-bearers, and the number of people on their rolls.</aside>

The branches, in the towns the league had reached, sent the names, and the numbers, back to the small office, in the city in the north.

And the office kept the lists, and kept them, in the years after, in the room where the league's work was done.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: one account says the league's letter, in September 1917, was sent from a small office, at a place called City Road, in a city in the north, and that it asked the branches to send the names of their office-bearers, and the number of people on their rolls. The exact wording of the letter, and the exact places the branches were in, are not settled in every record, so this story keeps the fact in the main text and the uncertainty in the note.</aside>

The letter did not just ask for the names, and the numbers. It asked for the quiet work, in the branches, in the towns the league had reached. And the branches, in the years after the two leagues were set up, kept the work going, in the rooms they had built, in the one country.

<aside class="note"><b>Word</b>: A <i>branch</i> of a group is a smaller version of it, in another town, run by the people there. The league's branches, in the years after 1917, kept the work going, in the towns the league had reached, in the rooms they had built.</aside>

The letter was a small thing, and it was not loud. And it was not a war cry, and it was not a flag. But it was the quiet work the two leagues did, in the years just after the First War, in the rooms they had built, in the one country.

<aside class="note"><b>Word</b>: A <i>roll</i> is the list of the people who are members of a small group. The league's letter, in September 1917, asked the branches to send the names of their office-bearers, and the number of people on their rolls.</aside>

The work of the league, in the years after 1917, was not loud, and it was not a march. It was the quiet work, in the branches, in the towns the league had reached, in the rooms they had built. And the letter, in September 1917, was the small thing that kept the names, and the numbers, in the room where the work was done.

The exact places the branches were in, in the years after, are not settled in every record, so this story keeps the fact in the main text and the uncertainty in the note.</aside>

The letter did not carry the word Home Rule, in the years after, the way the newspaper did, in Adyar, in the years before. And it did not carry the quiet work of the two leagues, in the two places, in the one country, in the years after the two leagues were set up.

<aside class="note"><b>Word</b>: To <i>carry</i> the word, in a letter, means to say it, in the rooms the people of the country kept, in the years after the two leagues were set up, in the same way they had said it, in the years before.</aside>

And that was the quiet thing the two leagues did, in the years just after the First War, in the rooms they had built, in the one country, in the years after the two leagues were set up.
