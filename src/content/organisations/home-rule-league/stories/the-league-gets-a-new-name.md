In August 1917 the British promised India self-government, step by step. For the Home Rule Leagues, that promise was both a win and a problem.

## A promise that divided

Many moderate members were pleased with the promise. They believed Britain would now slowly give Indians a bigger share in running the country. So they stopped working for the league, and some gave up their membership. The league itself agreed to stop spreading into new places.

Annie Besant was mostly satisfied with the promise too. But she could not make up her mind. Sometimes she seemed to accept the planned reforms, and sometimes to oppose them.

## Tilak goes to England

In September 1918 Bal Gangadhar Tilak left for England. He had a court case to fight there.

A British writer named Valentine Chirol had written a book called Indian Unrest. In it he called Tilak the "father of Indian unrest". Tilak sued him for libel. He lost the case and much money.

<aside class="note"><b>Libel</b>: writing something false that harms a person's good name. A person who is libelled can sue in court.</aside>

Gandhi asked Indians to give money to a Tilak Purse Fund, to help pay his costs. Gandhi and Tilak did not agree about everything. But Gandhi respected his courage.

With Tilak away, and Besant unsure, the movement had lost its leaders.

## A new leader with a new method

Meanwhile a new kind of leader had appeared. Gandhi had led farmers at Champaran in Bihar and at Kheda in Gujarat against the British. Those victories made him a national hero. His method was satyagraha: mass protest, but without violence.

<aside class="note"><b>Satyagraha</b>: Gandhi's name for his way of struggle. It meant resisting injustice in large numbers, but without violence.</aside>

Many young people left the Home Rule Leagues to follow him.

## 28 April 1920

In April 1920 Gandhi went to meetings of the Home Rule League in Bombay and Ahmedabad. On 28 April 1920 he joined the All India Home Rule League and became its president.

On 1 August 1920 Tilak died in Bombay.

That year the Congress voted on Gandhi's plan of non-cooperation. People would refuse to work with the British government. Annie Besant spoke against it. She had fought within the law all her life, and she did not trust mass law-breaking, whatever the cause. She and a few others stood against the plan, but it was carried.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: The Congress party's page on Besant says she opposed the plan at the Congress of 1920 "in Lahore". The Gandhi chronology says the non-cooperation resolution was carried at a special session of the Congress in Calcutta in September 1920.</aside>

## The Swarajya Sabha

Soon the league with Gandhi as its president sent out a circular letter. It told its members to carry out the Congress resolution. On 24 October 1920 Gandhi wrote about the league's new form. It had been renamed the Swarajya Sabha. Swarajya means self-rule.

Not everyone stayed. The next day, Gandhi wrote to Muhammad Ali Jinnah about his resignation from the Swarajya Sabha.

The league's branches and workers now became part of the non-cooperation movement. The league was also joined to the Congress.

## Why it matters

The Home Rule League lasted only four years under its own name. But it did not simply disappear. Its branches, its workers and its habit of year-round politics passed into the hands of the next great movement.
