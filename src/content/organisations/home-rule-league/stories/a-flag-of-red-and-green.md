Look closely at the flag in the picture. It is not the flag India flies today. It has red and green stripes, eight stars, a moon, and a small British flag in the corner.

This was the flag of the Home Rule movement. In 1916, Annie Besant and Bal Gangadhar Tilak took it up for their campaign.

## Reading the flag

The picture above is a modern drawing. An artist drew it in 2006, copying an older picture of the flag whose source is not known. Here is how the flag was made up.

It had nine stripes across it. Five were red and four were green.

In the top left corner was the Union Jack. That is the flag of Great Britain. It showed what the movement wanted. It did not ask Britain to leave. It asked for India to become a dominion, ruling itself but still inside the British Empire.

<aside class="note"><b>Dominion</b>: a country inside the British Empire that made its own laws, like Canada or Australia at that time.</aside>

In the top right corner was a white crescent moon and a white star.

Seven more white stars ran in a slanting line across the flag. They were set out like the Saptarishi, the group of seven stars that English speakers call the Great Bear. These stars are sacred to Hindus.

<aside class="note"><b>Saptarishi</b>: a group of seven bright stars in the northern sky.</aside>

## A flag in the hills

In June 1917 the government of Madras sent Annie Besant away to a hill station, Ootacamund, and kept her there for three months. While she was held there, she flew a red and green flag. It was an act of defiance.

## The first ban

Not everyone in government liked the new flag. A magistrate in Coimbatore, in the Madras Presidency, banned its use.

That ban was the first time a government in India had acted against a nationalist flag. People argued about it in public. What is a national flag for? Why does it matter so much? The ban started a debate that was bigger than one district.

<aside class="note"><b>Magistrate</b>: an official who keeps order in a district and can hear small court cases.</aside>

## Why it matters

The Home Rule movement faded after 1918. Its flag was never the flag of a free India.

But it showed what the movement hoped for, all on one piece of cloth: Indians ruling themselves, with the British King still at the top. The ban in Coimbatore showed something too. To the government, even a flag could be dangerous. A piece of coloured cloth had become a political question.
