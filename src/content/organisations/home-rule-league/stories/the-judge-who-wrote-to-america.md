S. Subramania Iyer was born on 1 October 1842. He became a lawyer, then a judge of the Madras High Court. He retired in 1907. People called him the Grand Old Man of South India.

The British had made him a knight. That is why people called him "Sir". He explained later how it happened. Indian judges could not become Chief Justice for good. So any Indian judge who did the job, even for a short time, was made a knight instead. He had asked for the badge to be sent by post.

## The honorary president

In September 1916 Annie Besant started the All India Home Rule League in Madras. The old judge agreed to be its honorary president. He wrote to the Congress, asking it to welcome the new league as a son and a co-worker.

Then, on 16 June 1917, the Governor of Madras ordered Besant's arrest. She and two helpers, George Arundale and B. P. Wadia, were sent away to Ooty. The judge started a fund to pay their legal costs. He also began a campaign for their release.

## A letter across the ocean

On 24 June 1917, he wrote a letter to Woodrow Wilson, the President of the United States.

He reminded Wilson that the Congress and the Muslim League had asked for self-government for India. The British had given no answer. Indian soldiers were dying in France, in Mesopotamia and at Gallipoli. They gave their lives, he wrote, for a nation that ruled them against their will.

He listed his complaints. Foreign officials paid themselves huge salaries. Taxes were crushing, and Indians had no say in them. Thousands had been jailed for speaking up for their country. He asked Wilson to persuade the King and the British Parliament to listen.

<aside class="note"><b>Mesopotamia and Gallipoli</b>: places where Indian soldiers fought in the First World War. Mesopotamia is now Iraq. Gallipoli is in Turkey.</aside>

## The storm

The letter angered the British. It was attacked in both houses of the British Parliament. In the House of Commons, Edwin Montagu, the minister for India, called its charges "too wild and baseless". In the same debate, Sir J. D. Rees mocked the judge's age. A Madras newspaper, the Madras Mail, demanded that his knighthood be taken away.

In 1918 Montagu and the Viceroy, Lord Chelmsford, visited Madras. The judge went to see them about the coming reforms. They used the meeting to scold him for the letter.

## Giving it back

A few days later he gave his honours back. He returned the badge of his knighthood. He also returned an older medal, his title of Dewan Bahadur. He asked that no letter be addressed to him as "Sir" again.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: The Week and Wikipedia's list of his honours say he gave up the knighthood in 1917. Wikipedia's main story of his life and Madras Musings put it in June 1918, a few days after the meeting with Montagu and Chelmsford. The Tribune's obituary says he did it to protest Besant's internment.</aside>

In a letter to the newspapers, he made a joke of it. The Madras Mail, he said, should explain how a man is "dis-knighted".

## Why it matters

Subramania Iyer died in December 1924. The Tribune of Lahore wrote that people might argue about whether his letter was proper. But nobody, it said, could miss its love of freedom. He had served in British courts for most of his life. In the end he chose his country over a British title.
