Chandra Singh Bhandari was born on 25 December 1891 in the village of Meason, in the Pauri district of Garhwal. He is remembered today as Veer Chandra Singh Garhwali.

## A soldier of the Garhwal Rifles

He joined the army on 3 September 1914 at Lansdowne. In August 1915 he was sent to France. He came back in February 1916. In 1917 he took part in the battle of Baghdad.

After the war he was moved down from havildar, which is a sergeant's rank, to sepoy, which is an ordinary soldier. Later he was promoted again. He served in Waziristan in 1920 and at the Khyber Pass. He rose to be a Major Havildar.

<aside class="note"><b>Havildar</b>: a sergeant in the Indian army.</aside>

## 23 April 1930

On that day the Khudai Khidmatgar planned a peaceful protest in Peshawar. Abdul Ghaffar Khan had been arrested after a speech in Utmanzai, and other leaders had been taken too. A large crowd gathered in the Qissa Khwani Bazaar.

British forces moved to clear it. The soldiers of the 2nd Battalion of the Royal Garhwal Rifles were told to fire on the crowd. Chandra Singh was one of the men in command. One page says he called out, "Garhwalis, Cease Fire!"

The soldiers did not shoot. One page says that Hindu soldiers refused to fire on Muslim protesters.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: One page says "a troop" was sent. Another says "two platoons" were under Chandra Singh. The pages do not give the number of men. The site's story of Qissa Khwani Bazaar tells more about the day.</aside>

## The trial

Chandra Singh and 59 other soldiers were put on trial in a court-martial. Their ranks were taken away. Chandra Singh's property was seized.

A lawyer from Lansdowne, Mukandi Lal, spoke for them. One page says that he argued for about 60 soldiers charged with mutiny. Many of their death sentences, it says, were changed to life imprisonment, and he is credited with saving Chandra Singh from death.

Chandra Singh was sent to the jail at Abbottabad. One page says his sentence was 14 years, later cut short. He was freed on 26 September 1941, after about eleven years.

<aside class="note"><b>Sentences</b>: One page says Chandra Singh's death sentence was changed to life in prison. Another says he was given 14 years. The pages do not explain the difference.</aside>

## After jail

The government would not let him return to Garhwal. He went to Wardha and met Mahatma Gandhi. In the Quit India movement of 1942 he was arrested again and spent three years in prison. He was freed in 1945.

He went back to Garhwal on 22 December 1946. In 1957 he stood for election as a Communist Party candidate. He lost.

He died on 1 October 1979, aged 87.

## Remembered

In 1994 India issued a stamp in his honour. Schools and a medical institute carry his name. A statue of him was unveiled in Pauri in 2021.

## Why it matters

Chandra Singh was a soldier whose training told him to obey. He chose not to, when the order was to fire at people who had no weapons. He knew the price would be high. The pages that tell his story agree that he paid it.
