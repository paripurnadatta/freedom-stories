In 1921 an army doctor in the town of Mardan wrote a letter of resignation. He was to serve in Waziristan. He did not want to go. He chose to give up his commission instead.

His name was Khan Abdul Jabbar Khan. Most people called him Dr Khan Sahib.

## A doctor in two countries

Dr Khan Sahib was born in 1883 in Utmanzai, in Charsadda, in the North-West Frontier Province. His family were Muhammadzai Pashtun landlords. He was eight years older than his brother, Abdul Ghaffar Khan.

He studied medicine at the Grant Medical College in Bombay. Then he went on to St Thomas' Hospital in London. When the First World War began, he served in France. There he met a Scottish woman named Mary and married her.

After the war he joined the Indian Medical Service, the army's doctors. He was posted at Mardan. That was where he gave up his post in 1921.

## The brother in the red shirt

His younger brother founded schools and later the Khudai Khidmatgar, the Servants of God. Their volunteers wore red. They took an oath not to use violence. You can read about them in the story of the red shirts.

In the province, the movement was led politically by Dr Khan Sahib. In 1935 he was elected to the Central Legislative Assembly in New Delhi.

## 1937

The Frontier had only recently been given the same kind of government as the other provinces. In 1932 it was raised to the administrative level of the others, and elections followed.

In February 1937 the provinces voted. The North-West Frontier had a Legislative Assembly of 50 seats. Polling in the province took place on 1 February. The Congress won 19 of them. The Muslim League won none.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: One page calls Dr Khan Sahib's win in 1937 "decisive". Another calls it "narrow". Both agree that his party and the Congress won, and that he became chief minister. The Frontier was the one province where the Congress kept the support of Muslim voters.</aside>

According to the infobox on one page, Dr Khan Sahib became chief minister on 7 September 1937. Because the Congress did not have a majority on its own, his government rested on a coalition, which means a group of parties ruling together.

As chief minister he sat at the head of a provincial government in a country still ruled by Britain. A British governor could overrule the ministers. Still, the Frontier now had a government led by its own people. His brother Abdul Ghaffar Khan came back to Peshawar on 29 August 1937.

## Leaving office

In 1939 war came. Britain declared India at war without asking Indians. All the Congress ministries, including his, resigned in protest. His term ended on 10 November 1939.

He came back. In 1945 and 1946 he led the province again, in an alliance with the Congress. One page dates this second term from 16 March 1945. Another says the movement won again in 1946.

## The year of partition

In 1947 the Congress agreed to the partition of India. The brothers did not. The Khudai Khidmatgar wanted a different outcome. In June 1947 they passed the Bannu Resolution, asking for a Pashtun homeland. The British would give only two choices in the referendum, India or Pakistan.

Mid-1947 was also a time of killing between Hindus and Muslims in many places. One writer says the Khidmatgars protected thousands of Hindus in their villages during the fighting.

Dr Khan Sahib and his brother boycotted the vote. Of 572,798 registered voters, 51 per cent voted. Pakistan won by a wide margin. You can read more about this in the story of the vote that the Red Shirts did not cast.

On 22 August 1947, Jinnah's government dismissed Dr Khan Sahib from office. The Muslim League's Abdul Qayyum Khan became chief minister the next day. Dr Khan Sahib was later put in jail. He was released after others spoke up for him.

<aside class="note"><b>Dismissed or not?</b> One page says only that he was "appointed" to a post when Pakistan was created. The infobox on the same page gives the end of his term as 22 August 1947. Another page says that Jinnah dismissed him. This story follows the pages that call it a dismissal.</aside>

## A different road

After this the brothers made different choices. In September 1947 the movement passed the Sardaryab resolution, which accepted Pakistan and cut its ties with the Congress. Dr Khan Sahib pledged his loyalty to Pakistan. In 1954 he joined the cabinet of Muhammad Ali Bogra as Minister for Communications. That caused a split with his brother.

In October 1955 he became the first chief minister of West Pakistan, when its provinces were joined into a single unit. Abdul Ghaffar Khan's movement was banned again in 1955 over its opposition to this One Unit plan. In the same month Dr Khan Sahib founded the Republican Party, with the help of Governor-General Iskander Mirza.

The two brothers, who had stood side by side for years, were now on opposite sides of a question. People still disagree about which of them was right.

He resigned in 1957 after the provincial budget was rejected. The page gives the date as March in one place and 27 August in another. In June 1957 he was elected to the National Assembly from Quetta.

## The end

On 9 May 1958, Dr Khan Sahib was killed in the garden of his son's home in Lahore, at about half past eight in the morning. A man named Atta Mohammad, a patwari, a village land-records clerk who had lost his job, was the killer. Some sources say the killing was ordered by Allama Mashriqi, the leader of the Khaksars. That claim is not proved.

He was buried at Utmanzai, next to Mary.

## Why it matters

A market in New Delhi is named after him. Khan Market was founded in 1951 for people from the Frontier who had come to India as refugees. Iskander Mirza called Dr Khan Sahib "the greatest Pathan of his times". He is remembered as a doctor who gave up his army post. He ran a government for the Red Shirts and the Congress. And he took a road his brother would not.
