Takkar is a village in the Mardan district of the Frontier. In 1930 it was one of the strongest places of the Khudai Khidmatgar, after Peshawar and Utmanzai.

A month had passed since the day in Qissa Khwani Bazaar. The British government was arresting Red Shirt leaders everywhere. On 26 May 1930 it was the turn of Takkar.

## The five elders

A police officer named Murphy came to the village with a force of police. He wanted to arrest the movement's leaders. Five men were named: Malik Masim Khan, Salar Shamroz Khan, Malik Khan Badshah, Pir Shahzada and Malik Hameed of Fazalabad.

The village elders agreed to a plan. The five would give themselves up in Mardan the next day.

## 27 May

On 27 May the five set out in a procession. British forces stopped the procession and demanded that the men surrender on the spot. There was a quarrel, and then a clash.

According to one account, Murphy rode his horse into the crowd. Shots were fired. He fell from his horse. The women in the procession threw their water pitchers at him, and he died.

<aside class="note"><b>Who killed Murphy?</b> The account says that a man called Pehlawan Sultan claimed to have shot Murphy, and that Murphy died when the women hit him with pitchers. Malik Masim and others believed that two brothers, Amin and Said Amin, killed him. The sources do not settle it.</aside>

<aside class="note"><b>Nonviolence</b>: The Khudai Khidmatgar had taken an oath never to use violence. The death of an officer in this clash is one of the points on which people disagree about the movement. One writer says protesters in this period killed a British officer, but the pages do not describe how the fight began.</aside>

## 28 May

The next day British soldiers surrounded the village. One account says they beat and mistreated women and children. It says they fired at trees to frighten villagers who were hurrying to help. People from nearby villages came to stand with Takkar. Many people were killed or hurt. The guest houses of the leading Khidmatgars were burned.

The number of the dead is told in one source only. A Pashto book, *Da Khpal Waakaye Tarun*, says about 70 were killed and 150 wounded. The book was published in Afghanistan. No official count is given.

<aside class="note"><b>Another date</b>: a database of nonviolent movements says a British officer was killed at Takar on 30 May. The two pages that describe Takkar in detail give 28 May for the attack.</aside>

## Remembered

Villagers of Takkar mark 28 May every year. A monument, the Shuhada-i-Takkar, stands there. A folk song, *Pa Takkar Jang Di*, is still popular and recalls the day.

## Why it matters

Takkar's story is not as famous as Qissa Khwani. Its facts come from local writers and a book published abroad, not from official records. That makes it harder to be sure of the numbers. What is certain is that the village has kept the memory alive in songs, a monument and a yearly day.
