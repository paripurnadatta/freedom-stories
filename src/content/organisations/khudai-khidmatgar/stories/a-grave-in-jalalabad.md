Abdul Ghaffar Khan lived a very long life. After 1947 much of it was spent in Pakistani prisons or in exile.

## The later years

In the 1950s and 1960s he was often jailed. He was arrested in 1956 over the One Unit plan, and again in 1958. In September 1964 the authorities let him go to Britain for treatment. Later he went into exile in Afghanistan. He came back in December 1972, after governments of the National Awami Party took office in the North-West Frontier and in Balochistan.

In November 1973 he was arrested again at Multan, under the government of Zulfiqar Ali Bhutto. He called that government "the worst kind of dictatorship".

<aside class="note"><b>Two dates</b>: One page says he "remained in prison till 1957" and also that he was freed in 1964, after illness. The page does not reconcile these two.</aside>

In the 1980s he stepped back from politics. He was named for the Nobel Peace Prize in 1984. In 1985 he went to India for the hundredth birthday of the Indian National Congress. In 1987 India gave him its highest civilian honour, the Bharat Ratna.

## 20 January 1988

He died in Peshawar on 20 January 1988. One page says it was after a stroke.

He had left a will. It asked to be buried in Jalalabad, in Afghanistan, about halfway between Peshawar and Kabul. Some critics, mostly in Punjab, condemned the wish. His followers saw it differently. They thought it showed the unity of Pashtuns on both sides of the border.

<aside class="note"><b>Died at what age?</b> The page gives his age as 97 in one place. A newspaper headline quoted on the same page says 98. He was born in 1890.</aside>

## The funeral

Mourners came from many places. India's Prime Minister, Rajiv Gandhi, went to Peshawar to pay his respects. One page says that the Pakistan president, Zia ul-Haq, tried to discourage the visit on grounds of safety. India declared five days of mourning.

At that time a war was going on in Afghanistan, between the Soviet army and Afghan fighters. One writer says a one-day ceasefire was called so that thousands of people could cross the border from Peshawar. Visas were set aside for the day. Another page says that both sides in the war declared a ceasefire for the burial.

Afghan President Mohammad Najibullah attended. One page adds that two bombings killed 15 people, and that page marks the claim as needing a better source.

<aside class="note"><b>How many came?</b> The page says "tens of thousands" in one place and "over 200,000" in another. Two other pages say "thousands". The march through the Khyber Pass is tagged as unconfirmed.</aside>

He was buried at his house in Jalalabad, as his will asked.

## Why it matters

Abdul Ghaffar Khan was a man who would not take up weapons. At his funeral, both sides of a war put down theirs for a day. It was a rare thing, and it was given to a man who had asked for it all his life.
