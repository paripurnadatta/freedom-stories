On 5 March 1931, Mahatma Gandhi and the Viceroy, Lord Irwin, signed an agreement. It is called the Gandhi-Irwin Pact, or the Delhi Pact. The civil disobedience movement was put on hold.

Eighteen days later, on 23 March 1931, Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev and Rajguru were hanged in Lahore. Gandhi had pleaded with the government to spare them. It had not listened.

Three days after that, the Congress met in Karachi. The meeting ran from 26 to 31 March 1931. Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel was its president. It had been called mainly to decide whether to approve the pact.

## The black flags

Mahatma Gandhi came to Karachi. A crowd was waiting for him with black flags. These were young men from the Punjab Naujawan Bharat Sabha. They were angry that he had not saved the three men.

They shouted slogans, among them "Down with Gandhism" and "Long live Bhagat Singh". Gandhi took the black flowers they offered him and smiled. Their anger died down.

<aside class="note"><b>Black flag</b>: showing black flags is a way to say "we do not welcome you". It is a peaceful form of protest.</aside>

## What the Congress decided

The Congress approved the Gandhi-Irwin Pact, though there was debate in the hall about it. It named Gandhi as its only representative at the Second Round Table Conference in London. It repeated the demand for Purna Swaraj, complete independence.

It also honoured Bhagat Singh and his comrades for their courage, while saying that it did not agree with political violence. It condemned the hangings.

Gandhi spoke in the meeting. He said: "Purna Swaraj is not a pious wish to-day. It is the incessant yearning of the soul of the nation."

## The seven promises

The most lasting thing about Karachi was a resolution on Fundamental Rights. It was the first time the Congress had written down the rights that a free India should give to every person. It promised:

1. Freedom of speech, of the press, of assembly and of forming associations.
2. Freedom of conscience, and the right to follow any religion.
3. Equality before the law, whatever a person's caste, creed or sex.
4. The vote for every adult, in the central and provincial councils.
5. Safeguards for the culture, languages and scripts of minorities.
6. A state that does not take sides in religion.
7. Free and compulsory primary education.

<aside class="note"><b>Who wrote it?</b> One page says Jawaharlal Nehru drafted the resolution. Another says it took in eleven points of Gandhi's and extra items that Nehru added. A third gives no name.</aside>

Along with it came a second resolution, on the National Economic Programme. It asked for lower rents and land taxes, relief from debt, a living wage and shorter hours for workers, and the right of farmers and workers to form unions. It also asked for state control of major industries, mines and transport.

## Why it matters

In 1931 India was not yet free. But the Congress was already writing down what a free India should promise its people. Speech, religion, equality, the vote and schooling were all on the list.

The same session shows the other side of the Congress too. Young people were angry with its leaders. They said so, loudly, in the street. And Gandhi accepted their flowers.
