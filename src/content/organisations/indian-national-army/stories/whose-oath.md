A soldier in the British Indian Army took an oath. He promised to serve the King-Emperor. That promise was at the centre of the trial in the Red Fort.

## The charge

In November 1945 three INA officers were tried: Shah Nawaz Khan, Prem Sahgal and Gurbaksh Singh Dhillon. All had once been officers of the British Indian Army. The charge was "waging war against the King-Emperor", a crime under Section 121 of the Indian Penal Code.

The government's case was that these men had served the King's army and had then fought against him.

## What the defence said

Bhulabhai Desai was one of the lawyers who spoke for the officers. He argued about what had happened at Farrer Park in Singapore in February 1942.

On 17 February, a British colonel named Hunt of Malaya Command had handed the Indian troops over to the Japanese. Desai said that in doing so, the British command had given up its duty to protect them. He argued that this cancelled their first promise to the King-Emperor. After that, he said, the men were free to give their loyalty to Azad Hind.

<aside class="note"><b>Oath</b>: a serious promise, often made in a ceremony.</aside>

## What was said at Farrer Park

Nobody agrees on what Colonel Hunt said that day. Many soldiers remembered that he told them they now belonged to the Japanese army and must obey it. Hunt himself remembered only that he told them they were prisoners of the Japanese.

Shah Nawaz Khan said later that the men were "handed over like cattle". Other INA veterans said they felt left behind and dishonoured. Many of them gave this as a reason for joining the INA.

Hunt was in England in 1945. He refused to give evidence at the trial. He said that he was ill.

## The verdict

The court found all three men guilty and sentenced them to be deported for life. The sentences were never carried out. Jawaharlal Nehru took part in the defence. After public protests the Commander-in-Chief freed the officers.

## Why it matters

Both sides had a real point. The government said a promise is a promise. The defence said a promise runs both ways. The court ruled against the defence. But across India, the public sided with the officers.
