On 23 December 1912, a bomb was thrown at the Viceroy, Lord Hardinge, as his procession passed through Delhi. Hardinge was hurt but lived. Rash Behari Bose was one of the men who took part.

He was never caught. Thirty years later he helped give the Indian National Army its start.

## A clerk in Dehradun

Rash Behari Bose was born on 25 May 1886 in the village of Subaldaha, in Bengal. He studied at Dupleix College in Chandernagar and then at Morton School in Calcutta.

In 1908 he left Bengal to avoid the Alipore bomb case. He took a job as head clerk at the Forest Research Institute in Dehradun. There he met revolutionaries from Bengal, the United Provinces and Punjab. After the attack on Hardinge he went into hiding, and then returned to his office in Dehradun.

## Escape to Japan

In February 1915 he was a leading figure in the Ghadar plot, an attempt to start a revolt in India. The plan failed and most of those involved were arrested. Bose escaped British spies and reached Japan in 1915 under the name Priyanath Thakur.

<aside class="note"><b>Two years</b>: One page says he reached Japan in 1915. Another says 1923. This story follows the 1915 date, because that page also gives the false name he used on the journey.</aside>

For three years he moved from place to place while Britain pressed Tokyo to send him back. He hid at the home of the Soma family in Shinjuku. They owned a bakery in Tokyo, the Nakamuraya. In 1918 he married the daughter of the family, Toshiko Soma. They had a son, Masahide, born in 1920, and a daughter, Tetsuko, born in 1922. In 1923 he became a Japanese citizen. Toshiko died in 1924, and he never married again.

He wrote, worked as a journalist, and is said to have helped make Indian-style curry well known in Japan.

<aside class="note"><b>Citizen</b>: a person who legally belongs to a country.</aside>

## The League

In March 1942 Rash Behari Bose called a conference in Tokyo, from 28 to 30 March. It set up the Indian Independence League and agreed to raise an army to win India's freedom. In June at Bangkok he was chosen to head its Council of Action. Some delegates did not trust him because he had lived in Japan for so long.

The Indian National Army was the League's army. The Bangkok conference also invited Subhas Chandra Bose to lead the League. When Subhas Bose reached Singapore in 1943, he took over the League and the army. One page says Rash Behari handed over the flag and the leadership. Historians disagree about whether he stepped aside by choice or under Japanese pressure.

## The last years

In 1943 Japan gave him the Order of the Rising Sun, second class. He died of tuberculosis in Tokyo on 21 January 1945. He was fifty-eight. He was buried next to Toshiko.

## Why it matters

Rash Behari Bose spent nearly thirty years away from India. He died before it was free. But the League and the army that he built became the base on which another Bose built his own.
