If you had walked into a gym in Calcutta in 1902, you might have seen young men wrestling. Others were practising with sticks. Some were reading.

Nothing looked strange. Clubs like this were popping up all over Bengal. The name of this one was Anushilan Samiti. *Anushilan* means practice or training. *Samiti* means a society.

## Body, mind and character

The idea behind the clubs was simple. Young people should grow strong in body, sharp in mind and good in character. Writers such as Bankim Chandra Chatterjee, Swami Vivekananda and Aurobindo Ghosh had said so. Many Bengalis agreed.

The Calcutta club was led by a barrister named Pramathanath Mitra. A young man named Jatindranath Banerjee helped him. Jatindranath had trained with the army of the Maharaja of Baroda. Barindra Kumar Ghosh, the younger brother of Aurobindo, helped too.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: Sources give two names for the founder. Banglapedia says Pramathanath Mitra started the Calcutta club in 1902. The long Wikipedia account of the Alipore case says a student named Satish Chandra Basu founded it, with Mitra's support. The Wire calls Mitra its president. They may both be right. One may have started it and the other led it.</aside>

## What the members read

New members were given a reading list. It was mostly stories from Hindu tradition. The books of Swami Vivekananda came first.

When a person joined, he took a vow while holding the Gita. For that reason, Banglapedia says, a Muslim could not be admitted. So the Samiti was a mainly Hindu group. Later writing from the movement called on Hindus and Muslims to join together.

<aside class="note"><b>Gita</b>: the Bhagavad Gita, a holy book of the Hindus.</aside>

## Becoming something else

For the first few years the club mostly trained its members. Banglapedia says it did little else until about 1907 or 1908.

Then things changed. In 1905 the British government divided Bengal into two parts. Many Bengalis were angry. Some of the young men in the clubs began to say that asking politely would never work. They began to prepare for a fight.

Banglapedia says nobody is quite sure when the Samiti took up violence. It names 1905 as the year usually given.

The club did not stay a single group. It grew branches, and later split into two main parts. One was based in Dhaka. The other was the group around the newspaper *Jugantar* in Calcutta. You can read about them in "The teacher in Dhaka" and "A newspaper called Jugantar".

## Why it matters

The Samiti began as exercise and ended as a secret society. Its story shows how a group of young people moved, step by step, from lifting weights to throwing bombs. Whether they were right to take that road is a question Indians have argued about ever since.
