In March 1906, a small group in Calcutta decided to start a newspaper. They called it Jugantar.

The word is from Sanskrit. It means the ending of an age, or the time of great change. It was a name with fire in it.

<aside class="note"><b>Word</b>: <i>Jugantar</i> is a Sanskrit word. It can mean the end of a world-age, or a moment when everything changes. The group chose it on purpose.</aside>

The newspaper was not run by the whole Anushilan Samiti. It was run by a group that had broken away from it. That group was led by a man named Barin Ghose, who was the younger brother of the thinker Aurobindo Ghosh. Barin Ghose's full name was Barindra Kumar Ghosh.

Along with Aurobindo and another leader, Bipin Chandra Pal, the group started two papers at the same time. Jugantar was one. The other was called <i>Bande Mataram</i>, which means "Hail the Motherland."

Both papers argued that quiet protest was not enough. They called for people to act, to fight, to take the country back.

The British did not allow this kind of paper to be printed openly. In June 1908, the Jugantar office was shut. But the group did not stop. It moved the press to a new room, and to a new room again. It kept printing by hand, and kept passing the pages around.

<aside class="note"><b>Why a hand press?</b> A small printing machine that a person pushes the bar of by hand is called a hand press. It was the kind of machine a small group could keep hidden, and it was how many early Indian papers were printed before bigger machines came.</aside>

Jugantar's pages did not just call for action. They named the people who thought of it. They wrote about the men and women who would go to prison for the idea. And in 1908, they were linked, in a trial, to two boys who threw a bomb in Muzaffarpur, and to a famous poet and thinker named Aurobindo Ghosh, who was also arrested.

The trial was called the Alipore bomb case. It ran from 1908 to 1910. It was one of the most watched courtrooms in India at that time.

Jugantar itself had stopped printing openly by 1908. But its name stayed in the record. It is the name of the secret group that carried Anushilan Samiti's ideas from a quiet gym into the open court.
