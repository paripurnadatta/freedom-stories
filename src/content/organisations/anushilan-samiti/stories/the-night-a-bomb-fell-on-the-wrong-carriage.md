On the night of 30 April 1908, in the town of Muzaffarpur in Bihar, a horse-drawn carriage moved along a road.

Two young men were waiting for it. They threw a bomb.

The bomb did not land on the carriage the two men had been watching. It landed on a different carriage, and it hit two people who were not the people they had been aiming at.

The two people were a mother and her daughter. The mother was called Mrs Kennedy, and the daughter was called Ms Kennedy. Both of them died.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: Some accounts say the carriage the two young men had been watching held the Chief Presidency Magistrate, a senior official. Others say the two carriage drivers simply mistook which carriage was which. In either version, the bomb did not hit the person the two men had planned to hit. It hit two people who were not there for any political reason.</aside>

The two young men were named Khudiram Bose and Prafulla Chaki. Khudiram Bose was born on 3 December 1889. Prafulla Chaki was born on 10 December 1888.

<aside class="note"><b>Word</b>: To be <i>hanged</i> means to be put to death by the gallows, a wooden frame with a rope. It was the British way of putting people to death who were found guilty of very serious crimes.</aside>

The two men had come from the same secret group. They had been trained, in the years before, by a network called the Anushilan Samiti, which had opened in Calcutta in 1902 as a fitness club and had, under its quiet rooms, planned something much more than exercise.

After the bomb went off, the two men did not run together. They ran separate ways. Chaki went to a small station called Mokama, near Patna. He was cornered by a police officer there, on 2 May 1908. The officer had only a few bullets left. He used his last one, on himself.

Khudiram Bose walked, some forty kilometres, to a place called Waini, before he was caught. He was held, and a court sat down to try him. The first hearing was on 21 May 1908. On 13 June, the court gave him the longest punishment the law allowed: death by hanging. On 8 July, a higher court heard his lawyers' case. On 13 July, that court said the decision would stand.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: Bose's own record in one account puts the day of his hanging on 11 August 1908. Another account, written about his partner, puts it on 19 August 1908. The two accounts do not agree on the exact day, though they agree on the place (Muzaffarpur jail) and the year.</aside>

In the Muzaffarpur jail, in August 1908, Khudiram Bose was hanged. He was eighteen years old.

The bomb in Muzaffarpur was not the first act of that group. It was, in many ways, the act that pushed the British to look harder at who was behind it. That looking led, in Calcutta, to a long trial called the Alipore bomb case, and to the arrest of a thinker and poet named Aurobindo Ghosh, who had been, in the years before, the quiet head of the group's ideas.

The two names, Khudiram Bose and Prafulla Chaki, went from that one night into the history of the freedom struggle. Khudiram Bose, in particular, became the name of a boy who had gone to prison and had not flinched.
