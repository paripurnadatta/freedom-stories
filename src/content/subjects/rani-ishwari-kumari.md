## Research notes

Documented in the cited source in connection with Awadh resistance in 1857.

### A frontier refuge in a collapsing rebellion

Tulsipur's importance in the final phase of 1857 lay partly in its geography: a resistant estate close to the Nepal frontier could shelter leaders pushed out of the Awadh heartland.

### Resistance without a secure ending

The available record supports her refusal to submit and her protection of fellow rebels more strongly than it supports a precise later-life chronology.

### Chronology

- **1857-1858**: Tulsipur remains in rebellion. Ishwari Kumari directs resistance from Tulsipur while the Awadh uprising is being militarily compressed
- **1858**: Protection of Begum Hazrat Mahal and Birjis Qadr. The displaced Awadh leadership takes shelter in the Tulsipur sphere as British forces advance
- **1858-1859**: Withdrawal toward Nepal. After the protected party escapes, Ishwari Kumari retreats toward the Nepal frontier; subsequent details remain uncertain

### Evidence claims

- Ishwari Kumari was the Rani of Tulsipur, an estate on the Awadh-Nepal frontier that remained hostile to British authority during the 1857 uprising. (medium confidence)
- The strongest supplied narrative places Begum Hazrat Mahal and Birjis Qadr under her protection at Tulsipur after their displacement from Lucknow and Baundi. (medium confidence)
- She rejected a British offer that would have allowed her to retain power in exchange for surrendering the Begum and Birjis Qadr. (medium confidence)
- After ensuring their escape, she withdrew toward the Dang-Deukhuri valleys near Nepal; her later fate is not securely established in the supplied sources. (medium confidence)
