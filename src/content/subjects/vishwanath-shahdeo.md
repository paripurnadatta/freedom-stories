## Research notes

Documented in the cited source in connection with Awadh resistance in 1857.

### A regional command structure

Chotanagpur in 1857 was not an isolated local disturbance: Shahdeo's coalition joined mutinous troops, zamindars and regional commanders in a coordinated challenge to British administration.

### Connecting Ranchi to Bihar

The attempted move toward Kunwar Singh demonstrates the strategic desire to connect regional theatres even when the campaign ultimately failed.

### Chronology

- **1857**: Leadership of the Chotanagpur rising. Shahdeo accepts leadership as rebel troops and local allies challenge British administration around Ranchi
- **1857-10-02**: Defeat at Chatra. The combined rebel force suffers a major defeat while attempting to connect the Chotanagpur struggle with wider Bihar resistance
- **1858**: Capture and execution. After continuing resistance with Ganpat Rai, Shahdeo is captured and executed by colonial authorities

### Evidence claims

- The Dictionary of Martyrs identifies Vishwanath Shahdeo as the Raja/Zamindar of Barkagarh who organized and led resistance to British authority in the Ranchi region in 1857. (high confidence)
- He joined the mutinous Ramgarh Battalion and helped prevent British officials from re-establishing control in Chotanagpur. (high confidence)
- Ministry of Culture material describes Pandey Ganpat Rai assuming command under Shahdeo's leadership and the combined force moving north in an attempt to link with Kunwar Singh. (high confidence)
- The archival reference places Shahdeo in a wider rebel coalition including Ganpat Rai, Umrao Singh and Sheikh Bhikhari. (high confidence)
