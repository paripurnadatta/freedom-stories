## Research notes

Documented in the cited source in connection with Delhi uprising of 1857.

### Overview

Mughal prince and mobile 1857-58 resistance leader whose campaigns continued across Hardoi, Sandila and Bangar Mau and whose political program is linked to the Azamgarh Proclamation.

### Chronology

- **1857**: Led resistance in the Hardoi/Sandila region
- **1857**: Associated with the Azamgarh Proclamation
- **1858-06**: Continued resistance in the Bangar Mau/Unnao region
- **1877**: Died in Mecca according to the Ministry profile

### Evidence claims

- Firoz Shah is identified by Ministry sources as a Mughal prince active in the 1857 uprising. (HIGH confidence)
- He challenged British authority in Hardoi/Sandila and continued resistance into 1858 in Bangar Mau. (HIGH confidence)
- The Ministry account attributes most of the Azamgarh Proclamation to Firoz Shah and identifies him as a grandson of Bahadur Shah II. (HIGH confidence)
- The Bangar Mau profile records that he died in Mecca in 1877. (HIGH confidence)

### Places

- Hardoi / Sandila: 1857 regional resistance
- Bangar Mau / Unnao: 1858 continuation of resistance
- Delhi / Mughal political network: Dynastic-political identity
- Mecca: Place of death recorded by source
