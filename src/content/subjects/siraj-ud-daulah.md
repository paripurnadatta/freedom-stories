## Research notes

Defeated and overthrown after the Battle of Plassey.

### A regional ruler at an imperial turning point

Siraj's profile should be situated in the politics of the Bengal nawabship and Company expansion, not retroactively recast as a twentieth-century nationalist.

### Plassey was not simply a duel

The battle combined military action with elite defection and political conspiracy; the profile should preserve that complexity.

### Chronology

- **1756**: Accession to the nawabship. Siraj ud-Daulah succeeds in Bengal shortly before his decisive confrontation with the East India Company
- **1757-06-23**: Battle of Plassey. Siraj's army confronts Clive's Company force at Palashi; the defeat becomes a major turning point in the Company's acquisition of political power

### Evidence claims

- Siraj ud-Daulah was Nawab of Bengal in 1756–1757 and is described by Nadia district history as the last independent ruler of Bengal before the Company's political ascendancy. (high confidence)
- The Battle of Plassey was fought at Palashi on 23 June 1757 between Siraj's forces and the East India Company under Robert Clive. (high confidence)
- Mir Madan remained one of the officers actively fighting on Siraj's side at Plassey while other senior commanders withheld effective support. (medium confidence)
