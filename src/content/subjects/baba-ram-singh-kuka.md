### Overview

Guru Ram Singh, leader of the Namdhari/Kuka movement in Punjab, represents one of the clearest bridges between the post-1857 decades and organized anti-colonial boycott. Government sources describe a programme of social reform, rejection of British institutions and goods, parallel organisational structures and escalating confrontation that culminated in severe repression and Ram Singh's exile. His profile should present the movement's religious, social-reform and political dimensions together.

### Historical significance

This profile was selected for Batch 008 because it repairs one or more post-175 gaps while adding a historically distinct strand to the archive. The final implementation should privilege the verified evidence claims and chronology above over generic fame-based biography.

### Chronology

- **1860s**: Namdhari organization expanded and Ram Singh's followers came under colonial surveillance
- **1871-1872**: Kuka conflict intensified, followed by severe colonial repression
- **1872**: Ram Singh was exiled from Punjab by the colonial authorities

### Evidence claims

- Guru Ram Singh became the central leader of the Namdhari/Kuka movement in Punjab. (HIGH confidence)
- He encouraged boycott of British schools, courts, post/telegraph and other institutions while building alternative organizational structures. (HIGH confidence)
- The movement combined religious/social reform with anti-colonial political mobilisation. (HIGH confidence)
- British authorities exiled Ram Singh after the violent repression of the movement in 1872. (MEDIUM_HIGH confidence)
