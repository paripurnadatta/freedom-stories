## Research notes

Documented in the cited source in connection with Formation of the Indian National Congress.

### National awakening was not one argument

Agarkar's dispute with Tilak matters because nineteenth-century public politics contained serious disagreements over caste, women's status, religion and the sequencing of reform and self-rule.

### Education as political infrastructure

Schools, colleges and newspapers were institutions through which a new political public was being built before mass nationalism.

### Chronology

- **1880**: New English School. Agarkar joins Tilak and colleagues in building a nationalist-controlled educational institution in Pune
- **1881**: Kesari begins. Agarkar becomes the Marathi paper's first editor as education and journalism merge into public politics
- **1884**: Deccan Education Society. Agarkar participates in creating a larger institutional base for education
- **1888**: Sudharak. After breaking with Tilak, Agarkar creates a reformist journal committed to reason and social change

### Evidence claims

- Government historical material identifies Gopal Ganesh Agarkar as a major nineteenth-century reformer, educator and journalist from Maharashtra. (high confidence)
- He worked closely with Bal Gangadhar Tilak in establishing the New English School and in the early Deccan Education Society. (high confidence)
- Agarkar served as the first editor of Kesari before ideological differences with Tilak widened. (high confidence)
- He later founded and edited Sudharak as a platform for social reform, rationalism and criticism of caste and social orthodoxy. (high confidence)
- His disagreements with Tilak centred in part on the priority and methods of social reform versus political mobilization. (high confidence)
