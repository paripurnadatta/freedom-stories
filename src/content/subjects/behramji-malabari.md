## Research notes

Documented in the cited source in connection with Early nationalist press activity.

### Reform politics beside nationalism

Malabari belongs in the timeline because social reform and emerging nationalism shared institutions, audiences and arguments—but he should not be inaccurately recast as a mass anti-colonial campaigner.

### Print as pressure

His method demonstrates how a pamphlet, correspondence and journalism could produce political pressure without a street movement.

### Chronology

- **1884**: Notes on Infant Marriage and Enforced Widowhood. Malabari publishes the work that becomes the centre of his social-reform campaign
- **1885**: A national reform correspondence. The Notes circulate among officials and public figures, including A.O. Hume, producing a documented argument over method and legislation
- **late 1880s-1890s**: Reform campaign broadens. Malabari continues using journalism and transnational advocacy to press questions of marriage, widowhood and women's status

### Evidence claims

- Malabari's 1884 Notes on Infant Marriage and Enforced Widowhood made child marriage and the treatment of widows central subjects of public reform debate. (high confidence)
- His campaign circulated the Notes among influential officials and public figures and helped generate a large correspondence on possible reform. (high confidence)
- A.O. Hume's 1885 published response demonstrates that Malabari's proposals were actively debated within the same late-colonial public sphere that produced organized Indian politics. (high confidence)
- Malabari pursued reform through journalism, lobbying and public advocacy in India and Britain. (high confidence)
