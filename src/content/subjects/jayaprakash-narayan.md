## Research notes

Socialist leader who escaped prison and organized underground resistance.

### A socialist current inside the national movement

JP's pre-1947 significance lies in demonstrating that Congress nationalism contained organized left alternatives rather than a single ideological centre.

### Quit India underground

His prison escape and clandestine activity connect the mass uprising of 1942 to the networks that tried to sustain resistance after the leadership arrests.

### Chronology

- **1929**: Return to India and Congress work. JP joins national politics after years of study and labour in the United States
- **1934**: Congress Socialist Party. He becomes one of the principal organizers of a socialist current within the wider Congress movement
- **1942-11**: Escape from Hazaribagh. JP escapes imprisonment during Quit India and re-enters the struggle underground
- **1943-1946**: Rearrest and release. The underground phase ends in renewed imprisonment; he is freed near the end of British rule

### Evidence claims

- Government biographies identify Jayaprakash Narayan as a nationalist and socialist who returned from the United States in 1929 and entered Congress politics. (high confidence)
- Jawaharlal Nehru drew him into Congress organizational work, and JP later became a central founder/organizer of the Congress Socialist Party. (high confidence)
- He was imprisoned during Civil Disobedience and again during the wartime crackdown on nationalist politics. (high confidence)
- During Quit India he escaped from Hazaribagh Central Jail in November 1942 and worked underground to continue resistance. (high confidence)
- The government profile records his later underground work, including efforts associated with the Azad Dasta, followed by rearrest in 1943 and release in 1946. (high confidence)
