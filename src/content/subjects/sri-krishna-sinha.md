### Overview

Prominent Bihar Congress leader, later the province/state's premier political executive. He left legal practice to join Gandhi's Non-Cooperation movement in 1921, was repeatedly imprisoned, and became an important provincial organiser. Government sources also connect him to the early Bihar Kisan Sabha tradition and anti-Simon mobilisation. His profile should separate his anti-colonial career from his long post-independence chief-ministerial legacy.

### Historical significance

This profile was selected for Batch 008 because it repairs one or more post-175 gaps while adding a historically distinct strand to the archive. The final implementation should privilege the verified evidence claims and chronology above over generic fame-based biography.

### Chronology

- **1921**: Left legal practice and joined Non-Cooperation
- **1922**: First imprisonment in the freedom struggle
- **1928**: Helped mobilize opposition to the Simon Commission in Bihar
- **1937**: Became Premier of Bihar under provincial autonomy; post-movement office context begins

### Evidence claims

- Sri Krishna Sinha left legal practice in 1921 to join the Non-Cooperation Movement. (HIGH confidence)
- He was first jailed in 1922 and spent substantial periods in prison during the freedom struggle. (HIGH confidence)
- Government of Bihar historical material links him to the early Bihar Kisan Sabha and to provincial agrarian politics. (HIGH confidence)
- He was a prominent organiser of the 1928 boycott of the Simon Commission in Bihar. (MEDIUM_HIGH confidence)
