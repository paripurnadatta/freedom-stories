## Research notes

Editor of Almora Akhbar from 1913 and a leading figure in the anti-begari movement that culminated in the 1921 Bageshwar mobilisation.

### Regional nationalism through journalism and organization

Badri Datt Pandey is a useful test of how the project handles figures whose importance is greatest at a regional scale. His politics linked journalism, Kumaon Parishad and agitation against systems of forced porterage.

### Kumaon Parishad

A government-hosted historical document names Pandey among the organizers of Kumaon Parishad and connects the organization to the Kuli Begar struggle.

### Kuli Begar

The Kuli Begar movement should eventually be represented as a specific regional movement with its own people, places, chronology and sources rather than being buried under a generic nationalist label.

### Different source strengths for different claims

The pilot intentionally distinguishes official evidence for organizational and movement links from secondary reporting used for some biographical and imprisonment details.

### Chronology

- **1916**: Kumaon Parishad. Participates in building a regional political organization in Kumaon
- **1918**: Shakti and political journalism. Regional journalism becomes an important means of political mobilization
- **1921**: Kuli Begar movement. The campaign against forced porterage reaches its best-known mass phase
- **1942**: Quit India-era imprisonment. Secondary biographical reporting records imprisonment during the Quit India period

### Evidence claims

- Associated with the organization of Kumaon Parishad in 1916 alongside Govind Ballabh Pant and other regional leaders. (high confidence)
- Played a leading role in the Kuli Begar movement, which reached a major phase in 1921. (high confidence)
- Played a leading role in the Kuli Begar movement, which reached a major phase in 1921. (high confidence)
- Used journalism as a vehicle for anti-colonial and regional mobilization. (medium confidence)
- Was imprisoned in multiple phases of the national movement, including the 1942 period according to the secondary biographical account. (medium confidence)
