## Research notes

Documented in the cited source in connection with Early nationalist press activity.

### A nationalist inheritance made organizational

Being Naoroji's granddaughter mattered, but Perin's significance rests on her own sustained work in women's organizations and the Bombay Congress.

### Women as political infrastructure

Khadi sales, boycott organization and provincial committees were not peripheral activities; they were mechanisms through which mass nationalism functioned.

### Chronology

- **1920s**: Women's swadeshi organization. Perin builds women's participation through khadi, boycott and the Rashtriya Stree Sabha
- **1930**: Bombay Congress leadership. She holds provincial Congress leadership as Civil Disobedience reshapes urban politics
- **1930s-1940s**: Imprisonment and organizational work. Her political activity continues through repeated colonial repression and language/constructive-work organizations
- **1947**: Hindustani correspondence. Nehru's letter records her continuing involvement in language-related public work on the eve of independence

### Evidence claims

- The Nehru Archive identifies Perin Captain as a granddaughter of Dadabhai Naoroji and a participant in the freedom struggle who was imprisoned on several occasions. (high confidence)
- Government of Maharashtra history records her as a swadeshi worker and as a founder of the Rashtriya Stree Sabha. (high confidence)
- She served as president of the Bombay Provincial Congress Committee during the Civil Disobedience era. (high confidence)
- Press Information Bureau material records Perin and her sisters selling khadi door to door as part of women's nationalist work in Bombay. (high confidence)
- A 1947 Nehru letter documents continuing contact around Hindustani language work. (high confidence)
