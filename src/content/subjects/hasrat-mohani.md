## Research notes

Advocated complete independence early and participated in nationalist politics.

### A poet's politics were not decorative

For Mohani, the Urdu press and literary expression were part of anti-colonial organization, not separate from it.

### Radicalism across organizations

His trajectory resists neat classification: Congress participation, revolutionary language and communist association overlapped without becoming identical.

### Chronology

- **1903**: Urdu-e-Mualla launched. Mohani turns journalism and poetry into an openly political platform in Aligarh
- **1907**: Repression of the journal. Colonial authorities imprison Mohani and suppress Urdu-e-Mualla amid its anti-imperial writing
- **1921**: Complete independence and revolutionary language. Mohani becomes associated with the demand for complete independence and with the slogan 'Inquilab Zindabad'

### Evidence claims

- Hasrat Mohani was the pen name of Syed Fazl-ul-Hasan, born in Mohan in present-day Unnao district. (high confidence)
- He began publishing Urdu-e-Mualla in 1903 and used it for sharp criticism of colonial policy; the journal was subjected to repression and banning. (high confidence)
- Public-broadcaster and Ministry sources associate Mohani with the 1921 demand for complete independence. (high confidence)
- Akashvani's profile credits him with coining 'Inquilab Zindabad' in 1921, later popularized by other revolutionaries. (medium confidence)
- He moved through Congress and communist political currents rather than remaining within a single ideological organization throughout his life. (medium confidence)
