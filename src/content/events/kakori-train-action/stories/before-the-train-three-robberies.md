The Kakori train action took place on 9 August 1925. Most people know that day. Fewer people know that it was the fourth robbery in the case, not the first.

The court called the whole case the Kakori train dacoity and conspiracy case. The judges looked at four robberies together. Three of them happened before the train. All three were at the homes of rich people in villages. Each one ended with a person killed.

<aside class="note"><b>Dacoity</b>: a robbery by a gang. In the old law, a dacoity in which someone is killed was punished very hard.</aside>

## The three houses

The Allahabad High Court's history lists them like this.

**Bamrauli.** This happened on Christmas night, 25 December 1924, in the district of Pilibhit. The house belonged to a rich moneylender, who also owned a sugar factory. A wrestler stood up to the robbers. He was shot dead.

**Bichpuri.** This was also in Pilibhit. The court history gives the date as 9 March 1925. The house belonged to a rich man. One person was killed.

**Dwarkapur.** This was in the Pratapgarh district. The court history gives the date as 25 May 1925. The house belonged to a rich man. A villager lost his life.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: The dates do not match everywhere. The Hindi account says Bichpuri was on 7 March, and Dwarkapur on 24 May. English Wikipedia says Dwarikapur was on 24 May, and gives 25 December 1924 for Bamrauli. We use the court history. The days are very close, but we cannot say which are exact.</aside>

## What the group learned

The Hindi account says that Bichpuri and Dwarkapur each brought in very little money, and that each cost a life.

It says that after these two robberies, Ram Prasad Bismil decided something. From now on, the group would loot only government money. They would not take the property of rich Indians again.

Bismil gave a similar reason in his own voice. He told his friends that if they robbed the government, they would not have to harm their own people.

That is why the next target was a train. The government's money was carried in an iron safe in the guard's van.

## Who was at Bamrauli?

The courts treated these four robberies as one case. This matters for one of the men who was hanged.

Thakur Roshan Singh was not on the train at Kakori. English Wikipedia says he did not take part in it. The National Book Trust book says so too. He was tried for what happened at Bamrauli. The book prints words it says come from the judge. They say Roshan Singh led the Bamrauli robbery, because he knew the village well, and that two people had said he carried a pistol that day.

So Roshan Singh was sentenced to death for the earlier robbery, where a man had died. He was not sentenced for the train.

<aside class="note"><b>Not settled</b>: One government book, the Dictionary of Martyrs, is reported to describe Roshan Singh as part of the train action. We could not open that book. Wikipedia and the National Book Trust book say he was not there. We follow those two, and we say so openly.</aside>

## Why it matters

Kakori is remembered as a daring plan with a clean aim. The court record shows a harder picture. Before the train, there were three robberies at village homes. Three people died.

It is right to remember the young men's love for their country. It is also right to remember the three villagers. A story told fairly keeps both in view.
