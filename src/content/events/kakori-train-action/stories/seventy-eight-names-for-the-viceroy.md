When the Kakori judgment came, in April 1927, many people in India were shocked. Three young men had been sentenced to death. A fourth, Ashfaqulla Khan, would soon follow.

The National Book Trust book on the Kakori prisoners says the whole country was "aghast". It says leaders protested, and that members of the Central Legislature wrote to the Viceroy. That is where this story begins. It is the story of a plea that did not work, and of the people who made it anyway.

## Who the four were

Ram Prasad Bismil was a poet and the organiser of the group. Ashfaqulla Khan was his close friend and also a poet. Rajendra Lahiri was a university student of history. Roshan Singh was older than the rest. He had been a village schoolteacher.

The people who spoke up for them came from different parties and different faiths. This story looks at who they were, and what happened to their plea.

## The first pleas

After the Chief Court at Lucknow kept the death sentences in August 1927, the next step was mercy. A hanging could still be stopped if a higher authority agreed to a lighter sentence.

The Hindi Wikipedia account says some members of the provincial council wrote first to the Governor of the United Provinces, Sir William Morris. He refused.

<aside class="note"><b>Commute</b>: to change a punishment into a lighter one. A death sentence can be commuted to life in prison.</aside>

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: When did the Chief Court give its decision? The court history says 11 August 1927. English Wikipedia and the Hindi account say 22 August. We show both, because we cannot check the original judgment.</aside>

## The Viceroy and the memorial

The next plea went higher, to the Viceroy, Lord Irwin. English Wikipedia says it was a memorandum, or formal request, from Madan Mohan Malaviya, signed by 78 members of the Central Legislature. Two other sources agree on the number.

The Hindi account lists some of the people who signed. They include Malaviya himself, Muhammad Ali Jinnah, N. C. Kelkar, Lala Lajpat Rai and Govind Ballabh Pant.

Think about that list. Malaviya and Jinnah belonged to different faiths and different parties. On this one matter, their names stood on the same page.

English Wikipedia says other well-known people also spoke for the accused. It names Motilal Nehru and Jawaharlal Nehru. Some writers also say that Mahatma Gandhi asked for mercy. The two sources that mention this both say it carefully: "reportedly" and "claimed". We did not find a clear record, so we leave it as a maybe.

The Viceroy said no. The Hindi account adds that a delegation of five people, led by Malaviya, then went to see the authorities. It also failed.

## A letter from the death cell

On 9 September 1927, Ram Prasad Bismil wrote a letter from Gorakhpur jail to Malaviya. Two sources mention this letter. We could not find its words, so we cannot tell you what he said.

## The last door: London

The highest court that Indians could reach was the Privy Council in London. It was also possible to ask the King-Emperor himself.

The Hindi account says the lawyer Mohan Lal Saxena wrote the petition. It says he sent it to a man in England named S. L. Polak, who was to hand it in. English Wikipedia says the final appeal was dated 16 September 1927, and that it went to both the Privy Council and the King-Emperor, through Henry Polak.

It was turned down.

The Hindi account gives the reason it says the London officials gave. They called Bismil a dangerous professional criminal. This is the government's view, as one source reports it. The other sources do not give a reason.

<aside class="note"><b>Privy Council</b>: a group of senior advisers in London. In those days, it was the last court of appeal for people in British India.</aside>

## News in the jail

The National Book Trust book prints a letter that Rajendra Lahiri is said to have written to his friends on 14 December 1927. In it, he says that the day before, he heard that the Privy Council had turned down his appeal. The book gives these words as his: "You have done everything to save my life but it seems that the country needs my life."

The same book prints a letter that Roshan Singh is said to have written on 13 December. In it, he tells his friend not to feel sorry for him.

<aside class="note"><b>Handle with care</b>: We could not check these letters against the originals. They come from a book written in 2009, more than eighty years later. We give them as that book gives them, not as proven words.</aside>

## What the appeal did to the others

The plea was for four lives. But the Chief Court's decision had also changed the lives of other accused men. The court history says the judges made the sentences heavier for five of them: Jogesh Chandra Chatterji, Suresh Chandra Bhattacharji, Vishnu Saran Dublish, Govind Charan Kar and Pranavesh Kumar Chatterji.

Some of the accused had been defended in court by well-known lawyers. Others had no lawyer of their own. The court history says C. B. Gupta and H. N. Misra spoke up for those who had nobody.

## Dates in December

After the plea failed, the British government fixed the dates. One source, LegalBites, says all four men were to be hanged by 16 December. English Wikipedia says by 19 December.

What actually happened was this. Rajendra Lahiri was hanged on 17 December 1927 in the Gonda jail. The other three were hanged on the morning of 19 December. Bismil was hanged at Gorakhpur. Roshan Singh was hanged at Allahabad. Ashfaqulla Khan was hanged at Faizabad.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: Why was Lahiri hanged two days early? The National Book Trust book says the government feared that his friends might free him from the jail. It says Chandrashekhar Azad and Manmathnath Gupta had come to Gonda for that purpose, and that someone leaked the plan. Another source gives no reason at all. We could not check this in a government record. So it stays an unproven story.</aside>

<aside class="note"><b>The National Book Trust book gets a date wrong</b>: It says Ashfaqulla was hanged in 1929. The same book says Bismil, hanged on the same day, died in 1927. All the other sources say 1927.</aside>

## After the hangings

The National Book Trust book says the people of Gorakhpur took Bismil's body around the city, and that people showered flowers and money on it. English Wikipedia says that after the four were hanged, their group changed its name and its aims. It became the Hindustan Socialist Republican Association.

<aside class="note"><b>Why "Association"?</b> Before September 1928 the group was called the HRA, the Hindustan Republican Association. After that it was the HSRA, with "Socialist" added.</aside>

## Why it matters

A plea can fail and still mean something. The 78 names did not save the four men.

But they show that in 1927, people who disagreed about nearly everything were able to agree on one thing. They felt that these young men, whatever they had done, should not be hanged.

The government saw it differently. According to the Hindi account, officials in London thought Bismil was too dangerous to be spared. Both views were real, and both belong in the story.
