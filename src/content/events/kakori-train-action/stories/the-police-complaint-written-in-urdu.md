In a small police station near Lucknow, there is a photo frame on the wall. Inside it is not a photograph. It is a page of old paper, yellow and faded.

It is part of a police report. It was written almost a hundred years ago, about one of the most famous robberies in India's history.

## What happened at Kakori

On the evening of 9 August 1925, a train called the Number 8 Down was on its way to Lucknow. It was carrying money that belonged to the British government.

Near a village called Kakori, someone pulled the emergency chain. The train stopped. A group of young revolutionaries took the government's money bags from the guard's carriage.

The leader was Ram Prasad Bismil. With him were nine others, among them Ashfaqulla Khan, Rajendra Lahiri and a young Chandrashekhar Azad.

The robbery did not go exactly as planned. A passenger named Ahmad Ali had stepped down from the train. He was hit by a gunshot and died.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: the Hindustan Times report calls it an accidental shot. Wikipedia says the bullet was fired by one of the young men, Manmathnath Gupta.</aside>

## The report

Two days later, on 11 August 1925, the robbery was reported at the Kakori police station. The report came from the government, through an officer of the railways.

<aside class="note"><b>FIR</b>: First Information Report. It is the first written report the police make about a crime.</aside>

In 1925, police reports in this part of India were written in Urdu. So this one was too. Later, someone translated it into Hindi and attached the translation to it.

## What the report says

The report is not complete. Parts of it have faded so much that they cannot be read. Even the number of the report is lost. But the Hindi translation gives every detail that could still be read.

It says that 20 to 25 unknown people robbed the train. In fact, there were only about ten. The police did not yet know who they were.

It says the news of the robbery was first sent to the police from the moving train itself, through a machine for sending messages that was fitted in it.

It lists the crime under Section 396 of the Indian Penal Code. That is the law for "dacoity with murder": a robbery by a gang of five or more people, in which someone is killed.

And it gives the amount of money stolen, very exactly: 4,601 rupees, 15 annas and 6 pies.

<aside class="note"><b>Annas and pies</b>: in those days a rupee was divided into 16 annas, and each anna into 12 pies. So 15 annas and 6 pies was nearly one more rupee.</aside>

<aside class="note"><b>How much money?</b> The report says 4,601 rupees, 15 annas and 6 pies. Wikipedia gives a much bigger figure, about 8,000 rupees. The sources do not explain the difference.</aside>

## Found in an old register

A few years ago, a reporter from the Hindustan Times asked to see the report. The police at Kakori searched through old files for hours. At last they found the register for 1925. It was in bad shape. Its pages were torn and yellow, and the Urdu writing was fading.

Inside was the report of the train robbery, with its Hindi translation.

Today, a copy of one page hangs in a frame at the police station. "It is not a complete copy, only one page has been preserved," the officer in charge told the newspaper in 2024. "We do not know where the original copy is."

The Kakori police station itself is older than the robbery. It opened in 1903.

## What happened next

The report does not say how the case ended. But we know. About forty people were arrested. After a long trial, four men were sentenced to death: Ram Prasad Bismil, Ashfaqulla Khan, Rajendra Lahiri and Roshan Singh. Many others were sent to prison.

## Why it matters

The report was written by people who saw a crime and did not yet know the names behind it. To them, it was just "20 to 25 unknown people".

Today, we know every one of their names, and India remembers them as freedom fighters. A single faded page in Urdu reminds us how it all began: as an ordinary police case, on an ordinary page, in a small station near Lucknow.
