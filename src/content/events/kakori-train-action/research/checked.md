# Kakori FIR: what was checked (21 October 2026)

Source: Hindustan Times, "Kakori FIR copy: Priceless page from the past", Aakash Ghosh,
10 August 2024 (PDF sent by the owner; the site is blocked here). Saved as ht-kakori-fir-2024.pdf.

Used, checked against a second source where possible:
- FIR lodged 11 August 1925, two days after the 9 August robbery (date of robbery also on Wikipedia).
- Written in Urdu, later translated into Hindi; one page kept framed at Kakori police station (HT only).
- 20-25 unknown accused; Section 396 IPC (section text checked on courtbook.in).
- Amount 4,601 rupees 15 annas 6 pies. Wikipedia says about 8,000 rupees: shown as "Two versions" note.
- Passenger killed: HT says accidental shot; Wikipedia names Ahmad Ali and Manmathnath Gupta: both given.
- Police station set up 1903; quote from SHO Nawab Ahmad (HT only, labelled as said to the newspaper).

Left out:
- "EEO of the railways": the abbreviation is not explained anywhere; written as "an officer of the railways".
- HT's sentence that the money was "deposited in the guard's carriage" (unclear wording).
- Udai Khatri's remark about the State Archives (not needed for the story).
- The HT photograph of the printed FIR copy (copyright, cannot be reused).

## Second round (22 October 2026): four new stories (trial, plea to the Viceroy, earlier robberies, Ahmad Ali)

Opened this session: NBT book "Hanged for Patriotism" (full text), Allahabad High Court history (Kakori section), English Wikipedia (Kakori train robbery), Hindi Wikipedia (Kakori Kand), LegalBites case study, a Substack article (Kakori 1925, used only for cross-checking).

### Where the sources disagree (all shown in stories)
- Trial start: 4 Jan 1926 at the Ring Theatre (NBT, which says the building is now Lucknow GPO), 1 May 1926 (English Wikipedia, LegalBites), 21 May 1926 (Substack). High Court history: "about 8 months". Judgment 6 Apr 1927 (all agree).
- Special judge: Hamilton (High Court, NBT, English Wikipedia) vs "Ainuddin" (Hindi Wikipedia). Followed the three.
- Chief Court judgment: 11 Aug 1927 (High Court history, LegalBites) vs 22 Aug 1927 (English and Hindi Wikipedia, Substack).
- Who was sentenced together: High Court history says 3 in the main case (Bismil, Lahiri, Roshan Singh), Ashfaqulla in a supplementary case. NBT says all four were sentenced together. Followed the court.
- Dates of the three earlier robberies: Bamrauli 25 Dec 1924 (all); Bichpuri 9 Mar 1925 (High Court) vs 7 Mar (Hindi Wikipedia); Dwarkapur 25 May (High Court) vs 24 May (both Wikipedias).
- Hanging deadline: "by 16 December" (LegalBites) vs "by 19 December" (English Wikipedia). Not used as a fact.
- Why Lahiri was hanged early: NBT says fear of a rescue (Azad, Manmathnath Gupta at Gonda); no other source gives a reason. Kept as an unproven story.
- NBT slips: says Ashfaqulla was hanged on 19 Dec 1929 (should be 1927).
- Loot: unchanged from the first round (Rs 4,553-3-6 / 4,601-15-6 / about 8,000).
- Wikipedia's own list of sentences contradicts itself in places (Banwari Lal 3 vs 5 years, Pranawesh Chatterjee 5 vs 4, Mukundi Lal 10 years vs Kala Pani). Only sentences that were not contradicted were used.

### Used with a label ("one account says")
- Courtroom scenes (Bismil asking to argue his own case; the "employees" exchange; the 76-page argument): Hindi Wikipedia only. The "king-maker needs no degree" exchange was left out.
- Roshan Singh as present at Kakori (Dictionary of Martyrs, reported by an earlier researcher, PDF not opened) vs absent (NBT, English Wikipedia).
- Gandhi asking for mercy: English Wikipedia and LegalBites both hedge it ("claimed", "reportedly"). Left as a maybe.
- Lahiri's 14 Dec and Roshan Singh's 13 Dec letters: printed in NBT, not checked against originals. Labelled.

### Left out
- The laundry-mark story (the sheet left behind led police to Banwari Lal): Hindi Wikipedia only.
- "Mera Rang De Basanti Chola" sung in court: Hindi Wikipedia only; and a separate question of authorship.
- Claims that officials bribed accused men to become approvers: no source cited.
- Who the approvers were: the Wikipedia and Substack lists differ (Banwari Lal / Banarsi Lal, Indubhushan Mitra, Gopi Mohan).
- The exact contents of Bismil's 9 Sept 1927 letter to Malaviya: not found.
