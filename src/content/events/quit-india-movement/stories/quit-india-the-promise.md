On the night of 8 August 1942, Gandhi told the crowd at Gowalia Tank Maidan how he wanted the fight to be fought. "There is nothing but purest Ahimsa in all that I am saying and doing today," he said. "Speaking for myself, I can say that I have never felt any hatred." And then: "Our quarrel is not with the British people, we fight their imperialism."

The Congress resolution said the same: "They must remember that nonviolence is the basis of this movement." That was the promise.

<aside class="note"><b>Ahimsa</b>: non-violence, the refusal to harm any living being.</aside>

## What Gandhi had said before

In 1922, Gandhi had called off a movement when it turned violent. Before Quit India began, someone asked whether he would do the same this time. "I am the same Gandhi as I was in 1922," he replied.

But in June 1942, he had also written in his paper, *Harijan*, that he had waited long enough. "If in spite of all precautions rioting takes place, it cannot be helped," he wrote. The historian Sankar Ghose, quoted by a Congress writer, says Gandhi was in a hurry that year, and was ready to put up with "fifteen days of chaos".

## What happened

Then the leaders were arrested and the call was left to the people. Violence was frequent, even though Gandhi had asked for non-violence. Post offices, railway stations and police stations were set on fire. In September, crowds in Madhya Pradesh, Bombay and the United Provinces threw bombs at the police. Bridges were destroyed and rail tracks removed.

A Congress leader, Acharya Kripalani, wrote that if the people had had the leaders to guide them, "such wanton destruction would not have taken place".

## Ballia

The Ballia district in the United Provinces shows how it could go. On 16 August, a crowd attacked the treasury at Rasra. On 18 August, about 25,000 people came back to the police station at Bairia. An officer had taken down the flag they had put up there three days before. The crowd tried again and again to raise it.

The police fired for about six hours. At least twenty people died, and about a hundred were hurt. Among the dead was the local leader Dharam Das Misra, and a boy of twelve. Both died while trying to hoist the flag.

The district's own history says what followed. The firing "compelled the people to take up arms, ignoring altogether the spirit of non-violence which had been their guiding principle till then".

By 20 August, the people of Ballia declared independence and formed a government under Chittu Pandey. It lasted only a few days. In the night of 22 and 23 August, the army marched in. The British officer in charge is said to have telegraphed the Governor: "Ballia reconquered."

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: The district history says Ballia declared independence on 20 August. Wikipedia's life of Chittu Pandey says the government was set up on 19 August.</aside>

## Silence

Gandhi was in the Aga Khan Palace by then. On 20 August 1942, he took a vow of silence. He broke it 91 hours later, on 24 August.

## Why it matters

The promise was easy to make on a calm night in Bombay. In Ballia, the people tried to keep it for days. Then, according to the district's own history, the firing pushed them to take up arms.

Those who write about that August have to say two things together. In many places, the movement broke the rule of non-violence. And in many places, it met heavy firing first.
