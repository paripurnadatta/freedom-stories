In the early months of 1942, the war came close to India. Singapore fell to Japan on 15 February. Rangoon fell on 7 March. The war had reached India's border. Could Britain still defend the country? Many Indians were not sure.

In March, Britain sent Sir Stafford Cripps to offer India a plan for the future. Congress turned it down. The plan did not offer complete independence, and it set no date for self-government. After that, Gandhi felt that the time for waiting had gone. In June he wrote that he "cannot afford to wait".

<aside class="note"><b>Congress</b>: the Indian National Congress, the largest party fighting for India's freedom.</aside>

## A hard decision

Not everyone in Congress agreed. In April, the Working Committee voted 11 to 6 for Gandhi's own draft. But Nehru wrote a milder one, and to avoid a split, Congress accepted that instead. Nehru and Maulana Azad still had doubts. They did not want to weaken the fight against Japan. Another senior leader, Rajagopalachari, left Congress over the plan. Patel and Rajendra Prasad backed Gandhi strongly.

On 14 July, the Working Committee met at Wardha and passed a resolution calling on the British to leave India. It was a compromise. At Nehru's request, it also said that Allied armies could stay to fight Japan. Before the movement began, someone asked Gandhi if he would call it off, as he had in 1922, if violence broke out. He answered, "I am the same Gandhi as I was in 1922." The full All India Congress Committee would now meet in Bombay.

## The maidan

The meeting began on 7 August 1942 at Gowalia Tank Maidan. Nehru moved the resolution and Patel seconded it. The words were plain: "the immediate ending of British rule in India is an urgent necessity, both for the sake of India and for the success of the cause of the United Nations."

The resolution did not only speak to the British. It spoke to Indians too, and it gave them a warning. "They must remember that nonviolence is the basis of this movement." It also said the power, when it came, would not belong to Congress. "The power, when it comes, will belong to the whole people of India."

The most unusual part was a plan for the day when there would be no leaders. "A time may come when it may not be possible to issue instructions or for instructions to reach our people." On that day, the resolution said, every man and woman in the movement must act alone, "within the four corners of the general instructions issued". Each person must be "his own guide".

The committee approved the resolution by a very large majority. It created an "electrifying atmosphere" in the country. Gandhi spoke that night, and the meeting ended after midnight. He told the crowd to keep two words in their hearts: "Do or Die". His speech is told in another story on this site.

<aside class="note"><b>Two words, one story</b>: Mani Bhavan, the Gandhi museum in Mumbai, tells how the slogan "Quit India" was chosen. One of Gandhi's colleagues suggested "Get Out", and Gandhi said no, because it was rude. Rajagopalachari offered "Retreat" and "Withdraw", and Gandhi said no again. Then Yusuf Meherally gave him a bow with the words "Quit India" on it. Gandhi said, "Amen". This account comes from one source only.</aside>

## Five in the morning

The government did not wait. At five in the morning on Sunday, 9 August, police arrested Gandhi, Nehru, Patel, Azad and the other top leaders. Gandhi was taken to the Aga Khan Palace near Poona. The whole Working Committee was arrested. The Congress committees were declared illegal.

The government also banned processions, meetings and gatherings. Police warned people to stay away from the maidan. A large crowd came anyway.

## The flag

The session of the All India Congress Committee had not yet been closed. Someone had to preside over what was left of it. Aruna Asaf Ali, who was 33, took on the task. She hoisted the Congress flag at Gowalia Tank Maidan.

Then the police moved in. They used lathis and tear gas to break up the crowd. The flag was pulled down. The volunteers who ran to save it were beaten back.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: The Mani Bhavan museum and the Swarthmore database speak of a lathi charge and tear gas. Wikipedia's life of Aruna Asaf Ali says the police also fired on the gathering. The Swarthmore database says at least nine people died in clashes with police in Bombay that day. We do not know which of them died at the maidan.</aside>

Within three or four days, the Congress was declared an unlawful body. The leaders were in prison. Nobody at the top was free to tell the country what to do.

## The woman who vanished

A warrant was issued against Aruna Asaf Ali. She went into hiding. Her property was seized and sold, and the government offered a reward of 5,000 rupees for her capture.

She did not stop working. Hidden, she edited a monthly magazine for Congress called *Inquilab*, together with Ram Manohar Lohia. In a 1944 issue, she urged young people to forget long arguments about violence and non-violence, and to join the revolution.

Gandhi even sent her a handwritten note. He asked her to come out of hiding and give herself up. Her work was done, he wrote, and she could use the reward money for the Harijan cause. She kept the note all her life, but she stayed hidden. She came out only in 1946, when the warrant against her was taken back.

## A voice from nowhere

Other people kept the movement talking. There was a secret radio station, the Congress Radio, broadcasting on 42.34 metres. It was "somewhere in Bombay" and was moved from place to place so that the police could not find it. Its brain was Usha Mehta, then a student. The station carried news of the underground movement and messages for its workers.

## Why it matters

The Quit India resolution had a plan for a day with no leaders. On 9 August 1942, that day came before sunrise. What happened next depended on thousands of people who had never met the leaders.

Aruna Asaf Ali's flag at Gowalia Tank Maidan was one answer. It was a small act, and she was in danger for four years because of it. Many others, in many other places, made their own answers in the weeks ahead.
