On 31 August 1942, the Viceroy, Lord Linlithgow, sent a telegram to Prime Minister Winston Churchill in London. He wrote that he was "engaged here in meeting by far the most serious rebellion since that of 1857". He added a hard sentence: "the gravity and extent of which we have so far concealed from the world for reasons of military security."

The Revolt of 1857 was the great uprising against the East India Company and the British. The Viceroy was now putting what he saw around him beside it.

## The soldiers

Britain was fighting a world war. Even so, it sent 57 battalions of British troops to put down the movement. The whole police force was used too. Airfield building, which the war needed, was delayed by four to six weeks.

Communications had been cut so badly that ground forces often could not reach some areas. More than 100,000 people were arrested. By the end of 1943 alone, over 60,000 had been held. Crowds were fined, and some demonstrators were flogged in public.

## The numbers

Then came the question of how many had died. There are three answers.

The British government gave one. By its estimate, 1,028 people were killed and 3,125 wounded. The Secretary of State for India gave a nearby figure for the months from 9 August to 30 November 1942: 1,008 killed and 3,275 seriously hurt.

Congress gave another. Its estimates ran from 4,000 to 10,000 dead. Jawaharlal Nehru's own guess was close to 10,000.

<aside class="note"><b>Three answers</b>: Wikipedia lists the British estimate of 1,028 dead and the Congress estimate of 4,000 to 10,000. The Indian National Congress magazine gives the Secretary of State's figure of 1,008. Another writer says Nehru thought it nearer 10,000. These numbers cannot all be right.</aside>

The Congress writer Praveen Davar gives the Secretary of State's figure and then adds: "The popular estimate was however very much higher." The count has been argued over ever since.

## Why it matters

The numbers tell us how big the fight was. A government that sends 57 battalions in the middle of a world war is taking it seriously. The true number of the dead is not known. It lies somewhere between 1,028 and 10,000, and the sources cannot say where.

By March 1943, the movement had mostly died down. The Viceroy had called it a rebellion, and he had kept the size of it from the world for the sake of the war.
