Why would someone walk towards a police station, carrying a flag, after the police had told them to stop? In the autumn of 1942, two people did exactly that, eleven days apart, in two different corners of India.

## Gohpur

Kanaklata Barua was born on 22 December 1924 in Borangabari, a village in Gohpur, Assam. Her mother died when she was five. Her father died when she was thirteen. She went to school only up to class three, and then she left to look after her younger brothers and sisters.

When the Quit India movement came, she joined a group of young people in the Gohpur area. They called themselves the Mrityu Bahini, or "death squad". On 20 September 1942, the group decided that she would raise the national flag at the local police station.

She led a procession of unarmed villagers towards it. The officer in charge, Rebati Mahan Som, warned them of the consequences. The procession went on. The police fired. Kanaklata Barua was shot. A young man called Mukunda Kakati picked up the flag, and he was shot too. Both of them died.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: Wikipedia gives her age as 17, which fits her date of birth. The Swarthmore database describes a 15-year-old girl in an Assam village who led 500 men to a police station. In that account, she was shot in the chest, and someone else in the crowd raised the flag. We use 17, because it matches her birth date.</aside>

## Tamluk

Eleven days later, on 29 September, a crowd of about 20,000 marched on the Tamluk police station in Bengal. They came in five processions along five roads. At the head of one was Matangini Hazra, a widow aged 73. She carried the flag.

She was shot, and she died holding it. Nine others died in Tamluk that day. Two of them were boys, aged 13 and 14.

The story of the marching widow is told in more detail in "The Government Across the Water".

<aside class="note"><b>Last words</b>: Many tellings give both women a speech before they died. Wikipedia says Kanaklata Barua made "a passionate speech", but it does not give her words. No source we read gives any words spoken by Matangini Hazra. This story does not make any up.</aside>

<aside class="note"><b>Mukunda Kakati</b>: Wikipedia spells his name Kakoti. The Commons statue is labelled Kakati. This story uses Kakati.</aside>

## Why it matters

Kanaklata Barua had left school after class three to bring up her brothers and sisters. Matangini Hazra was 73. Neither was a general or a minister. One carried a flag because the young people around her chose her to carry it, and the other carried one at the head of a line of thousands. The police had warned the marchers at Gohpur, and the marchers walked on.

In 1997, the Indian Coast Guard named a ship after Kanaklata Barua. A statue now stands in front of the police station at Gohpur, where she and Mukunda Kakati were shot.
