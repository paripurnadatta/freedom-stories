# Production research: quit-india-movement

Prepared 26 October 2026. This is a writing pack for Claude, not a set of finished stories.
ChatGPT did the research; Claude writes the stories. No story file has been written by ChatGPT,
and nothing here is approved for `main`.

Six angles are planned: two flagship and four short. Every fact below carries a source tag that is
resolved in "Sources opened" at the end. Where only one opened source supports a fact, the tag
carries `(single)`. Quotations are copied exactly from the opened page; where the page itself has a
typo, it is marked `[sic]` and the story should paraphrase instead of quoting. Anything I could not
confirm is marked UNVERIFIED and must not be used.

Sizes: flagship means 900+ words of the writer's own prose; short means 350+.

Two things to know before reading:

- **The 8 August speech and Gandhi's imprisonment are already covered** by the person subject
  `mahatma-gandhi`, flagship story `do-or-die.md`. Do not write that story again. Angle 1 below
  starts on the evening of 8 August but its subject is the day after: the arrests, the empty
  platform, Aruna Asaf Ali and the crowd.
- **Three related subjects already exist as empty stubs**: `congress-radio` (E-0062),
  `ballia-parallel-government` (E-0115) and `satara-prati-sarkar` (E-0078). Their stories belong in
  their own folders when they are written, so the six planned here are movement-wide. They are
  listed at the end as extra angles for later.

## 1. Nine August - FLAGSHIP

Proposed slug: `quit-india-nine-august`
One city, thirty-six hours: the session that ended after midnight, the arrests at five in the
morning, the empty platform, the flag, the tear gas.

Facts, in the order a story would need them:

1. The All India Congress Committee (AICC) session in Bombay began on 7 August 1942 and ended after
   midnight of 8/9 August at Gowalia Tank Maidan. [MB]
2. On 8 August the AICC passed the resolution. Jawaharlal Nehru moved it and Sardar Patel seconded
   it. [INC]
3. The resolution's core demand: "the immediate ending of British rule in India is an urgent
   necessity, both for the sake of India and for the success of the cause of the United Nations."
   [RES]
4. The operative paragraph: "The Committee resolves, therefore, to sanction, for the vindication of
   India's inalienable right to freedom and independence, the starting of a mass struggle on
   non-violent lines on the widest possible scale, so that the country might utilize all the
   nonviolent strength it has gathered during the last twenty-two years of peaceful struggle."
   [RES]
5. The instruction the story turns on: "A time may come when it may not be possible to issue
   instructions or for instructions to reach our people, and when no Congress committees can
   function. When this happens, every man and woman who is participating in this movement must
   function for himself or herself within the four corners of the general instructions issued.
   Every Indian who desires freedom and strives for it must be his own guide urging him on along the
   hard road where there is no resting place and which leads ultimately to the independence and
   deliverance of India." [RES]
6. And: "They must remember that nonviolence is the basis of this movement." [RES]
7. And: "The power, when it comes, will belong to the whole people of India." [RES]
8. Gandhi spoke that night, 8 August 1942, a Saturday. He was 72. [SP has the speech; age 72 is
   from the existing `do-or-die` story and from his birth date, 2 October 1869]
9. "I want freedom immediately, this very night, before dawn, if it can be had." [SP]
10. "Here is a mantra, a short one, that I give you. You may imprint it on your hearts and let every
    breath of yours give expression to it. The mantra is : 'Do or Die'." [SP]
11. "We shall either free India or die in the attempt; we shall not live to see the perpetuation of
    our slavery." [SP]
12. "I want you to know and feel that there is nothing but purest Ahimsa in all that I am saying and
    doing today." [SP]
13. "Speaking for myself, I can say that I have never felt any hatred." [SP]
14. "Our quarrel is not with the British people, we fight their imperialism." [SP]
15. "The bond of the slave is snapped the moment he consider himself to be a free being." [SP,
    sic; paraphrase in the story]
16. "He who loses his life will gain it; he who will seek to save it shall lose it. Freedom is not
    for the coward or the faint-hearted." [SP]
17. At five in the morning on Sunday 9 August, Gandhi, Nehru, Patel, Azad and the other top leaders
    were arrested. [INC]
18. Gandhi was taken to the Aga Khan Palace in Poona. Members of the Working Committee were
    arrested too, and all Congress Committees were declared illegal. [GS]
19. Most of the Working Committee was in prison within twenty-four hours. [NVD]
20. The government had banned public processions, meetings and assemblies. A large crowd came to
    Gowalia Tank Maidan anyway, despite a police warning. [MB]
21. Aruna Asaf Ali presided over what was left of the session and hoisted the Congress flag. [WA]
    [MB] [W]
22. Then the police charged. "Lathi charge and tear gas was used by the police to disperse the crowd
    which had gathered at Gowalia Tank Maidan." The flag was pulled down, and the volunteers who
    went to rescue it were beaten back. [MB]
23. Aruna was 33 that day. She was born on 16 July 1909. [WA]
24. At least nine people died in clashes with the police in Bombay on the day of the arrests. [NVD]
    (single; the story should say "at least nine", not a round figure)
25. Within three or four days the Congress was declared an unlawful organisation. [NVD]
26. A warrant was issued for Aruna. She went underground instead. Her property was seized and sold,
    and the government announced a reward of 5,000 rupees for her capture. [WA] (reward: single)
27. In hiding she edited the monthly `Inquilab` with Ram Manohar Lohia. [WA]
28. She stayed underground until the warrant was withdrawn in 1946. [WA]

**Shape.** Open in the hall after midnight: the resolution has just passed, and the last line the
delegates hear is that they may soon have no one to give them orders. Cut to five in the morning.
Then the maidan: a crowd with no leaders on the platform, a 33-year-old woman stepping up, the flag,
the tear gas, the flag coming down, people going back for it. End with the number nine, and with what
the resolution had told ordinary people to do.

**Watch.** Do not invent the size of the crowd, the weather, or what Aruna was thinking. Nobody in
the sources gives the crowd's size. The nine deaths are a Bombay figure, not an all-India one.

## 2. The government across the water - FLAGSHIP

Proposed slug: `quit-india-the-government-across-the-water`
Tamluk, in Midnapore district, Bengal: a district that stopped taking orders and ran itself for
nearly two years.

Facts, in the order a story would need them:

1. Tamluk and Contai are two subdivisions of Midnapore district in Bengal, today Purba Medinipur.
   [WT]
2. The trouble began over rice. On 8 September 1942, about 2,500 people tried to stop mill owners
   exporting rice at Danipur, under Mahisadal police station. The police opened fire and killed
   three people. The bodies were not handed to the families; they were burnt on one pyre. The next
   day the District Magistrate raided the villages around and arrested 200 people. [IHC1]
3. On 27 September 1942, at Iswarpur in Nandigram, police went to arrest two men at the local
   Congress office. A crowd surrounded them. The police set the thatched office on fire and then
   fired to get out: four people died and twenty-five were injured. The crowd followed them five
   kilometres to the Narghat police camp. [IHC1]
4. On 28 September, at Brindabanpore near Iswarpur, two people died of bullet injuries and three
   were seriously injured. [IHC1]
5. That night, the night of 28 September, the link roads of Tamluk subdivision were cut in several
   places, culverts were broken, big trees were felled across the roads, holes were dug in the
   roads, and telegraph and telephone lines were cut. [IHC1]
6. On the afternoon of 29 September 1942, a Tuesday, about 20,000 people marched on Tamluk police
   station in five processions from different roads. [IHC1]
7. At the head of one procession was Matangini Hazra, a widow of 73, carrying the national flag.
   She was shot and died still holding it. [IHC1] (Her own subject folder gives her birth year as
   1870, which would make her 72. Use "in her seventies" in the story and put the disagreement in
   a "Two versions" note, or say "73" and cite IHC.)
8. Nine other people died that day at Tamluk, including Lakshminarayan Das, who was 13, and
   Purimadhab Parmanik, who was 14. [IHC1]
9. Mahisadal and Sutahata police stations were raided the same afternoon. Nandigram police station
   was raided on 30 September by a crowd of 10,000; four people died on the spot and one more in
   hospital. [IHC1]
10. Between 29 and 30 September the ordinary civil administration collapsed in Tamluk subdivision.
    The District Magistrate wrote: "The mob in Tamluk sub-division is apparently not afraid of
    (police or military) firing." [IHC1]
11. The parallel government was announced on 17 September 1942 by the Tamluk Sub-Divisional
    Congress Committee, under the name "Mahabharatiya Juktarashtra - Tamralipta Jatiya Sarkar".
    [IHC1] Other sources give 17 December 1942 as its founding date. [WT] [TRI] [N18]
    **Two versions: 17 September (announced) / 17 December (founded).** The story can say the
    announcement came in September and the government was fully working by December, and put the
    disagreement in a note.
12. It ran its own police stations, courts, revenue collection, army and intelligence service, and
    a postal system. It also ran schools. [WT] [IHC1] [IHC2] [TRI]
13. It was led by a `Sarbadhinayak` (supreme leader) with `Sachivas` (ministers). The first
    Sarbadhinayak was Satish Chandra Samanta. [WT] [IHC2]
14. Its armed wing was the `Bidyut Bahini` (Lightning Brigade), commanded by Sushil Kumar Dhara. It
    was raised in case Subhas Chandra Bose's Indian National Army reached Bengal. [WT]
15. It had an action squad called the `Garam Dal` (hot team) to act against people suspected of
    corruption, spying or working against the Sarkar. [IHC1]
16. Its newspaper was `Biplabi` (Revolutionary), which had been the Quit India paper of the Tamluk
    Sub-Divisional Congress Committee and became the mouthpiece of the Sarkar. [IHC1] [WT]
17. Its courts handled 1,681 cases. [SCROLL] (single)
18. On 16 October 1942 a cyclone and tidal wave hit the district. The government delayed relief and
    delayed letting the news out. The Sarkar organised relief, went after hoarders and shared out
    paddy. [IHC2] [SCROLL]
19. The Governor of Bengal, Richard Casey, wrote to the Viceroy, Lord Wavell: "A high proportion of
    the inhabitants are in sympathy with the local 'National Government' or 'Jatiya Sarkar' as it
    calls itself." [IHC1]
20. The Chief Minister of Bengal, A. K. Fazlul Haque, told the legislative assembly: "Midnapore had a
    parallel government with its military and police forces and intelligence branch. It had its
    jails where people were imprisoned and, in some cases, the people had actually paralysed the
    government." [IHC1]
21. It lasted 21 months. [TRI] [IHC1]
22. It was dissolved on Gandhi's advice. [TRI] [IHC1] [N18]
23. The date of dissolution is disputed: 8 August 1944 [TRI] [WT] or 31 August 1944 [IHC1] [IHC2]
    [N18]. **Two versions.**
24. Gandhi visited Tamluk in December 1945. He said the rising had been "brave and spontaneous" but
    that it had moved away from non-violence, and that without non-violence swaraj could not be
    reached. [IHC2]
25. A reporter at that visit wrote: "Unfortunately, Bengal does not possess a Jawaharlal and that is
    perhaps why Calcutta does not know enough of Tamluk's glorious record". [IHC2]
26. Several of its leaders later held office in West Bengal; Ajoy Mukherjee became Chief Minister
    three times. India issued a commemorative stamp in 2002. [WT]

**Shape.** Open with rice leaving the district by lorry and a crowd standing in the road. Then the
firings, one by one, each a little worse. Then the night the roads were cut, and the five processions
on 29 September, and the old woman with the flag. Then the strange part: a government that issues
notices, hears cases, collects money, runs a post and a newspaper, and is described in the Raj's own
letters as a government. End with the cyclone, the two dates, and the reporter's line about Bengal
not having a Jawaharlal.

**Watch.** Do not make the Sarkar a fairy tale: it had an armed wing and an action squad. Say plainly
that it was not non-violent in the way Gandhi asked. Matangini's death belongs in this story and in
angle 5; check with the owner that both is acceptable, or keep the detail in one place.

## 3. Seven days - SHORT

Proposed slug: `quit-india-seven-days`
The first week of the movement, day by day, across the country: how a leaderless revolt spread.

Facts:

1. Sunday 9 August: the arrests; at least nine dead in Bombay. [NVD]
2. Within twenty-four hours the unrest had spread to Delhi, the United Provinces and Bihar. [NVD]
3. In the first two days of street fighting, police opened fire and killed twenty-four people. [NVD]
4. Monday 10 August onwards: mill workers struck. In Ahmedabad 8,000 mill workers left their jobs;
   mills and factories shut in Bombay and Poona. [NVD]
5. Students stopped going to school and joined the marches. [NVD]
6. In Delhi the police fired on 47 separate occasions on 11 and 12 August. [INC]
7. In the United Provinces there were 29 firings between 9 and 21 August: 76 people killed and 114
   seriously injured. [INC]
8. In the Central Provinces the police killed 64 people, wounded 102 and arrested 11,088 in the
   first three weeks. [INC]
9. For a week, business was paralysed in Bombay, Ahmedabad, Delhi, Bangalore and Calcutta. [INC]
10. The government banned processions, meetings and assemblies. [MB] [NVD]
11. "Even the private cars were not allowed to proceed unless there was a Gandhi cap on the head of
    at least one of the passengers." [MB]
12. In mid-August, protesters cut railway traffic to and from Bihar; air force planes machine-gunned
    unarmed satyagrahis lying on the ground. [NVD]
13. Between 9 August and 21 September, according to John F. Riddick, the movement attacked 550 post
    offices and 250 railway stations, destroyed 70 police stations, damaged 85 other government
    buildings, and cut telegraph wires about 2,500 times. The greatest violence was in Bihar. [W,
    citing Riddick]
14. The Government of India deployed 57 battalions of British troops to restore order. [W] [CJP]
15. At Ballia in the United Provinces, on 16 August the Rasra treasury was attacked. On 18 August a
    crowd of about 25,000 went back to the Bairia police station because an officer had taken down
    the flag they had planted. The police fired for about six hours. At least 20 people died and
    about 100 were injured; Dharam Das Misra and a boy of 12 died trying to hoist the flag. [BAL]
16. On 19 August about 50,000 people walked towards the Ballia jail to free their leaders. The
    District Magistrate released them. On 20 August the crowd declared independence and formed a
    popular government with Chittu Pandey at its head. [BAL]
17. On the night of 22/23 August the army entered Ballia and the popular government was overthrown.
    [BAL]

**Shape.** Seven short sections, Sunday to Sunday: the arrests, the first deaths, the mills, the
schools, the trains, the wires, the flag on the car. Each section one or two paragraphs, with one
concrete number or object. End on the second Sunday (16 August) with Ballia and the boy of 12, and
then pull back to say what the week had cost.

**Watch.** Do not say "the whole country rose". Say where each thing happened. The Delhi 47 and UP
29 figures are police firings, not deaths.

## 4. One thousand and twenty-eight - SHORT

Proposed slug: `quit-india-one-thousand-and-twenty-eight`
The bill, and the argument about the bill. Three different sets of numbers, and the Viceroy's private
letter.

Facts:

1. British estimates: 1,028 killed, 3,125 wounded, over 100,000 arrested. [W]
2. Congress estimates: 4,000 to 10,000 killed. [W]
3. The Secretary of State for India gave the figures for 9 August to 30 November 1942 as 1,008
   killed and 3,275 seriously injured. [INC]
4. Nehru's own estimate was close to 10,000 dead. [CJP]
5. Over 60,000 people had been arrested by the end of 1943 alone. [CJP]
6. More than 100,000 people were arrested in all. [W] [NVD]
7. Mass fines were levied and demonstrators were publicly flogged. [W] [CJP]
8. 57 battalions of British troops, with the whole police force, were used to suppress the movement.
   [CJP] [W]
9. The diversion delayed airfield construction by four to six weeks. [W, citing Dunlop]
10. Viceroy Linlithgow to Churchill, 31 August 1942: "I am engaged here in meeting by far the most
    serious rebellion since that of 1857, the gravity and extent of which we have so far concealed
    from the world for reasons of military security. Mob violence remains rampant over large tracts
    of the countryside and I am by no means confident that we may not see in September a formidable
    attempt to renew this widespread sabotage of our war effort. The lives of Europeans in outlying
    places are in jeopardy." [W] [CJP]
11. After Ballia was retaken, the British officer in charge is reported to have sent a telegram to
    the Governor of the United Provinces: "Ballia reconquered". [BAL]
12. The movement failed to end British rule at once. By March 1943 it had largely died down. [NVD]

**Shape.** Begin with a number on a page: 1,028. Then show that the same events come out as 1,008,
or 10,000, depending on who is counting. Explain plainly why: records kept by the rulers, villages
that never reported, families who did not go to the police. Then the Viceroy's letter, and the two
words "Ballia reconquered". End with what a number cannot hold.

**Watch.** Never present 1,028 as the true figure. The story's subject is the disagreement. Do not
say "thousands" when a source says a specific number.

## 5. The girls with the flag - SHORT

Proposed slug: `quit-india-the-girls-with-the-flag`
Two flag processions, eleven days apart: Gohpur in Assam on 20 September 1942 and Tamluk in Bengal on
29 September 1942.

Facts:

1. Kanaklata Barua was born on 22 December 1924 in Borangabari, in the Gohpur area of the undivided
   Darrang district of Assam. Her mother died when she was five and her father when she was thirteen.
   She left school after class three to look after her younger brothers and sisters. [WK]
2. She joined the `Mrityu Bahini` (death squad), a group of young people in the Gohpur subdivision.
   [WK]
3. On 20 September 1942, a Sunday, the Bahini decided she would hoist the national flag at the local
   police station. She led a procession of unarmed villagers towards it. [WK]
4. The officer in charge of the police station, Rebati Mahan Som, warned the procession not to go on.
   It went on. The police fired. Kanaklata was shot. The flag was picked up by Mukunda Kakati, who
   was shot too. Both were killed. She was 17. [WK]
5. A separate account says the girl was 15 and that she led 500 men; someone in the crowd hoisted
   the flag in the end. [NVD] **Two versions of her age (15 or 17).** Her birth date makes her 17;
   use 17 and note the other figure.
6. Matangini Hazra, a widow of 73, was at the head of one of the five processions that marched on
   Tamluk police station on 29 September 1942. She was shot and died holding the flag. [IHC1]
7. Nine other people died at Tamluk that day, including two boys, aged 13 and 14. [IHC1]
8. The crowd at Tamluk that afternoon was about 20,000. [IHC1]
9. Matangini was known as `Gandhi Buri`, "the old Gandhi woman". [Photo title on Commons; treat as
   a nickname, and check before using it in the story.]

**Shape.** Two scenes, side by side. A 17-year-old in a village in Assam, and a woman in her
seventies in a Bengal town, eleven days apart, doing the same thing: walking towards a police
station with a flag. Then the two names, and the boys of 13 and 14. End with the flag, not with the
shooting: in both places, the flag went up in the end.

**Watch.** Do not describe the shooting itself. No invented last words: several popular tellings give
Kanaklata a speech; no opened source has it. Keep the deaths off-page and let the walk carry the
story.

## 6. The promise - SHORT

Proposed slug: `quit-india-the-promise`
Gandhi asked for a fight without hatred. Within a week, in many places, that promise broke. What
happened, and what Gandhi did about it.

Facts:

1. The resolution: "They must remember that nonviolence is the basis of this movement." [RES]
2. Gandhi, 8 August: "there is nothing but purest Ahimsa in all that I am saying and doing today".
   [SP]
3. Gandhi: "Speaking for myself, I can say that I have never felt any hatred." [SP]
4. Gandhi: "Our quarrel is not with the British people, we fight their imperialism." [SP]
5. "Despite Gandhi's call to non-violent direct action, campaigner violence was frequent." [NVD]
6. Bombs exploded, government buildings were set on fire, electricity was cut, and transport and
   communication lines were cut. [W]
7. In September, crowds in Madhya Pradesh, Bombay and the United Provinces threw bombs at police.
   [NVD]
8. At Ballia, after the police fired for six hours at Bairia on 18 August: "The indiscriminate firing
   at the Bairia police-station and at other places compelled the people to take up arms, ignoring
   altogether the spirit of non-violence which had been their guiding principle till then." [BAL]
9. In Ballia, between 1942 and 1944, nobody dared wear a Gandhi cap; men found wearing one were shot
   dead. [BAL] (single; handle with great care, and do not make it the centre of the story)
10. Gandhi took a vow of silence on 20 August 1942 and broke it after 91 hours, on 24 August. [GS]
11. His 21-day fast came later, from 10 February to 2 March 1943, and is already told in
    `mahatma-gandhi/stories/do-or-die.md`. [existing story; do not retell]

**Shape.** Set the promise out first, in Gandhi's own words. Then three scenes where it broke: a
burnt post office, a crowd with bombs, the six hours at Bairia. Then the silence: four days in which
the man who had told India what to do said nothing at all. End on the difficulty rather than a
moral: the movement was meant to be non-violent, and the government's firing made that very hard.

**Watch.** Do not blame the people who fought back, and do not pretend the violence did not happen.
This is the honest story, and it is also the one that helps a child think.

## Extra angles (not in the six; for later, and for the three stub subjects)

7. **Congress Radio** - SHORT, belongs in `events/congress-radio/`. First broadcast 27 August 1942
   (Wikipedia, Mani Bhavan) or 14 August 1942 (BBC, Swarthmore) - **two versions**. Usha Mehta was
   22. Opening words: "This is the Congress Radio calling on (a wavelength of) 42.34 metres from
   somewhere in India." [WCR] It moved from flat to flat in Bombay, once to Nashik; police found it
   on 12 November 1942. She was sentenced to four years and released in March 1946. [WU] [WCR]
   [BBC] It reported the Chittagong raid, the firings at Ashti and Chimur, and the Jamshedpur
   strike. [CJP]
8. **Ballia** - SHORT, belongs in `events/ballia-parallel-government/`. See facts 15-17 of angle 3.
   [BAL] [WCP] Within a week soldiers marched in and the leaders had to flee. [WCP] A police van
   drove round the town firing at passers-by on 20 August. [BAL]
9. **Satara Prati Sarkar** - SHORT, belongs in `events/satara-prati-sarkar/`. It ran from August 1943
   to May 1946 in more than 150 villages, led by Nana Patil, with raids on treasuries and armouries.
   [PD] Nana Patil was underground for 44 months during Quit India. [WN] (Dates disagree with the
   1942 start often given; mark as two versions.)
10. **Why some Indians said no** - SHORT, politics. The Muslim League and Jinnah opposed the
    movement and supported the British war effort. [W] [WJ] The Hindu Mahasabha boycotted it;
    Savarkar wrote a letter called "Stick to your Posts". [W] Syama Prasad Mookerjee wrote to the
    Bengal government on 26 July 1942 about how to defeat the movement. [W] The Communist Party of
    India opposed it and supported the war after the Soviet Union was attacked. [W] Some princes
    opposed it too. [W] **Only Wikipedia supports the detail here, plus the CJP article on Savarkar's
    1939 meeting with Linlithgow. Do not write this one without one more source.**
11. **The slogan** - THIN. Gandhi wanted a short slogan. Someone suggested "Get Out"; he thought it
    impolite. Rajagopalachari suggested "Retreat" or "Withdraw". Yusuf Meherally gave Gandhi a bow
    with "Quit India" written on it, and Gandhi said "Amen". [MB] (single source; do not build a
    story on it, but it is a good detail inside another story.)
12. **The battleship** - THIN. The British kept a battleship ready to take Gandhi and the Congress
    leaders out of India, perhaps to South Africa or Yemen, and did not use it for fear of making
    the revolt worse. [W] (single)
13. **After the storm** - SHORT, 1943-47. The Bengal famine of 1943 suspended the movement in Bengal.
    [W] Gandhi was released on health grounds on 6 May 1944. [W] [existing story] Labour won the
    British election in 1945 promising independence. [W] Prisoners were released in 1945. [W] By
    March 1943 the campaign had largely died down, but it had shown that the Raj could not govern as
    before. [NVD]
14. **The underground** - SHORT. Leaders who were not caught went into hiding, printed pamphlets,
    ran radio stations and set up parallel governments. [W] [NVD] Women took on independent roles,
    printed and distributed leaflets, collected money and gave safe houses. [NVD] The Hazaribagh
    jailbreak has its own subject folder and should be written there.
