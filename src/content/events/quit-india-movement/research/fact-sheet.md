# Fact sheet: quit-india-movement

One page, for writing. Everything here was opened on 26 October 2026; the long version with every
tag is `chatgpt-report-2026-10-26.md`. Source keys are at the end. If a fact is not here and not in
the long report, do not write it.

Six stories: two flagship (900+ words), four short (350+). Do **not** re-tell the 8 August speech,
the Aga Khan Palace or the 1943 fast: that is already `mahatma-gandhi/stories/do-or-die.md`.

## 1. Nine August — FLAGSHIP

- The AICC session began in Bombay on 7 August 1942 and ended after midnight of 8/9 August at
  Gowalia Tank Maidan. Nehru moved the resolution, Patel seconded it. [MB; INC]
- The resolution's core: "the immediate ending of British rule in India is an urgent necessity, both
  for the sake of India and for the success of the cause of the United Nations." [RES]
- The instruction the story turns on: "A time may come when it may not be possible to issue
  instructions or for instructions to reach our people, and when no Congress committees can function.
  When this happens, every man and woman who is participating in this movement must function for
  himself or herself within the four corners of the general instructions issued. Every Indian who
  desires freedom and strives for it must be his own guide urging him on along the hard road where
  there is no resting place and which leads ultimately to the independence and deliverance of India."
  [RES]
- Also: "They must remember that nonviolence is the basis of this movement." And: "The power, when it
  comes, will belong to the whole people of India." [RES]
- Gandhi, that night: "I want freedom immediately, this very night, before dawn, if it can be had."
  "Here is a mantra, a short one, that I give you. You may imprint it on your hearts and let every
  breath of yours give expression to it. The mantra is : 'Do or Die'." [SP]
- At five in the morning on Sunday 9 August, Gandhi, Nehru, Patel, Azad and the other leaders were
  arrested. Gandhi went to the Aga Khan Palace, Poona; the Working Committee was arrested too; all
  Congress committees were declared illegal. [INC; GS]
- Processions, meetings and assemblies were banned. A large crowd came to the maidan anyway, despite
  a police warning. [MB]
- Aruna Asaf Ali presided over what was left of the session and hoisted the Congress flag. She was
  33 (born 16 July 1909). [MB; WA]
- "Lathi charge and tear gas was used by the police to disperse the crowd which had gathered at
  Gowalia Tank Maidan." The flag was pulled down; the volunteers who went to rescue it were beaten
  back. [MB]
- At least nine people died in clashes in Bombay that day. [NVD] (single source; say "at least nine",
  and say Bombay)
- Within three or four days the Congress was declared unlawful. [NVD]
- A warrant was issued for Aruna; she went underground, her property was seized and sold, and a
  reward of 5,000 rupees was announced. In hiding she edited the monthly `Inquilab` with Ram Manohar
  Lohia, and stayed underground until 1946. [WA]
- **Watch:** nobody gives the size of the crowd, the weather, or what she thought. Do not invent
  them, and do not give her a speech.

## 2. The government across the water — FLAGSHIP

Tamluk and Contai, two subdivisions of Midnapore, Bengal (today Purba Medinipur). [WT]

- 8 September 1942: about 2,500 people tried to stop mill owners taking rice out at Danipur,
  Mahisadal. Police fired, three killed; the bodies were burnt on one pyre and not given to the
  families. Next day the District Magistrate raided villages and arrested 200. [IHC1]
- 27 September, Iswarpur in Nandigram: police came to arrest two men at the Congress office; a crowd
  surrounded them; police set the thatched office alight and fired to get out — four dead, twenty-five
  injured. The crowd followed them five kilometres to the Narghat camp. [IHC1]
- 28 September, Brindabanpore: two died of bullet wounds, three seriously hurt. [IHC1]
- That night the link roads were cut in several places: culverts broken, trees felled across roads,
  holes dug, telegraph and telephone wires cut. [IHC1]
- 29 September, afternoon: about 20,000 people marched on Tamluk police station in five processions
  from different roads. Matangini Hazra, a widow of 73, carried the flag at the head of one and was
  shot; she died holding it. Nine other people died at Tamluk that day, including Lakshminarayan Das
  (13) and Purimadhab Parmanik (14). [IHC1]
- Mahisadal and Sutahata stations were raided the same afternoon; Nandigram on 30 September by about
  10,000, four dead at once and one more in hospital. [IHC1]
- The District Magistrate wrote: "The mob in Tamluk sub-division is apparently not afraid of (police
  or military) firing." [IHC1]
- Announced 17 September 1942 by the Tamluk Sub-Divisional Congress Committee as "Mahabharatiya
  Juktarashtra – Tamralipta Jatiya Sarkar". Other sources give 17 December 1942 as its founding.
  **Two versions.** [IHC1; WT; TRI; N18]
- It ran its own police stations, courts, revenue collection, army, intelligence service, postal
  system and schools. [WT; IHC1; IHC2; TRI]
- Led by a `Sarbadhinayak` with `Sachivas`; first Sarbadhinayak Satish Chandra Samanta. Armed wing
  `Bidyut Bahini` under Sushil Kumar Dhara, raised in case Bose's INA reached Bengal. Action squad
  `Garam Dal` against suspected corruption, spying or disloyalty. Newspaper `Biplabi`. [WT; IHC1]
- Its courts heard 1,681 cases. [SCROLL] (single)
- 16 October 1942: cyclone and tidal wave. The government delayed relief and delayed the news; the
  Sarkar organised relief, went after hoarders and shared out paddy. [IHC2; SCROLL]
- Governor of Bengal Richard Casey to the Viceroy: "A high proportion of the inhabitants are in
  sympathy with the local 'National Government' or 'Jatiya Sarkar' as it calls itself." [IHC1]
- Chief Minister A. K. Fazlul Haque told the assembly: "Midnapore had a parallel government with its
  military and police forces and intelligence branch. It had its jails where people were imprisoned
  and, in some cases, the people had actually paralysed the government." [IHC1]
- It lasted 21 months and was dissolved on Gandhi's advice: 8 August 1944 or 31 August 1944.
  **Two versions.** [TRI; WT; IHC1; IHC2; N18]
- Gandhi visited Tamluk in December 1945: the rising had been "brave and spontaneous" but had moved
  away from non-violence, and without non-violence swaraj could not be reached. [IHC2]
- A reporter at that visit: "Unfortunately, Bengal does not possess a Jawaharlal and that is perhaps
  why Calcutta does not know enough of Tamluk's glorious record". [IHC2]
- Ajoy Mukherjee, one of its leaders, later became Chief Minister of West Bengal three times; India
  issued a commemorative stamp in 2002. [WT]
- **Watch:** do not make it a fairy tale. It had an armed wing and an action squad, and it was not
  non-violent in the way Gandhi asked. Say so plainly.

## 3. Seven days — SHORT

- Sunday 9 August: the arrests; at least nine dead in Bombay. [NVD]
- Within twenty-four hours the unrest had spread to Delhi, the United Provinces and Bihar. In the
  first two days of street fighting police fired and killed twenty-four people. [NVD]
- From 10 August: 8,000 mill workers left their jobs in Ahmedabad; mills and factories shut in Bombay
  and Poona; students stopped going to school and joined the marches. [NVD]
- Delhi: police fired on 47 separate occasions on 11 and 12 August. [INC]
- United Provinces: 29 firings between 9 and 21 August — 76 killed, 114 seriously injured. [INC]
- Central Provinces: 64 killed, 102 wounded, 11,088 arrested in the first three weeks. [INC]
- For a week business was paralysed in Bombay, Ahmedabad, Delhi, Bangalore and Calcutta. [INC]
- "Even the private cars were not allowed to proceed unless there was a Gandhi cap on the head of at
  least one of the passengers." [MB]
- Mid-August: railway traffic to and from Bihar was cut, and air force planes machine-gunned unarmed
  satyagrahis lying on the ground. [NVD]
- 9 August to 21 September, per John F. Riddick: 550 post offices and 250 railway stations attacked,
  70 police stations destroyed, 85 other government buildings damaged, telegraph wires cut about
  2,500 times; worst in Bihar. [W]
- 57 battalions of British troops were deployed. [W; CJP]
- Ballia, 16 August: the Rasra treasury was attacked. 18 August: about 25,000 people went back to the
  Bairia police station because an officer had taken down the flag they had planted. Police fired for
  about six hours; at least 20 died and about 100 were hurt; Dharam Das Misra and a boy of 12 died
  trying to hoist the flag. [BAL]
- 19 August: about 50,000 walked towards the Ballia jail to free their leaders; the District
  Magistrate released them. 20 August: independence declared and a popular government formed under
  Chittu Pandey. Night of 22/23 August: the army entered and it was over. [BAL; WCP]
- **Watch:** do not say "the whole country rose". Say where each thing happened. The Delhi 47 and UP
  29 are firings, not deaths.

## 4. One thousand and twenty-eight — SHORT

- British estimates: 1,028 killed, 3,125 wounded, over 100,000 arrested. Congress estimates: 4,000 to
  10,000 killed. [W]
- Secretary of State for India, 9 August to 30 November 1942: 1,008 killed, 3,275 seriously injured.
  [INC]
- Nehru's own estimate was close to 10,000 dead. [CJP]
- Over 60,000 had been arrested by the end of 1943 alone; more than 100,000 in all. [CJP; W; NVD]
- Mass fines were levied and demonstrators were publicly flogged. [W; CJP]
- 57 battalions of British troops, with the whole police force. [CJP; W]
- The diversion delayed airfield construction by four to six weeks. [W]
- Linlithgow to Churchill, 31 August 1942: "I am engaged here in meeting by far the most serious
  rebellion since that of 1857, the gravity and extent of which we have so far concealed from the
  world for reasons of military security. Mob violence remains rampant over large tracts of the
  countryside and I am by no means confident that we may not see in September a formidable attempt to
  renew this widespread sabotage of our war effort. The lives of Europeans in outlying places are in
  jeopardy." [W; CJP]
- After Ballia was retaken, the British officer in charge is reported to have telegraphed the Governor
  of the United Provinces: "Ballia reconquered". [BAL]
- By March 1943 the movement had largely died down. [NVD]
- **Watch:** the subject is the disagreement, not the number. Never present 1,028 as the truth.

## 5. The girls with the flag — SHORT

- Kanaklata Barua, born 22 December 1924 at Borangabari, Gohpur, undivided Darrang, Assam. Her mother
  died when she was five, her father when she was thirteen; she left school after class three to
  look after her younger brothers and sisters. [WK]
- She joined the `Mrityu Bahini` (death squad), a group of young people in the Gohpur subdivision.
  [WK]
- Sunday 20 September 1942: the Bahini decided she would hoist the national flag at the local police
  station, and she led a procession of unarmed villagers towards it. [WK]
- The officer in charge, Rebati Mahan Som, warned them to stop. They went on. Police fired; she was
  shot; Mukunda Kakati picked up the flag and was shot too. Both were killed. She was 17. [WK]
- **Two versions:** another account says she was 15 and led 500 men, and that someone in the crowd
  hoisted the flag in the end. [NVD] Her birth date makes her 17; use 17 and note the other figure.
- Matangini Hazra, a widow of 73, led one of the five processions at Tamluk on 29 September 1942 and
  was shot holding the flag. Nine others died that day, including boys of 13 and 14; the crowd was
  about 20,000. [IHC1]
- She was known as `Gandhi Buri`, "the old Gandhi woman" (the nickname appears in the Commons file
  title; use it only as a nickname). [Commons]
- **Watch:** do not describe the shooting. No invented last words: popular tellings give both women
  speeches; no opened source has one.

## 6. The promise — SHORT

- The resolution: "They must remember that nonviolence is the basis of this movement." [RES]
- Gandhi, 8 August: "there is nothing but purest Ahimsa in all that I am saying and doing today";
  "Speaking for myself, I can say that I have never felt any hatred"; "Our quarrel is not with the
  British people, we fight their imperialism." [SP]
- "Despite Gandhi's call to non-violent direct action, campaigner violence was frequent." [NVD]
- Bombs exploded, government buildings were set on fire, electricity was cut, transport and
  communication lines were cut. [W] In September crowds in Madhya Pradesh, Bombay and the United
  Provinces threw bombs at police. [NVD]
- Ballia, after the six hours at Bairia: "The indiscriminate firing at the Bairia police-station and
  at other places compelled the people to take up arms, ignoring altogether the spirit of
  non-violence which had been their guiding principle till then." [BAL]
- In Ballia, between 1942 and 1944, nobody dared wear a Gandhi cap; men found wearing one were shot
  dead. [BAL] (single; do not make it the centre of the story)
- Gandhi took a vow of silence on 20 August 1942 and broke it after 91 hours, on 24 August. [GS]
- His 21-day fast (10 February to 2 March 1943) is already told in `do-or-die.md`. Do not retell it.
- **Watch:** do not blame the people who fought back, and do not pretend the violence did not happen.
