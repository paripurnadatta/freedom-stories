## What I did

I opened every page listed under "Sources opened" in the report on 26 October 2026 and saved a copy
of each. I then re-read my own report against those copies, fact by fact. Where a fact rests on a
single page, the report marks it `(single)` and it is listed again below. Where two pages disagree,
the report marks a "Two versions" note and the disagreement is listed below.

## What held up

- The resolution text, the "Do or Die" passage and the non-violence passages are quoted from
  mkgandhi.org and from the Bucknell Living Gandhi Archive. The two do not contradict each other;
  Bucknell gives the final resolution, mkgandhi.org gives the speech.
- The arrests at five in the morning, the arrest of the Working Committee, the Aga Khan Palace, the
  47 firings in Delhi, the 29 firings in the United Provinces, the Central Provinces figures and the
  Secretary of State's casualty figures all come from the INC article, which is a party account and
  should be labelled as such in the story's sources.
- The Linlithgow telegram of 31 August 1942 appears word for word in two independent pages
  (Wikipedia and CJP), which is the strongest quotation in the pack.
- The Tamluk sequence (rice at Danipur on 8 September, Iswarpur on 27 September, Brindabanpore on
  28 September, the roads cut that night, the five processions on 29 September, Matangini Hazra, the
  two boys) comes from the Indian History Collective's archival piece, which quotes the District
  Magistrate and uses the Nehru Memorial Library papers.
- The Ballia sequence comes from the district's own official history, which is unusually detailed
  and gives the Bairia firing, the 50,000-strong march on the jail, the release of the leaders, the
  declaration on 20 August and the entry of the army on the night of 22/23 August.
- Kanaklata Barua's birth date (22 December 1924), the `Mrityu Bahini`, the officer Rebati Mahan Som,
  and Mukunda Kakati picking up the flag are all in the Wikipedia article and are consistent with
  the Swarthmore account, except for her age.

## Quotations marked UNVERIFIED

- Anything said by Aruna Asaf Ali at the maidan on 9 August. There is no opened source for a speech
  by her that day. Popular retellings give her lines; do not use them.
- Any last words of Kanaklata Barua or Matangini Hazra. Popular tellings give both women speeches.
  No opened source has them.
- Gandhi's reaction to the violence in the first weeks. I could not find a dated quotation. The only
  hard fact is his silence from 20 to 24 August 1942 (GandhiServe chronology).
- The number of people at Gowalia Tank on 9 August. No opened source gives a figure.

## Where the sources disagree

1. **When the Tamralipta Jatiya Sarkar began.** 17 September 1942 (announced, Indian History
   Collective) or 17 December 1942 (founded, Wikipedia, The Tribune, News18).
2. **When it ended.** 8 August 1944 (The Tribune, Wikipedia) or 31 August 1944 (Indian History
   Collective, News18, and the second Indian History Collective piece).
3. **Kanaklata Barua's age.** 17 (Wikipedia, from her birth date of 22 December 1924) or 15
   (Swarthmore).
4. **Matangini Hazra's age.** 73 (Indian History Collective) or 72 (her own subject page, born 1870).
5. **Casualties.** British estimates 1,028 killed and 3,125 wounded (Wikipedia); Secretary of State
   1,008 killed and 3,275 seriously injured for 9 August to 30 November 1942 (INC); Congress
   estimates 4,000 to 10,000 killed (Wikipedia); Nehru "closer to 10,000" (CJP).
6. **Arrests.** "Over 100,000" (Wikipedia, Swarthmore) or "over 60,000 by the end of 1943" (CJP).
   These may be compatible; the report keeps both, with dates.
7. **The first broadcast of Congress Radio.** 27 August 1942 (Wikipedia, Mani Bhavan) or 14 August
   1942 (BBC, Swarthmore).

  pack uses the speech only as the opening of angle 1.
- **Gandhi's 1943 fast and Kasturba's death.** Already in the same story. Not repeated.

  appears only as a fact that suspended the movement in Bengal, and in the Tamluk relief work.
- **Dhekiajuli (Assam) firing, 20 September 1942.** There is a subject folder for it, but the only
  source in that folder is a Dictionary of Martyrs PDF entry for a different person. Not used.
- **Chimur and Ashti firings.** Mentioned in the CJP article as things the Congress Radio reported,
  but I did not open a page that gives the events themselves. Not written up.
- **The INA and Subhas Chandra Bose.** They are a separate subject and only appear as context in the
  Tamluk story (the Bidyut Bahini was raised in case the INA reached Bengal).
- **Numbers of people at any single gathering**, except where an opened page gives one.

Corrections to the fact sheet and report:

- **Casey quotation dropped.** The fact sheet says Governor Richard Casey wrote "A high proportion of
  the inhabitants are in sympathy with the local 'National Government'..." to the Viceroy. The IHC
  article says Casey wrote it to *Wavell*, which means 1944 or later; Casey was not Governor in 1942.
  The Governor in 1942 was Herbert, who called the rising a "large scale rebellion" (IHC). The
  story uses Herbert's phrase only.
- **Ballia "19 August, 50,000 walk to the jail" dropped.** It is not in the district history or in
  Wikipedia's Chittu Pandey page. The story uses what the district history gives (16, 18, 20, 22/23
  August). Ballia independence: 20 August (district history) or 19 August (Chittu Pandey page): a
  "Two versions" note.
- **"Eight thousand mill workers from 10 August"**: Swarthmore gives the number with no date, so the
  story gives no date.
- **Tamluk**: the Danipur dead are "three"; Mahisadal on 29 September had thirteen dead (IHC,
  not in the fact sheet); the District Magistrate's quotation "not afraid of firing" is by the
  Additional District Magistrate M. M. Basu and has "(police or military)" inside it; the story keeps
  the words as written. Founding date (17 September vs 17 December 1942) and dissolution (31 August
  vs 8 August 1944): two "Two versions" notes. "1,681 cases in its courts" (Scroll) left out: one
  source, not opened again.
- **Kanaklata**: 17 (Wikipedia, birth date 22 December 1924) vs a 15-year-old girl who led 500 men
  (Swarthmore, which does not name her): note in the story. Wikipedia spells Mukunda "Kakoti", the
  Commons statue "Kakati"; the story uses Kakati and says so.
- **Matangini Hazra's age**: 73 (IHC) vs Commons label 1870-1942 (72): note.
- **Gowalia Tank**: Mani Bhavan and Swarthmore say lathi charge and tear gas; Wikipedia's Aruna Asaf Ali
  page adds that police fired. Swarthmore: at least nine dead in Bombay that day. Not placed at the
  maidan; the story says so in a note.
- **Aruna Asaf Ali**: Wikipedia gives the 5,000 rupee reward, the Inquilab editorship with Lohia,
  the 1946 return and Gandhi's note about the reward and the Harijan cause. No speech by her exists in
  any opened source; the story does not give her one.
- **"Quit India" slogan**: Mani Bhavan only (Meherally's bow). Marked in the story as one source.
- **Aircraft machine-gunning satyagrahis on the Bihar tracks**: Swarthmore only. Marked as one source.
- **Riddick figures** (550 post offices, 250 railway stations, 70 police stations, 85 other buildings,
  2,500 wire cuts): found in Wikipedia's article, not opened in Riddick's book.
- **Not used**: the Muslim League angle; the Tribune and News18 articles (not opened: blocked);
  the Scroll article (opened, not needed).
