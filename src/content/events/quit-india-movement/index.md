## Research notes

The old note was one line and the `related:` list had about 170 names, most of them added
automatically. Both have been replaced. Everything below comes from pages opened on 26 October 2026.

- **What happened.** The All India Congress Committee met in Bombay on 7 and 8 August 1942 and passed
  a resolution demanding the immediate end of British rule. Gandhi spoke on the night of 8 August and
  gave the slogan "Do or Die". At five in the morning on 9 August the government arrested Gandhi,
  Nehru, Patel, Azad and almost the whole Working Committee, and declared the Congress illegal.
  Aruna Asaf Ali hoisted the flag at Gowalia Tank Maidan; the police tear-gassed and lathi-charged
  the crowd. With no leaders, the movement spread on its own: strikes, school closures, attacks on
  post offices and railway stations, telegraph wires cut, and parallel governments in Ballia,
  Tamluk and Satara.
- **What it cost.** British figures: 1,028 killed, 3,125 wounded, over 100,000 arrested. The
  Secretary of State gave 1,008 killed and 3,275 seriously injured for 9 August to 30 November 1942.
  Congress estimates ran from 4,000 to 10,000 dead. 57 battalions of British troops were used, and
  airfield construction was delayed by four to six weeks.
- **What is disputed.** The casualty figures (above). The founding date of the Tamralipta Jatiya
  Sarkar, 17 September or 17 December 1942, and its end, 8 or 31 August 1944. Kanaklata Barua's age,
  15 or 17. Matangini Hazra's age, 72 or 73 (her own page says born 1870). The first broadcast of
  Congress Radio, 14 or 27 August 1942.
- **What it changed.** It did not end British rule at once. By March 1943 the campaign had largely
  died down, and in Bengal the famine of 1943 stopped it. But the Raj had shown it could not govern
  as before, and in 1945 a British government promising independence was elected.
