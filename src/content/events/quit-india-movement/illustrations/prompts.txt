Bombay, the morning of Sunday 9 August 1942, Gowalia Tank Maidan. Seen from the edge of a large, quiet crowd: a low raised platform ahead with a few empty chairs and a small microphone on a stand, and a wooden flagstaff beside it. Hundreds of people stand on the open ground in white shirts, dhotis, saris and cotton caps, most of them turned towards the platform; a woman in a plain sari stands at the foot of the flagstaff, seen from behind and to one side, raising the Congress flag of 1942: a tricolour of saffron, white and green with a spinning wheel in the centre. A bicycle lies on the ground; a boy sits on a wall. The morning light is warm, the mood is tense and expectant, and there are no police and no violence in the picture.

Tamluk, in Midnapore district, Bengal, late 1942. A village schoolroom used as an office: a long wooden table with bundles of paper tied with string, a cloth-bound register, a brass inkwell, a hurricane lantern and a small mound of paddy in an open bowl. Three men stand around it, one reading aloud from a list, one in a dhoti writing with a pen, a middle-aged woman in a white sari waiting with a folded cloth in her hands. On the wall behind them a large hand-drawn map of a river district, its markings unreadable. The shutters are open onto palm trees, a pond and wet green fields. Plain, workaday, orderly; this is a government being run from a schoolroom.
