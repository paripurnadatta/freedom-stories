On 25 July 1857, soldiers of three regiments rose at Danapur, near Patna. They were from the 7th, 8th and 40th Bengal Native Infantry. They crossed the river Sone and went towards Jagdishpur.

They had come to see one man. They wanted Kunwar Singh to lead them.

## The old landlord

Kunwar Singh was born on 13 November 1777 in Jagdishpur in Bihar. His father was Sahabzada Singh. He belonged to a branch of the Ujjainiya family, who were Rajputs. He became the ruler of Jagdishpur after his father died in 1826.

In 1857 he was nearly eighty. A British officer who met him described him as a very tall man, about seven feet high. That is one officer's word, and the page gives no second report.

<aside class="note"><b>Landlord</b>: a zamindar, a man who held land and collected rent from the farmers on it.</aside>

He agreed to take command. His younger brother Babu Amar Singh helped him. So did his chief commander, Hare Krishna Singh.

## Arrah

Two days later, on 27 July, his men took Arrah, the headquarters of the district of Shahabad. A small group of British officials and about fifty loyal Sikh soldiers were shut up in a house there. They were fifteen British men in all.

A relief party of about 400 soldiers under Captain Dunbar was sent from Danapur. It was destroyed near Arrah on 29 July.

Then Major Vincent Eyre came with a stronger force. On 3 August he defeated Kunwar Singh at Bibiganj and relieved the house. Eyre then destroyed the palace and fort of Jagdishpur.

<aside class="note"><b>Two voices</b>: Some argue that Hare Krishna Singh, not Kunwar Singh, was the main reason the early fighting went well. The page that says so gives no more detail.</aside>

## A long march

Kunwar Singh did not give up. He left Bihar and fought on in other places. In December 1857 he reached Lucknow to meet the other leaders. One page says he joined Nana Sahib and Tantia Tope at Kanpur and fought at Rewa.

In March 1858 he took Azamgarh. He held it against early attempts to take it back, but left when Brigadier Douglas came after him.

But he kept moving. One page says he was an expert in guerrilla fighting and that his tactics puzzled the British.

## The river

The army had to cross the Ganges near Shivpur Ghat. Douglas's troops fired at them while they crossed. Kunwar Singh was hit. One page says a bullet broke his left wrist. Another says a cannonball.

Then comes a well-known story. He is said to have cut off his own arm, below the elbow, to stop the wound from turning bad. Some tellings add that he offered the arm to the river.

<aside class="note"><b>A tale, not proved</b>: The story of the arm rests mostly on a 1999 book and a local history website. It is told as fact on some pages. Some pages tell it as plain fact, but no other record is given.</aside>

## Home again

On 23 April 1858 Kunwar Singh's men met a force under Captain Le Grand near Jagdishpur and beat it. In Bihar this day is remembered as Vijay Diwas, the Day of Victory.

Three days later, on 26 April 1858, Kunwar Singh died in his village. His brother Amar Singh took over. Amar Singh set up a moving army in the Kaimur Hills and ran a government of his own. He fought on until the end of 1859, when he was pushed into the Terai in Nepal.

## Why it matters

Kunwar Singh was nearly eighty when he took up the fight. He is remembered in Bhojpuri folk songs. India issued a stamp in his honour, in 2016 according to one page, and in 1966 according to another.
