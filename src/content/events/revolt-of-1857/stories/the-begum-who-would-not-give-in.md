In 1856 the British took over the kingdom of Awadh. They said its ruler, Nawab Wajid Ali Shah, governed badly. They sent him away to Calcutta.

His wife did not go with him. She stayed in Lucknow.

## The wife of the Nawab

She is known as Begum Hazrat Mahal. She was born around 1820 in Faizabad, which had once been the capital of Awadh. Her name as a girl is spelt Muhammadi Khanum or Mohammadi Khanum. She became one of the Nawab's wives. After the birth of their son, Birjis Qadr, she was given the title Hazrat Mahal.

<aside class="note"><b>Not certain</b>: Her birth year is given as about 1820, but the page marks that date as needing a better source.</aside>

## Lucknow, 1857

In 1857 the rebels took Lucknow. The Begum crowned her son Birjis Qadr as the ruler. One page says he was twelve. She ruled as regent, which means she governed for him while he was too young.

Her government held the city against the British for about six months. She also worked to bring Indian soldiers of the Company's army over to her side. Later she worked with Nana Sahib, and joined the Maulvi of Faizabad in an attack on Shahjahanpur.

She wrote a proclamation that made fun of the British promise of freedom of religion. She was angry that the British pulled down temples and mosques to build roads.

<aside class="note"><b>Her words</b>: The proclamation is quoted in a book by William Dalrymple. The Wikipedia page does not say how the text was checked.</aside>

## The fall of the city

The British came back in force. Sir Colin Campbell took Lucknow again on 21 March 1858. A Nepalese force under Jung Bahadur, the prime minister of Nepal, fought with him.

The Begum left. One page says she refused British offers of safety and money. She went north through thick forest, towards Nepal.

## Asylum in Nepal

Jung Bahadur, who had fought against her, at first said no to her request for shelter. Later she was allowed to stay. One page says he took her jewels and treasures in return. She arrived about 1858 with a small group of followers.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: The pages do not explain why Jung Bahadur changed his mind. One says only that little reliable information survives about her years in Kathmandu.</aside>

## The end

She died in Kathmandu in 1879. She was buried in an unmarked grave.

<aside class="note"><b>Where is the grave?</b> One page says she lies in the grounds of the Jama Masjid in Kathmandu, and that a committee looks after the tomb. Another describes a small brick-walled plot in the Bag Bazaar area, beside a lone tree, with nothing to say who is buried there.</aside>

In 1887, for Queen Victoria's jubilee, the British pardoned Birjis Qadr. He was allowed to go home.

## Remembered

In 1962 the Old Victoria Park in Lucknow was renamed for her. A marble memorial was built there. India issued a stamp in her honour in 1984. A scholarship for students is named after her.

## Why it matters

The Begum lost, but she did not give in. She refused British offers, and she died in exile.  She governed a city at war for months, with her young son as its ruler.
