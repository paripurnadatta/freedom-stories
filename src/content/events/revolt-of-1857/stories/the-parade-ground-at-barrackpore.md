Mangal Pandey came from Nagwa, a village in what is now the Ballia district of Uttar Pradesh. His family was Brahmin. He joined the Bengal Army in 1849. By March 1857 he was a sepoy in the 5th Company of the 34th Bengal Native Infantry.

His regiment was stationed at Barrackpore.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: One page says he came from Nagwa in Ballia district and gives no birth date. Another says he was born in July 1827 at Nagwa near Faizabad. The pages place the village differently.</aside>

## 29 March

At about five in the afternoon, Pandey walked up and down in front of the guard room. He had a loaded musket. He called on the other sepoys to rise. He shouted about biting cartridges, and he said he would shoot the first European he saw.

Then he heard that British soldiers were coming from a steamer on the river. He ran to the quarter-guard.

Lieutenant Baugh, a British officer, rode out to him. Pandey fired. He missed Baugh and hit his horse. Baugh fired back and missed. Pandey then went at him with a sword, called a talwar, and wounded him in the shoulder and neck.

A sepoy named Shaikh Paltu tried to hold Pandey back. Other sepoys threw stones and shoes at Pandey. The guard said they would shoot Paltu if he did not let go. Sergeant-Major Hewson was knocked down with a blow from a musket.

General Hearsey arrived. He said he would shoot any man who did not obey. He ordered the guard to seize Pandey. Pandey turned the musket on himself and shot himself in the chest. He was hurt, but he lived.

<aside class="note"><b>Was it his own hand?</b> The British accounts say Pandey shot himself. One page says some people later asked whether the wound had really been self-inflicted.</aside>

## The trial

Pandey got better. Less than a week later he was tried. He was found guilty and sentenced to be hanged.

At the trial he said he had taken bhang and opium and did not know what he was doing. One page says that the evidence recorded at the time also described him as drugged with bhang. His motives, the page says, remain confused.

The jemadar of the guard, Ishwari Prasad, was tried too. Three Sikh members of the guard said he had told them not to arrest Pandey. He was also sentenced to hang.

Pandey was hanged on 8 April 1857. All the Indian and British units at Barrackpore were made to watch. He did not make any statement that gave away other men. Ishwari Prasad was hanged on 21 April.

<aside class="note"><b>Jemadar</b>: an Indian officer of a company. Ishwari Prasad commanded the quarter-guard.</aside>

## After him

Shaikh Paltu was promoted to havildar and given a medal. He was murdered shortly before his regiment was dismissed.

On 6 May seven of the ten companies of the 34th were disbanded in disgrace. It was a punishment for the whole regiment, though an inquiry had found no link between the regiment and the trouble at Berhampore.

During the revolt the British began to call any rebel sepoy a "pandy". The word came from his name.

## What did he mean?

Many Indians see Mangal Pandey as the first fighter of 1857. V. D. Savarkar wrote of him in that way. One recent study says there is little evidence for the largest of these claims. His own regiment did not rise with him that day.

There is a twist about the cartridges. The new rifles had not yet been issued. The cartridges in the magazine of the 19th Regiment were, by one account, free of grease.

## Why it matters

Pandey's act was a spark that did not catch at once. Six weeks later the cavalry at Meerut broke open their jail, and the revolt began in earnest.

India honoured him with a postage stamp on 5 October 1984. At Barrackpore a park now marks the place where he attacked and where he was hanged. The parade ground there has been renamed for him.
