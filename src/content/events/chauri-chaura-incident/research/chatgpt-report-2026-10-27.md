# Research report: Chauri Chaura incident — 27 October 2026

Subject: `src/content/events/chauri-chaura-incident/` (event, Gorakhpur district, United Provinces).

Everything below was read on 27 October 2026. Each fact has the source key and page or section
where it appears; the key list with full URLs is at the end. Where two sources disagree, both
versions are given and I have not chosen between them. Nothing here comes from memory.

**One correction to the existing `index.md`:** it gives the date as `1922-02-05`. The incident
was on **Saturday 4 February 1922**. 5 February is a date some Indian textbooks and exam guides
give, and it is wrong. I have corrected the date in `index.md`.

---

## The short version, for orientation

- 1 or 2 February 1922: volunteers picketing Mundera Bazaar are beaten by police; Bhagwan Ahir,
  a retired soldier, is badly hurt.
- Morning of 4 February: volunteers gather at Dumri Khurd and march to the Chaura police station
  to ask the sub-inspector for an explanation.
- Early afternoon: an argument at the station, a taunt, warning shots, stone-throwing, lathis,
  then police firing that kills three people.
- The crowd sets the thana (police station) on fire. Twenty-two policemen and watchmen die in
  the fire; one more dies of injuries the next day.
- 11–12 February: the Congress Working Committee at Bardoli suspends mass civil disobedience.
  Gandhi begins a five-day fast on the evening of Sunday 12 February.
- 12 February: Gandhi writes "Gorakhpur's Crime" in *Navajivan*. 16 February: "The Crime of
  Chauri Chaura" in *Young India*.
- 24–25 February: the All-India Congress Committee at Delhi adopts the Bardoli resolutions.
- 1922–23: trial at Gorakhpur Sessions Court; January 1923 the Sessions Judge sentences a large
  number to death; Malaviya appeals; 30 April 1923 the Allahabad High Court leaves 19 death
  sentences; the 19 are hanged between 2 and 11 July 1923.

## Angles

### 1. The Beating at Mundera Bazaar — SHORT

The cause, three days before the fire. Use this to show that the crowd had a grievance that a
court later accepted as real, without excusing what followed.

- Date: **1 February 1922** [BRIT; WEEK21A]. Wikipedia says **2 February** [WIKI]. Kushwaha's
  reviewer does not date it [FRONTIER14]. **Two versions: 1 or 2 February.**
- Place: **Mundera Bazaar**, the bigger market near Chaura [BRIT; WEEK21A]. Wikipedia calls it
  "Gauri Bazaar" [WIKI]; some exam guides call it "Gauri Bazaar" too. The Sessions Court and the
  High Court records say Mundera. Use Mundera, and note that older retellings say Gauri Bazaar.
- What they were doing: picketing meat, fish and liquor shops, partly to push prices down and
  partly because many believed Gandhi's vegetarianism and abstinence meant such goods should not
  be sold [BRIT]. The Week says "a prosperous market of pulses, raw sugar and rice" [WEEK21A].
- The three men: **Bhagwan Ahir, Ramrup Barai and Mahadeo** [WEEK21A]. Britannica mentions only
  "a war pensioner" being severely injured [BRIT]. Wikipedia names Bhagwan Ahir as the leader, "a
  retired soldier of the British Indian Army" [WIKI].
- Bhagwan Ahir: served two years in the First World War, posted at Basra, and drew a government
  pension. The sub-inspector's anger was greatest against him for that reason [WEEK21A].
- The order came from **Gupteshwar Singh**, sub-inspector of the Chaura station [WEEK21A; WIKI].
  Some accounts call him "Daroga" (inspector); the Sessions Court records call him sub-inspector.
- Exact quote, Kallan Ahmed, grandson of Nazar Ali [WEEK21A]:
  "Ahir was a strong man. But so merciless was the beating that he struggled to get back home."
- Also: some accounts say the men were first stopped by the musclemen of the landlord Sant Baksh
  Singh, who owned the bazaar, and handed over to the police [TOI 2022, seen only as a search
  snippet — blocked, do not build on it].
- Aftermath: several leaders were arrested and put in the lock-up at the police station [WIKI];
  this is single-source and not needed.

**Watch:** do not write that the beating "caused" the fire as a simple chain. The Week's account
has three days of meetings in between, and a march that began peacefully.

### 2. The Morning at Dumri Khurd — FLAGSHIP

The best scene in the subject, and the one that lets a child understand who these people were.

- The centre of the movement was **Dumri Khurd** (also Chhotki Dumri), a village of mostly Dalit
  Chamar families and Muslims, not the market town itself [FRONTIER14; WEEK21A].
- A mandal committee of the Non-Cooperation Movement was formed at Dumri Khurd on **13 January
  1922** [WEEK21A].
- After the beating, meetings were held over three days and it was decided that volunteers would
  march to the police station to **ask Gupteshwar Singh for an explanation** [WEEK21A].
- Letters went out to villages within about **10 km**, asking volunteers to gather at Dumri Khurd
  on the morning of 4 February [WEEK21A].
- On the morning itself, volunteers went door to door asking for **jaggery and roasted rice** for
  the marchers [WEEK21A].
- Meeting place: a **threshing ground** in the village, according to the main accounts; elderly
  residents told The Week it was an **orchard of mango, jackfruit and shisham trees in the local
  cemetery**. Sacks were laid out for volunteers to sit on, in groups of six [WEEK21A].
  **Two versions — record both, choose neither.**
- Nazar Ali spoke and said that anyone who left midway would be guilty of defiling their religion.
  Not a single volunteer left [WEEK21A].
- The march started between **1.30 and 2.00 p.m.** and was orderly; the crowd swelled as people
  joined on the way [WEEK21A].
- Size of the crowd: the **High Court judgment says 1,000 to 1,500** [WEEK21A, citing the
  judgment]; Wikipedia says **2,000 to 2,500** [WIKI]; Kushwaha says 3,000 to 5,000 by noon
  [FRONTIER14]. **Three versions. Use "more than a thousand" and note the disagreement.**
- What the volunteers said they were doing, from trial testimony quoted by Amin [AMIN]:
  peasants going to a meeting at Dumri on the morning of 4 February said they were "going to hold
  a Gandhi Mahatma Sabha" which would bring about "Gandhi Swaraj".
- Sarju Kahar, personal servant of the thanedar, testified that "two or four days before the
  affair [he] had heard that Gandhi Mahatma's Swaraj had been established, that the Chaura thana
  would be abolished, and that the volunteers would set up their own thana" [AMIN, quoting
  *Tajwiz Awwal, Chauri Chaura Trials*, p. 358].
- The Allahabad High Court found it "remarkable... how this name of 'Swaraj' was linked, in the
  minds of the peasantry of Gorakhpur, with the name of Mr Gandhi", and that peasants saw Swaraj
  as a "millenium in which taxation would be limited to the collection of small cash
  contributions or dues in kind" [AMIN, quoting Appeal No. 51 of 1923, *King Emperor vs. Abdullah
  and others*, 30 April 1923, p. 9].
- Names and ages of the leaders [FRONTIER14]: **Nazar Ali**, 30, of Dumri Khurd; **Lal Mohammad**,
  40, of Chaura village; **Bhagwan Ahir**, 24, of Dumri Khurd; **Abdullah Sai**, 40, of Rajdhani
  village. Nazar Ali was an ex-soldier and Lal Mohammad sold coarse cloth; both were wrestlers,
  and news travelled through the *akharas* (wrestling gymnasiums) [WEEK21A].
- 90 per cent of the accused were Dalit, backward caste or Muslim, according to Kushwaha
  [FRONTIER14]. This is one author's count; label it.

**Watch:** the "Gandhi Mahatma Sabha" and "Gandhi's Swaraj" material is what peasants said in
court, not what Gandhi taught. Say clearly that the Congress did not teach that the police station
would be abolished.

### 3. The Thana on Fire — SHORT (the day itself)

Handle gently: no gore, no cheering, no blame of a whole village.

- At the station the volunteers spoke to Gupteshwar Singh. By some accounts it was courteous, and
  the sub-inspector said Ahir was like a brother to him. The crowd then moved on towards Mundera
  Bazaar [WEEK21A].
- Then someone taunted Singh. Sharda Nand Yadav, great-grandson of Bikram Ahir (one of the 19
  hanged), remembers the words as: "What a shameful act this is. You being a Thakur bowing to
  these low-caste men" [WEEK21A]. This is a family memory of a reported remark; label it as
  remembered.
- Singh ordered the chowkidars to strike their lathis on the ground and the armed police to fire
  in the air [WEEK21A]. Wikipedia says he ordered 15 local officers to fire warning shots [WIKI].
- The crowd came back and threw **ballast picked up from the railway track** [WEEK21A]. Britannica
  says stones and brickbats [BRIT].
- A lathi charge followed, then the police fired into the crowd, killing at least three and
  wounding many [WEEK21A; BRIT; GORAKHPUR].
- Outnumbered, the police went inside the thana. Volunteers took **kerosene from the market** and
  set it on fire. Others **cut the telegraph wires** of the local post office so the news would
  not spread [WEEK21A].
- Deaths: **22 policemen and watchmen died in the fire; one badly injured chowkidar died a day
  later** [WEEK21A] — so 23. Britannica says 23 police officers [BRIT]; the Gorakhpur district
  page says three civilians and 23 policemen [GORAKHPUR]; Gandhi's first article says 21 [CWMG22,
  p. 386]. **Versions: 21, 22, 23, and 24 (see angle 9).**
- The Congress inquiry report said: "Twenty-three men were beaten to death and all except a few of
  them burned. The thana was set on fire and the men who attempted to escape were driven back
  into it" [WEEK21A, quoting the Congress report].
- The Sessions Court said: "The burning of the victims took place while some of them were still
  alive.… They poured kerosene oil on the corpses and set fire to them" [WEEK21A, quoting the
  judgment]. **Do not put this sentence in a children's story.** Use it only as background.
- Even in that fury the volunteers let women and children go, including the pregnant wife of
  Gupteshwar Singh [WEEK21A]. Single source, but it is a reported detail of the inquiry, and it
  matters: use it, attributed.
- Britannica's "Did You Know" says police officers escaped by taking off their red turbans and
  blending into the crowd [BRIT]. Colourful, single source, unverified — optional.

**Watch:** the crowd was not a mob of strangers. It was villagers from named villages, many of
them the poorest, and the High Court itself said the movement's promise had been twisted in their
minds. Keep both truths in view.

### 4. The Crime of Gorakhpur — FLAGSHIP

Gandhi's reaction, in his own words. This is the strongest documentary material in the subject
because the Collected Works prints the articles in full.

- **First news was wrong.** Writing in *Navajivan* on 12 February 1922, Gandhi says the people
  "set fire to a police station, killed 21 innocent policemen and burned their bodies; a young son
  of a sub-inspector was also among the killed. According to newspaper reports, people went to
  disperse a Friday market which was being held there" [CWMG22, pp. 386–7]. 4 February 1922 was a
  **Saturday**, and the market was Mundera Bazaar. He was writing on a train to Bombay, from
  newspaper reports, before he had the details.
- In the same article: "I am certainly the one most responsible for the crime of the people of
  Gorakhpur district, but every genuine non-co-operator is also responsible for it. All of us
  should be in mourning for it" [CWMG22, p. 387].
- The Bardoli resolution, 11–12 February 1922: "The Working Committee deplores the inhuman conduct
  of the mob at Chauri Chaura in having brutally murdered constables and wantonly burnt the Police
  Thana and tenders its sympathy to the families of the bereaved." It suspended mass civil
  disobedience at Bardoli and elsewhere and told local committees to advise cultivators to pay
  land revenue [CWMG22, pp. 377–9].
- The resolution gives the reason: "In view of Nature's repeated warnings, every time mass civil
  disobedience has been imminent some popular violent outburst has taken place indicating that the
  atmosphere in the country is not non-violent enough for mass disobedience" [CWMG22, p. 378].
- *Young India*, 16 February 1922, "The Crime of Chauri Chaura": "God has been abundantly kind to
  me. He has warned me the third time..." He counts 1919 (Rowlatt Act, "a Himalayan
  miscalculation"), the Bombay mob of 17 November 1921, and now Chauri Chaura [CWMG22, p. 415].
- He accepts that the constables had provoked: "I understand that the constables who were so
  brutally hacked to death had given much provocation. They had even gone back upon the word just
  given by the Inspector that they would not be molested..." — and then: "No provocation can
  possibly justify the brutal murder of men who had been rendered defenceless and who had
  virtually thrown themselves on the mercy of the mob" [CWMG22, pp. 415–16].
- He answers the charge of cowardice: "It is better to be charged with cowardice and weakness than
  to be guilty of denial of our oath and sin against God" [CWMG22, p. 419].
- The fast: "After deep consideration, therefore, I am imposing on myself a five days' continuous
  fast, permitting myself water. It commenced on Sunday evening; it ends on Friday evening. This is
  the least I must do" [CWMG22, p. 419]. Sunday was 12 February; the fast ended on Friday 17
  February [CWMG22, p. 438 note; GANDHISERVE].
- He asked others not to copy him: "I hope, therefore, that no one will join me in fasting, either
  through false sympathy or an ignorant conception of the spiritual value of fasting" [CWMG22,
  p. 420].
- On 19 February he wrote to Jawaharlal Nehru in Lucknow jail: "the Chauri Chaura news came like a
  powerful match to ignite the gunpowder, and there was a blaze"; and "36,000 volunteers were
  enlisted in Gorakhpur, not 100 of whom conformed to the Congress pledge" [CWMG22, pp. 435–6].
- The same letter lists earlier warnings he had ignored: the "Madras doings", the Ferozepur Jirka
  shooting of 23 December 1921, complaints from Bareilly, Jajjar, Shahjahanpur, Kanouj, and
  Calcutta [CWMG22, p. 436].
- The All-India Congress Committee met at Delhi on 24–25 February 1922 and adopted the Bardoli
  resolutions with modifications [CWMG22, chronology, p. 521].

**Watch:** do not make the fast a punishment of the villagers. Gandhi's own words are that it was
penance for himself and a public warning, not a sentence on them. Also do not say the movement was
"suspended because of Chauri Chaura alone" — Nehru explicitly denies that (angle 6).

### 5. Almost Everyone Disagreed — SHORT

The other side of the decision, from a leader who was in prison when it happened.

- Nehru, in jail: "Suddenly, early in February, the whole scene shifted, and we in prison learnt,
  to our amazement and consternation, that Gandhiji had stopped the aggressive aspects of our
  struggle..." [NEHRU, p. 81]. (The scanned edition prints "1932" in this sentence; the year is
  1922. Do not quote the year from that sentence.)
- "We were angry when we learnt of this stoppage of our struggle at a time when we seemed to be
  consolidating our position and advancing on all fronts" [NEHRU, p. 81].
- "The sudden suspension of our movement after the Chauri Chaura incident was resented, I think, by
  almost all the prominent Congress leaders—other than Gandhiji of course. My father (who was in
  gaol at the time) was much upset by it. The younger people were naturally even more agitated"
  [NEHRU, p. 82].
- The Bardoli workers' meeting, 10 February, as reported at the time and printed in the Collected
  Works: "almost everyone, young and old, declared ... that if Mahatmaji retreated after throwing
  out a challenge to Lord Reading in the manner he had done by his rejoinder to the Government
  communique, the whole country would be disgraced before the world. Only three persons dissented
  from this view" [CWMG22, p. 377 note].
- Nehru's later judgement: "It may be, therefore, that the decision to suspend civil resistance in
  1922 was a right one, though the manner of doing it left much to be desired and brought about a
  certain demoralisation" [NEHRU, p. 84].
- Britannica adds that Motilal Nehru, Subhas Chandra Bose, C. R. Das and Lala Lajpat Rai criticised
  the decision, and that Bose called it a "national calamity" in *The Indian Struggle, 1920–1934*
  [BRIT]. I did not open Bose's book, so the Bose phrase is **reported by Britannica, not checked
  in the original**.
- Gandhi's own answer to the charge that he had gone soft: "The drastic reversal of practically the
  whole of the aggressive programme may be politically unsound and unwise, but there is no doubt
  that it is religiously sound" [CWMG22, p. 417].

**Watch:** fair on politics. Neither side was foolish. Nehru's final view is generous to Gandhi;
use it, or the story becomes a quarrel.

### 6. One Hundred and Seventy — SHORT

The trial, and the lawyer who came out of retirement.

- Charges: rioting and arson, tried at the Gorakhpur Sessions Court. The case name in the High
  Court was **King Emperor vs. Abdullah and others**, Appeal No. 51 of 1923 [AMIN, note 116;
  WEEK21A].
- Numbers tried: **225** [BRIT; WIKI; MALAVIYA]; **228** [WEEK21B; FRONTIER14 says the trial
  covered 228 out of 273 originally challenged]. **Two versions.**
- Sessions Judge: **H. E. Holmes** [WIKI]. Single source; the Malaviya account does not name him.
- Length: the trial lasted about **eight months**, ending on **9 January 1923** [WIKI].
- Death sentences from the Sessions Court: **170** [WIKI; MALAVIYA] or **172** [BRIT;
  FRONTIER14]. **Two versions.**
- Acquittals at that stage: **47** [WIKI; FRONTIER14].
- Malaviya's appeal: he had left the High Court about 1913 and appeared "thereafter only once in
  1923 to plead for the life of the large number sentenced to death in the Chauri Chaura case"
  [MALAVIYA]. He argued for all the accused for four or five days; a dozen other lawyers appeared
  for individual accused. Crown counsel was a barrister from Aligarh named Qadri. The bench was
  Chief Justice Sir Grimwood Mears and Justice Sir Theodore Piggot [MALAVIYA].
- Result at the High Court, **30 April 1923**: **19 death sentences upheld**, 38 acquitted, the
  rest given terms; 14 got life imprisonment [WIKI; FRONTIER14; BRIT]. Malaviya's biographer puts
  it as "151 of the 170 condemned men were saved from the gallows and strictures were passed on
  the police" [MALAVIYA].
- The 19 hanged: Nazar Ali, Bhagwan Ahir, Lal Mohammad, Shyamsundar, Abdullah, Vikram Ahir, Dudhi
  Singh, Kali Charan, Lauti Kumar, Mahadev Singh, Meghu Tiwari, Raghuvir, Ramlakhan, Ramroop,
  Sahdev, Rudali, Mohan, Sampat, Sitaram [WIKI]. Hanged between **2 and 11 July 1923** in different
  jails of the United Provinces [WIKI; FRONTIER14; BRIT].
- The Chief Justice's compliment to Malaviya is quoted by his biographer ("Mr. Malaviya, we are
  extremely grateful to you for putting this case so fairly and well before us...") [MALAVIYA].
  **UNVERIFIED as a court record** — a memorial biography, not a transcript. Do not put it in
  quotation marks in a story; paraphrase and say "his biographer records".

**Watch:** this is a story about a lawyer saving lives, not about a court being kind. 19 men were
still hanged. Say both.

### 7. Twenty-Three Names — SHORT (reserve)

The dead policemen were Indians, mostly constables and village watchmen. This is the angle that
stops the subject becoming a simple "us and them".

- The Gorakhpur police memorial has **23 niches, one for each slain policeman**, and "Jai Hind" was
  added on top after independence [HINDU24].
- Names as listed by Wikipedia [WIKI]: Inspector (Daroga) Gupteshwar Singh; Sub-Inspector Prithvi
  Pal; Constables Bashir Khan, Kapil Dev Singh, Visheshwar Ram Yadav, Mohammad Ali, Hasan Khan,
  Gadabaksh Khan, Jama Khan, Manglu Chaubey, Rambali Pandey, Kapil Dev, Indrasan Singh, Ramlakhan
  Singh, Mardana Khan, Jagdev Singh, Jaigai Singh; Chaukidars Lakhai Singh, Wazir, Ghisai Ram,
  Jathai Ram, Katwaru Ram. That is 22 names; the 23rd is the chowkidar who died later.
- Hindu and Muslim names side by side in one list. That is the point of the story.
- **Raghuvir Singh**, a constable, was declared dead in the first report but was found alive months
  later and produced in court as the sole witness [WIKI]. Single source; verify before use.
- Malaviya's account says the case was for causing "the death of one Sub-Inspector and 21
  constables trapped in it" [MALAVIYA] — i.e. 22 including the inspector.
- The families: the government gave the killed policemen's families jobs and pensions, while the
  convicted villagers' families got nothing for decades [FRONTIER14].

**Watch:** do not turn this into a "both sides" story that hides who set the fire. It is a story
about who died, and about a memorial that is still standing.

### 8. Two Memorials — SHORT

Remembrance, and the argument about it.

- **Police memorial**: built by the British on the grounds of the police station in **1923 or
  1924**; 23 niches; "Jai Hind" added after 1947 [BRIT; HINDU24]. It still stands, and is not well
  kept [HINDU24].
- **1973**: a local committee, the Chauri Chaura Shaheed Smarak Samiti (formed 1971), built a
  **12.2 m triangular minaret** near the lake, with a figure hanging from a noose on each side,
  paid for by public subscription of Rs 13,500 [WIKI, citing Times of India 2007 — blocked, so
  treat the measurements as Wikipedia's].
- **1982**: Indira Gandhi laid the foundation stone of the Shaheed Smarak [BRIT; HINDU24;
  PRINT24]. The story the family tells is that Ram Narayan Tripathi, grandson of Meghu Tiwari,
  asked her for it during a 1978 by-election visit to Gorakhpur, and money was sanctioned after
  the Congress won [PRINT24].
- **1993**: the Shaheed Smarak was inaugurated, under P. V. Narasimha Rao [BRIT; FRONTIER14;
  HINDU24]. In the same year the families of those hanged began to receive freedom fighters'
  family pensions — **110 families** [FRONTIER14; PRINT24]. The pension started at about Rs 800
  and is now Rs 20,000 [PRINT24].
- **2021**: on 4 February the Prime Minister inaugurated the centenary celebrations at Chauri
  Chaura and released a postage stamp; the programme ran in all 75 districts of UP until 4
  February 2022 [WEEK21B; PIB via ET, see note]. Modi said: "It is unfortunate that the martyrs of
  Chauri Chaura have not been spoken about much. The incident was not just about a police station
  being burnt down; the message it sent was huge. It should not be seen as a physical fire; it was
  a fire in the hearts of the people" [WEEK21B].
- **2022**: the UP government agreed to rename Mundera Bazar Nagar Panchayat as Chauri Chaura Nagar
  Panchayat [BRIT].
- The memorial's own wall says "Shaheed Smarak—Chauri Chaura **Kand**—February 1922", using the
  word *kand* (an abominable incident) and without the date [WEEK21A].
- The library at the memorial had, in 2021, **not one book on Chauri Chaura** except a booklet
  written for the centenary; the only historical document was a copy of the High Court judgment
  [WEEK21A].
- Mukul Kesavan, historian, in The Hindu: "Chauri Chaura establishes Gandhi's moral credentials and
  demonstrates his total grip on the Congress... The non-cooperation movement is the first big
  pan-Indian experiment in civil disobedience. Chauri Chaura ends it" [HINDU24].

**Watch:** politics. The centenary was a government event and Modi did not mention Gandhi in his
speech [WEEK21A]. Say that plainly and without sneering, and give the government's own words too.

### 9. The Word They Used — SHORT (reserve)

How the event was remembered and argued over.

- Shahid Amin's book (1995) is the first academic study. UC Press describes it: "residents from the
  villages around the north Indian market town of Chauri Chaura attacked the local police station,
  burned it to the ground and murdered twenty-three constables" [UCPRESS].
- Amin's subject is memory: he describes the people as "anonymous characters from a self-defining
  event of Gandhian nationalism" [WEEK21A, quoting Amin's prologue].
- The Hindu summarises Amin: Chauri Chaura became a "...figure of speech, a trope for all manner of
  untrammeled peasant violence…" [HINDU24].
- Amin on Gandhi's role: "Without his visit to Gorakhpur the preceding year, and the electric
  excitement it generated, the kind of mobilisation that happened would not have been possible"
  [WEEK21A, quoting Amin].
- The caste argument: Kushwaha says 17 of the 19 hanged were recorded without surnames while two
  high-caste men kept theirs, and that "claiming a role in what was done by people from the
  margins of society is a distortion of history" [WEEK21A].
- Nehru's warning about the aftermath: the suppressed violence "had to find a way out", and he
  thought it "perhaps aggravated the communal trouble" of the following years [NEHRU, p. 84]. This
  is a historian's hindsight, not a fact about 1922; label it.
- Bhagat Singh and Chandrashekhar Azad are said to have turned away from Gandhi because of the
  withdrawal [FRONTIER14]. This is a common claim in the literature; it is one author's framing
  here, and I did not open a source for it. **UNVERIFIED — do not use without a second source.**

**Watch:** this is the "why it matters" angle, and the easiest one to get preachy. Keep it to what
people did with the memory — a word on a wall, a pension, a train, a film.

### 10. The Case Named After Abdullah — SHORT (reserve)

A single family, followed across a hundred years. Good for a quiet ending.

- **Abdullah** of Rajdhani village, 40, one of the main leaders; the High Court case is *King
  Emperor vs. Abdullah and others* [FRONTIER14; WEEK21A; AMIN note 116].
- His great-grandson **Akhtar Ali** lives in Rajdhani and works on construction sites in different
  states: "It is not a question of glory when all you are struggling for is two meals a day"
  [WEEK21A].
- **Meghu Tiwari** (also Lal Behari), hanged at 37, from Mehiyan village about 2 km from Chauri
  Chaura station; his grandson Ram Narayan Tripathi, 82 in 2024, was the families' unofficial
  spokesman: "Except one or two, most of us are living hand to mouth. This is the truth of the
  Chauri Chaura freedom fighters' families" [PRINT24].
- **Lal Mohammad**'s great-grandson Mohammed Mainuddin says the family got no respect from the
  connection [WEEK21A].

**Watch:** do not invent feelings. These are reported words from named descendants.

### 11. Devdas at Gorakhpur — SHORT (reserve, thin)

- Gandhi's son Devdas was doing Congress work in Gorakhpur in February 1922 [CWMG22, telegram 16
  February: "I TOO DID NOT UNDERSTAND YOUR TELEGRAM. COULD NOT MAKE OUT THE HORRORS YOU
  REFERRED."].
- 17 February, from Bardoli: "I am sorry that there are no letters from you. I can understand that
  you should be busy all the while, but at a time like this I do expect to have detailed reports
  from you" [CWMG22, p. 438].
- 19 February, to Nehru: "Let us not be obsessed by Devdas's youthful indiscretions. It is quite
  possible that the poor boy has been swept off his feet and that he has lost his balance"
  [CWMG22, p. 435].
- **THIN.** Three short passages and no context. Only worth writing if another story needs a
  family angle.

### 12. The Movement in Gorakhpur Before February — SHORT (reserve, partly single-source)

- Gandhi visited Gorakhpur on **8 February 1921**, addressed a meeting estimated at between 1 lakh
  and 2.5 lakh people, and returned the same evening to Banaras [AMIN, p. 289].
- The decision to invite him was taken at a public meeting in Gorakhpur city on **17 October 1920**
  [AMIN, p. 289].
- He was in the district for less than a day; the "Mahatma" as an idea was made afterwards, in the
  villages [AMIN, p. 290].
- Kushwaha's reviewer says Gandhi stayed 6–7 hours in Gorakhpur and addressed about two lakh people,
  and that 30,000 came to Devria station [FRONTIER14]. **Numbers disagree; use Amin's range.**
- Gorakhpur had 36,000 volunteers enlisted and fewer than 100 conforming to the Congress pledge, by
  Gandhi's own count [CWMG22, p. 436].
- The movement's local promise was twisted in the countryside into a belief that rents would fall
  to four or eight annas a bigha and that the police station would be abolished [AMIN, quoting
  *Gyan Shakti*, April 1921].

**Watch:** this is background, not a story. It belongs inside angle 2.

## What the sources cannot support

- **No eyewitness narrative by a named villager** of the firing itself. Everything is court
  testimony summarised by historians, or family memory.
- **No reliable count of the crowd.** 1,000–1,500 (High Court), 2,000–2,500 (Wikipedia),
  3,000–5,000 (Kushwaha).
- **No reliable count of the policemen.** 21 (Gandhi's first article), 22 (Wikipedia infobox,
  Malaviya), 23 (Britannica, Gorakhpur district, The Week, The Hindu), 24 (Kushwaha, counting a
  chowkidar who died of wounds).
- **No photograph of the burned thana on a free licence.** Commons has only the modern memorial and
  a railway station sign.
- **Nothing on the families of the policemen** beyond the fact that the government gave them jobs
  and pensions [FRONTIER14].
- **Bose's "national calamity"** is Britannica's quotation, not checked in *The Indian Struggle*.
- **The Chief Justice's compliment to Malaviya** is from a memorial biography, not a transcript.
- **Raghuvir Singh, the constable found alive**, is Wikipedia only.
