# Fact sheet: chauri-chaura-incident

One page, for writing. Everything below was opened on 27 October 2026. The full evidence, with
page numbers, is in `research/chatgpt-report-2026-10-27.md`; this sheet is the writing version of
it. If a fact is not here or in the report, do not write it. Source keys are at the end.

Six stories: two flagship (900+ words), four short (350+).

The spine: 1 February (beating at Mundera Bazaar) → 4 February (the march from Dumri Khurd, the
fire) → 11–12 February (Bardoli resolutions) → 12 February (Gandhi's fast begins) → 16 February
(*Young India* article) → 24–25 February (AICC Delhi) → January 1923 (Sessions Court) → 30 April
1923 (High Court) → 2–11 July 1923 (the hangings) → 1923/24 and 1973–1993 (the two memorials).

The best material in this subject is not the fire. It is that everyone in it thought they were
following Gandhi, and Gandhi did not agree.

**Date.** The incident was on **Saturday 4 February 1922**. Some Indian textbooks and exam guides
say 5 February; that is wrong. 4 February 1922 was a Saturday, which matters because Gandhi's first
article describes a "Friday market".

---

## 1. The Beating at Mundera Bazaar — SHORT

- **1 February 1922** [BRIT; WEEK21A]. Wikipedia says 2 February [WIKI]. **Two versions.** Use
  1 February and say the other date is also given.
- **Mundera Bazaar**, the larger market near Chaura [BRIT; WEEK21A]. Older retellings and Wikipedia
  call it "Gauri Bazaar" [WIKI]; the court records say Mundera. Use Mundera, and note the other name.
- The volunteers were picketing meat, fish and liquor shops, partly to bring prices down and partly
  because many believed Gandhi's vegetarianism and abstinence meant such goods should not be sold
  [BRIT]. The Week calls it "a prosperous market of pulses, raw sugar and rice" [WEEK21A].
- Three men were beaten: **Bhagwan Ahir, Ramrup Barai and Mahadeo** [WEEK21A]. Britannica mentions
  only "a war pensioner" badly hurt [BRIT].
- **Bhagwan Ahir**, 24, of Dumri Khurd: two years' service in the First World War, posted at Basra,
  drawing a government pension. The sub-inspector was angriest with him for that reason [WEEK21A].
- The order came from **Gupteshwar Singh**, sub-inspector of the Chaura police station [WEEK21A;
  WIKI]. Some accounts call him "Daroga" (inspector); the court records say sub-inspector.
- Exact words of Kallan Ahmed, grandson of Nazar Ali [WEEK21A]: "Ahir was a strong man. But so
  merciless was the beating that he struggled to get back home."
- Over the next three days there were meetings; it was decided to march to the police station and
  **ask Gupteshwar Singh for an explanation** [WEEK21A].
- **Watch:** do not write that the beating "caused" the fire in a straight line. There were three
  days of meetings and a march that began peacefully. Do not invent the market, the shopkeepers or
  the beating itself. Do not describe injuries in detail.

## 2. The Morning at Dumri Khurd — FLAGSHIP

- The movement's centre was **Dumri Khurd** (also Chhotki Dumri), a village of mostly Dalit Chamar
  families and Muslims, not the market town [FRONTIER14; WEEK21A].
- A Non-Cooperation committee was formed at Dumri Khurd on **13 January 1922** [WEEK21A].
- Letters went to villages within about **10 km**, asking volunteers to gather at Dumri Khurd on the
  morning of 4 February [WEEK21A].
- That morning volunteers went door to door asking for **jaggery and roasted rice** for the marchers
  [WEEK21A].
- Meeting place: the main accounts say a **threshing ground**; elderly residents told The Week it was
  an **orchard of mango, jackfruit and shisham trees in the local cemetery**, with sacks laid out for
  volunteers to sit on in groups of six [WEEK21A]. **Two versions — give both, choose neither.**
- Nazar Ali spoke and said anyone who left midway would be guilty of defiling their religion. Not
  one volunteer left [WEEK21A].
- The march started between **1.30 and 2.00 p.m.**, in order, and grew as people joined on the way
  [WEEK21A].
- Crowd size: **1,000–1,500** in the High Court judgment [WEEK21A]; **2,000–2,500** on Wikipedia
  [WIKI]; **3,000–5,000** by noon in Kushwaha [FRONTIER14]. **Three versions.** Write "more than a
  thousand" and say the counts disagree.
- The leaders [FRONTIER14]: **Nazar Ali**, 30, of Dumri Khurd; **Lal Mohammad**, 40, of Chaura
  village; **Bhagwan Ahir**, 24, of Dumri Khurd; **Abdullah Sai**, 40, of Rajdhani. Nazar Ali was an
  ex-soldier; Lal Mohammad sold coarse cloth; both were wrestlers, and news travelled through the
  *akharas* (wrestling gymnasiums) [WEEK21A].
- What the peasants said in court, quoted by Amin [AMIN]: men going to a meeting at Dumri on the
  morning of 4 February said they were "going to hold a Gandhi Mahatma Sabha" which would bring
  about "Gandhi Swaraj".
- Sarju Kahar, the thanedar's servant, testified that "two or four days before the affair [he] had
  heard that Gandhi Mahatma's Swaraj had been established, that the Chaura thana would be abolished,
  and that the volunteers would set up their own thana" [AMIN, quoting *Tajwiz Awwal, Chauri Chaura
  Trials*, p. 358].
- The Allahabad High Court found it "remarkable... how this name of 'Swaraj' was linked, in the minds
  of the peasantry of Gorakhpur, with the name of Mr Gandhi", and that they expected a "millenium in
  which taxation would be limited to the collection of small cash contributions or dues in kind"
  [AMIN, quoting *King Emperor vs. Abdullah and others*, Appeal No. 51 of 1923, p. 9].
- Gandhi had visited Gorakhpur on **8 February 1921**, addressed a meeting estimated at between one
  and two and a half lakh people, and went back to Banaras the same evening [AMIN, p. 289]. The
  invitation had been decided at a public meeting in Gorakhpur city on **17 October 1920** [AMIN,
  p. 289].
- By Gandhi's own count, **36,000 volunteers were enlisted in Gorakhpur, not 100 of whom conformed to
  the Congress pledge** [CWMG22, p. 436].
- **Watch:** the "Gandhi Mahatma Sabha" and "Gandhi's Swaraj" material is what peasants said in
  court, not what the Congress taught. Say plainly that nobody in the Congress had promised to
  abolish the police station. Do not make the volunteers a crowd of strangers; they came from named
  villages. Do not invent speeches, songs or the walk.

## 3. The Thana on Fire — SHORT

Handle gently: no gore, no cheering, no blaming a whole village.

- At the station the volunteers spoke to Gupteshwar Singh; by some accounts it was courteous, and he
  said Ahir was like a brother to him. The crowd then moved on towards Mundera Bazaar [WEEK21A].
- Then someone taunted Singh. Sharda Nand Yadav, great-grandson of Bikram Ahir (one of the 19
  hanged), remembers the words as: "What a shameful act this is. You being a Thakur bowing to these
  low-caste men" [WEEK21A]. This is family memory of a reported remark; label it as remembered.
- Singh ordered the chowkidars to strike their lathis on the ground and the armed police to fire in
  the air [WEEK21A]. Wikipedia says he ordered 15 local officers to fire warning shots [WIKI].
- The crowd came back and threw **ballast from the railway track** [WEEK21A]. Britannica says stones
  and brickbats [BRIT].
- A lathi charge followed, then the police fired into the crowd, killing at least three and wounding
  many [WEEK21A; BRIT; GORAKHPUR].
- Outnumbered, the police went inside the thana. Volunteers brought **kerosene from the market** and
  set it alight. Others **cut the telegraph wires** of the local post office so the news would not
  spread [WEEK21A].
- Deaths: **22 policemen and watchmen died in the fire; one badly injured chowkidar died a day later**
  [WEEK21A]. Britannica says 23 [BRIT]; the Gorakhpur district page says three civilians and 23
  policemen [GORAKHPUR]; Gandhi's first article says 21 [CWMG22, p. 386]. **Versions: 21, 22, 23, 24.**
- The Congress inquiry report said: "Twenty-three men were beaten to death and all except a few of
  them burned. The thana was set on fire and the men who attempted to escape were driven back into
  it" [WEEK21A, quoting the report].
- Even in that fury the volunteers let women and children go, including the pregnant wife of
  Gupteshwar Singh [WEEK21A]. Single source, from the inquiry; use it, attributed.
- The Sessions Court recorded that some were burned while still alive and that kerosene was poured on
  the bodies [WEEK21A, quoting the judgment]. **Background only. Do not put this in a children's
  story.**
- **Watch:** do not describe the burning, the bodies, the smell or the sounds. Do not write that the
  crowd "cheered". Do not use the Sessions Court sentence. The honest frame is: frightened men did a
  terrible thing, and other frightened men died in it.

## 4. The Crime of Gorakhpur — FLAGSHIP

Gandhi's own words. The strongest documentary material in the subject.

- **His first news was wrong.** *Navajivan*, 12 February 1922: the people "set fire to a police
  station, killed 21 innocent policemen and burned their bodies; a young son of a sub-inspector was
  also among the killed. According to newspaper reports, people went to disperse a Friday market
  which was being held there" [CWMG22, pp. 386–7]. 4 February was a **Saturday**, and the market was
  Mundera. He was writing on a train to Bombay from newspaper reports, before he had the details.
- In the same article: "I am certainly the one most responsible for the crime of the people of
  Gorakhpur district, but every genuine non-co-operator is also responsible for it. All of us should
  be in mourning for it" [CWMG22, p. 387].
- The Bardoli resolution, 11–12 February: "The Working Committee deplores the inhuman conduct of the
  mob at Chauri Chaura in having brutally murdered constables and wantonly burnt the Police Thana
  and tenders its sympathy to the families of the bereaved." It suspended mass civil disobedience at
  Bardoli and elsewhere and told local committees to advise cultivators to pay land revenue [CWMG22,
  pp. 377–9].
- The reason given: "In view of Nature's repeated warnings, every time mass civil disobedience has
  been imminent some popular violent outburst has taken place indicating that the atmosphere in the
  country is not non-violent enough for mass disobedience" [CWMG22, p. 378].
- *Young India*, 16 February 1922, "The Crime of Chauri Chaura": "God has been abundantly kind to me.
  He has warned me the third time..." He counts 1919 (the Rowlatt Act, "a Himalayan miscalculation"),
  the Bombay mob of 17 November 1921, and now Chauri Chaura [CWMG22, p. 415].
- He accepts the provocation and refuses it as an excuse: "I understand that the constables who were
  so brutally hacked to death had given much provocation. They had even gone back upon the word just
  given by the Inspector that they would not be molested..." — then: "No provocation can possibly
  justify the brutal murder of men who had been rendered defenceless and who had virtually thrown
  themselves on the mercy of the mob" [CWMG22, pp. 415–16].
- On the charge of cowardice: "It is better to be charged with cowardice and weakness than to be
  guilty of denial of our oath and sin against God" [CWMG22, p. 419].
- The fast: "After deep consideration, therefore, I am imposing on myself a five days' continuous
  fast, permitting myself water. It commenced on Sunday evening; it ends on Friday evening. This is
  the least I must do" [CWMG22, p. 419]. Sunday was 12 February; it ended Friday 17 February
  [CWMG22, p. 438 n.; GANDHISERVE].
- He asked others not to copy him: "I hope, therefore, that no one will join me in fasting, either
  through false sympathy or an ignorant conception of the spiritual value of fasting" [CWMG22,
  p. 420].
- To Jawaharlal Nehru in Lucknow jail, 19 February: "the Chauri Chaura news came like a powerful
  match to ignite the gunpowder, and there was a blaze" [CWMG22, pp. 435–6].
- The same letter lists warnings he had let pass: the "Madras doings", the Ferozepur Jirka shooting
  of 23 December 1921, and complaints from Bareilly, Jajjar, Shahjahanpur, Kanouj and Calcutta
  [CWMG22, p. 436].
- The All-India Congress Committee met at Delhi on **24–25 February 1922** and adopted the Bardoli
  resolutions with modifications [CWMG22, chronology, p. 521].
- **Watch:** the fast was penance for himself and a public warning, not a punishment of the
  villagers; his own words say so. Do not say the movement was suspended "because of Chauri Chaura
  alone" — Nehru denies that (story 5). Do not make him calm; he calls it a Himalayan warning and
  says he is ashamed.

## 5. One Hundred and Seventy — SHORT

The trial, and the lawyer who came out of retirement.

- Charges: rioting and arson, tried at the Gorakhpur Sessions Court. In the High Court the case was
  **King Emperor vs. Abdullah and others**, Appeal No. 51 of 1923 [AMIN, n. 116; WEEK21A].
- Numbers tried: **225** [BRIT; WIKI; MALAVIYA]; **228** [WEEK21B; FRONTIER14 says 228 of 273
  originally challenged]. **Two versions.**
- Sessions Judge: **H. E. Holmes** [WIKI]. Single source; the Malaviya account does not name him.
- The trial lasted about **eight months**, ending **9 January 1923** [WIKI].
- Death sentences from the Sessions Court: **170** [WIKI; MALAVIYA] or **172** [BRIT; FRONTIER14].
  **Two versions.** Acquittals at that stage: **47** [WIKI; FRONTIER14].
- Malaviya had left the High Court about 1913 and appeared "thereafter only once in 1923 to plead for
  the life of the large number sentenced to death in the Chauri Chaura case" [MALAVIYA]. He argued
  for all the accused for four or five days; a dozen other lawyers appeared for individuals. Crown
  counsel was a barrister from Aligarh named Qadri; the bench was Chief Justice Sir Grimwood Mears
  and Justice Sir Theodore Piggot [MALAVIYA].
- High Court result, **30 April 1923**: **19 death sentences upheld**, 38 acquitted, the rest given
  terms, 14 of them life imprisonment [WIKI; FRONTIER14; BRIT]. Malaviya's biographer puts it as
  "151 of the 170 condemned men were saved from the gallows and strictures were passed on the police"
  [MALAVIYA].
- The 19 hanged: Nazar Ali, Bhagwan Ahir, Lal Mohammad, Shyamsundar, Abdullah, Vikram Ahir, Dudhi
  Singh, Kali Charan, Lauti Kumar, Mahadev Singh, Meghu Tiwari, Raghuvir, Ramlakhan, Ramroop, Sahdev,
  Rudali, Mohan, Sampat, Sitaram [WIKI]. Hanged between **2 and 11 July 1923** in different jails of
  the United Provinces [WIKI; FRONTIER14; BRIT].
- The Chief Justice's compliment to Malaviya ("Mr. Malaviya, we are extremely grateful to you for
  putting this case so fairly and well before us...") is quoted by his biographer [MALAVIYA].
  **UNVERIFIED as a court record.** Paraphrase it and say "his biographer records"; do not put it in
  quotation marks.
- **Watch:** this is a story about a lawyer saving lives, not about a court being kind. Nineteen men
  were still hanged. Say both. Do not name the hangman, the gallows or the last words. Do not say
  "justice was done" in either direction.

## 6. Two Memorials — SHORT

Remembrance, and the argument about it.

- **Police memorial**: built by the British on the police station grounds in **1923 or 1924**; it has
  **23 niches**, one for each slain policeman, and "Jai Hind" was added on top after independence
  [BRIT; HINDU24]. It still stands and is not well kept [HINDU24].
- **1973**: a local committee, the Chauri Chaura Shaheed Smarak Samiti (formed 1971), built a
  **12.2 m triangular minaret** near the lake, with a figure hanging from a noose on each side, paid
  for by public subscription of Rs 13,500 [WIKI, citing a Times of India report I could not open;
  treat the measurements as Wikipedia's].
- **1982**: Indira Gandhi laid the foundation stone of the Shaheed Smarak [BRIT; HINDU24; PRINT24].
  The family story is that Ram Narayan Tripathi, grandson of Meghu Tiwari, asked her for it during a
  1978 by-election visit to Gorakhpur, and money was sanctioned after the Congress won [PRINT24].
- **1993**: the Shaheed Smarak was inaugurated, under P. V. Narasimha Rao [BRIT; FRONTIER14;
  HINDU24]. The same year the families of those hanged began to receive freedom fighters' family
  pensions — **110 families** [FRONTIER14; PRINT24]. The pension started at about **Rs 800** and is
  now **Rs 20,000** [PRINT24].
- **2021**: on 4 February the Prime Minister inaugurated the centenary celebrations at Chauri Chaura
  and released a postage stamp; the programme ran in all 75 districts of UP until 4 February 2022
  [WEEK21B]. Modi said: "It is unfortunate that the martyrs of Chauri Chaura have not been spoken
  about much. The incident was not just about a police station being burnt down; the message it sent
  was huge. It should not be seen as a physical fire; it was a fire in the hearts of the people"
  [WEEK21B].
- **2022**: the UP government agreed to rename Mundera Bazar Nagar Panchayat as Chauri Chaura Nagar
  Panchayat [BRIT].
- The memorial's own wall says "Shaheed Smarak—Chauri Chaura **Kand**—February 1922", using the word
  *kand* (an abominable incident) and without the date [WEEK21A].
- In 2021 the memorial's library had **not one book on Chauri Chaura** except a centenary booklet;
  the only historical document was a copy of the High Court judgment [WEEK21A].
- Mukul Kesavan in The Hindu: "Chauri Chaura establishes Gandhi's moral credentials and demonstrates
  his total grip on the Congress... The non-cooperation movement is the first big pan-Indian
  experiment in civil disobedience. Chauri Chaura ends it" [HINDU24].
- **Watch:** politics. The centenary was a government event and Modi did not mention Gandhi in that
  speech [WEEK21A]. Say so plainly, without sneering, and give the government's own words too. Do not
  invent a visitor, a caretaker or a plaque.

## Do not say

- Not 5 February. **Saturday 4 February 1922.**
- Do not put a number on the dead policemen without saying the counts disagree (21, 22, 23, 24).
- Do not describe burning bodies, gore, the smell, or the sounds of the fire.
- Do not glorify the killing of the policemen, and do not call the crowd a "mob" in your own voice
  unless you are quoting a source.
- Do not invent crowd feelings, cheers, chants, weather, or the thoughts of Bhagwan Ahir or anyone
  else.
- Do not say the Congress promised to abolish the police station; that was what peasants believed.
- Do not say the fast punished the villagers.
- Do not say the movement was suspended because of Chauri Chaura alone.
- Do not put the Chief Justice's compliment in quotation marks; it is a biography's memory.
- Do not use the Bose "national calamity" phrase as a verified quotation (Britannica only).
- Do not use "HRA" for 1922; the organisation was the **HSRA** from September 1928, and it does not
  appear in these stories at all.
- Do not mention Bhagat Singh or Chandrashekhar Azad's disillusionment; it is single-source.
- No flag in the ordinary scenes. If a flag appears, it is the 1922 Congress tricolour with the
  spinning wheel — never the Ashoka Chakra.
