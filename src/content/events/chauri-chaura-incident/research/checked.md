# Source check — Chauri Chaura — 27 October 2026

What held up, what could not be checked, what I left out. Every page below was opened on
27 October 2026 unless marked otherwise.

## What held up

- **The date.** Saturday **4 February 1922**. The existing `index.md` had `1922-02-05`; that is the
  date some textbooks and exam guides print, and it is wrong. 4 February 1922 was a Saturday, which
  is why Gandhi's first article calling it a "Friday market" is worth quoting as evidence of how
  badly he had been briefed.
- **The beating at Mundera Bazaar.** Britannica, The Week and Wikipedia all carry it; the three
  names (Bhagwan Ahir, Ramrup Barai, Mahadeo) and the sub-inspector Gupteshwar Singh come from The
  Week. Bhagwan Ahir's war service at Basra and his pension also from The Week.
- **The march from Dumri Khurd.** The 13 January committee, the letters to villages within 10 km, the
  jaggery and roasted rice, the 1.30–2.00 p.m. start, Nazar Ali's speech, and the leaders' names and
  ages all come from The Week's cover story, which is reporting from the village and the court
  records.
- **The court testimony quoted by Amin.** The "Gandhi Mahatma Sabha", "Gandhi Swaraj", Sarju Kahar's
  statement and the High Court's "millenium" passage are all quoted from the trial record in Amin's
  open essay, with the note numbers. I checked each against the essay text.
- **Gandhi's articles and letters.** Read in the full CWMG vol. XXII scan: "Gorakhpur's Crime"
  (*Navajivan*, 12-2-1922, pp. 386–7), "The Crime of Chauri Chaura" (*Young India*, 16-2-1922,
  pp. 415–420), the Bardoli resolutions (pp. 377–9), the letter to Nehru (19-2-1922, pp. 435–7), the
  two letters to Devdas (16 and 17 February), and the chronology (p. 521). All quotations in the fact
  sheet are copied from that text.
- **The fast.** Began Sunday evening 12 February, ended Friday evening 17 February; CWMG's own note
  on the Devdas letter of 17 February says "The fast will end today in an hour's time", which fixes
  it.
- **Nehru in jail.** Pages 80–85 of the 1936 autobiography, read in a full scan. The "we were angry"
  and "resented... by almost all the prominent Congress leaders" passages are exact.
- **Malaviya's appeal.** The Mahamana Malaviya Mission's legal biography gives the 1923 appearance,
  the four or five days of argument, the bench (Mears C.J. and Piggot J.), Crown counsel Qadri, and
  the "151 of the 170" result. It is a memorial biography, not a court record, and is labelled as
  such.
- **The memorials.** The 23 niches and "Jai Hind" (The Hindu 2024, Britannica), the 1973 minaret and
  the 1982 foundation stone (Wikipedia, The Hindu, ThePrint), the 1993 inauguration and pensions
  (Frontier, ThePrint), the 2021 centenary and stamp (The Week), the 2022 renaming (Britannica).

## Two versions, left as two versions

- **1 or 2 February** for the beating: Britannica and The Week say 1 February, Wikipedia says 2.
- **Mundera or Gauri Bazaar**: court records and Britannica/The Week say Mundera; Wikipedia and older
  retellings say Gauri Bazaar.
- **Crowd size**: 1,000–1,500 (High Court, via The Week), 2,000–2,500 (Wikipedia), 3,000–5,000
  (Kushwaha, via Frontier).
- **Policemen killed**: 21 (Gandhi's first article), 22 (Wikipedia infobox, Malaviya), 23
  (Britannica, Gorakhpur district, The Week, The Hindu), 24 (Kushwaha, counting the chowkidar who
  died of wounds). The fact sheet says 22 died in the fire and one later; it does not choose a single
  total.
- **Tried**: 225 (Britannica, Wikipedia, Malaviya) or 228 (The Week, Frontier).
- **Death sentences**: 170 (Wikipedia, Malaviya) or 172 (Britannica, Frontier).
- **Meeting place at Dumri Khurd**: threshing ground in the main accounts; orchard in the cemetery
  according to elderly residents speaking to The Week.
- **Gandhi's "five days"**: he calls it five days and says it began Sunday evening and ended Friday
  evening. Sunday evening to Friday evening is four and a half days by the clock; the sources all
  call it five, and the fact sheet follows them.

## What could not be checked

- **Bose's "national calamity"** — Britannica's quotation only. I did not open *The Indian Struggle*.
  Not used in the fact sheet as a verified quotation.
- **Chief Justice Mears's compliment to Malaviya** — a memorial biography's memory, not a transcript.
  Flagged UNVERIFIED; stories must paraphrase and attribute.
- **Sessions Judge "H. E. Holmes"** — Wikipedia only; the Malaviya account does not name the judge.
- **Raghuvir Singh found alive** — Wikipedia only. Left out.
- **Bhagat Singh and Chandrashekhar Azad turning away from Gandhi** — one author's framing in
  Frontier, no second source opened. Left out of the fact sheet.
- **1973 minaret measurements** (12.2 m, Rs 13,500) — Wikipedia citing a Times of India report I
  could not open. Marked as Wikipedia's.
- **"Jai Hind" verse on the police memorial** — Britannica and The Hindu, but the wording itself is
  Wikipedia citing a blocked paper. Not used.
- **The British memorial's exact year** (1923 or 1924) — the sources say one or the other. Fact sheet
  keeps both.
- **Kushwaha's "90 per cent Dalit, backward caste or Muslim"** and "17 of the 19 hanged had no
  surnames" — one author, reported by a reviewer. Available as a labelled claim, not as fact.
- **Crowd feelings, cheers, chants** — no source describes them, so none are written.

## Sites that would not open

- **pib.gov.in** — both centenary releases (PRID=1695103 and PRID=1694344) returned HTTP 403. The same
  PMO text is quoted by The Week and by Economic Times/ANI, which I did open. Nothing rests on PIB.
- **Times of India, Hindustan Times, Indian Express** — not opened. Two Wikipedia claims (the 1973
  minaret measurements and the "Jai Hind" verse) rest on them and are marked as Wikipedia's.
- **archive.org copy of Shahid Amin, *Event, Metaphor, Memory*** — the full text is lending-only
  (HTTP 401), and the inside-search endpoint returned 403. I used Amin's separate open essay "Gandhi
  as Mahatma" and Amin quotations printed in The Week and The Hindu instead.
- **The Hindu, 14 August 2024** — opened, but the page is partly behind a subscription wall; the
  passages used (the memorial, the 23 niches, Kesavan's quotation) were readable in the page text.
- **upload.wikimedia.org** rate-limits requests without a User-Agent (HTTP 429). Downloaded the two
  archive pictures with a named User-Agent; recorded in `image-credits.md`.

## Left out on purpose

- The Sessions Court's sentence about burning while alive and pouring kerosene on the bodies. It is
  in the judgment as quoted by The Week. True, and not for a children's story.
- Any description of wounds, blood, the smell of the fire, or the gallows.
- The "five million" style crowd numbers, and any single figure presented as certain.
- Devdas Gandhi's "youthful indiscretions" material — three short passages, no context, THIN.
- The 1921 Gorakhpur visit as a story of its own — background, folded into the Dumri Khurd story.
- The caste argument as a story — one author, and it needs a second source before it goes to
  children.

## Quotations: exact or not

Every quotation in `fact-sheet.md` is copied from a page I opened, with the key and page number.
The two exceptions are marked in the sheet itself: the Chief Justice's compliment (biography, not
transcript) and Bose's "national calamity" (Britannica, not the book). Neither may appear in
quotation marks in a story.
