## Research notes

Chauri Chaura was a village in Gorakhpur district, United Provinces, near the border with what is
now Bihar. On Saturday 4 February 1922 a crowd of villagers, many of them volunteers in the
Non-Cooperation Movement, marched from Dumri Khurd to the Chaura police station to demand an
explanation from the sub-inspector about a beating at Mundera Bazaar three days earlier. The
argument turned into stone-throwing, the police fired and killed three people, and the crowd set the
police station on fire. Twenty-two policemen and village watchmen died in the fire and one more died
the next day.

Gandhi was at Bardoli, about to launch mass civil disobedience. He treated the news as proof that
India was not ready for non-violent mass action, had the Congress Working Committee suspend the
whole campaign on 11–12 February, and began a five-day fast on the evening of 12 February. Most
Congress leaders, including Jawaharlal Nehru in jail, thought the decision was wrong or at least
badly handled.

The trial at Gorakhpur Sessions Court ended in January 1923 with 170 or 172 death sentences.
Madan Mohan Malaviya, who had left the High Court about ten years earlier, came back to argue the
appeal. On 30 April 1923 the Allahabad High Court upheld 19 death sentences; those men were hanged
between 2 and 11 July 1923. The rest were acquitted or imprisoned.

The date is often printed as 5 February. It was 4 February 1922, a Saturday. Gandhi's own first
article, written on a train from newspaper reports, calls it a "Friday market", which shows how
little he knew when he first wrote about it.
