The volunteers reached the police station at Chauri Chaura in the afternoon. A *thana* is a police station, and this one stood in front of the railway station.

They had come to ask a question. Three of their companions had been beaten at Mundera Bazaar three days before. The volunteers wanted to hear the Sub-Inspector, Gupteshwar Singh, explain why.

## A calm start

The volunteers spoke to Singh. By some accounts, the talk was polite. Singh is said to have told them that Bhagwan Ahir, the man who had been beaten hardest, was like a brother to him. The matter seemed settled. The crowd moved on, as it had planned, to picket Mundera Bazaar.

## The turn

Then came a taunt. A family memory, told by Sharda Nand Yadav, great-grandson of one of the volunteers, says someone said: "What a shameful act this is. You being a Thakur bowing to these low-caste men."

Families remember such words after a hundred years, but nobody wrote them down at the time. We can say only that this is what his family says.

After the taunt, Singh ordered the watchmen to strike their sticks on the ground. He ordered the armed police to fire in the air. Much of the crowd had already gone ahead. They heard the noise and turned back.

They threw stones from the railway track. The police made a lathi charge. Then the police fired into the crowd, and at least three people were killed. Many more were hurt.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: Gandhi printed an account sent to him by a person who knew the district. In it, the constables had gone back on the Inspector's word. They abused the stragglers at the end of the crowd. The volunteers returned, and the constables' bullets ran out. Wikipedia says the police ran short of ammunition, or else that the crowd's anger frightened them. Either way, the police pulled back into the thana.</aside>

## The fire

The police were few and the crowd was large. They went inside the thana and shut themselves in.

Some volunteers took kerosene from the market. They set fire to the building. Others went to the post office and cut the telegraph wires, so that no news could travel quickly.

<aside class="note"><b>Telegraph</b>: A telegraph was a way of sending messages along wires, a bit like an early email. It was the fastest way to send news in 1922.</aside>

The Week reports that 22 policemen died in the fire and that a watchman who was hurt died a day later. Other writers give other numbers.

<aside class="note"><b>Counting the dead</b>: Britannica and the Gorakhpur district website say 23 policemen died. Gandhi himself wrote 21 in the *Navajivan*. The biography of Madan Mohan Malaviya says the Sub-Inspector and 21 constables. Even the best sources do not agree on one number.</aside>

The volunteers did not harm everyone. A report by a Congress inquiry noted that they let women and children go safely. This included Singh's wife, who was expecting a baby.

## What came next

The police station was destroyed. The men inside had died, and so had at least three of the volunteers. Afterwards, the police began a great hunt for everyone who had been there. In the villages, the women went away to their parents' homes.

The news travelled to Gandhi. What he did about it is told in "The Crime of Gorakhpur".

## Why it matters

Chauri Chaura began as a march to ask for an explanation. It ended in many deaths. The movement's leaders had asked everyone to be peaceful. In a large and angry crowd, some people did not keep that promise.
