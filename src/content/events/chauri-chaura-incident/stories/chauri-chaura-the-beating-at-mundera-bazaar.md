Chauri Chaura was not one village. It was the name of a railway station, and the name came from two villages that lay side by side. The station stood just behind the local police station. The place was more than 20 kilometres from the city of Gorakhpur, in eastern Uttar Pradesh.

In early 1922, the Non-Cooperation movement was more than a year old. Gandhi had asked Indians to stop helping the British government. Volunteers in many places had taken up his call. In this area, they also picketed shops, which means they stood outside and asked people not to buy.

## The market

Mundera Bazaar was a larger market near Chaura. It was a rich market, full of pulses, raw sugar and rice. On 1 February 1922, volunteers went there to picket the shops that sold meat, fish and liquor. They were also unhappy about high food prices.

Three of the volunteers were Bhagwan Ahir, Ramrup Barai and Mahadeo. The sub-inspector of the Chaura police station, Gupteshwar Singh, gave the order to beat them. All three were hurt. Bhagwan Ahir was hurt the worst.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: Wikipedia gives the date as 2 February and the name of the market as Gauri Bazaar. It also says the volunteers' leaders were locked up in the police station. The Week and Britannica give 1 February and Mundera. This story follows The Week and Britannica.</aside>

## Why him?

Bhagwan Ahir was 24 years old and lived in the village of Dumri Khurd. During the First World War, he had spent two years in the army and had been posted at Basra. He drew a government pension as an old soldier.

That is why the sub-inspector was angry with him. Ahir took money from the government, and yet he was working against it. That, at least, was how Singh saw it.

Kallan Ahmed is the grandson of Nazar Ali, another leader from Dumri Khurd. He told a reporter in 2021: "Ahir was a strong man. But so merciless was the beating that he struggled to get back home."

## Three days of meetings

In the next three days, there were many meetings. The people decided on a plan. They would march to the police station, and they would ask Gupteshwar Singh to explain why he had beaten three men.

Messages went out to villages about ten kilometres around, asking volunteers to come to Dumri Khurd on the morning of 4 February.

The next story, "The Morning at Dumri Khurd", tells what happened on that day.

## Why it matters

The beating at the bazaar did not cause the fire at the police station. Three days of meetings lay in between, and the march began in good order. But the beating was the reason people gathered. It was the first link in a chain of events that none of them could have seen.
