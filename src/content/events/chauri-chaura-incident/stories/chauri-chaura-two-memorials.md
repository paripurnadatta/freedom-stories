If you visit Chauri Chaura today, you can see two memorials. They stand near each other. They remember different people.

## The first memorial

The first one is a small, plain building. It is the memorial for the policemen who died in the fire of 1922. The British government put it up soon afterwards, in 1923 or 1924. It has 23 niches at the back, one for each policeman. A few tombstones stand in a line. The words "Jai Hind", which mean "Victory to India", were added on top much later.

The policemen who died were Indians too, though they worked for the British Crown.

For many years this was the only memorial in the place. The men who were hanged had nothing.

## The second memorial

In 1971, a group of local people formed the Chauri Chaura Shaheed Smarak Samiti, a committee to raise a memorial. Two years later, in 1973, they built a triangular tower of stone, 12.2 metres high. It cost 13,500 rupees, which people paid by subscription. In 1982, Prime Minister Indira Gandhi laid the foundation stone of a larger memorial. It was finished when P. V. Narasimha Rao was Prime Minister, and it was opened in 1993.

Today, the memorial has a stone column. On either side are plaques with the names of the nineteen men who were hanged. A library and a museum stand beside it.

## The families

For the families of the nineteen, a memorial was not the same as being honoured. Ram Narayan Tripathi's grandfather, Meghu Tiwari, was one of the men hanged in 1923. Tripathi told a writer that the family lost everything after the event: their lands, their cattle and their cloth business in Mundera Bazaar.

Until 1993, the families were not even counted as the families of freedom fighters. That year, the government gave them a family pension. According to Tripathi, it began at about 800 rupees, and it is now 20,000 rupees.

The weekly *Frontier* says that 110 families were entitled.

## One hundred years later

In February 2021, Prime Minister Narendra Modi began the hundred-year celebrations at Chauri Chaura. He released a postage stamp. He said, "It is unfortunate that the martyrs of Chauri Chaura have not been spoken about much," and called the event "a fire in the hearts of the people".

A reporter for The Week visited the memorial the same year. The library had almost no books on Chauri Chaura. It had a small booklet, written for the celebration, and a copy of the High Court judgment. Mohammed Mainuddin, whose great-grandfather Lal Muhammad was one of the leaders, said that the fame of his great-grandfather had brought no respect to the family.

In 2022, the town was renamed Chauri Chaura Nagar Panchayat.

<aside class="note"><b>Two views</b>: The Week says Mr Modi did not mention Gandhi in his speech. The historian Shahid Amin says that without Gandhi's visit to Gorakhpur in 1921, the movement there would not have happened in the way it did.</aside>

## Why it matters

The historian Mukul Kesavan sums up the place of Chauri Chaura in the freedom struggle: "The non-cooperation movement is the first big pan-Indian experiment in civil disobedience. Chauri Chaura ends it."

The two memorials ask a hard question. How should a country remember a day when the people it honours also took lives? One answer is to remember everyone: the policemen, the volunteers and the families who waited.
