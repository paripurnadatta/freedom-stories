Gandhi was travelling from Petlad station to Dantali in a bullock cart. On the way, he saw animals being urged forward with pointed goads. When he reached the meeting, he spoke about what he had noticed.

The date was 17 April 1918. People had gathered during the Kheda revenue struggle. Gandhi had come to discuss justice for farmers. He began by asking them to consider how they treated their own working animals.

<aside class="note"><b>Goad</b>: A pointed stick used to urge an animal onward.</aside>

He challenged the people who claimed to protect cattle while hurting bullocks at work. Wanting justice for themselves, he said, required giving justice to others. The lesson from the road belonged in the meeting.

Then he returned to the revenue demand. Some people argued that farmers should simply find the money. They could borrow it. They could sell land or an animal. Why accept the trouble of resisting?

Gandhi answered that the question was one of principle. The farmers disputed an official demand they believed was unfair. Paying from fear would leave that dispute unanswered. They were asking the government to recognise a claim to relief.

He did not say that every refusal was automatically right. The claim about the crops needed evidence. He reminded his listeners that they had asked for an inquiry. They had offered to accept the official assessment if it proved correct.

He also warned that the hard test might still be ahead. People could declare themselves ready at a meeting. Holding to a promise when land was seized would be more difficult.

The speech joined courage with courtesy. Gandhi wanted neither frightened obedience nor angry abuse. He urged the listeners to keep to truth. If the struggle made them morally weaker, it would fail his test of satyagraha.

His later account of Kheda returned to the same concern. Learning not to fear officials had come more easily than learning to remain civil towards them. A strong protest still needed restraint.

The bullock-cart journey had supplied a nearby example. Before asking distant rulers to behave justly, people could examine an act taking place beside them. Gandhi had arrived to speak about the government. He also asked his listeners to look at the animals pulling their carts.
