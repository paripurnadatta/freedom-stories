On 10 October 1905 the Chief Secretary of Bengal, R. W. Carlyle, sent an order to schools and colleges. It is called the Carlyle Circular. It barred students from political rallies. It forbade them to sing *Vande Mataram* and to join swadeshi gatherings. Schools that did not obey could lose their grants and their link with the university.

The order made students angry. Many left their government schools rather than obey.

## Where would they study?

An Anti-Circular Society was started in October 1905 by Sachindra Prasad Basu. It arranged teaching for students who had been thrown out of school. It also held processions and raised money for students.

There was an older group too. The Dawn Society was founded in 1902 by Satish Chandra Mukherjee at the Metropolitan Institution in Calcutta. It taught moral values, national history and self-reliance. Its industrial section trained students in handicrafts and weaving.

On 5 November 1905 the Dawn Society held a protest meeting. Rabindranath Tagore spoke. He urged students to break their ties with the university run by the government.

## Money for a new college

Money came quickly. At a mass meeting on 9 November 1905, Subodh Chandra Mullick promised one lakh rupees. People began to call him Raja. Brojendra Kishore Roy Chowdhury gave five lakh rupees in cash and an estate that earned twenty thousand rupees a year.

<aside class="note"><b>Lakh</b>: one hundred thousand. One lakh rupees is 100,000 rupees.</aside>

## The college

The National Council of Education was set up in 1906, with Dr Rashbehari Ghosh as its first president. A public meeting at the Town Hall on 14 August 1906 opened the Bengal National College and School. Classes began the next day, 15 August 1906, in a rented house at 191/1 Bowbazar Street.

<aside class="note"><b>Two dates</b>: One page says the Council was founded on 11 March 1906. Another says it was established on 15 August 1906. They may be describing the founding and the opening.</aside>

Sri Aurobindo Ghosh was the first Principal. He had resigned from Baroda College to take the post. Satish Chandra Mukherjee was the Honorary Superintendent. Scholars gave their time to teach for nothing.

The college had four departments: literary, scientific, technical and commercial. Teaching was mostly in Bengali and other Indian languages. It stressed character, moral lessons, exercise and science. Students also learned Indian history, philosophy and literature.

Tagore, one page says, agreed to lecture on literature. That page says these were agreements, and does not confirm that every lecture was given. Aurobindo did not like the syllabus. He thought it was too elaborate and too costly, because it copied the Western way of teaching many subjects at once.

A separate body, the Bengal Technical Institute, opened on 25 July 1906. In 1910 it joined the National Council.

## A hard road

In August 1907 Aurobindo resigned as Principal. He was on trial in the Bande Mataram sedition case, and he did not want the college to suffer. He took the post again for a short time after he was cleared.

After 1908 the college declined. The government would not recognise its degrees, so graduates found it hard to get government jobs. Police watched the donors, and some stopped giving. The split of the Congress at Surat in 1907 upset the movement's plans and money.

## Why it matters

The national colleges did not last as they were. But the National Council and the Technical Institute are the roots of Jadavpur University, which became a university by law in 1955.
