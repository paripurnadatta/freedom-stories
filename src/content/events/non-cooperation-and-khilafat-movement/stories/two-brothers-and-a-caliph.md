In 1920 two brothers from Rampur were among the best-known men in India. They were Muhammad Ali and Shaukat Ali. People called them the Ali brothers. They stood beside Gandhi, and for a while their cause and his were one.

## A mother's wish

Muhammad Ali was born on 10 December 1878 in Rampur. His family was rich. His father died when he was about five. His mother, Abadi Begum, was determined that her sons would be educated. His brothers included Shaukat Ali and Zulfiqar Ali.

He studied at Aligarh and Allahabad. In 1898 he went to England and studied modern history at Lincoln College, Oxford. When he came home he directed education in the state of Rampur. Afterwards he joined the civil service of Baroda.

Shaukat Ali was older, born on 10 March 1873. He studied at Aligarh, where he captained the cricket team. He served for seventeen years in the civil service of the United Provinces.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: One page says Shaukat Ali was born in Rampur. The text of the same page says his family had roots in Najibabad.</aside>

## Pen and press

Muhammad Ali wrote for British and Indian newspapers. In 1911 he started an English weekly, *The Comrade*, in Calcutta. He moved to Delhi in 1912. In 1913 he founded an Urdu daily, *Hamdard*. Shaukat worked with him on both papers.

He had also been at the founding meeting of the All-India Muslim League in Dacca in 1906. One page says he served as its president in 1918.

In 1915 Shaukat Ali published an article saying that the Turks were right to fight the British. In 1919 he was jailed for printing material the government called seditious. While he was in prison he was elected the last president of the Khilafat Conference.

## The caliph

The Ottoman Sultan was also the caliph, the nominal head of Sunni Muslims. After the First World War the British planned to break up his empire. The Treaty of Sèvres in 1920 cut Palestine, Syria, Lebanon and Iraq away from it.

Many Indian Muslims were upset. They began the Khilafat movement. In 1919 a delegation went to Britain to ask that the Sultan's position be kept. It failed. After that, Khilafat committees called for protests and boycotts. The All India Khilafat Committee published a Khilafat Manifesto in 1920.

The Ali brothers, Maulana Azad and Hakim Ajmal Khan were its main leaders.

Money was collected to send to Mustafa Kemal's government in Ankara. Some people left the North-West Frontier for Afghanistan, under Amanullah Khan, as a protest.

## With Gandhi

In 1920 the Khilafat leaders and Gandhi agreed to work for Khilafat and swaraj together. Muhammad Ali's wife, Amjadi Bano Begum, whom he had married in 1902, took part in the national and Khilafat movements.

On 29 October 1920 a new institution was founded at Aligarh. Its students and teachers wanted a Muslim university free from government influence. It was Jamia Millia Islamia. Muhammad Ali was one of its founders and became its first vice-chancellor. He served from 1920 to 1923.

Mahmud Hasan laid the foundation stone. His speech was read out by his student Shabbir Ahmad Usmani. Hakim Ajmal Khan was the first chancellor. Gandhi backed the new university and sent his youngest son, Devdas, to teach Hindi there. In 1925 Jamia moved to Karol Bagh in Delhi, and in the 1930s to Okhla. It became a central university in 1988.

In 1921 he was arrested over a speech at a Khilafat Conference, and he was sent to prison. Shaukat Ali was arrested again in 1921 too. In March 1922 he was in Rajkot jail. He was let out in 1923.

<aside class="note"><b>The sentence</b>: One page gives Muhammad Ali's sentence at Karachi as two years. Another page, in the same article, says two and a half.</aside>

## After Chauri Chaura

When Gandhi stopped the movement in February 1922, Muhammad Ali was disillusioned. Muhammad Ali left the Congress, though he had been chosen its president in 1923. His term lasted only a few months.

In 1924 the new Turkish Republic abolished the caliphate. The cause of the Khilafat movement had gone. By then Muslim support was divided among the Congress, the Khilafat committees and the Muslim League.

<aside class="note"><b>Mixing causes</b>: Critics, including Jinnah, said a movement about a religious office in Turkey should not be mixed with India's secular struggle. The brothers' friends said they were standing by fellow Muslims abroad. The pages report both views and do not decide between them.</aside>

## Different roads

The brothers followed different roads from Gandhi's. Muhammad Ali started *Hamdard* again. He opposed the Nehru Report, which proposed a constitution for India, and he supported Jinnah's Fourteen Points. He became a critic of Gandhi. He also said Gandhi and Motilal Nehru had given in to Hindu demands. That was his own view, and the page does not say it was true.

In 1928 Shaukat Ali also opposed the Nehru Report and asked for separate electorates for Muslims. Muhammad Ali went to the first Round Table Conference in London in 1930 for the Muslim League. Shaukat Ali attended the first and the second.

## The end

Muhammad Ali had diabetes, and his health had suffered in prison. He died of a stroke in London on 4 January 1931. He was buried in Jerusalem, near the Dome of the Rock. His grave carries the words "Here lies al-Sayyid Muhammad Ali al-Hindi."

India and Pakistan have both put Muhammad Ali on a stamp. India's came out in 1978, for his hundredth birthday. A university in Rampur carries his name. Pakistan issued a stamp for Shaukat Ali in 1995.

From 1934 to 1938 Shaukat Ali sat in the Central Assembly. He joined the Muslim League in 1936 and became a close ally of Jinnah. He died at his sister-in-law's home in Karol Bagh, Delhi, on 26 November 1938, aged 65. He was buried near the Jama Masjid.

<aside class="note"><b>Year of death</b>: A list of people named Shaukat Ali gives 1873 to 1939. The article on him gives 26 November 1938.</aside>

## Why it matters

For a short time, the Ali brothers, Gandhi and the Congress were one force. When the partnership broke, each side took away a different lesson. Historians still argue about whose.
