This is a sad story. It is told as carefully as the pages allow, because some of what happened is still argued over.

Chauri Chaura was a town near Gorakhpur, in what is now Uttar Pradesh. In early 1922 the non-cooperation movement was in its second year. Gandhi had promised swaraj within a year of starting it. He had also said that it must be peaceful in every part.

## 2 February

On 2 February 1922 volunteers led by Bhagwan Ahir, a retired soldier, held a protest at Gauri Bazaar. They were against high food prices and the sale of liquor. The police inspector, Gupteshwar Singh, and other policemen drove them back. Several of the volunteers' leaders were locked up at the Chauri Chaura police station.

## 4 February

Two days later a crowd of about 2,000 to 2,500 people gathered. They meant to picket the liquor shop at Gauri Bazaar. They marched towards the market, shouting slogans against British rule.

Inspector Gupteshwar Singh told his fifteen local policemen to fire warning shots. This made the crowd angry, and people began to throw stones. Then Sub-Inspector Prithvi Pal ordered the police to fire on the crowd as it came on. Three people were killed and several were wounded.

The policemen were outnumbered. They fell back into the police station. The crowd set the building on fire. The policemen inside were killed, among them Gupteshwar Singh. Some died in the flames. One page says some were killed at the door and thrown back in.

<aside class="note"><b>How many?</b> Accounts give 22 or 23 policemen killed. The difference may be about Constable Raghuvir Singh, who was first reported dead but was found alive and gave evidence in court.</aside>

<aside class="note"><b>Why did the police fall back?</b> Some say they ran out of ammunition. Others say the bold response of the crowd was the reason. The pages do not settle it.</aside>

The authorities declared martial law in the area. Police raided homes and arrested hundreds of people.

## Not the first time

Violence had touched the movement before. In November 1921, riots broke out in Bombay during the visit of the Prince of Wales. Gandhi thought about pausing the movement then, but decided against it. In the same year, the Moplah rebellion in Malabar moved away from non-violence.

## Gandhi's answer

Gandhi fasted for five days as a penance. He believed he had moved too quickly towards revolt. He had not stressed non-violence enough, he felt, and had not prepared people to keep calm.

On 12 February 1922 he stopped the non-cooperation movement across the country. That month he had also written to the Viceroy, Lord Reading, to propose civil disobedience at Bardoli.

Most of the Congress leaders were in prison. Jawaharlal Nehru and others thought the decision was hasty and a mistake. They felt that a whole movement was being stopped for the crimes of a small group of men. Muhammad Ali Jauhar and his brother Shaukat Ali, leaders of the Khilafat cause, were disappointed too, and later left the Congress. Motilal Nehru and C. R. Das formed the Swaraj Party.

<aside class="note"><b>Gandhi's reasoning</b>: He believed the masses had not yet taken in the idea of non-violence. He did not want the movement to become a fight between police and crowds.</aside>

Gandhi himself was arrested on 10 March 1922, and on 18 March a court sentenced him to six years for sedition. He was freed in February 1924 on grounds of health. You can read about his trial in "Gandhi in the Circuit House".

## The trial

The police arrested many people. In all, 225 men were put on trial at the sessions court in Gorakhpur before Judge H. E. Holmes. They were charged with rioting and arson. Six died in police custody before the end.

The trial took about eight months. On 9 January 1923 the court gave its verdict. Two men were sentenced to two years in prison. Forty-seven were set free. 170 were sentenced to death.

The number shocked many people. M. N. Roy called the verdicts "legalised murder", and he urged a general strike.

The case went to the High Court at Allahabad. On 30 April 1923 it gave its final judgments. Nineteen men were sentenced to death. Fourteen were given life in prison. Others got eight years (nineteen men), five years (fifty-seven), three years (twenty) or two years (three). Thirty-eight were freed.

<aside class="note"><b>The totals</b>: One summary on the page says 99 people received prison terms. The list of the High Court's sentences adds up to 113.</aside>

The nineteen condemned men were hanged between 2 and 11 July 1923.

## After

The struggle did not end. Motilal Nehru and C. R. Das, who felt Gandhi had been wrong to stop, formed the Swaraj Party and rejected his leadership. Gandhi took up the fight again in the Salt Satyagraha of 1930 to 1934. One page says it showed his commitment to non-violence.

The Hindi writer Rambriksh Benipuri said that the non-cooperation movement had turned Indian society upside down. Others had a plainer view. The movement had not ended British rule. Many nationalists were discouraged, and felt it should not have been stopped over a few acts of violence.

Jawaharlal Nehru and the other leaders who sat in prison in 1922 had been taken in the arrests that began on 6 December 1921. By the end of that month the main leaders of the movement were in jail. So the news of Chauri Chaura reached them behind bars.

## Remembering both

The British authorities put up a memorial to the dead policemen in 1923. In 1973 a local association built a minaret near the lake. Later the government built a larger memorial with the names of the men who were hanged. It also built a library and a museum. A train named the Chauri Chaura Express runs between Gorakhpur and Kanpur.

## Why it matters

Chauri Chaura asked a hard question. If a movement is peaceful in its aim, who is to blame when part of a crowd turns violent? Gandhi took the blame on himself and ended the campaign. His critics said that was too much. Policemen died, and so did men who joined a protest. The families of both are part of the story.
