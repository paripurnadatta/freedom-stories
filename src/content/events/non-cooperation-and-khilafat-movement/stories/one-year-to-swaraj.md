On 1 August 1920 Bal Gangadhar Tilak died. On the same day the movement of non-cooperation was formally announced. One page says it carried Indian nationalism to the masses.

Its idea was simple. Indians were asked to withdraw from the institutions of the Raj: its schools, its courts, its police, its army and its civil service. They were to do it peacefully.

## Why 1920?

Two wrongs had angered people. The first was Jallianwala Bagh. On 13 April 1919, troops under Brigadier-General Reginald Dyer fired on a crowd in Amritsar. The crowd had gathered to protest arrests and to mark the Baisakhi festival. The Rowlatt Act, passed on 18 March 1919, had already taken away rights from people accused of sedition.

The second was the fate of Turkey. After the First World War the British planned to break up the Ottoman Empire. The Sultan of Turkey was also the caliph, the head of Sunni Muslims all over the world. Indian Muslims began the Khilafat movement to save the caliph's position. Gandhi joined the two causes. He and the Khilafat leaders agreed to pursue Khilafat and swaraj together. Leaders like Maulana Azad, Hakim Ajmal Khan and the Ali brothers stood with him.

<aside class="note"><b>Swaraj</b>: self-rule. <b>Sedition</b>: speech or writing that stirs up hatred of the government.</aside>

## Calcutta, September 1920

The Congress met in a special session at Calcutta in early September 1920. Lala Lajpat Rai was the president. Gandhi's plan was put to the delegates. The date is given as 4 September 1920.

Gandhi said that swaraj could be won within one year if the plan was carried out in full. C. R. Das did not agree, at least at first. He rejected the one-year promise.

<aside class="note"><b>Two dates</b>: One page says Gandhi launched the campaign on 4 September 1920. Another says it was formally announced on 1 August 1920, and that the Calcutta session was held on 4 September. Both agree on the Congress session in September.</aside>

## Nagpur, December 1920

At Nagpur in December the Congress made its decision. By now Das and Gandhi had settled most of their differences. Das himself moved the main resolution. The Congress now stated its aim: swaraj by peaceful and rightful means.

The party also changed how it worked. Anyone could join for four annas. There was a chain of committees, from villages to talukas to districts. The provincial committees were redrawn on the lines of language.

Not everyone agreed. Mohammad Ali Jinnah left the Congress. One page says he was confused by the new aim. Bipin Chandra Pal and Annie Besant also opposed the plan. The Muslim League criticised it too.

## What people were asked to do

The plan had many parts.

- Give up titles and honours given by the government.
- Keep away from the new legislative councils.
- Leave government schools and colleges.
- Lawyers were asked to leave the courts.
- Give up foreign cloth and wear khadi, spinning thread on the charkha.
- Close liquor shops by picketing them.
- Keep to Hindu–Muslim unity and to non-violence.

<aside class="note"><b>Khadi</b>: cloth spun and woven by hand in India. <b>Charkha</b>: the spinning wheel.</aside>

Gandhi also wished to end untouchability. And all of it, he said, must be peaceful.

## Who joined

The Rowlatt Act was never used, one page says, and was declared void a few years later. But it had done its work of stirring anger.

Many lawyers gave up their practice. C. R. Das, Motilal Nehru, Vallabhbhai Patel and C. Rajagopalachari are named among them.

New schools were started. On 29 October 1920 the foundation stone of Jamia Millia Islamia was laid at Aligarh. The stone was set by Maulana Mahmud Hasan. Its students and teachers wanted a Muslim university free of government influence. Bihar Vidyapeeth was founded too.

Some Muslims went further. A number of people left the North-West Frontier for Afghanistan, in protest. Money was raised to send to Mustafa Kemal's government in Ankara.

## A year of strikes and arrests

In November 1921 the Prince of Wales toured India. The Congress and Khilafat leaders called a general strike, called a hartal, wherever he went. In Bombay the day ended in a riot. You can read about it in "The days the city burned".

In December 1921 the government declared the Congress Volunteer Corps illegal and banned public meetings. On 6 December 1921 Jawaharlal Nehru and Motilal Nehru were arrested. By the end of the month the main leaders had been taken. In February 1922 Gandhi wrote to the Viceroy, Lord Reading, and proposed to begin civil disobedience at Bardoli.

<aside class="note"><b>Civil disobedience</b>: openly breaking a law as a protest.</aside>

## Not everyone agreed

Critics raised questions. The Khilafat cause was about the Sultan of Turkey. Some said it was not truly about India's freedom, and that its religious language hid a weak case. Jinnah warned that the secular fight for independence should not be mixed with a religious cause. Arab nationalists, meanwhile, saw the movement as a threat to their own freedom from Turkish rule.

The Treaty of Sèvres in 1920 had cut Palestine, Syria, Lebanon and Iraq away from the Ottoman Empire. The All India Khilafat Committee, based at Lucknow, published a Khilafat Manifesto the same year. It asked the British to protect the caliphate.

## The argument about cloth

Part of the case for the boycott was about cloth. The author Shashi Tharoor writes that India's cotton was bought cheaply, spun and woven in the mills of Manchester, and sold back to Indians. Heavy duties of 70 to 80 per cent fell on Indian cloth. One page says India's share of the world's textile trade fell by a quarter. British cotton exports rose from 60 million yards in 1830 to nearly a billion by 1870.

## Why it matters

Gandhi did not win swaraj in a year. But the movement carried the Congress to the villages and small towns for the first time. Anyone who paid four annas could be a member. Many nationalists then and later were also unhappy that it ended as it did, which the next stories tell.
