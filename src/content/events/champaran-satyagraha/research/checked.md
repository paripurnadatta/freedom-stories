# Source check — 25 October 2026

The supplied dossier contains four events, including Dandi. It was treated as a starting point, not independent proof. Its preserved copy is at src/content/events/champaran-satyagraha/research/source-dossier.pdf.

## Checks and qualifications

- Gandhi describes a police notice during his attempted village visit and a summons at Motihari; chapters 138–139. His account reports withdrawal of the case, not a judicial finding that all planters were guilty.

- Chapter 140 describes cross-questioning farmers and rejecting weak statements. Chapter 141 names volunteer helpers. Chapter 143 describes the later committee.

- Chapter 140 describes dismissing personal cooks and establishing a common vegetarian kitchen. Chapter 141 supplies the team context.

- Chapter 141 describes six village schools, local provision of buildings and food, and volunteer teachers. Chapter 142 says sustained staffing proved difficult.

- Chapter 142 recounts the unnamed woman at Bhitiharva who told Kasturba she owned only one sari. This is Gandhi’s recollection, not a recorded interview transcript.

- Chapter 143 gives Gandhi’s conditions for joining the committee and credits Lieutenant-Governor Edward Gait. Tinkathia ended through subsequent legislation, not merely the court appearance.

## 27 October 2026: Claude rewrite (all six stories written fresh)

Sources opened directly this session: Gandhi's Autobiography Part V, chapters 136 to 143 (mkgandhi.org);
Rajendra Prasad, *Satyagraha in Champaran* (full PDF, mkgandhi.org); Ramachandra Guha's Scroll.in article
(mkgandhi.org copy); Wikipedia "Champaran Satyagraha". The fact sheet was used as a map only.

Fact sheet checks
- Held up: the dates (9, 10, 11, 14, 15, 16, 18 April; 4 and 13 June; 3, 4 and 18 October 1917; 29 November
  1917; 20 February 1918), the court quotes, the money figures, the school volunteers, the medicines, the sari
  quotation, the three conditions, the 20 and 26 per cent rent cuts and the one-quarter tawan refund.
- Corrected: the sheet says no source gives the crowd size on 18 April. Rajendra Prasad's book does: several
  thousand tenants in the court compound and about 2,000 following Gandhi to the courtroom, breaking the glass
  of the doors. Used, in a note, with his name on it.

Disagreements shown in "Two versions" notes
- Muzaffarpur arrival: 10 p.m. (Prasad) vs midnight (Gandhi).
- Kripalani and Gandhi: "had never met before" (Prasad) vs first met at Santiniketan in 1915 (Guha).
- How the order was served on 16 April: a messenger and a hired carriage (Gandhi) vs a sub-inspector on a
  bicycle, a bullock cart, an ekka, and a Deputy Superintendent with a tamtam (Prasad).
- When the summons came: straight away (Gandhi) vs evening of 17 April (Prasad).
- Who found the inquiry house: Brajkishore Prasad (Gandhi) vs Ramadayal Prasad of the Sahu family (Prasad).
- Committee size: seven members, four ICS (Guha) vs chairman plus five members and a secretary (the
  communique printed by Prasad).
- Report date: signed 3 October, submitted 4 October (Prasad) vs submitted 3 October (Guha).

Also noted, not used
- Distance to Jasaulipatti: Gandhi says about five miles from Motihari; Prasad says they had gone about nine
  miles by noon, to Chandrahia. Not needed in the story.
- Wikipedia's infobox dates the satyagraha "10 April - May 1917". Not used; the stories follow the dated
  sequence in Prasad.
- Prasad's book is a participant's history and very admiring of Gandhi (e.g. "tears streaming down the cheeks").
  Its feelings and praise were left out; only its dated facts were used.

Paintings: the six existing paintings fit the six rewritten stories (same slugs). One small mismatch: the
"order to leave" painting shows a uniformed officer handing Gandhi the order beside the elephant; the sources
say the order was handed over after he left the elephant, on the road back. Acceptable unless the owner wants it redone.
