This story comes from Gandhi himself. He told it at many meetings, and later wrote it down in his autobiography. He does not give the woman's name, or the day it happened.

## Teaching cleanliness

In 1917 Gandhi and his helpers opened schools in six villages of Champaran. The volunteers who ran them did more than teach children. They also tried to make the villages cleaner and healthier.

Gandhi thought that the village women should be approached by women. So the women volunteers did that work. One of them was his wife, Kasturba. She ran the school at Bhitiharva with a volunteer named Soman.

## A question

One day Gandhi visited a smaller village near Bhitiharva. He noticed that some of the women were wearing very dirty clothes.

He asked Kasturba to find out why they did not wash their clothes. Kasturba went and spoke to them.

## The answer

One of the women took Kasturba into her hut. Gandhi wrote down what she said:

"Look now, there is no box or cupboard here containing other clothes. The sari I am wearing is the only one I have. How am I to wash it?"

If she washed her only sari, she would have nothing to wear while it dried.

Then she made an offer. If Mahatmaji would get her another sari, she would bathe and put on clean clothes every day.

<aside class="note"><b>Mahatmaji</b>: a respectful name for Gandhi. "Mahatma" means "great soul", and "ji" is added to show respect.</aside>

## Not the only one

The woman's hut was not unusual. Gandhi wrote that it was a type found in many Indian villages. In countless cottages, he said, people lived without any furniture and without a change of clothes.

Gandhi had been to poor homes in Bihar before. On 14 April 1917, before he reached Champaran, he visited a village near Muzaffarpur. He went into the huts of poor families and talked to the women and children. As he left, according to Rajendra Prasad's book, he said India would get swaraj only when the lives of such people improved.

<aside class="note"><b>Swaraj</b>: self-rule, or freedom.</aside>

## Why it matters

The volunteers had come to teach cleanliness. But one woman showed them that cleanliness needs more than good advice. It needs something to change into. Advice is easy to give. A second sari costs money that many families did not have.

The teacher had come to give a lesson. Instead, she was given one.
