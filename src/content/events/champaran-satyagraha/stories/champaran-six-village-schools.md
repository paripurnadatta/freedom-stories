Gandhi needed teachers in Champaran. The volunteers he found did not all speak the same language. Some knew Gujarati. Others knew Marathi. They were being asked to work with children in Hindi-speaking villages.

The language difficulty worried the new teachers. Gandhi told them that the first lessons could be simple. Children could learn numbers. They could also learn habits of cleanliness. The work did not have to begin with difficult books.

The plan was to open schools in six villages. Villagers would provide a building and food for the teachers. Gandhi's group would find people willing to teach. Families who had little spare money could still contribute grain and help.

Volunteers answered the call. Avantikabai Gokhale and Anandibai Vaishampayan came. So did Soman and Pundalik. Gandhi asked several members of his own circle to help, including Kasturba. Other young workers joined the effort.

Teaching was only part of the task. Many villages needed help with sanitation. Dr Dev came to assist with health and cleanliness. Teachers were expected to support that work too. A school could become a place from which useful village service spread.

<aside class="note"><b>Sanitation</b>: Keeping water, streets, homes, and waste arrangements clean to protect health.</aside>

Gandhi gave the volunteers an important limit. They should not become investigators of every complaint against a planter. People with those grievances should be sent to him. Teachers needed to keep their attention on their own work.

At Bhitiharva, the school met in a building made with bamboo and grass. One night it burned down. Gandhi suspected that someone had set it alight. His account does not establish who did it.

Soman helped arrange a stronger replacement. A brick building took the place of the burned shelter. The school did not have to end with the loss of its first roof.

<aside class="note"><b>What is known</b>: Gandhi's account reports the fire and rebuilding. It does not prove who caused the fire.</aside>

The wider school programme was harder to keep going. Volunteers could not all remain in Champaran. Gandhi later wrote that the work did not become as permanent as he had hoped.

That unfinished ending belongs in the story too. Opening a school needed willing helpers and a room. Keeping it open needed people who could stay. The children needed lessons after the excitement of the indigo inquiry had passed.
