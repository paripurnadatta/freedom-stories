# Source check — 26 October 2026

This check covers the facts in `chatgpt-report-2026-10-26.md`. Nothing was taken from memory.
Every source named here was opened in this session; the full text of the Hunter report and the
Hansard debate were read from the saved copies, not from a summary.

## What held up

- The Hunter report (Cmd. 681) is the spine of the pack. The account of 13 April is in chapter III,
  paragraphs 35 to 41. The proclamation, the force, the entrances, the crowd, the ten minutes and
  the 1,650 rounds are all in that chapter in those words or close to them. The figure of 379 dead
  and about three times that many wounded is in paragraph 38.
- The "grave error" sentence is the majority's own wording, chapter III paragraph 40. It is safe to
  quote exactly.
- The minority report is printed in the same volume and is much harder on Dyer. Both versions are in
  one book, so the pack can quote both without needing two publishers.
- Hansard for 8 July 1920 was read in full. The division was Ayes 37, Noes 247; the Government won.
  Churchill's line about "a monstrous event" and Montagu's "doctrine of terrorism" are exact.
- The Congress inquiry did come first, on 20 February 1920, and the Hunter report followed on 8 March
  1920. The letter of transmittal in the report is dated Agra, 8 March 1920.
- The crawling order, the whipping triangle and the two pickets are in chapter XI paragraph 18 of the
  Hunter report, with the report's own verdict in the same paragraph.
- Tagore's knighthood and its renunciation are recorded in the 1922 Encyclopædia Britannica entry on
  Wikisource, which is a period source and not a modern retelling.
- Gandhi's comparison of the crawling to the massacre is in his autobiography, volume 2, part 5,
  chapter 34, on Wikisource. Exact wording taken from that page.

## What could not be checked, or does not agree

- **Date of the crawling order.** The Hunter report says the order was "issued on the 10th April,
  i.e., 9 days after the assault". That is impossible, because 10 April was the day of the assault.
  The Tribune says Dyer saw the injured Miss Sherwood on 19 April and gave the order then, and that
  it was withdrawn on 24 April. The Hunter report says it was withdrawn on 26 April. The pack tells
  the writer to use 19 April and to carry a "Two versions" note. This is a real disagreement, not a
  typing slip in one copy.
- **Time Dyer heard of the meeting.** Chapter III paragraph 36 says about 1 o'clock; the minority
  report says 12.40. The pack tells the writer to say "early in the afternoon".
- **Size of the crowd.** Dyer said 6,000. The majority report says 10,000 to 20,000. The minority
  report says 15,000 to 20,000. Only the range should be used.
- **The well.** The figure of 120 bodies taken from the well rests on the plaque at the site, quoted
  by Wikipedia. The Hunter report does not mention the well at all. The pack marks it single-source
  and tells the writer to say "a plaque at the site says".
- **Malaviya's count of the dead children.** The figure of 42 boys, the youngest seven months old, is
  in Wikipedia only. The Hunter report gives 337 men, 41 boys and a six-week-old baby. The two counts
  are close but not the same. The pack tells the writer to say "about 40 boys" and cite the
  commission.
- **The £26,000 Morning Post fund.** The Morning Post fund is in the Tribune. The sum is not in any
  page opened here. Left out of the pack entirely.
- **Dyer's "laughed at me ... fool of myself" answer.** This is in Wikipedia as a quotation from his
  evidence, but the minutes of evidence were not opened. Marked UNVERIFIED. The writer may paraphrase
  it and attribute it to his evidence, but must not print it in quotation marks.
- **Tagore's letter.** The wording of the letter comes through Wikipedia only. The letter itself was
  not found on Wikisource or in a scanned collection in this session. The famous "shorn of all special
  distinctions" sentence is therefore marked single-source. A story may describe what the letter said
  without quoting it, or quote it and name Wikipedia as the carrier. Best is to paraphrase and quote
  only the Britannica sentence about resigning the title.
- **Udham Singh in the Bagh.** That he was present on 13 April is his own account. No document proves
  it. The pack marks it a "Two versions" note.
- **The date of the Jallianwala Bagh meeting notice.** The Hunter report quotes evidence that the
  meeting was announced on 12 April at the Hindu Sabha School. That is one witness speaking inside a
  quotation; it is not corroborated elsewhere in the pack.
