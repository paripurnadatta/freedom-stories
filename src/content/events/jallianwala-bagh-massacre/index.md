## Research notes

The old note was one line: "British troops fired on a large gathering; event transformed
nationalist politics." Everything below replaces it.

- **What happened.** On Sunday 13 April 1919, Baisakhi day, a crowd gathered in the Jallianwala
  Bagh, a walled piece of unused ground in Amritsar. Brigadier-General R. E. H. Dyer entered with
  about 50 riflemen and 40 men with kukris, blocked the main entrance, and opened fire without any
  warning to disperse. The firing lasted about ten minutes and 1,650 rounds were fired. Some 379
  people were killed and about 1,200 wounded (Hunter report). The government first said about 200.
- **Why the crowd was there.** To protest the arrest and deportation of Dr Saif-ud-Din Kitchlew and
  Dr Satyapal two days earlier, and to hear the leaders who were left. A meeting had been announced
  for that afternoon; Dyer had proclaimed a ban on gatherings that same morning.
- **What followed.** Martial law in Amritsar district with effect from 15 April. The crawling order.
  Censorship, and a first death toll of about 200 in the newspapers. Congress's own inquiry reported
  on 20 February 1920; the Hunter Committee reported on 8 March 1920. The Commons debated it on
  8 July 1920 and voted 247 to 37 for the Government. Dyer was removed from his appointment and
  reverted to half pay. Nobody was prosecuted.
- **What is disputed.** The death toll (379 official; about 700 by Vishwa Nath Datta; about 1,000 by
  the Congress inquiry). The date of the crawling order (Hunter says 10 April, which cannot be right;
  the Tribune says 19 April, withdrawn 24 April; Hunter says withdrawn 26 April). The number of
  bodies in the well (120, from the plaque at the site only).
