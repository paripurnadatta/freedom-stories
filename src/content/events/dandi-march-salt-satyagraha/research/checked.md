# Source check — 25 October 2026

The supplied dossier contains four events, including Dandi. It was treated as a starting point, not independent proof. Its preserved copy is at src/content/events/champaran-satyagraha/research/source-dossier.pdf.

## Checks and qualifications

- Letter to Irwin, 2 March 1930, CWMG XLIII pp. 2–8: Gandhi linked salt to poverty, offered discussion and opposed harm to English people. The letter envisaged 11 March; the march actually began on 12 March.

- Bhatgam speech, 29 March 1930: Gandhi criticised expensive milk, waste and a labourer carrying a heavy lamp. He applied the campaign’s criticism of official expense to its own conduct.

- Aslali speech, 12 March 1930: salt was a basic necessity and the campaign concerned public control over taxation. Avoid repeating the speech’s detailed livestock arithmetic as independently audited statistics.

- Bareja speech, 13 March 1930: reported zeroes concerned local khadi and spinning activity. Gandhi asked for practical village work. The problematic translated caste phrase is omitted.

- Navagam speech, 13 March 1930: coerced resignations should be withdrawn and volunteers should protect freedom of choice. A resignation itself did not establish sincere consent.

- March began 12 March; Dandi reached 5 April; salt-law breach 6 April 1930. The act began further civil disobedience and did not repeal the salt law. No exact clock time is asserted.

## Rewrite of 27 October 2026 (by Claude, from sources opened that day)

All six stories were written fresh from the Sevagram speech and letter pages, the Irwin letter in CWMG XLIII and the mkgandhi.org chronology, with Wikipedia as second source where noted. The old stories were not used.

- Held up: the Irwin letter quotations, the salary comparison, Reynolds as messenger, the Aslali, Bareja, Navagam and Bhatgam speeches as quoted, 6.30 a.m. start with 78 marchers, 8.30 a.m. on 6 April.
- Two versions, shown in notes: the Viceroy's reply (CWMG: short regret; Wikipedia: ignored and "bread and stone"); salt sale (Rs 525 per mkgandhi, 1,600 rupees per Wikipedia).
- The government "social boycott" remark and Gandhi's "five rupees a month" line come from the chronology, from other Kheda halts, not the Navagam speech itself. The story says so.
- Left out: the "five million people at 5,000 meetings" figure, any crowd size at Dandi, the Bareja caste sentence, the old 385 km figure (not found in the pages opened).
- Not checked: the Nadiad speech text itself and the press talk of 14 March were not used in stories.
