Village officials at Navagam announced their resignations. Gandhi congratulated them. Then he added a warning that might have surprised some listeners. If anyone had been forced to resign, that person should take the resignation back.

The marchers reached Navagam during the journey to Dandi. Gandhi was asking people to withdraw support from a government they believed treated them unfairly. For village officers, giving up a post was one way to do that.

Such a choice had a cost. A post brought income and a place in local government. A public promise to leave it could also bring pressure from neighbours. Gandhi wanted to know whether the promise belonged to the person making it.

He said he would protect anyone being forced to resign. The campaign rested on truth. It should not win through help obtained in an unworthy way. An honest admission of doubt was better than a false display of courage.

<aside class="note"><b>Resignation</b>: A formal decision to leave a job or official position.</aside>

Gandhi knew the Kheda district from earlier work. In his speech, he recalled travelling through its villages on foot. The Salt March was bringing him through places connected with previous struggles.

Patel was already in prison. Gandhi spoke about his arrest and challenged the villagers to act for themselves. A leader behind bars could not make their decisions for them. Their own work had to show their strength.

At Nadiad, Gandhi again urged government servants to leave their jobs. He also called for volunteers prepared to face imprisonment. The demand was serious, and its seriousness made the condition at Navagam important.

He praised both kinds of honest courage. Someone could keep a resignation freely offered. Someone could also admit truthfully that they lacked the strength to carry it through. What he rejected was a promise made under pressure and then hidden behind brave words.

The speech does not prove that every resignation along the route was free of pressure. It records the rule Gandhi told this gathering to follow. Public enthusiasm could not remove a person's right to decide.

The march asked people to resist a powerful government. At Navagam, Gandhi reminded its supporters that they must also resist the temptation to force their own neighbours.
