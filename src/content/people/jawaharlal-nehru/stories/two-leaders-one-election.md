Two friends can want the same thing, and still argue about how to get it.

In 1939, Jawaharlal Nehru and Subhas Chandra Bose were both leaders in the Congress. Both wanted India free. They did not agree about the Congress presidency.

## The election

Bose was already the president of the Congress. In January 1939, he stood for election again. Nehru's statements show that a contest followed.

Nehru wrote about this from Almora on 26 January 1939. His statement appeared in *The Hindu* the next day.

He said he had hoped Maulana Abul Kalam Azad would stand. Azad did not agree. About Bose, Nehru wrote: "I was equally clear that Subhas Babu should not stand."

He gave a reason that was about himself and Bose: "I felt that his and my capacity for effective work would be lessened by holding this office at this stage."

Nehru said he saw no clash of ideas in this election. In his words: "Personally I do not see what principles or programmes are at stake in this election."

He also feared that a contest would hurt. He wrote, "Whoever wins, the federation loses." He asked everyone to remember "the high dignity of our cause".

## What Bose said

The Selected Works editors printed a footnote from a statement by Bose. He wrote that "the presidential election in India should be fought on the basis of definite problems and programmes".

He added: "An election contest in these circumstances may not be an undesirable thing."

So Bose thought an election was healthy, like those in free countries. And he linked it to the coming year, with "increasing international tension and the prospective fight over federation".

The editors quote Bose's statements in footnotes. One gives his date as 21 January 1939, and another gives 24 January. We have not seen Bose's own text. We use only what they printed.

<aside class="note"><b>Federation</b>: a plan from the British for India to be a union of provinces and princely states. Some Congress leaders opposed it.</aside>

## After the vote

On 22 February 1939, Nehru wrote again, from Wardha. The Selected Works prints this second statement too.

He said: "I was opposed to Subhas Babu's re-election as I knew the consequences that would flow from it." He added that his reasons "had nothing to do with right or left."

He objected to some of Bose's remarks in the campaign about members of the Working Committee. These remarks, he said, "astonished and pained" him.

He also said he had asked Bose to explain his policies in writing, since he disagreed with him on "some important matters".

And he said what he wanted: "We must present a united front and subordinate our individual opinions where these tended to impair that front."

Nehru also noted Bose's "sudden and regrettable illness". He did not say more about it.

## Whose view?

These pages are Nehru's own statements. We do not have Bose's full replies. So this story cannot say who was right.

Nehru feared a divided Congress. Bose believed in open choice and clear programmes. Each side had a reason.

## Why it matters

When people who want the same thing disagree, the quarrel can be harder than a fight with an enemy.

Nehru's two statements show a leader trying to explain his side without calling the other a traitor. We should read them the same way.
