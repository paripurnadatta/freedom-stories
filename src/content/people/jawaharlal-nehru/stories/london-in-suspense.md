What does it feel like when a whole city thinks a war may start tomorrow?

Jawaharlal Nehru was in London in the last days of September 1938. He wrote a diary of what he saw. It is part of a book he published in 1941, and we can read it.

## Why was an Indian leader in Europe?

In 1938, Nehru was travelling in Europe. He was not there as a government person. He spoke for the Indian National Congress.

On 23 and 24 July 1938, he spoke in Paris at a peace conference. It was about the bombing of towns in Spain and China. The book prints his speech.

In it, he said that he had "recently visited Barcelona". He had seen "its ruined buildings" and bombs falling. The picture was "imprinted in my heart."

He had also spent time in Czechoslovakia. He met both Czechs and Germans there. He admired how calm they stayed "in face of grave danger".

a country in central Europe. Today it is two countries, Czechia and Slovakia.

A letter from him printed in the same book is dated 8 September 1938, from 27 St James's Street in London. So he was in London by then.

## What the crisis was

In 1938, Adolf Hitler's Germany demanded part of Czechoslovakia called the Sudetenland. Britain and France were afraid of a big war. The leaders talked for weeks.

a border area of Czechoslovakia. It had about three million people, mostly ethnic Germans.

## 28 September 1938

Nehru's diary entry is headed "September 28, 1938". It begins with a radio talk by the British Prime Minister, Neville Chamberlain. Nehru "listened in".

<aside class="note"><b>Prime Minister</b>: the head of the British government.</aside>

Wikipedia dates that talk 27 September 1938. In it, Chamberlain spoke of "a quarrel in a far-away country between people of whom we know nothing." Nehru's entry seems to cover two days. It describes the talk and then the next day in Parliament.

Nehru thought the talk was short and weak. It lasted "hardly eight minutes." He felt it did not praise the Czech people. He wrote, "I felt depressed, and my heart was heavy within me."

## The city waits

Then he describes London. Trenches were being dug in parks and open spaces. Anti-aircraft guns were being put up.

Posters about air-raid precautions were "on every hoarding". In many depots, men and women tried on gas masks.

People went about their business. But their faces "were strained and full of apprehension," Nehru wrote.

There was "sorrow in many homes as their loved ones were summoned to put themselves in readiness for the coming war."

## The gas mask

Friends told him to get a gas mask. He did not like the idea. He said that he would have to look like "a beast", with a snout.

He did not believe masks were very useful. He thought they mainly gave confidence to the people who wore them.

But he was curious. So he went to an air-raid depot. He was fitted for a mask and later fetched it.

## In Parliament

The next day, Nehru went to the Houses of Parliament. The streets were crowded. The visitors' galleries were "overfull". Many Lords were there. He also saw the diplomats' gallery with the Czechoslovak Minister, Mr. Masaryk.

Chamberlain spoke for an hour. In Nehru's view, the Prime Minister "was not a man of destiny". He also thought Chamberlain praised Hitler too much and did not speak of high principles.

Then came news. Chamberlain said that he was going to Munich the next day. Mussolini of Italy and Daladier of France would be there too. And Hitler had agreed to wait twenty-four hours before calling up his army.

Nehru saw the effect. "Relief appeared in all the faces." He wrote: "It was good that war had been pushed off, even though this might be only for a day or two longer."

But then he asked what would happen to Czechoslovakia. He feared that "another betrayal" was coming.

## Two views of Munich

The Munich conference was held on 29 and 30 September 1938. Hitler, Chamberlain, Mussolini and Daladier took part. Czechoslovakia was not invited. The Sudetenland was handed to Germany.

Many people were glad. Chamberlain was greeted as a hero in Britain. Polls in Britain, France and the United States showed that most people supported the deal. Daladier was cheered in Paris.

But some people spoke against it from the start. Clement Attlee's Labour Party opposed the agreement. On 5 October 1938, Winston Churchill called it "a total and unmitigated defeat". And on 15 March 1939, Germany took the rest of Czechoslovakia.

So Nehru's fear was shared by some people and not by others. Whether the deal was wise is something people still argue about.

## What Nehru wanted for India

Nehru did not only write about Europe. He linked it to India. He said that India should not follow Britain into a war unasked. "The people of India have no intention of submitting to any foreign decision on war," he wrote.

He added that India would gladly stand for freedom and democracy. But only free countries, he wrote, could help freedom elsewhere.

His view was strong. He was talking about a government that ruled his own country. These are his words as his opinion. British replies from that time were not opened for this story.

## Why it matters

Nehru's diary shows an Indian leader in the middle of a world story. He saw trenches in London and ruins in Barcelona. He used what he saw to ask about India's own freedom.

It also shows how a person can feel two things at once. He was glad war was pushed off for a day. And he was afraid of the price.
