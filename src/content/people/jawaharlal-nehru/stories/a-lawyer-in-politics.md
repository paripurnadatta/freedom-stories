What would make a lawyer who had studied in England walk with farmers?

This story follows Jawaharlal Nehru from his birth to his first arrests, using two short official records.

## A boy at home

Jawaharlal Nehru was born in Allahabad on 14 November 1889. The Nehru Memorial Fund says he was the son of Motilal and Swaruprani Nehru.

The Prime Minister's Office says: "He received his early education at home under private tutors."

## Harrow and Cambridge

He then went to England. The Memorial Fund lists his schools in order.

- 1905 to 1907: Harrow School.
- 1907 to 1910: Trinity College, Cambridge.
- 1910 to 1912: the Inner Temple, in London.

At Cambridge, the Prime Minister's Office says, he "took his tripos in Natural Sciences". It adds that he was later "called to the Bar from Inner Temple".

<aside class="note"><b>Tripos</b>: the final exams at Cambridge. <b>Called to the Bar</b>: allowed to work as a barrister, a type of lawyer.</aside>

## Back home

In 1912 he came back. The Memorial Fund says he returned to India as a lawyer in the Allahabad High Court. The Prime Minister's Office says he "plunged straight into politics".

It says he attended the Bankipore Congress as a delegate.

In 1916 something important happened. The office says: "In 1916 he had his first meeting with Mahatma Gandhi and felt immensely inspired by him." In the same year, on 8 February, he married Kamala Kaul. The next year, on 19 November 1917, their daughter Indira was born.

## Home Rule and farmers

In 1919, he became secretary of the Home Rule League in Allahabad.

<aside class="note"><b>Home Rule League</b>: a group that asked for Indians to rule India's own affairs.</aside>

In 1920, he organised something new. The Prime Minister's Office says, "He organised the first Kisan March in Pratapgarh District of Uttar Pradesh in 1920."

"Kisan" means farmer. We do not have details about the march, such as how many people came or what they asked for. We also do not know how much of the work was Nehru's and how much was done by local farmers and leaders.

## The first jail

In 1921, Nehru was arrested. The Memorial Fund says the date was 6 December. The reason was that he took part in the Volunteer Movement and urged a boycott of the visit of the Prince of Wales.

He was freed on 3 March 1922. On 11 May 1922 he was arrested again, this time for picketing cloth shops. In 1923 he was released on 26 January. In September 1923 he was arrested again for defying an order that banned him from entering Nabha State. He was freed on 6 October.

<aside class="note"><b>Picketing</b>: standing outside a shop, peacefully, to ask people not to buy there.</aside>

## What we do not know

Both of our sources are short official records. They tell us what happened, but not what he felt. So we cannot say why he chose the farmers, or what he thought about on the road in Pratapgarh.

## Why it matters

Nehru was taught by private tutors and educated in England. Within eight years of coming home, he was organising farmers and going to jail.

How and why he made that choice is something his own writing can tell us more about.
