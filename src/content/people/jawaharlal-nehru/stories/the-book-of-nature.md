Have you ever picked up a smooth, round pebble and wondered where it came from?

Jawaharlal Nehru asked his young daughter to wonder about that too.

## A father and a daughter

Indira was born on 19 November 1917. The Nehru Memorial Fund's chronology gives that date. In 1928 she was about ten.

The first letter in a famous collection says that the two were living apart. In the letter, Nehru writes "Now that you are at Mussoorie and I am in Allahabad". The letters were later collected in a book called *Letters from a Father to His Daughter*.

<aside class="note"><b>Mussoorie</b>: a hill town in the mountains, now in Uttarakhand.</aside>

## How the letter begins

The first letter is called "The Book of Nature". It begins: "When you and I are together you often ask me questions about many things and I try to answer them."

So the letters began as a way to keep answering her questions from a distance.

## Stones as pages

In the letter, Nehru tells her that stones can teach us something. He wrote: "Every little stone that you see lying in the road or on the mountain side may be a little page in nature's book".

He then explains how a pebble gets its shape.

"If you break a big rock into small bits, each bit is rough and has corners and rough edges."

But a pebble in a river changes. He writes: "And all the while it rolled at the bottom of the river and its edges were worn away".

So a smooth stone is a sign of a very long story. The river has been rolling it for a very long time.

## Look for yourself

He also gave her some advice about learning. He wrote that "the real way to understand this story is not merely to read about it in other people's books".

He hoped she would learn by looking at the world itself. In his words: "You will I hope soon begin to learn how to read this story from the rocks and mountains."

## Do not mix up two sets of letters

There were two different sets of letters. The short ones from 1928 are the ones in this story.

Nehru also wrote a different, longer set of letters to her later, from prison. We have not read those, so we do not tell that story here.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: One editor's note in the Selected Works says the early letters were written from Mussoorie. But Nehru's own first letter says Indira was at Mussoorie and he was at Allahabad. We follow the letter.</aside>

## Why it matters

Many of the best teachers begin with a question. Nehru's first answer to a girl was to send her outside, to look at a stone.

A letter like this is also a small gift. Even when a parent is away, they can still teach and keep you company.
