How do you speak about a happy day and a sad day at the same time?

Jawaharlal Nehru had to do exactly that in the summer of 1947. India was about to become free. But it was also about to be divided.

## The British plan

On 3 June 1947, the British government announced a plan. India would become free. But it would be divided into two countries. The new country of Pakistan would be made from areas where many Muslims lived.

The Nehru Memorial Fund's chronology says Nehru broadcast his acceptance of the partition proposals that same day, 3 June.

<aside class="note"><b>Partition</b>: the division of British India into two countries, India and Pakistan.</aside>

Ten days later, on 13 June, the All India Congress Committee, the Congress's large governing body, met in Delhi. Its resolution said it "accepts the proposals embodied in the announcement of June 3".

It also said: "However much this may be regretted, the A.I.C.C. accepts this possibility, in the circumstances now prevailing."

And it said: "The Congress has consistently upheld that the unity of India must be maintained."

## Nehru explains

On 15 June 1947, Nehru spoke to the same committee in New Delhi. The speech was printed in a newspaper, the *National Herald*, the next day.

He began with these words: "India's heart has been broken but her essential unity has not been destroyed."

Then he explained why the Congress had said yes. He gave several reasons.

First, he spoke of the riots. He said that "Homesteads burnt, women and children murdered" had happened in many places. He said the administration had broken down in some areas. He said the Congress had to find a way to stop the killing.

Second, he said no one should be forced to stay. His words were: "it is not possible to coerce even with swords unwilling parts to remain under the Indian domain."

Third, he said the Congress was not giving in. He said, "there is no question of any surrender to the Muslim League."

He said partition was "better than murder of innocent citizens". And he said the wound "must be healed".

He called himself "sad and bitter".

<aside class="note"><b>Muslim League</b>: the political party that asked for a separate country for Muslims. Its leader was Muhammad Ali Jinnah.</aside>

## Two sides

This story gives Nehru's side, in his own words. It does not give the League's side or the views of other Indians, such as people who thought partition was wrong. We did not open pages for those in this session. Other stories can do that.

The page also shows that some of the argument was about a hard question. Can a country keep people together by force? Nehru said no. Others had other answers.

## A pledge

Two months later, at midnight on 14 August 1947, Nehru spoke in the Constituent Assembly. This was the body that was also writing India's Constitution. The page says the speech is from the Assembly's official reports.

He started a resolution. It asked all members to take a pledge after the last stroke of midnight. Members who were not there that night were to take it at their next meeting.

His speech began: "Long years ago we made a tryst with destiny, and now the time comes when we shall redeem our pledge."

<aside class="note"><b>Tryst</b>: a promise to meet someone at a set time and place. Nehru used it for a promise India had made to itself.</aside>

Then came the lines that are still remembered: "At the stroke of the midnight hour, when the world sleeps, India will awake to life and freedom."

He added: "when we step out from the old to the new, when an age ends, and when the soul of a nation, long suppressed, finds utterance."

## Work still to do

The speech was not only about joy. Nehru also spoke about pain. He said, "Some of those pains continue even now."

He said work lay ahead: "The service of India means the service of the millions who suffer."

He spoke of "the greatest man of our generation", whose ambition was "to wipe every tear from every eye". He added that "as long as there are tears and suffering, so long our work will not be over."

At the end, he said: "We have to build the noble mansion of free India where all her children may dwell."

The next day, 15 August, Nehru became the first Prime Minister of free India. The Nehru Memorial Fund's chronology gives that date.

## A note about the dates

One page on the Nehru archive is titled "3 June 1947". But when we opened it, it was not a speech from 3 June. It was a Congress resolution dated 13 June 1947, answering the British statement of 3 June. We use it for what it is.

## Why it matters

The same man spoke at both of these meetings in the same summer. One was sad, and one was hopeful. He said both of them honestly.

A free country was born on a day of joy for many, and sorrow for many. A good story about that day leaves room for both.
