Tilak died in Bombay on 1 August 1920. He was 64. Wikipedia gives the date and his age. The sources we opened do not say what caused his death.

## The house in Bombay

Two articles on Live History India describe where he died. They name a house called Sardar Griha, near Crawford Market. One article says he died in a guest room. The other says it was a room on the fourth floor.

Both articles say that this was also the day Gandhi began a movement against British rule. One calls it the Non-Cooperation Movement. The other calls it the civil disobedience movement. The two articles name it differently.

## The funeral

The author of the first article, Sandeep Dahisarkar, writes that "official estimates" counted about two lakh people, that is 200,000, on the streets for the funeral. The second article says the city had never seen so many people come out together. Neither article gives the source of the number.

The Bombay Police allowed the cremation at Chowpatty, on the sea. Dahisarkar writes that the police did so on one condition: that no memorial be built on the spot.

## The memorial

People wanted a memorial anyway. The article says the city already had statues of Ranade, put up in 1913, and Gokhale, in 1922. It says that in 1925 the city's council, the BMC, accepted the plan for a Tilak memorial, after pressure from Vithalbhai Patel and K. F. Nariman. A committee was set up on 1 August 1925, with Sarojini Naidu as its head. The Governor of Bombay gave leave in 1926, and the money came from the public.

The sculptor was Raghunath Krishna Phadke. The article says he had eighteen months and was paid Rs 19,000. The statue was uncovered on 1 August 1933, thirteen years after Tilak's death. The article says an iron box was buried thirty feet below, holding some of Tilak's belongings and a short life story. It adds that this makes the spot a samadhi, a memorial place.

## Other memories

Wikipedia lists later honours. The Lokmanya Tilak Smarak Trust was founded in 1920. In 1956 Nehru uncovered a portrait of Tilak in the Central Hall of Parliament. In 2007 a coin was issued to mark 150 years since his birth.

In 1954, Nehru had written a note about a plaque at Mandalay, where Tilak had been held. He wanted only a tablet. The Nehru Archive keeps the note.

## Why it matters

A leader's death can show what people thought of him. If the figure of two lakh is right, a very large crowd came to Chowpatty. The statue followed only after years of asking, and after the police had set the condition that no memorial be built.
