In 1908 Tilak stood in a courtroom in Bombay for the second time in his life. Eleven years earlier he had gone to jail for sedition. Now the charge was the same, and the sentence would be far longer.

## The bomb

On 30 April 1908, two young men, Prafulla Chaki and Khudiram Bose, threw a bomb at a carriage in Muzaffarpur, in Bihar. They meant to kill a British judge named Douglas Kingsford. He was not in the carriage. Two women were killed instead.

Tilak wrote about it in *Kesari*. The Bar & Bench column dates two articles, 12 May and 9 June 1908. Wikipedia says that the paper defended the young men and called for self-rule at once. The government charged him with sedition for the two articles.

## The date of the arrest

When was he arrested? One page from the Sankalp India Foundation says 24 June 1908. Maps of India says 3 July 1908. The Sankalp page also names one article behind the charge: "The Country's Misfortune."

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: We found two different arrest dates, 24 June and 3 July 1908, and neither source shows its proof. The trial itself began on 13 July, so both dates come before it.</aside>

## Eight days in court

The trial was held in the Bombay High Court. The Bar & Bench column says it lasted eight days, from 13 to 22 July 1908. The judge was Justice Dinshaw Davar. He had once been Tilak's own lawyer.

Wikipedia dates the trial to 1909, and the same Wikipedia article says Jinnah was Tilak's lawyer. The Bar & Bench column gives 1908. It also says Tilak's defence was led by two barristers from Calcutta, Pugh and Garth, and that Jinnah was linked with the case "at some stage". Dates from 1908 appear in the Nehru Archive too, so we use them.

The jury had nine members. Seven were Englishmen and two were Indians. The seven voted guilty. The two voted not guilty.

Justice Davar called Tilak's articles "seething with sedition." He told the jury that they could ask whether talking of patriotism in the same breath as bombs tended to make people hate the government.

## The sentence

The judge sentenced Tilak to six years. He would serve them in Mandalay, in Burma. Wikipedia adds a fine of Rs 1,000.

The Bar & Bench column gives Tilak's words to the court: "All that I wish to say is that in spite of the verdict of the Jury I still maintain that I am innocent."

## After the trial

Jinnah wrote a note against the Bombay Bar's plan for a dinner to honour Justice Davar after he was knighted. The note called the plan shameful. When the judge asked him why, Jinnah said he believed it was the truth. This comes from the opinion column, which names other writers as its sources.

## Why it matters

The seven English jurors voted one way. The two Indian jurors voted the other. The same had happened in 1897, when the three Indian jurors voted for Tilak and lost. We do not know why each juror voted as he did.
