# Tilak: what was checked (22 October 2026)

This note is private and never published. The stories were written fresh from sources opened in this session. Nothing was copied from the ChatGPT dossier.

## What held up (two or more sources I opened)

- Birth 23 July 1856, Ratnagiri; death 1 August 1920, Bombay (Wikipedia; Tribune fact file; Live History India).
- New English School opened 2 January 1880 with 35 pupils, 336 the next year (Pradhan, National Book Trust). Deccan Education Society 24 October 1884; Fergusson College 2 January 1885 (Pradhan; Wikipedia gives 1884/1885).
- 1897 trial: Justice Strachey, jury of six Europeans and three Indians, 6 to 3 guilty, 18 months (Indian History Collective; Bar & Bench; Pradhan; Wikipedia).
- 1908 trial: Justice Davar, jury 7 to 2, six years, Mandalay (Bar & Bench x2; Wikipedia; Nehru Archive footnote; Jog introduction).
- 9 November 1916: Batchelor and Shah JJ set aside the Poona Magistrate's order. Speeches at Belgaum (1 May) and Ahmednagar (31 May, 1 June 1916). Bond Rs 20,000 (both Indian Kanoon versions agree; Bar & Bench agrees on date and bench).
- Release from Mandalay in June 1914 (Wikipedia, Nehru Archive, Tribune, Maps of India all say June or 1914).
- Gita Rahasya written in Mandalay, published 1915 (Wikipedia; Sankalp).

## Where sources disagree (all flagged in the stories)

- Arrest 1897: 27 July (Tribune, Pradhan) or 29 July (Pradhan). Release: 6 September 1898 (Pradhan, Tribune) versus "Deepavali 1898" and a cut sentence (Sankalp).
- Arrest 1908: 24 June (Sankalp) or 3 July (Maps of India).
- Trial year 1908: Wikipedia text says 1909 in one place. Bar & Bench, Nehru Archive and Maps of India say 1908. I used 1908.
- Jinnah's role in 1908: Wikipedia says he was Tilak's lawyer. Bar & Bench says only "associated at some stage". For 1916 only Bar & Bench (an opinion column) names Jinnah; the judgments do not name counsel.
- Release date: 16 June (Wikipedia) or 17 June (Nehru Archive footnote).
- Diabetes: began in Mandalay (Wikipedia) or already present (Sankalp).
- Where the slogan "Swaraj is my birthright" was first said: 1907 Congress (Tribune), Nasik 17 May 1917 (Nehru Centre newsletter, where the printed text says "Freedom is my birthright"), coined by Kaka Baptista (Wikipedia). The dossier's "Belgaum 1916" was NOT confirmed by any source I opened and is left out.
- Ganesh festival: Rangari 1892 vs Tilak 1893/1894 (Wikipedia, Brut). The Deccan Herald page gave only a summary.
- Rakhmabai case year: 1886 (Jog) or 1887 (Wikipedia).
- Chapekar link: Metcalfs say Tilak "almost surely concealed" the killers (via Wikipedia). Kamra says it was never proved (via Indian History Collective). Stated both ways.

## Single source only (said so in the stories)

- J. A. Guider moving the Magistrate on 22 July 1916 and Jinnah's courtroom exchange (Bar & Bench column by Sanjoy Ghose, which cites other writers).
- Mandalay room, 400 books, German and French (Sankalp).
- Tilak lost 24 pounds in Dongri jail (Pradhan).
- Two lakh at the funeral and the "no memorial" police condition (Live History India).
- Gita Rahasya written "in less than four months" (Wikipedia, citing Datta 1988).

- The Times of India (N. C. Kelkar piece) on cause of death: blocked. The cause of death is not given in any story.
- Jog's chapters on Mandalay, Chirol and Last Days: the archive.org text I could read stopped before them.
- Hansard 17 July 1918; Tilak v. Chirol detail (only hearing dates 29 January to 21 February 1919 from Wikipedia's Chirol page).
- Commons licence of the portrait "Bal G. Tilak.jpg": I could not open the Commons page from here. No picture was added.

## Left out on purpose

- The dossier's claim of grain shops and a hospital run by Tilak in the 1897 plague: not found in a source I could open.
- The "father of Indian unrest" phrase and the Chirol case: only one thin source each. Not enough for a story.
- A harsh quotation about a girl who died in 1890 (Wikipedia). Not repeated, per the gentle rule. The story says so.
