Some history comes from letters, maps and court papers. Some comes from songs. The story of Kuyili comes from songs.

The Hindu wrote about her in a report on the memorial to Velu Nachiyar. She is believed to have been a brave warrior and aide to the queen, the paper says. It adds a plain warning: "no written history exists about her life".

So everything we say about Kuyili must begin with "the songs say".

## What the songs say

Kuyili was Velu Nachiyar's faithful friend. She is "said to have led the unit of women soldiers named after Udaiyaal", according to the Tamil Nadu school textbook. In 1780 she walked into the British arsenal, and all the ammunition was destroyed. The textbook says she did this at the cost of her own life.

<aside class="note"><b>Arsenal</b>: a place where weapons are stored.</aside>

Wikipedia's page on Kuyili tells a similar story. In 1780, during an attack on a Company fort, she went into the weapons store and destroyed it, and so helped the queen to win.

The oral accounts differ on how Kuyili met the queen and what they were to each other. But they all agree "on her valour and sacrifice", says The Hindu.

## Who collected the songs

The Hindu names two men who gathered the oral material.

One is R. Lenin, who writes under the name Santhimavo. He says that elderly villagers in Sivaganga district sing folk songs which mention Kuyili. He also says that a goddess worshipped in the village of Muthupatti, called Theepanja Amman, is Kuyili. And one family in the village of Idaikattur holds a ritual for her every year in the Tamil month of Maasi.

The other is R. Kaleeswaran, of Loyola College, Chennai. He did similar work earlier. He argues that people should not demand a written record from communities that were left out of the written record. "Folk songs are an important source of information," he says.

## The doubts

Some critics think Kuyili never lived, and that the events told about her were unlikely, The Hindu says. Other people dispute the common view of which community she belonged to.

This cannot be settled. A historian who wants a record from her own time will not find one. A person who trusts the songs will not be convinced by that absence.

## Who was Udaiyaal?

The name Udaiyaal appears with Kuyili in different ways.

<aside class="note"><b>Three versions</b>: the textbook says that Udaiyaal was a shepherd girl who was killed for refusing to reveal information about Kuyili, and that the women's unit was named after her. Wikipedia's page on Sivaganga district says the women's army was named after the queen's adopted daughter. The News Minute says the army was named in memory of another adopted daughter who died fighting the British. We cannot say which, if any, is right.</aside>

## What the memorial does

In July 2014 the Chief Minister of Tamil Nadu opened a memorial in Sivaganga district, with a statue of Velu Nachiyar and a monument to Kuyili. The plan for it had met open opposition from some people, The Hindu says.

## Why it matters

A story can matter even when we cannot prove it. People in Sivaganga sing about Kuyili and hold rituals for her. That is a fact about them, whether or not it is a fact about 1780.

When we tell her story we say: this is what the songs say, this is who collected them, and this is why some people doubt them. Then you can decide for yourself.
