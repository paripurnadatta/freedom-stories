By 1780, Velu Nachiyar had waited eight years. Then she went home.

How she did it depends on who tells the story. There is a story of a battle, and there is a story of an agreement.

## The story of the battle

A government feature of 2014 says she formed an army at Virupachi and joined with Gopala Nayaker and Hyder Ali. In 1780 she defeated the British with the military help of her allies. "Thus she became the first queen who successfully fought against British in India," it says.

The Tamil Nadu school textbook says something similar. She "was crowned as Queen with the help of Marudhu brothers". After eight years of planning she waged war on the East India Company in 1780 and took back her kingdom. Hyder Ali helped her.

All these tellings agree that she fought and she won. But none of them says where the battle was. None gives a date for it. None says how many soldiers fought on each side, or what happened.

## The story of the agreement

The district history of Sivaganga does not describe a battle at all.

The two Marudu brothers, Vellai Marudu and Chinna Marudhu, joined Velu Nachiyar after she fled. The history says: "Frustrated by the joining of forces against him, the Nawab ordered that Velu Nachiar and Marudhu Brothers were permitted to return to Sivaganga." This was "subject to payment of Kist to the Nawab".

A kist is a payment due at fixed times. Here it was a payment that the queen had to make to the Nawab.

Then, the history says: "An agreement was reached where by Rani Velu Nachiar was permitted to govern the Sivaganga Country." Chinna Marudu, the younger brother, became her minister. Vellai Marudu, the elder, became commander-in-chief.

In 1780 the queen "granted powers to Marudhu Brothers to administer the country".

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: the government feature and the other accounts say she won her kingdom back by fighting. The district history says that the Nawab, annoyed by the joining of forces against him, allowed her to return, if she paid kist. The two might fit together: a show of force may have led to the agreement. No source says so.</aside>

## What was the price?

If the district history is right, Velu Nachiyar got back Sivaganga, but she did not become free of the Nawab. She had to pay him. That changes what "victory" means. It was a return, with a bill attached.

The history does not say how much the kist was, or how long the Nawab expected it to be paid.

The Marudu brothers' years tell us more. They ran the country from 1783 to 1801. On 10 March 1789 the Nawab appealed to the Madras Council. In 1789 the brothers surrounded Sivaganga with twelve thousand men. On 29 April 1789, their troops beat back a British attack at a place called Kollangudi.

So, nine years after the return, there was still trouble between Sivaganga and the Nawab. The agreement of 1780 did not end it.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: Wikipedia's page on the Marudhu brothers says that an alliance led by the Pandiyar "attacked Sivagangai and retook it in 1789". The district pages say the return was in 1780, and that there was fighting with the Nawab and the British in 1789. Different pages may be describing different events.</aside>

## A story told by singers

Some of the best-known stories about 1780 come from songs and tales. A woman called Kuyili "is said to have walked into the British arsenal (1780) after setting herself on fire, thus destroying all the ammunition". This is the textbook's wording. An arsenal is a store of weapons.

We tell this story on its own in "Kuyili: a song and a doubt". Here it is enough to say that the textbook itself says "is said to", and that no old record backs it up.

## How she ruled

Nothing in the pages read says how Velu Nachiyar ruled in these years. No laws, court cases or account of her days came to light. 1780 is given as the year of her coronation, but nothing describes the ceremony. The gap is left open rather than filled with guesses.

## A war going on elsewhere

In 1780 Hyder Ali also went to war with the Company, a different war in a different place. His forces destroyed a British column in September 1780, in the Second Anglo-Mysore War. No source links that war to Sivaganga. It is mentioned so that you can see how many fights were going on in southern India that year.

## How long she ruled

She governed for ten years, and in 1790 her daughter Vellachi succeeded her. The district history says Velu Nachiyar died a few years after 1780 and gives "about 1790" for her death, adding that the exact date is not known. A page on the same website says 25 December 1796. We look at that in "Two dates and one title".

## Why it matters

When we say that someone "won", we should ask: won what, and at what cost? Velu Nachiyar returned to Sivaganga in 1780 and ruled it. That is a real achievement. A woman who had lost everything got her country back and made one brother her minister and the other her army chief.

But part of the story is missing. There is no account of a battle, and the district's own history talks of permission and payment. A good reader keeps both in mind and waits for better records.
