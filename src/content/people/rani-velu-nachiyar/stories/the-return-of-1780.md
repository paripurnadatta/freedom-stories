By 1780, Velu Nachiyar had waited eight years. Then she went home.

How she did it depends on who you ask. A government feature from 2014 says that she "defeated the British" with the help of her allies. The district's own history of Sivaganga tells a quieter story. It talks about an order, a condition and an agreement.

We will look at both. Then we will say what we do not know.

## The story of the battle

The Press Information Bureau, part of the Government of India, wrote about her in 2014. It says she formed an army at Virupachi and joined with Gopala Nayaker and Hyder Ali. It says that in 1780 she defeated the British with the military help of her allies. It adds: "Thus she became the first queen who successfully fought against British in India."

The Tamil Nadu school textbook says something similar. It says she "was crowned as Queen with the help of Marudhu brothers". Wikipedia says that after eight years of planning she waged war on the East India Company in 1780 and took back her kingdom. The News Minute says she led an attack on her enemies with an army she had built, helped by Hyder Ali.

All these tellings agree that she fought and she won. But none of them says where the battle was. None gives a date for it. None says how many soldiers fought on each side, or what happened.

## The story of the agreement

The district history of Sivaganga does not describe a battle at all.

It says that the two Marudu brothers, Vellai Marudu and Chinna Marudhu, joined Velu Nachiyar after she fled. The history says: "Frustrated by the joining of forces against him, the Nawab ordered that Velu Nachiar and Marudhu Brothers were permitted to return to Sivaganga." This was "subject to payment of Kist to the Nawab".

A kist is a payment due at fixed times. Here it was a payment that the queen had to make to the Nawab. The history does not name the Nawab.

Then, the history says: "An agreement was reached where by Rani Velu Nachiar was permitted to govern the Sivaganga Country." Chinna Marudu, the younger brother, became her minister. Vellai Marudu, the elder, became commander-in-chief.

The history adds that in 1780 the queen "granted powers to Marudhu Brothers to administer the country".

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: The government feature and the other accounts say she won her kingdom back by fighting. The district history says that the Nawab, annoyed by the joining of forces against him, allowed her to return, if she paid kist. These might fit together: a show of force may have led to the agreement. But none of the sources we opened says so. We cannot choose between them, and we do not want to hide the agreement behind the battle.</aside>

## What was the price?

If the district history is right, Velu Nachiyar got back Sivaganga, but she did not become free of the Nawab. She had to pay him. That changes what "victory" means. It was a return, with a bill attached.

The history does not say how much the kist was. It does not say how long the Nawab expected it to be paid.

The Marudu brothers' years tell us more. Wikipedia's page on Sivaganga district says that the brothers ran the country from 1783 to 1801. It says that in 1789 they surrounded Sivaganga with twelve thousand men. It also says that on 29 April 1789, their troops beat back a British attack at a place called Kollangudi. Another page on the district website says that the Nawab had appealed to the Madras Council on 10 March 1789.

So, nine years after the return, there was still trouble between Sivaganga and the Nawab. The agreement of 1780 did not end it.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: Wikipedia's page on the Marudhu brothers says that an alliance led by the Pandiyar "attacked Sivagangai and retook it in 1789". The district pages say the return was in 1780. They also say there was fighting with the Nawab and the British in 1789. We cannot say which year is right for the retaking of Sivaganga. Different pages may be describing different events.</aside>

## A story told by singers

Some of the best-known stories about 1780 come from songs and tales. The textbook says that a woman called Kuyili "is said to have walked into the British arsenal (1780) after setting herself on fire, thus destroying all the ammunition". An arsenal is a store of weapons.

We tell this story on its own in "Kuyili: a song and a doubt". Here it is enough to say that the textbook itself says "is said to", and that no old record backs it up.

## How she ruled

None of the sources we opened tell us how Velu Nachiyar ruled in these years. We found no laws, no court cases and no account of her days. Wikipedia's side box gives 1780 as the year of her coronation, but the page says nothing about the ceremony. We prefer to leave the gap open rather than fill it with guesses.

## A war going on elsewhere

In 1780 Hyder Ali also went to war with the Company, a different war in a different place. Wikipedia's page on the Second Anglo-Mysore War says his forces destroyed a British column in September 1780. We have no source linking that war to Sivaganga. We mention it so that you can see how many fights were going on in southern India that year.

## How long she ruled

Wikipedia says she governed for ten years and that in 1790 her daughter Vellachi succeeded her. The district history says Velu Nachiyar died a few years after 1780 and gives "about 1790" for her death, adding that the exact date is not known. A page on the same website says 25 December 1796. We look at that in "Two dates and one title".

## Why it matters

When we say that someone "won", we should ask: won what, and at what cost? The sources tell us that Velu Nachiyar returned to Sivaganga in 1780 and ruled it. That is a real achievement. A woman who had lost everything got her country back and made one brother her minister and the other her army chief.

But we should also know that part of the story is missing. We have no account of a battle, and the district's own history talks of permission and payment. A good reader keeps both in mind and waits for better records.
