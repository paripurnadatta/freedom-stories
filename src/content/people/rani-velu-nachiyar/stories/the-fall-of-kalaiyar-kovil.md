Think of a girl who is the only child of a king. Velu Nachiyar had no brothers. With no male heir, she was "brought up like a prince, not a princess", says the Press Information Bureau. She was trained in weapons, horse riding and archery. The Tamil Nadu government's school textbook adds that she knew English, French and Urdu.

This story is about what happened when she was forty-two, and her husband's small kingdom was attacked.

## A kingdom cut out of another

Sivaganga is in the south of Tamil Nadu. It was not an old kingdom. It was once part of the bigger kingdom of Ramnad. Ramnad's eleventh king, Kattaya Thevan, divided it. He gave two parts to Sasivarna Thevar, who took a royal title. Sasivarna Thevar died about 1750. His son, Muthu Vaduganatha, became the second Rajah of Sivaganga.

Velu Nachiyar was born in Ramnad on 3 January 1730. She was the only child of Chellamuthu Sethupathy, the Ramnad king. She married Muthu Vaduganatha when she was sixteen. Wikipedia gives the year as 1746. They had a daughter, Vellachi Nachiyar. She was also her husband's close adviser and confidant, Wikipedia says, though it gives no example of it.

She learnt valari, silambam and kalaripayattu, along with horse riding and archery. Silambam is fighting with a long wooden staff. We have not seen any old record that tells how, when or from whom she learnt them. These details come from modern writers.

her mother's name is spelt Sakandhimuthal on the district website and Sakandhimuthathal in the text of Wikipedia. Wikipedia's side box gives it as Muthathal Nachiyar. We do not know which spelling is right.

<aside class="note"><b>Rajah</b>: a king or ruler. <b>Nachiyar</b>: a title for a queen or lady of a royal house in Tamil.</aside>

## Why the English came

Muthu Vaduganatha offered trading rights to the Dutch. He did this "only after the English rejected a similar offer", the district history says.

The English wanted more than trade. They wanted Sivaganga to serve the Nawab of Arcot, to pay him tribute, or to stop dealing with foreign powers. A tribute is a regular payment that a smaller ruler makes to a stronger one.

Wikipedia's page on the Marudhu brothers says it was over "refusal to pay taxes" that the Company killed Muthu Vaduganatha. We have only that one line for this reason. The district history does not say so in these words.

<aside class="note"><b>Nawab</b>: a Muslim governor or ruler. The Nawab of Arcot ruled much of the Tamil country at that time, and his troops fought together with those of the East India Company.</aside>

## Two armies

In June 1772, two English forces came against Sivaganga. One, led by an officer named Smith, came from the east. The other, led by Benjour, came from the west. The district history gives only their surnames.

On 21 June they joined and took the town of Sivaganga. The next day they moved towards Kalaiyar Kovil. This was a place with a royal palace. They took two posts on the way, at Keeranoor and Sholapuram.

Muthu Vaduganatha had seen it coming. He had prepared barriers and dug trenches and set up posts to stop the invaders.

## 25 June

On 25 June, Benjour's force met the main Sivaganga army. It was a fierce fight. "Muthu Vaduganatha Rajah with many of his followers fell dead in that heroic battle", the district history says.

That sentence is all the history tells us about his death. We do not know how it happened, and we do not know how many men fought on each side.

Historians praised the way Velu Nachiyar behaved on the battlefield, the district history adds. It does not say what she did. So we do not know where she was, or what she did, on that day.

The Press Information Bureau says her husband and his second wife were both killed. Wikipedia's page does not mention a second wife. We do not know more about this.

the school textbook says the Nawab and the Company soldiers "stormed the Kalaiyar Kovil Palace" and that Muthu Vadugar was killed there. The district history says he fell in a battle against Benjour's force. Wikipedia says he was killed at Kalaiyar Kovil, and then adds a muddled line about another place, Maravar Mangalam. The accounts are not the same, but they might describe the same fight.

## The flight

The queen did not stay. "The widow queen Velu Nachiar and daughter Vellachi Nachiar with Tandavaraya Pillai fled to Virupakshi in Dindigul," says the district history.

Tandavaraya Pillai was her minister. Two brothers, Vellai Marudu and Chinna Marudhu, joined them later. The brothers had served Muthu Vaduganatha and had been made commanders. We will meet them again.

Virupakshi, also spelt Virupachi, is a place near the town of Dindigul. Dindigul was a border state ruled by Hyder Ali of Mysore, Wikipedia says. She was there for years.

A letter written for her to Hyder Ali spoke of a time she spent with Kallar leaders in the forests after her escape, Wikipedia says. We will come to that letter in the next story.

## Why it matters

We often learn about the freedom struggle as a story of a long fight. This one begins with a loss. A small kingdom was beaten in four days. Its king was dead. A queen with a young daughter had to leave her home.

What she did next is the real story. But it helps to remember how it began. She was not a general with an army. She was a widow with a minister and a young daughter. The sources do not tell us what she was thinking, so we must not guess.

What they do tell us is that she came back. That is in the story "Eight years at Virupachi", and in "The return of 1780".
