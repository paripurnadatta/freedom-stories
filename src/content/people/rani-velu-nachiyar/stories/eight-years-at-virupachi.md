What does a queen do when she has lost her kingdom, her husband and her army? Velu Nachiyar wrote letters and waited. She waited for eight years.

This story is about those eight years. They are the least known part of her life. The sources are few, and the later tellings add things that no old paper proves. So we will say clearly which is which.

## The place

After the defeat at Kalaiyar Kovil in June 1772, Velu Nachiyar fled with her daughter Vellachi and her minister, Tandavaraya Pillai. They went to Virupakshi, which is also spelt Virupachi. It lies near the town of Dindigul.

The district history of Sivaganga says that they were sheltered by Hyder Ali. It says that the two Marudu brothers later joined them there.

Dindigul was a border land of Mysore, ruled by Hyder Ali, says Wikipedia. So the queen was living outside the reach of the Nawab of Arcot and the Company.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: Who protected her? The district history and the Press Information Bureau say Hyder Ali. Another page on the same district website says she lived "under the protection of Palayakaarar Kopaala Naayakkar at Virupachi near Dindigul for eight years". A palayakkarar, or poligar, was a local chief who held a fort and some land. The Bureau says she lived under Hyder Ali's protection and also allied with Gopala Nayaker. Both could be true. We do not have a source that settles it.</aside>

## Why Hyder Ali?

Hyder Ali was the ruler of Mysore. He was already at war with the East India Company. Wikipedia says the First Anglo-Mysore War lasted from 1767 to 1769. Hyder Ali rode with a fast cavalry force to the gates of Madras, and the war ended with a treaty signed on 29 March 1769.

So when the queen came to Virupachi in 1772, Hyder Ali had fought the Company before. None of our sources say why she chose him. But it would make sense for a queen who had been beaten by the Company and its ally, the Nawab, to look for someone who knew how to fight them. This is our guess, not a fact from a source.

## The letter

Wikipedia says that in 1772 her minister, Thandavarayan Pillai, wrote to Hyder Ali on her behalf. Wikipedia cites a Tamil source for the text, which we could not open. The page describes the letter in this way.

It said that the Nawab of Arcot was spreading into the lands of Ramnad and Sivaganga and causing great damage. It said the queen had escaped. After that she had stayed with Kallar leaders in the forests, and kept up the fight. It asked for five thousand horsemen. It said that if she could have five thousand soldiers, she could pay their allowances, retake both kingdoms, and send troops on to Madurai. And it said that the poligars of that area would join her.

The Tamil Nadu school textbook tells it a little differently. It says that Thandavarayanar wrote to Hyder Ali "asking for 5000 infantry and 5000 cavalry". Infantry are soldiers on foot, and cavalry are soldiers on horses.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: Wikipedia says the letter asked for five thousand horsemen. The textbook says five thousand foot soldiers and five thousand horsemen. We have not seen the letter itself. Even if it asked for so many, no source says that so many came.</aside>

## A talk in Urdu

The textbook adds a detail. It says that Velu Nachiyar "explained in detail in Urdu all the problems she had with East India Company" and told Hyder Ali she was determined to fight the English.

This fits with what other sources say about the languages she knew. But we have not found a record of the meeting itself. We do not know where or when this happened. We treat it as the textbook's account, not as something a record proves.

## Building an army

The Press Information Bureau says that during these years she raised an army and made an alliance with Gopala Nayaker and Hyder Ali "to attack the British". The textbook says she succeeded in making an alliance "with not only Gopala Nayakar but Hyder Ali as well".

Wikipedia says she spent "eight years of planning" and that her helpers were the nattu ambalars, the Marudu brothers and Thandavarayan Pillai. It also says she received support from Tipu Sultan, Hyder Ali's son, but gives no details. We do not know what that support was.

The district history says that Vellai Marudu and Chinna Marudhu joined her at Virupachi. They had served her husband, and they became important to her later. The district's page on eminent people says they were sons of a man called Udayar Servai and a woman called Anandayer, from Ramnad. It says that they "belonged neither to the family of the ancient poligars nor to their division of the caste". So they were not great chiefs by birth. They had risen as soldiers.

<aside class="note"><b>Nattu ambalars</b>: leaders of local communities in the Tamil villages. Wikipedia lists them as supporters but does not say who they were.</aside>

## What we do not know

We do not know where in Virupachi she lived. We do not know what her days were like. We do not know how big her army was, or who commanded it. We do not know what became of the five thousand soldiers the letter asked for.

The Press Information Bureau and the district page both say she was in Virupachi for eight years. Counting from 1772, that brings us to 1780. That is also the year the sources give for her return.

## Meanwhile, in the Carnatic

The year 1780 was a big one for Hyder Ali as well. Wikipedia's page on the Second Anglo-Mysore War says the Company had seized a French port, Mahé, in 1779. Hyder Ali depended on that port for French arms. So in 1780 he went to war and invaded the Carnatic. On 10 September, his forces destroyed a column led by Colonel William Baillie, which surrendered.

We have no source that links Hyder Ali's war to the queen's return. We mention it only because it happened in the same year, in the same part of the country.

## Why it matters

In a story of battles, the waiting years can seem empty. Here they are the main part. A queen who had lost everything spent eight years finding friends, writing letters and gathering a force.

There were no victories in this chapter. There was only patience. The next story shows what came after it.
