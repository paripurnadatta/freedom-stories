Velu Nachiyar was a queen of Sivaganga. She is remembered for taking back her kingdom in 1780. But two small questions about her are still open: when did she die, and was she really the first?

## When did she die?

Most pages say she died on 25 December 1796. She was 66, and was buried in Sivaganga on the same day. She died of an illness.

The district's history page says something else. She died a few years after 1780, about 1790. "The exact date of her death is not known," it adds.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: 25 December 1796 on one district page, Wikipedia and The News Minute. About 1790, with no exact date, on the district's history page. No record from her own time was seen.</aside>

The two dates matter because they change how long she ruled. If she died in 1796, she ruled for about sixteen years after 1780. If she died about 1790, she ruled for about ten.

Several pages say ten years. The Press Information Bureau says she ruled for a decade. In 1790 her daughter Vellachi succeeded her. These could all be true together. The queen might have handed over power to her daughter in 1790, and lived on until 1796. But no page says so.

## Was she the first?

A government feature of 2014 says that in 1780 she "became the first queen who successfully fought against British in India". A historian, Prof. Sanjeevi, says she revolted 85 years before the Rani of Jhansi. She is "widely known" as the first queen to fight the British, "even before the Rani of Jhansi".

There are two claims here, and they have different answers.

The first is a claim about queens. To test it, we would have to read about every Indian queen before 1780. That has not been done.

The second is a claim about rulers. Some people say she was the first Indian ruler to fight the British. This cannot be right as it stands. Hyder Ali, the ruler of Mysore, fought the Company from 1767 to 1769, in the First Anglo-Mysore War. He reached the gates of Madras, and the war ended with a treaty on 29 March 1769. That was before 1772, and Hyder Ali was a ruler.

So the careful way to put it is this. She was among the earliest Indian rulers to fight the Company, and she is called its first queen. How early she was depends on what "fight" and "win" mean. The district history describes the return in 1780 as an agreement with a price, which we explain in "The return of 1780".

## How she is remembered today

A postage stamp with her picture was issued on 31 December 2008. On 18 July 2014 the Chief Minister of Tamil Nadu, Jayalalithaa, opened a memorial to her in Sivaganga. She also declared 3 January her birth anniversary. The date 3 January 1730 is given by the district page and by Wikipedia.

No portrait of her made in her own lifetime is known.

## Why it matters

When we remember a person, we often add titles: the first, the bravest, the greatest. These are easy to say and hard to prove.

With Velu Nachiyar the safe facts are enough. She was a queen of Sivaganga. She lost her husband and her kingdom in 1772. She spent years finding allies. And in 1780 she was back, with the help of the Marudu brothers. Everything else is best written with "some say" and "about".
