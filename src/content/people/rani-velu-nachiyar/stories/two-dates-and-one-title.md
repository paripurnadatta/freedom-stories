Two pages on the same government website, the website of the Sivaganga district, give two different answers about the end of Velu Nachiyar's life. That is a good place to start.

## When did she die?

One page, the list of eminent people, says she died on 25 December 1796. Wikipedia also gives 25 December 1796. It says she was 66 and that she was buried in Sivaganga on the same day. The News Minute says she died of an illness in 1796.

The district's history page says something else. It says she died a few years after 1780. It gives "about 1790" and adds: "the exact date of her death is not known."

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: 25 December 1796 on one district page, Wikipedia and The News Minute. About 1790, with no exact date, on the district's history page. We have not seen any record from her own time. We cannot choose.</aside>

The two dates matter because they change how long she ruled. If she died in 1796, she ruled for about sixteen years after 1780. If she died about 1790, she ruled for about ten.

Several pages say ten years. The Press Information Bureau says she ruled for a decade. Wikipedia says she governed for ten years and that in 1790 her daughter Vellachi succeeded her. The News Minute says "more than 10 years". These could all be true together. The queen might have handed over power to her daughter in 1790, and lived on until 1796. But none of the pages says so.

## Was she the first?

A government feature of 2014 says that in 1780 she "became the first queen who successfully fought against British in India". It quotes a historian, Prof. Sanjeevi, who says she revolted 85 years before the Rani of Jhansi. The News Minute says she is "widely known" as the first queen to fight the British, "even before the Rani of Jhansi". Wikipedia calls her the first Indian queen to wage war on the East India Company.

There are two claims here. They are different, and they have different answers.

The first is a claim about queens. We cannot test it. To test it we would have to read about every Indian queen before 1780, and we have not done that.

The second is a claim about rulers. Some people say she was the first Indian ruler to fight the British. This cannot be right as it stands. Wikipedia's page on the First Anglo-Mysore War says that Hyder Ali, the ruler of Mysore, fought the Company from 1767 to 1769. He reached the gates of Madras, and the war ended with a treaty on 29 March 1769. That was before 1772, and Hyder Ali was a ruler.

So the careful way to put it is this. She was among the earliest Indian rulers to fight the Company, and she is called its first queen. How early she was depends on what "fight" and "win" mean. Also, the district history describes the return in 1780 as an agreement with a price, which we explain in "The return of 1780".

## How she is remembered today

The Press Information Bureau says that a postage stamp with her picture was issued on 31 December 2008. The News Minute says that on 18 July 2014 the Chief Minister of Tamil Nadu, Jayalalithaa, opened a memorial to her in Sivaganga. She also declared 3 January her birth anniversary. The date 3 January 1730 is given by the district page and by Wikipedia.

We found no portrait of her made in her own lifetime.

## Why it matters

When we remember a person, we often add titles: the first, the bravest, the greatest. These are easy to say and hard to prove.

With Velu Nachiyar the safe facts are enough. She was a queen of Sivaganga. She lost her husband and her kingdom in 1772. She spent years finding allies. And in 1780 she was back, with the help of the Marudu brothers. Everything else is best written with "some say", "about" and "we do not know".
