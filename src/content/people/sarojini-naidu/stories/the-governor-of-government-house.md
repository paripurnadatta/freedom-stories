When India became independent on 15 August 1947, Sarojini Naidu was sixty-eight years old. On that day she took on a new job.

## A new post

Wikipedia says she was appointed Governor of the United Provinces on 15 August 1947. The United Provinces is today the state of Uttar Pradesh. Britannica says she was the first woman in India to be appointed the governor of a state.

EBSCO calls the United Provinces the most politically active state in India. None of the sources we opened describes her work as governor. ETV Bharat, an Indian news site, says only that she became the first woman governor, and gives no dates.

## Before that

Wikipedia says she was elected to the Constituent Assembly of India for Bihar. Its page says that her only speech there was on 11 December 1946.

## The last weeks

EBSCO says her health declined in early 1949. It says she died on 2 March 1949, after asking a nurse to sing for her.

Wikipedia says she died of a cardiac arrest at about 3.30 in the afternoon, at Government House in Lucknow, aged 70. It says her last rites were held at the river Gomti. ETV Bharat and the Congress's tribute page also say she died at Government House in Lucknow, of cardiac arrest. Britannica gives the date as 2 March 1949 and the place as Lucknow.

<aside class="note"><b>Different versions</b>: Banglapedia says she died in Allahabad. The Victorian poetry website DVPP gives the date as 1 February 1949. Encyclopedia.com has two entries, and one of them gives only the year, 1949. The others agree on 2 March 1949 in Lucknow, so we use that.</aside>

## What came after

Wikipedia says that her birthday, 13 February, is observed as Women's Day in India. The Sarojini Devi Eye Hospital in Hyderabad is named after her. So is an annex of the University of Hyderabad, which houses the Sarojini Naidu School of Arts and Communication.

EBSCO calls 13 February "National Women's Day." Wikipedia adds that her daughter Padmaja Naidu, who had joined the Quit India movement, later held posts in the government, and edited her mother's unpublished poems.

## Why it matters

Her years as governor were short, and the sources say little about them. What they do say is the date and the post: on the day India was free, a woman who had been jailed by the British became governor of the state that EBSCO calls the most politically active in India. We have no source that says what she did in that job, and we would not guess.
