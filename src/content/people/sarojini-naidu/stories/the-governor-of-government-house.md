When India became independent on 15 August 1947, Sarojini Naidu was sixty-eight years old. On that day she took on a new job. She became the Governor of the United Provinces, the state we now call Uttar Pradesh.

She was the first woman in India to be appointed governor of a state. A woman who had been jailed by the British now held one of the highest posts in a free country.

<aside class="note"><b>Governor</b>: the person who represents the head of the country in a state.</aside>

## A busy state

EBSCO, a library of reference works, calls the United Provinces the most politically active state in India. She was in a place where much was happening.

Before that, she had been elected to the Constituent Assembly of India for Bihar. This was the body that wrote India's Constitution. She made a speech there on 11 December 1946. It was her only speech in the Assembly, according to Wikipedia.

## The last weeks

Her health began to fail in early 1949. EBSCO says she died on 2 March 1949, after she had asked a nurse to sing for her.

She died at Government House in Lucknow, of a cardiac arrest, at about half past three in the afternoon. She was seventy years old. Her last rites were held beside the river Gomti.

<aside class="note"><b>Different versions</b>: Banglapedia says she died in Allahabad. The Victorian poetry website DVPP gives the date as 1 February 1949. Encyclopedia.com has two entries, and one of them gives only the year, 1949. Wikipedia, Britannica, ETV Bharat, EBSCO and the Congress's tribute page agree on 2 March 1949 in Lucknow, so we use that.</aside>

## What came after

Her birthday, 13 February, is observed in India as Women's Day. The Sarojini Devi Eye Hospital in Hyderabad is named after her. So is an annex of the University of Hyderabad, which houses the Sarojini Naidu School of Arts and Communication.

Her daughter Padmaja Naidu had joined the Quit India movement. Later she held posts in the government. She also edited her mother's unpublished poems.

## Why it matters

Her years as governor were short, and she was an elderly woman by then. We found no account of what she did in the job, and we will not guess.

What we do have is the date and the post. On the day India became free, a woman who had been jailed by the British became governor of the state that EBSCO calls the most politically active in India. Her time in that post lasted about a year and a half, from 15 August 1947 to 2 March 1949.
