A nightingale is a small bird known for its beautiful song. In India people call Sarojini Naidu "the Nightingale of India." But the sources do not agree on who gave her the name.

## Three explanations

Britannica says that Mahatma Gandhi called her "the Nightingale of India." The Congress's tribute says that Gandhi called her "Bharat Kokila." Banglapedia says that her 1905 book of poems earned her the epithet "Bulbul-e-Hind." Kokila and bulbul are both Indian words for singing birds.

Wikipedia says the Gandhi story appears in its opening lines without a source. Encyclopedia.com only says she was called "the nightingale of India" and does not say who called her so.

<aside class="note"><b>Three versions</b>: Gandhi gave her the name (Britannica, and the Congress with a Hindi form of it). Her first book of poems earned it (Banglapedia). Or the sources do not say (Encyclopedia.com). We found no record from the time that settles it.</aside>

## Her books

Wikipedia lists her main books of poems. *The Golden Threshold* came out in London in 1905. *The Bird of Time* followed in 1912, and *The Broken Wing* in 1917, which was the last new collection printed in her lifetime. Another book, *The Sceptred Flute*, was printed in New York in 1928 and gathers poems that were already published. A last book, *The Feather of the Dawn*, was printed in 1961 from poems she had not published. It was edited by her daughter Padmaja Naidu.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: Banglapedia dates *The Sceptred Flute* to 1943. Wikipedia dates it to 1928.</aside>

The Congress's tribute says that her 1912 poem "In the Bazaars of Hyderabad" is one of her most popular. Wikipedia places that poem in *The Bird of Time*. Another source, Brewminate, names two of her poems with a political message: "Awake!", which closed a speech she gave to the Congress in 1915, and "The Gift of India," which criticised the British for taking advantage of Indian mothers.

We do not copy her poems here. They can be read in the books themselves.

## What critics said

Edmund Gosse, who helped her in her early years in England, called her in 1919 "the most accomplished living poet in India." Three of her poems were printed in the *Oxford Book of English Mystical Verse* in 1927, says EBSCO.

EBSCO also says her poetry was "overlooked in the West" but is "regarded as some of the most important in India." It gives no source for this. Encyclopedia.com has a different view. Its writer says her verse "tends to become monotonous and repetitive," and that her lasting fame will "likely rest" on her work as a leader. These are opinions, and readers can judge for themselves.

## Why it matters

A nickname is easy to repeat and hard to trace. How this one began is something the sources leave open.
