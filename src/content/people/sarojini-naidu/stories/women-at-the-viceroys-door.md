On 15 December 1917, a group of Indian women went to see Edwin Montagu, the Secretary of State for India, and Lord Chelmsford, the Viceroy of India. Montagu had taken up his post in June 1917 and visited India in the late part of that year.

According to Wikipedia's article on women's suffrage in India, the group was led by Sarojini Naidu. It lists the women who went with her: Annie Besant, Margaret Cousins, Parvati Ammal, Mrs. Guruswamy Chetty, Nalinibai Dalvi, Dorothy Jinarajadasa, Dr. Nagutai Joshi, Kamalabai Kibe, Mrs. Z. Lazarus, Begum Hasrat Mohani, S. Naik, Srirangamma and Herabai Tata. The article says telegrams of support from other women were also sent to Montagu.

<aside class="note"><b>Suffrage</b>: the right to vote.</aside>

## The request

They asked that women's suffrage be included in a new Franchise Bill that the Government of India was preparing. A *franchise* is the right to vote. The article does not give Montagu's answer.

## The group behind them

The Women's Indian Association had been founded earlier that year. Wikipedia's article on the Association says it was set up on 8 May 1917 at Adyar, Madras. Its first president was Annie Besant, and it names Margaret Cousins as its founder. It does not mention Sarojini Naidu.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: Wikipedia's article on Naidu says she co-founded the Association with Muthulakshmi Reddy. So does Brewminate, which says that most of its text is taken from Wikipedia. The Association's own article names Cousins, Besant and others, but not Naidu. We can say she was linked with it. We cannot say from these sources whether she was a founder.</aside>

## Two committees

In 1919 a committee called the Southborough Committee was working out rules for who could vote. It took petitions from all over India. It heard from women only in Bengal and Punjab. Its report, in April 1919, said that a few educated women might qualify. It said women as a whole were not ready for the vote.

In August 1919, Annie Besant and Sarojini Naidu presented the case for women's votes to another body in London, the Joint Select Committee. Brewminate puts a trip to London with Besant in 1917 instead. Banglapedia says Naidu went to England in 1919 with the Home Rule deputation and came back in July 1920.

## The result

The Government of India Act of 1919 did not give women the vote. It left each province free to decide. Wikipedia says the provinces could allow women to vote, if they met conditions based on income, land revenue or public service. In 1921 the Madras Presidency removed the rule that only men could stand for local elections.

## A national body

In 1927 a body called the All India Women's Conference held its first meeting, at Fergusson College in Poona. Wikipedia's article on the Conference names Margaret Cousins as its founder and Maharani Chimnabai as its first president. It names Naidu only as president in 1930. The article on Naidu says she co-founded it. Banglapedia says she was its president and puts the founding in 1926.

## Why it matters

The vote did not come quickly. It came province by province, with limits. The women of 1917 asked for it at the top, and the sources show that it took years of asking.
