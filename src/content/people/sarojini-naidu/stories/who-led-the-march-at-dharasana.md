On 21 May 1930, thousands of men walked towards a salt works at Dharasana, in Gujarat. They carried no weapons. Police with long wooden sticks, called lathis, waited for them.

A reporter named Webb Miller watched. His story was printed in newspapers around the world. Many accounts say that one of the people who led the march that day was Sarojini Naidu, the poet. But Miller did not name her. This story looks at what the sources say, and where they stop.

## Why salt?

The government taxed salt. On 2 March 1930, says Wikipedia, Gandhi wrote to the Viceroy, Lord Irwin. He offered to stop the Salt March if Irwin met eleven demands, including an end to the salt tax. Irwin ignored the letter. Gandhi's words, as Wikipedia gives them, were: "On bended knees, I asked for bread and I have received stone instead."

Gandhi made salt at the seashore at Dandi on 6 April 1930.

## Gandhi is arrested

The plan was to go on to the Dharasana Salt Works, about 40 kilometres south of Dandi. Then, around midnight on 4 May, officers arrived at Gandhi's camp. Wikipedia says the Surat District Magistrate came with two Indian officers and thirty armed constables. Gandhi was sleeping on a cot in a mango grove. He was arrested and held without a trial, near Poona.

Abbas Tyabji, a retired judge aged 76, took over. Wikipedia says he led the march with Gandhi's wife Kasturba beside him. Both were arrested before they reached Dharasana, and sentenced to three months. GKToday gives a different count: it says Tyabji and 58 marchers were arrested at Untadi on 12 May 1930.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: Was Kasturba arrested with Tyabji? Wikipedia says so. GKToday names only Tyabji and 58 marchers. We cannot settle this from what we read.</aside>

## The march of 21 May

After those arrests, Sarojini Naidu led the march, says the Wikipedia article on the Salt March. Wikipedia says that she told the volunteers: "You must not use any violence under any circumstances." GKToday gives her words as a call to stay "completely non-violent." It says she led more than 2,500 people, working with Manilal Gandhi, the Mahatma's son, and a man named Imam Sahib. The Wikipedia article on Dharasana says that Naidu and Maulana Abul Kalam Azad led the protest after Gandhi was arrested. It does not mention Imam Sahib or Manilal as leaders.

So three sources name different leaders. Next to them stands the report that made the raid famous.

## What the reporter wrote

Webb Miller was a correspondent for the United Press. His dispatch is dated 22 May 1930, from the camp at Dharasana. The Satyagraha Foundation prints it.

Miller wrote that more than 2,500 volunteers advanced on the salt pans. They did not hit back. He did not name the leader of the march. He wrote that an unnamed leader said: "These men have done a great work for India today." He wrote that the leaders stood in front of the ranks and urged the volunteers to remember that Gandhi's spirit was with them.

Wikipedia quotes another line from Miller: "Not one of the marchers even raised an arm to fend off the blows." The police, about 400 of them under six British officers, struck the volunteers with their sticks.

## How many were hurt?

The numbers differ.

- The government's own statement of 22 May, as Miller printed it, said that Congress itself counted 170 injured, and that only three or four were seriously hurt.
- Miller wrote that around noon, he counted more than 200 injured in the Congress camp hospital.
- British-owned newspapers the next day gave 320.
- Wikipedia gives 320 injured and two dead, and says the figure comes from Miller. The page cites no one else to confirm it.

Lord Irwin wrote to the King that the police had been forced to use "sterner methods." He said: "A good many people suffered minor injuries in consequence." That does not match what Miller described.

## The news travels

Wikipedia says that Miller's story was held back at first. It says the telegraph operators did not send it until he threatened to tell the world about the censorship. The story then appeared in about 1,350 newspapers. An American senator, John J. Blaine, had it read into the official record of the Senate.

Vithalbhai Patel, a former Speaker of the Assembly, said: "All hope of reconciling India with the British Empire is lost forever."

## And Sarojini Naidu?

What do we know about Naidu's arrest? Not much. Wikipedia's article on the Salt March says she was the first woman to be arrested in the Salt March, but marks that claim as needing a source. New World Encyclopedia says she was arrested soon after Gandhi and jailed for several months. The Congress paper says she was arrested for the Salt March with Gandhi, Nehru and Malaviya. But Wikipedia describes Gandhi's arrest at his camp in the night of 4 and 5 May, and mentions none of them. Two sources, Banglapedia and New World Encyclopedia, say she and Gandhi were freed on 31 January 1931.

We found no source that says when she was arrested. So we cannot say if she was still free on 21 May, or if she was in jail by then.

## Where the sources leave us

The leader of the raid is named in three ways: Naidu with Azad, Naidu with Manilal Gandhi and Imam Sahib, and, by Miller himself, no one. Miller's dispatch is dated the day after the raid, but it is a reporter's account, not a list of names. The Wikipedia articles and GKToday are later summaries. A careful reader keeps the three apart.

## Why it matters

The march was meant to be peaceful, and the world read about it because a reporter described what he saw. That is one fact on which all the sources agree. The question of who walked at the front is harder, and the records we have do not settle it.
