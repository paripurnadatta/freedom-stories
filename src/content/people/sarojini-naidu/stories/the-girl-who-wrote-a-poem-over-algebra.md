Imagine you are eleven. You are stuck on an algebra problem. You cannot solve it. And then, in the middle of the sum, a poem comes to you.

That story is told in the introduction to Sarojini Naidu's first book of poems. The introduction was written in 1905 by Arthur Symons, a British poet. He says that a whole poem came to her, that she wrote it down, and that she later said her "poetic career" began that day.

## A house in Hyderabad

She was born Sarojini Chattopadhyay on 13 February 1879, in Hyderabad. Several sources agree on the date. Her family came from Bengal.

Her father, Aghorenath Chattopadhyay, was a scientist. The sources do not say the same thing about his work. Wikipedia says he was the principal of Nizam College, in Hyderabad. Symons and Encyclopedia.com say he founded it. Encyclopedia.com and EBSCO say he held a doctor of science degree from the University of Edinburgh.

Her mother, Barada Sundari Devi, wrote poetry in Bengali. Sarojini was the eldest of eight children. Encyclopedia.com says her parents belonged to the Brahmo Samaj, a Hindu reform movement of the time.

Wikipedia names some of her brothers and sisters. Virendranath became a revolutionary. Harindranath became a poet, a playwright and an actor. Her sister Suhasini became a leader of the Communist movement.

New World Encyclopedia adds that her father was later dismissed from his post as principal and sent away from Hyderabad for his political activity. It gives no date. We found this in only one source.

EBSCO says her father believed in education for girls, and taught her himself, in English and French, and later in Persian. Symons describes the house as a place where learned men of many faiths came together.

## A clever girl, and a hard year

In 1891, when she was twelve, she passed the matriculation exam of Madras University. Wikipedia and Encyclopedia.com say she came first in the whole Madras presidency.

<aside class="note"><b>Matriculation</b>: an exam taken at the end of school, to enter a university.</aside>

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: How old was she when she finished school? Wikipedia and Encyclopedia.com say twelve. EBSCO says eleven. New World Encyclopedia says she entered the University at twelve. We use twelve, which two sources give.</aside>

Symons says she wrote a long poem and a play when she was thirteen. He says her health broke down for good around that time, and that it stopped her regular lessons. After that she read widely, mostly between fourteen and sixteen. In those years, he says, she also wrote a novel and a journal.

## Sent to England

The Nizam of Hyderabad was the ruler of the state. Wikipedia says he gave her a scholarship to study in England. Encyclopedia.com says he was impressed by her talent.

She sailed in 1895. Symons says she was sent against her will. Encyclopedia.com says she was sixteen. EBSCO says she was judged too young for college, so she first took classes at King's College, London, for a year. After that, she was admitted to Girton College, Cambridge. EBSCO says her time in England was "far from happy," partly because people saw her as exotic and distant. Encyclopedia.com says she left without a degree.

Wikipedia adds that in England she met artists of the Aesthetic and Decadent movements, two groups of writers and painters of the time. She also met two writers who helped her. One was Arthur Symons. The other was Edmund Gosse. EBSCO says Symons introduced her to English literary circles and Gosse advised her on her style. Encyclopedia.com quotes Gosse on her early poems: "skillful in form, correct in grammar, and blameless in sentiment."

A poem of hers, "Song of Sleep," was printed in a London magazine called *The English Illustrated Magazine* in July 1896. This is recorded by a website on Victorian poetry.

Symons says her health broke down again. In 1898 she went back to Hyderabad.

## A marriage

Wikipedia says that in England she met a doctor, Govindarajulu Naidu. They married in December 1898, says the Victorian poetry website. Banglapedia says the wedding took place in Hyderabad, after her return.

Her husband belonged to a different community from hers, which made the marriage unusual in those days. The sources do not agree on what her family thought.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: Wikipedia calls the marriage "groundbreaking and scandalous", and says both families approved. EBSCO says the marriage cut her off from her family, friends and society. Both cannot be fully true. We do not know which is closer.</aside>

The number of their children is also given in different ways. Wikipedia says five. Encyclopedia.com and EBSCO say four.

## The first book

Her first book of poems was *The Golden Threshold*, printed in London in 1905. Symons wrote the introduction. Wikipedia says Gosse suggested the book. In the book, she says that Gosse "first showed me the way to the Golden Threshold."

Banglapedia says that this book earned her the name "Bulbul-e-Hind," a name that means the nightingale of India.

## After the first book

*The Golden Threshold* was printed again in 1906, 1909 and 1914, says EBSCO. Her second book, *The Bird of Time*, came out in 1912, and a third, *The Broken Wing*, in 1917. EBSCO says her poems are mostly about life, love, death and destiny. Three of them were included in the *Oxford Book of English Mystical Verse* in 1927.

Wikipedia says her poems were written in English, in the style of the British Romantic poets. Edmund Gosse called her, in 1919, "the most accomplished living poet in India." One more thing is worth noting. The writer of the Encyclopedia.com article thinks her poetry "tends to become monotonous and repetitive," and that her fame will "likely rest" on her work as a leader. That is the writer's opinion, and other readers may disagree.

## Why it matters

By the time she was twenty-six, Sarojini Naidu was a published poet in London. Her life afterwards, in politics, would be very different.

The story of the poem over the algebra problem comes from Symons's introduction, which does not say where he heard it. We have not found it in any other source.
