Imagine you are eleven. You are stuck on an algebra problem. You cannot solve it. And then, in the middle of the sum, a poem comes to you.

That story is told in the introduction to Sarojini Naidu's first book of poems. It was written in 1905 by Arthur Symons, a British poet. He says that a whole poem came to her, that she wrote it down, and that she later said her "poetic career" began that day.

<aside class="note"><b>One source only</b>: Symons does not say where he heard the story, and we have not found it in any other source. It may be true, but it rests on one man's word.</aside>

## A house in Hyderabad

She was born Sarojini Chattopadhyay on 13 February 1879, in Hyderabad. Her family came from Bengal.

Her father, Aghorenath Chattopadhyay, was a scientist. He held a doctor of science degree from the University of Edinburgh, according to Encyclopedia.com and EBSCO. He was the principal of Nizam College in Hyderabad. Some writers go further and say he founded it.

Her mother, Barada Sundari Devi, wrote poetry in Bengali. Sarojini was the eldest of eight children. Her parents belonged to the Brahmo Samaj, a Hindu reform movement of the time.

Her brothers and sisters did well too. Virendranath became a revolutionary. Harindranath became a poet, a playwright and an actor. Her sister Suhasini became a leader of the Communist movement.

Her father believed in education for girls. He taught her himself, in English and French, and later in Persian, says EBSCO. Symons describes the house as a place where learned men of many faiths came together.

New World Encyclopedia adds that her father was later dismissed from his post as principal and sent away from Hyderabad for his political activity. It gives no date, and we found this in no other source, so it is best treated as a claim.

## A clever girl, and a hard year

In 1891 she passed the matriculation exam of Madras University. She came first in the whole Madras presidency, say Wikipedia and Encyclopedia.com.

<aside class="note"><b>Matriculation</b>: an exam taken at the end of school, to enter a university.</aside>

How old was she? Wikipedia and Encyclopedia.com say twelve. EBSCO says eleven, and New World Encyclopedia says she entered the university at twelve. We use twelve, which two sources give.

At thirteen she wrote a long poem and a play, Symons says. Her health broke down for good around that time, and it stopped her regular lessons. After that she read widely, mostly between fourteen and sixteen. In those years, he says, she also wrote a novel and a journal.

## Sent to England

The Nizam of Hyderabad was the ruler of the state. He gave her a scholarship to study in England. Encyclopedia.com says he was impressed by her talent.

She sailed in 1895, when she was sixteen. Symons says she was sent against her will. EBSCO says she was judged too young for college, so she first took classes at King's College, London, for a year. After that she was admitted to Girton College, Cambridge. Her time in England was "far from happy", says EBSCO, partly because people saw her as exotic and distant. Encyclopedia.com says she left without a degree.

In England she met artists of the Aesthetic and Decadent movements, two groups of writers and painters of the time. She also met two writers who helped her. One was Arthur Symons. The other was Edmund Gosse. Symons introduced her to English literary circles, and Gosse advised her on her style. Gosse described her early poems as "skillful in form, correct in grammar, and blameless in sentiment."

A poem of hers, "Song of Sleep", was printed in a London magazine called *The English Illustrated Magazine* in July 1896. A website on Victorian poetry records this.

Her health broke down again. In 1898 she went back to Hyderabad.

## A marriage

In England she had met a doctor, Govindarajulu Naidu. They married in December 1898, says the Victorian poetry website. Banglapedia says the wedding took place in Hyderabad, after her return.

Her husband belonged to a different community from hers, which made the marriage unusual in those days.

What did her family think? Here the sources disagree. Wikipedia calls the marriage "groundbreaking and scandalous", and says both families approved. EBSCO says the marriage cut her off from her family, friends and society. Both cannot be fully true. We do not know which is closer.

<aside class="note"><b>How many children?</b> Wikipedia says they had five children. Encyclopedia.com and EBSCO say four.</aside>

## The first book

Her first book of poems was *The Golden Threshold*, printed in London in 1905. Symons wrote the introduction. Gosse had suggested the book. In it she says that Gosse "first showed me the way to the Golden Threshold."

One account says this book earned her the name "Bulbul-e-Hind", the nightingale of India.

## After the first book

*The Golden Threshold* was printed again in 1906, 1909 and 1914, says EBSCO. Her second book, *The Bird of Time*, came out in 1912, and a third, *The Broken Wing*, in 1917. Her poems are mostly about life, love, death and destiny. Three of them were included in the *Oxford Book of English Mystical Verse* in 1927.

Her poems were written in English, in the style of the British Romantic poets, says Wikipedia. Edmund Gosse called her, in 1919, "the most accomplished living poet in India."

One more view is worth noting. The writer of the Encyclopedia.com article thinks her poetry "tends to become monotonous and repetitive", and that her fame will "likely rest" on her work as a leader. That is the writer's opinion, and other readers may disagree.

## Why it matters

By the time she was twenty-six, Sarojini Naidu was a published poet in London. Her life afterwards, in politics, would be very different.

The story of the poem over the algebra problem began with a book's introduction. Whether it happened exactly so, no one can say.
