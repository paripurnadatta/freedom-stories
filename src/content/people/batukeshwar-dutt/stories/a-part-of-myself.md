After the bombs in the Assembly, Bhagat Singh and Batukeshwar Dutt were tried together. On 12 June 1929, both were sentenced to transportation for life.

Three days later, on 15 June, they began a hunger strike. They refused to eat until Indian political prisoners were treated as fairly as European prisoners. They wanted proper food, books and newspapers.

<aside class="note"><b>How long?</b> Sources disagree about how long the strike lasted. One says 63 days. Another says 114 days.</aside>

## Different paths

Then their paths began to split.

Bhagat Singh was put on trial again, for the killing of a police officer in Lahore. Batukeshwar was tried too. But the court found there was not enough evidence against him. He was let off in that case.

Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev and Rajguru would later be sentenced to death. Batukeshwar would stay in prison for his life sentence.

## 16 July 1930

On 16 July 1930, the mood in Lahore Central Jail was gloomy. Batukeshwar was to be moved to Multan jail, far away.

Before he left, Bhagat Singh took out his diary. He asked Batukeshwar to sign it.

It was a small thing. A friend's autograph. But both of them knew they might never meet again.

They never did.

## Letters

After the death sentence, Bhagat Singh wrote to Batukeshwar. He told him that staying alive in prison would take as much courage as going to the gallows.

"I will die," he wrote, "but you will remain alive to bear the punishment of life imprisonment." He said he believed Batukeshwar would show the world that a revolutionary could bear even a lifetime of suffering.

Bhagat Singh also wrote to his own mother about his friend. "I am going," he wrote, "but in Batukeshwar Dutt I am leaving behind a part of myself."

<aside class="note"><b>Exact words</b>: these lines are quoted in later writing about Dutt. The wording given for the letter to his mother varies a little between accounts.</aside>

## The island prison

In 1933, Batukeshwar was sent to the Cellular Jail in the Andaman Islands, far out in the Bay of Bengal.

There, too, the prisoners went on hunger strike. One strike lasted 46 days. Some prisoners died. In the end, the authorities agreed to many of their demands: better food, better light, letters, books.

## A magazine written by hand

With books came study. Batukeshwar and a group of other prisoners formed a study circle. They read, argued and wrote.

They even made their own magazine, called The Call. It was written entirely by hand, because there was no printing press in a prison cell. Batukeshwar wrote for it.

## Coming home

Batukeshwar fell ill with tuberculosis. He was brought back to the mainland, and set free on 8 September 1938.

He had carried his friend's words with him through all those years. A part of Bhagat Singh had lived on, just as he had written.
