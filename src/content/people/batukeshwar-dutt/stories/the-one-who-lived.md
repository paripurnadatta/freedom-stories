On 8 April 1929, two young men stood up in the visitors' gallery of the Central Legislative Assembly in Delhi. They threw two small bombs onto empty benches below. Smoke filled the hall. Red leaflets fluttered down. The two did not run. They waited to be arrested.

One of them was Bhagat Singh. You may know his story.

The other was Batukeshwar Dutt. He was eighteen years old.

Bhagat Singh was hanged two years later. Batukeshwar lived until 1965. This is the story of the one who lived.

## A boy from Bengal

Batukeshwar Dutt was born on 18 November 1910, in a village called Oari, in the Burdwan district of Bengal. His friends called him Battu. In the secret group he later joined, his code name was Mohan.

He grew up far from Bengal, in the busy city of Kanpur, where he went to school.

As a boy there, he once saw a British man beat an Indian child, just for walking on a road kept for Europeans. He never forgot it.

<aside class="note"><b>Which year?</b> Most sources say he was born in 1910. One says 1911.</aside>

## A friend for life

In 1924, in Kanpur, Batukeshwar met a Sikh boy from Punjab a few years older than him. It was Bhagat Singh.

That year, the river Ganga flooded Kanpur. The two boys volunteered to help. Batukeshwar later wrote that they stood side by side on the river bank at night, holding lanterns, so that anyone struggling in the water could find the shore.

Batukeshwar taught his friend to speak Bengali. He sang him the poems of the great Bengali poet Kazi Nazrul Islam.

Both boys joined a secret revolutionary group. In 1928, it took the name Hindustan Socialist Republican Association, or HSRA.

## "To make the deaf hear"

In 1929, the British were about to pass two harsh new laws. The HSRA decided to protest in a way nobody could ignore.

The plan was to throw bombs in the Assembly, but only small ones, onto empty seats, so that nobody would be killed. The bombs were meant to make a noise, not to hurt.

Batukeshwar and Bhagat Singh were chosen. The leaflets they threw that day began with the words: "It takes a loud noise to make the deaf hear."

<aside class="note"><b>Who was chosen first?</b> Accounts differ. One says Dutt and Sukhdev were picked first. Another says Bhagat Singh and Sukhdev were picked, and Dutt angrily demanded to be included.</aside>

## Prison

In June 1929, both friends were sentenced to transportation for life. Three days later, they began a hunger strike. They wanted Indian political prisoners to be treated as fairly as European prisoners.

Then their paths split. Bhagat Singh was tried again for the killing of a police officer in Lahore, and sentenced to death. Batukeshwar was found not guilty in that case.

On 16 July 1930, Batukeshwar was moved to another jail. Before he left, Bhagat Singh took out his diary and asked for his friend's signature. They never met again.

Later, Bhagat Singh wrote to his mother about his friend: "I am going, but in Batukeshwar Dutt I am leaving behind a part of myself."

## The island of black water

In 1933, Batukeshwar was sent to the Cellular Jail in the Andaman Islands. Indians called it Kala Pani, the black water. It was one of the most feared prisons in the world.

He took part in two hunger strikes there. One lasted 46 days. When the prisoners won the right to books, they formed a study circle and made their own magazine, written entirely by hand. It was called The Call. Batukeshwar wrote for it.

In prison, he caught tuberculosis, a serious lung disease.

He was brought back to the mainland, and set free on 8 September 1938. He was still not done. In 1942 he joined the Quit India Movement, and went to prison again for four years.

## Free India

India became free in 1947. Batukeshwar married Anjali that year, and they had a daughter, Bharati. They lived in Patna.

<aside class="note"><b>1937 or 1947?</b> Most accounts say he married in November 1947. One says 1937.</aside>

But life was hard. He had no money and poor health. He worked as a tourist guide. He worked as an agent for a cigarette company, and sold biscuits and bread.

Once, an official in Patna asked him to prove that he really was a freedom fighter. The man who had stood in the Assembly gallery with Bhagat Singh had to show papers.

<aside class="note"><b>Which official?</b> One account says it was the commissioner of Patna. Another says the district collector.</aside>

Later, he was made a member of the Bihar Legislative Council for a short time. Accounts disagree on when, and for how long. By then, his health was already failing.

## A visit to Khatkar Kalan

In 1963, Batukeshwar went to Punjab. He visited Bhagat Singh's village, Khatkar Kalan, for the first time. The villagers garlanded him with flowers.

Bhagat Singh's mother, Vidyavati Devi, was there. She was an old woman now. She held Batukeshwar and wept, as though she were meeting her own son again.

## The last journey

In 1964, Batukeshwar fell very ill with cancer. He was taken to hospital in Delhi. Vidyavati Devi, by then about 85, often came to sit with him. She was with him in his last days.

Batukeshwar Dutt died on 20 July 1965, in a hospital in Delhi. He was 54.

He was cremated at Hussainiwala, by the river Sutlej in Punjab. That is the same place where his friends Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev and Rajguru had been cremated thirty-four years before.

## Why we should remember him

It is easy to remember heroes who die young. It is harder to remember those who live on, grow old, fall ill and struggle to pay their bills.

Batukeshwar Dutt did both kinds of brave things. He stood beside his friend in 1929. Then he carried on, for thirty-six more years, in a country that had almost forgotten him.

Today a colony in New Delhi, near AIIMS, is named after him: B. K. Dutt Colony.
