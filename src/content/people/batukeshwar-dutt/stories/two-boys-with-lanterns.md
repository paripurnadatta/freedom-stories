Kanpur, in the early 1920s, was a big, busy city of mills and factories. Batukeshwar Dutt's family came from a village in Bengal. But Batukeshwar grew up here, and went to school here.

He was a quiet boy. But something he saw one day changed him.

## The road for Europeans only

In those days, some roads in Kanpur were kept for Europeans. Indians were not allowed to walk on them. One of these was the Mall Road.

One day, a little Indian child wandered onto the Mall Road. A British man saw him. He beat the child badly, just for being there.

Young Batukeshwar saw it happen. He never forgot it. His life as a revolutionary began, people say, with that moment.

<aside class="note"><b>Mall Road</b>: many British-ruled towns in India had a main street called the Mall. It was often the smartest road in town, with shops and offices for Europeans.</aside>

## New friends

Soon after, Batukeshwar met a newspaper editor in Kanpur, who worked for a paper called Pratap. Through him, he met revolutionaries, including a leader named Sachindranath Sanyal.

Then, in 1924, he met someone who would become his closest friend. A Sikh teenager from Punjab had come to Kanpur. His name was Bhagat Singh.

Batukeshwar was about thirteen or fourteen. Bhagat Singh was about sixteen.

## The flood

That same year, the river Ganga flooded. The water rose and poured into the low parts of Kanpur. Homes were swept away. People were caught in the current.

A group of young volunteers came together to help. It was called the Tarun Sangh, which means "youth group". Batukeshwar and Bhagat Singh both joined.

## Lanterns in the dark

Years later, Batukeshwar wrote about one night of that flood.

He and Bhagat Singh stood side by side on the bank of the Ganga, in the dark. Each of them held up a lantern. Anyone who had been swept into the river, and was trying to swim to safety, could see the lights and head for the shore.

They stood there through the night.

<aside class="note"><b>His own words</b>: "Both of us stood by the side of Ganga in night, holding lanterns so that somebody who went into the stream and made an attempt to reach the shore could be saved."</aside>

## Bengali lessons

The two boys became inseparable. Batukeshwar taught Bhagat Singh to speak Bengali.

He also loved the songs and poems of Kazi Nazrul Islam, a Bengali poet who wrote fiery songs about freedom. Batukeshwar sang them to his friend. Bhagat Singh learned to love them too.

## Why it matters

Five years later, these two friends would stand together again, in the Assembly in Delhi. The whole country would know their names.

But it began here, in Kanpur, with two boys holding lanterns in the dark, so that strangers could find their way home.
