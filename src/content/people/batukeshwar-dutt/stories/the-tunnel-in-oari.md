In December 1928, a British police officer named John Saunders was shot dead in Lahore. The police were sure Bhagat Singh had been involved. They searched everywhere for him.

Bhagat Singh had to get far away from Punjab. And he had to stop looking like Bhagat Singh.

## A new look

Bhagat Singh was a Sikh. He wore a turban and had long hair. To escape, he cut his hair. He put on a long coat and a hat, like an Englishman.

In this disguise, he travelled all the way across India, to Calcutta.

There, he met his old friend from Kanpur, Batukeshwar Dutt.

## Working in Calcutta

Batukeshwar had been living in Calcutta. He worked there with a party that organised workers and farmers. He knew the city and its people well.

## Home to Oari

So Batukeshwar took his friend home.

His family came from a small village called Oari, in the Burdwan district of Bengal. It was quiet, far from any big city. Who would look for a Punjabi revolutionary there?

For a while, the two friends stayed in the village.

## Underground

But even in Oari, they were not safe for long. The police got wind of where they were.

So the two friends went underground. In Oari, there was a tunnel. They hid inside it.

<aside class="note"><b>The tunnel today</b>: one account says the tunnel where they hid can still be seen in the village today.</aside>

Look at the picture of the old brick house in Oari. It was Batukeshwar's family home. It still stands in the village.

## What they planned next

Hiding was not enough for Bhagat Singh and his friends. They wanted to do something that would make the whole country listen.

In 1929, their group, the HSRA, planned to throw harmless bombs in the Central Legislative Assembly in Delhi, and be arrested on purpose.

Who would do it? The group argued about it. One account says the first names chosen were Bhagat Singh and Sukhdev. The usually calm Batukeshwar was furious. He was one of the oldest members of the group, he said. He must be included.

<aside class="note"><b>Another version</b>: a different account says the first plan was for Dutt and Sukhdev to do it, while Bhagat Singh went abroad. Later the plan changed to Bhagat Singh and Dutt.</aside>

In the end, it was Bhagat Singh and Batukeshwar Dutt who went to the Assembly on 8 April 1929.

The two friends who had hidden together in a tunnel in Bengal now stood together in front of the whole country.
