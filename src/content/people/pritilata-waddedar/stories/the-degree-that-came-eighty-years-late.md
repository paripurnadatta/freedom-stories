On 6 February 1932, the University of Calcutta held its convocation. A convocation is the ceremony where students walk up and receive their degrees.

Pritilata Waddedar's degree was due that day. She did not collect it.

It stayed uncollected for eighty years.

## The student

Her results had come steadily. Banglapedia says she passed matriculation in the first division in 1927, and the Intermediate in 1929. Those were two big exams in two years, and she did very well in both.

Pritilata had been a star student for years. At Eden College in Dhaka, she joined a group of young women called the Sree Sangha. Its leader was Leela Nag. Banglapedia says it was linked to the Dipali Sangha.

<aside class="note"><b>Sangha</b>: a Bengali word for a society or association.</aside>

Then she moved to Calcutta. There she joined another group, the Chhatri Sangha, led by Kalyani Das. And she studied at Bethune College.

Her next target was a degree. Her subject was philosophy. Philosophy is the study of big questions, such as what is true and how we should live. She passed with distinction.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: Banglapedia says she took her degree two years after the Intermediate, which would be 1931. The Dhaka Tribune also says 1931. The Business Standard says she graduated in 1932. The sources do not agree, so we do not give one year.</aside>

## Back in Chittagong

After her studies she went back to Chittagong. There she became the headmistress of a school, the Nandankanan Aparnacharan School.

So the first years of her working life were spent teaching. The Dhaka Tribune says that when she returned, her brother was already in a group working against the British, and she began by carrying messages for it.

## The missing certificate

The degree did not reach her hands. What happened to it?

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: Wikipedia says Calcutta University held back her degree. The Business Standard says it was simply not collected at the convocation. Both agree that it was not given to her in 1932.</aside>

She died in September of that year, at the age of twenty-one.

## Eighty years later

Eighty years is a very long time. A child born in 1932 would have been an old person by 2012.

In 2012, the university finally gave her the degree. The Business Standard gives the date as 22 March 2012. It was given after her death, and so was the degree of another woman revolutionary, Bina Das.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: Wikipedia and The Business Standard call it a degree. The Dhaka Tribune calls it a merit certificate. All agree that it came in 2012.</aside>

## Why it matters

A person can pass an exam and still be left out of the record. Colleges and schools sometimes mend such gaps late. The certificate of 2012 says what her teachers could not say in 1932: she was a first-rate student before she was anything else.
