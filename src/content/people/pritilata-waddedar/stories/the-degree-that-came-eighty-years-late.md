Imagine passing every exam, and then never receiving your certificate. For Pritilata Waddedar, it took eighty years.

## Student days

At Eden College in Dhaka, Pritilata joined a group called the Sree Sangha. Banglapedia says it was linked to the Dipali Sangha, run by Leela Nag. Wikipedia also names Leela Nag as the leader of the group she joined.

<aside class="note"><b>Sangha</b>: a Bengali word for a society or association.</aside>

Later she moved to Calcutta. There, Banglapedia says, she was in another group, the Chhatri Sangha, whose leader was Kalyani Das. She studied at Bethune College.

Wikipedia says that she studied philosophy and passed with distinction. Philosophy is the study of big questions, such as what is true and how we should live. She had already done very well in her Intermediate exam in 1929. Our sources do not say what she thought of the subject.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: Banglapedia says she took her degree two years after the Intermediate, which would be 1931. The Dhaka Tribune also says 1931. The Business Standard says she graduated in 1932. The sources do not agree, so we do not give one year.</aside>

## After Bethune

When her studies ended, she went back to Chittagong. There she became the headmistress of a school, the Nandankanan Aparnacharan School. Wikipedia and Banglapedia both say so.

So the first years of her working life were spent teaching. The Dhaka Tribune adds that when she returned, her brother was already in a group working against the British. It says she first carried messages for it.

The certificate reminds us that she was a student and a teacher before anything else.

## The missing certificate

The Business Standard says that her degree was due to be given out at the University of Calcutta's convocation on 6 February 1932. A convocation is the ceremony where graduates receive their degrees. She did not collect hers.

Wikipedia says that Calcutta University held back her degree. The Business Standard says it was simply not collected. We cannot tell from these sources which of the two is the full truth.

## 2012

In 2012, eighty years later, the university gave her the degree. The Business Standard gives the date as 22 March 2012. The same honour went to another woman revolutionary, Bina Das. Wikipedia says both degrees were given after their deaths.

The Dhaka Tribune calls it a merit certificate from Calcutta University in 2012.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: Wikipedia and The Business Standard call it a degree, the Dhaka Tribune a merit certificate. Both agree that it came in 2012.</aside>

## Why it matters

A person can pass an exam and still be left out of the record. Schools and colleges sometimes mend such gaps late. When they do, they remind us that her studies were real, and that she was a first-rate student before she was anything else.
