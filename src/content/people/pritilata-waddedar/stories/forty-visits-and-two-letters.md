Forty times, a young woman went to a jail to see a man who was going to be hanged.

She wrote it down herself. How do we know it is true? A court counted the visits. And two letters were found in a hut.

## The prisoner

His name was Ram Krishna Biswas. On 2 December 1930, he and another young man, Kalipada Chakravarty, were arrested. A man named Tarini Mukherjee had been killed. Wikipedia says the two had killed him by mistake.

Biswas was tried and sentenced to death. He was held in Alipore Jail in Calcutta.

At that time a young woman from Chittagong was studying in Calcutta. Her name was Pritilata Waddedar.

She had been at Eden College in Dhaka, where she joined a group of young women called the Sree Sangha. In Calcutta she joined another, the Chhatri Sangha. She studied philosophy at Bethune College.

Her brother, the Dhaka Tribune says, was already in a group working against the British. She had begun by carrying messages.

## The visits

Pritilata went to Alipore Jail again and again.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: Wikipedia says she got in "disguised as his cousin sister". We found this in only one source. Banglapedia says she visited him in 1930. The court record says forty visits while he waited for his sentence. She may have started in 1930 and gone on after.</aside>

Later, a note was found on her body. In it she wrote that she had visited him forty times while he waited to be hanged.

The judges in Calcutta did not simply believe her. They checked. The jail registers agreed with what she said.

What did they talk about? Another paper in the case, Exhibit 105, describes her conversations with him. The court quoted a few lines. We do not have more.

The judges also noticed something else. The papers in the case showed that she had "the highest regard" for Ram Krishna.

<aside class="note"><b>Guesses</b>: a British official wrote a report at the time that hinted at more. Wikipedia says no other source supports those claims. We do not repeat them. They are guesses, and they help nobody.</aside>

## How a court checks a story

The judges did not take her word for the visits. They set her note beside the jail registers. They set the letters beside the house where they were found. They set a witness beside the house too. Each piece was a small test.

The judges wrote that the statement by Pritilata "has been proved". That did not mean every word in it was true. It meant that the court had checked what it could, and the checks agreed.

## The hut at Dhalghat

In June 1932, Pritilata was in a village called Dhalghat, near Chittagong. Surya Sen and his friends were hiding in a house there. A British officer, Captain Cameron, was killed at Dhalghat on 13 June. Then the police searched the house.

On 22 June, they found two letters. They had been written by Ram Krishna Biswas to Pritilata in July 1931. He had written them from jail.

Biswas had written them to her in July 1931.

Why did two old letters matter? A court was trying to decide whether she had been at Dhalghat. Letters with her name on them, found in that house, answered the question. The judges decided she had been there, and that she had "been compelled to leave there in haste".

<aside class="note"><b>The widow of Dhalghat</b>: our story about Dhalghat tells what happened to the woman who owned that house.</aside>

## The girl in the house

There was one more witness. A girl named Snehalata, witness number 26 in the case, had been in the house. The judgment describes her as about fourteen years old.

Snehalata said that Priti had been downstairs. Surya Sen had been upstairs.

But when Snehalata first spoke to the Magistrate, she had not mentioned Priti. She admitted this. She gave one word as her reason. The word was "lojja". It means shyness, or delicacy.

The court found that both Priti and Surya Sen had been in the house at Dhalghat.

## Kalpana remembers

Kalpana Dutt, Pritilata's friend, remembered the same days in her book. It was Kalpana who first told Preeti about Masterda. Preeti had never met him.

A court paper, Exhibit 257, also records how Priti was introduced to Nirmal Sen, who was with Surya Sen at Dhalghat.

When the police were close, Preeti was "unknown to the police". She was not hiding from anyone. She "had merely come to see Masterda".

One of the men, Nirmal Sen, had been wounded. Preeti did not want to leave him. In the end Masterda "tore himself away from Nirmalda, together with Preeti".

After that she went into hiding.

## The notice with two photographs

From then on, the police were looking for her. A police gazette of 22 July 1932 carried a notice. It offered fifty rupees to anyone who could trace "Miss Prithi Waddadar". It said she was about twenty or twenty-one, and looked younger than her age. It asked police to keep "a special look-out" for her, and to watch her closely but quietly.

The notice came from the Bengal Criminal Intelligence Gazette. This was a paper the police printed for their own officers. The page you see here is under the heading "Reward". It is dated 22 July 1932, only a month after Dhalghat. The police now had her name and her description in print, and they asked their officers to use them.

The notice printed two photographs. One was from her days at college in Dhaka. The other was found when the police searched the belongings of a young man named Apurba Sen, who was called Bhola. They searched them after Dhalghat.

## Why it matters

We often think of a revolutionary as a person made only of courage. This story shows something quieter. She was loyal to a friend. She kept his letters. She walked into a jail forty times to see a man who was going to be hanged.

A court counted the visits. A friend remembered the hut. A police notice kept two photographs. Together they show us a real young woman, and what she chose to do.
