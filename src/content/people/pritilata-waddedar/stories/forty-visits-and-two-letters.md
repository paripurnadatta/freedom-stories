How many times would you visit a jail to see a friend? Once? Twice?

Pritilata Waddedar wrote that she went forty times. The man she went to see was Ram Krishna Biswas. He was a prisoner, and he was waiting to be hanged.

Why did a young student do that? And how do we know it is true? The answers are in a court record, a friend's book and a police notice.

## Who was Ram Krishna Biswas?

Wikipedia tells us the outline. Ram Krishna Biswas and another young man, Kalipada Chakravarty, were arrested on 2 December 1930. A man named Tarini Mukherjee had been killed. Wikipedia says the two killed him by mistake.

They were tried. Biswas was sentenced to death. He was held in Alipore Jail in Calcutta.

At that time Pritilata was a student in Calcutta. She had finished her studies at Dhaka and then went to Bethune College. So the jail was not far from her.

## The visits

Wikipedia says she met him in Alipore Jail "disguised as his cousin sister". We should be careful with this detail. It is a claim in one source, and we have not found it in a second.

Banglapedia also says she went to see him in Alipore Central Jail. It gives the year as 1930.

The court record gives us more. The judges wrote that she herself said, in the paper found on her body, that she visited him forty times while he waited for his sentence. The judges then did something careful. They checked. They wrote that the jail records agreed with her. So the number was not boasting. It was in the books of the jail.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: Banglapedia places her visit to the jail in 1930. The court record speaks of forty visits while he waited for execution. These do not have to disagree. She may have started in 1930 and gone on after. But our sources do not give a full list of dates.</aside>

## Why did she go?

We do not have her words about her feelings. We have what the judges found.

They wrote that the documents in the case showed that she had "the highest regard" for Ram Krishna. That is all. We do not know what they talked about for those forty meetings.

Another document in the case, called Exhibit 105, describes her talks with him in jail. The court quoted from it. We have only the few lines that the judges chose.

Some writers have said more than this. A report by a British official, written at the time, made nasty hints about the two. Wikipedia says that these claims are not backed by any other source. We do not repeat them. They are guesses, and they help nobody.

What the record does show is simple. She respected him. She visited him again and again.

## The two letters

The second part of the story is at a hut in Dhalghat.

In June 1932, the police searched the house at Dhalghat where Surya Sen and his friends had been hiding. This is the same house that our story "The widow of Dhalghat" tells about. Captain Cameron, a British officer, had been killed there on 13 June.

On 22 June the police found two letters in the house. They had been written by Ram Krishna Biswas to Pritilata in July 1931. That was when he was still in jail.

Why did that matter to the court? Because the judges were trying to answer a question: was she at Dhalghat? Letters addressed to her, found in that house, were a clue.

The judges decided that the letters showed she had been there. They wrote that she had "been compelled to leave there in haste". We do not know how the letters were left behind. The court says only that she had to run.

## Two witnesses

There were two other witnesses on whether she was at Dhalghat.

One was a girl called Snehalata. The judgment describes her as about fourteen years old. She said that Priti was downstairs in the house while Surya Sen was upstairs.

But Snehalata had said nothing about Priti when she first spoke to the Magistrate. She admitted this. She gave one word as her reason: "lojja". It means shyness, or delicacy.

The court found that both Priti and Surya Sen were in the house at Dhalghat.

Kalpana Dutt, Pritilata's friend, remembered it in her own book. She wrote that Preeti was "unknown to the police" at that time. She was not hiding from anyone. She had "merely come to see Masterda". Masterda was the teacher, Surya Sen.

Kalpana says that when the time came to leave, Preeti did not want to abandon the wounded Nirmal Sen. In the end Masterda "tore himself away from Nirmalda, together with Preeti".

## The notice with two photographs

After that, she was a wanted woman. A police gazette of 22 July 1932 carried her name. It offered fifty rupees to anyone who could trace "Miss Prithi Waddadar". It said she looked younger than her age.

The notice printed two photographs. One was from her college days at Dhaka. The other was found when the police searched the belongings of a young man named Apurba Sen, who was called Bhola. They searched them after Dhalghat.

The picture on this page is the notice itself. It asks police to keep "a special look-out" for her.

## Why it matters

It is easy to think of a revolutionary as a person made only of courage. This story shows something quieter. She was loyal to a friend. She kept letters. She walked into a jail forty times to see a man who was going to be hanged.

We also learn how a historian works. A court counted visits. A friend remembered a hut. A police notice kept two photographs. Each source gives a small piece. Together they show us a real young woman, and what she chose to do.
