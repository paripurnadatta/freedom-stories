If you visit Pahartali, near Chittagong, you can still see the building. It is a low bungalow with red walls, set among trees. It was once the European Club.

In September 1932, about forty people were inside. That was the night of the attack that Pritilata Waddedar led.

## The club today

A report in the Dhaka Tribune in April 2023 describes what has become of it. The red bungalow is locked. Its condition is called a shambles. The report's title says the club is "lost in oblivion". That means nearly forgotten.

So the place where the attack happened has not been looked after like a museum.

<aside class="note"><b>A sign that may not be there</b>: several writers say a sign once hung at the club that read "Dogs and Indians not allowed". Wikipedia and the Dhaka Tribune repeat it as part of the club's story. We could not find a photograph of the sign or a record that mentions it, so we do not treat it as proved.</aside>

The judges of 1933 wrote that the club had been chosen because its users were Europeans and Anglo-Indians. The club was not for all the people of Chittagong.

## The statue in front

Others have honoured her more carefully.

In front of the old club, a bronze sculpture called "Birkanya Pritilata" was opened on 2 October 2012. "Birkanya" means a brave girl.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: The Business Standard says the sculpture stands in front of the former club. The Dhaka Tribune says a bronze sculpture of 2012 stands in front of the school next to the club. They describe the same area in different words.</aside>

## Across two countries

In Dhalghat, her birth village, there is another bust. Chittagong has a road with her name. At Chittagong University and at Jahangirnagar University, halls are named after her. The Daily Star mentions a trust that carries her name, the Birkannya Pritilata Trust.

Across the border, there is a statue of her on the Maidan in Calcutta. In the photograph on Wikimedia Commons, a woman stands with one arm raised.

## Films and plays

Her story has also reached the screen. Wikipedia lists films about her from 2010 and 2012, and a monologue on the BBC in 2018. The writer Selina Hossain, in the Daily Star, calls her an ideal for every woman.

Not everybody sees her that way. Some people remember most the woman who died at the club. A fair story keeps both memories.

## Where she was born

At Dhalghat, trees and grass have grown over the ruins at her birthplace. A photograph on Wikimedia Commons shows it as it was in October 2010.

## Why it matters

A country chooses what to remember. Pritilata has statues in two countries, while the building from that night is locked and crumbling. Perhaps people remember a person more easily than a place. Perhaps it is only a lack of money. Nothing we read says which.
