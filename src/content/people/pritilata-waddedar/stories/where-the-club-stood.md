If you go to Pahartali near Chittagong, you can see the building. It is the old European Club, and it looks like a bungalow with a red wall.

In September 1932 about forty people were inside it, Wikipedia says. That was the night of the attack that Pritilata Waddedar led.

## The building today

A report in the Dhaka Tribune, published in April 2023, describes what has become of it. The red bungalow is locked, and the writer calls its condition a shambles. The paper's title says the club is "lost in oblivion". That means nearly forgotten.

So the place where the attack happened has not been looked after as a museum.

## Statues and busts

Other places have honoured her more carefully.

The Business Standard says a sculpture called "Birkanya Pritilata" was put up in front of the former club. It was opened on 2 October 2012. "Birkanya" means a brave girl. The Dhaka Tribune says a bronze sculpture of 2012 stands in front of the school next to the club. The two reports describe the same area in slightly different words.

The Business Standard also lists a bust at Dhalghat, her birth village. Halls are named after her at Chittagong University and Jahangirnagar University. Chittagong has a road with her name. And the Daily Star mentions a trust, the Birkannya Pritilata Trust.

Across the border, there is a statue of her in the Maidan in Calcutta. The photograph on Wikimedia Commons shows a woman standing with one arm raised.

## A sign that may not be there

Several writers say that a sign once hung at the club that read "Dogs and Indians not allowed". The Dhaka Tribune and Wikipedia repeat it as part of the club's history.

We could not find a photograph of the sign, or a list from the club that mentions it. So we do not say it as a fact. We say that many people tell it, and that the sign is not proved by anything we opened.

Even without the sign, the judges of 1933 wrote that the club was chosen because its users were Europeans and Anglo-Indians. Its users were not all of the people of Chittagong.

## Why it matters

A country chooses what to remember. Here, a hero has statues in two countries, while the building where the night happened is locked and crumbling. Perhaps that is a sign that people prefer to remember a person more than a place. Perhaps it is only a lack of money. The sources do not say.
