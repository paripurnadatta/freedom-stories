## Research notes

Led agrarian agitation in Punjab and spent years in exile.

### Agrarian politics as anti-colonial politics

The 1907 agitation shows land law, peasant grievances and imperial security converging into a colonial crisis.

### Punjab unrest

The movement should remain a regional agrarian campaign rather than a generic nationalist episode.

### A network across generations

His ties to Lajpat Rai and Bhagat Singh connect early Punjab nationalism to later revolutionary politics.

### Chronology

- **1907**: Punjab agrarian agitation. Becomes a major organizer against colonial land legislation
- **1907**: Mandalay deportation. Deported with Lala Lajpat Rai
- **1907 onward**: Exile and anti-colonial networks. Continues activity through revolutionary and expatriate networks

### Evidence claims

- Born on 23 February 1881 and was an uncle of Bhagat Singh. (high confidence)
- Led agitation against colonial agrarian legislation during the Punjab unrest of 1907. (high confidence)
- Was deported to Mandalay in 1907 along with Lala Lajpat Rai. (high confidence)
- Was associated with the Bharat Mata Society and anti-colonial revolutionary publishing. (high confidence)
- The supplied record connects Ajit Singh with Punjab Agrarian Unrest of 1907 as participant in. (high confidence)
- The supplied record connects Ajit Singh with Lala Lajpat Rai as worked with. (high confidence)
- The supplied record connects Ajit Singh with Bhagat Singh as family relation. (high confidence)
