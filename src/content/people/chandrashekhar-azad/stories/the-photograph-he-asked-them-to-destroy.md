Think of Chandrashekhar Azad, and one picture comes to mind. A strong young man, without a shirt, looks straight at the camera. His hand is on his thick moustache, giving it a twirl.

You can find that picture on posters, in schoolbooks and on walls all over India.

But Azad never wanted anyone to see it.

## A man with no face

Azad was a wanted man for years. The British police had no official photograph of him. That was one of the reasons they could never catch him.

Think about it. If the police do not know your face, you can walk right past them. You can wear a disguise, change your name, and move to a new town.

For Azad, having no photograph was a kind of shield.

## The photographer friend

In Jhansi, Azad hid in the house of a man called Master Rudra Narayan. He was an artist. He painted, he made statues, and he took photographs.

One day, Rudra Narayan took a picture of Azad. That was the famous one, with the moustache. He also took a picture of Azad together with his own family.

## "Destroy it"

Later, Azad left Jhansi. He began to worry about those pictures. What if the police raided the house? What if they found a photograph of him?

So he sent a message to his friend. It was carried by another young revolutionary, Vishwanath Vaishampayan. The message asked Rudra Narayan to destroy the photograph.

Rudra Narayan made a brave choice. He did not destroy it. He hid it safely instead. He believed that one day, people would want to see the face of this hero.

He kept it safe for the rest of his life. British officers tried hard to get hold of it, but they never did. Only after India became free did he let the world see it.

Rudra Narayan was a brave man in other ways too. In 1945, while the British still ruled, he put up a statue of Rani Lakshmibai in Jhansi. Many saw it as a direct challenge to British rule.

<aside class="note"><b>How do we know?</b> The story of the message asking for the photo to be destroyed comes from one news report, which spoke to Rudra Narayan's family. Other reports agree that he took the photo and hid it from the British.</aside>

<aside class="note"><b>Was it the only photo?</b> Some writers call it Azad's "first and last" photograph. Other pictures said to show him exist too. But this is the best-known by far.</aside>

## Why it matters

Rudra Narayan's family still keeps the picture in Jhansi. They were right about it. Today, millions of people know Azad's face because of it.

There is something special in that. Azad hid his face to stay free. His friend saved it so that Azad would be remembered.

Both of them, in their own way, were protecting him.
