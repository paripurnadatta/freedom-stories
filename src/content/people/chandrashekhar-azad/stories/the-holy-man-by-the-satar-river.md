After the Kakori train robbery in 1925, the police caught almost everyone who took part. They searched for one man most of all. His name was Chandrashekhar Azad.

They looked everywhere. But Azad had vanished.

## A friend in Jhansi

Azad had gone to Jhansi. That is the old fort town of the famous queen, Rani Lakshmibai.

A man named Master Rudra Narayan lived there. He was a painter, a sculptor and a photographer. He was also a secret friend of the revolutionaries.

He gave Azad a place to hide. At first, Azad lived in the basement of his house. The family treated him like a younger brother.

But more and more police came to Jhansi. Azad needed a better hiding place.

## The sadhu in the forest

About fifteen kilometres from Jhansi is the town of Orchha. It has thick forests. Near it flows a small river called the Satar.

On the bank of the Satar stood a temple of Lord Hanuman. Azad loved Hanuman. He built a small hut beside the temple, and moved in.

Now he was not Azad any more. He was Pandit Harishankar Brahmachari, a quiet holy man.

<aside class="note"><b>Brahmachari</b>: a person who lives a simple, religious life and does not marry.</aside>

The holy man did not just pray. He taught the children of the nearby village to read and write. The villagers liked him. Nobody guessed who he really was.

## Lessons of a different kind

The forest had another use too. Deep among the trees, out of sight, Azad practised shooting. He was an expert marksman. He trained other young revolutionaries there as well.

He learned one more skill in Jhansi. He learned to drive a car, at a motor garage in the town. For a man who always had to be ready to escape, that was a useful thing to know.

## The village that remembers

Azad hid around Jhansi for a long time. Some accounts say it was about three years. The police never found him there.

They tried to buy him, though. Historians say the British offered Rudra Narayan 25,000 rupees to give Azad away. That was a huge sum in those days. Rudra Narayan said no.

Today, a village near the old hiding place is called Azadpura. It was named in his honour.

And Master Rudra Narayan's family still lives in Jhansi. They have kept Azad's bed, in the house where he once hid.
