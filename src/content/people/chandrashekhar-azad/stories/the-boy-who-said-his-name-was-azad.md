His name at birth was Chandrashekhar Tiwari. He was born on 23 July 1906, in a village called Bhabhra in Central India. Today the area is part of Madhya Pradesh.

As a boy he loved to swim and wrestle. He was good with a bow and arrow too. He was a devoted follower of Lord Hanuman, the strong and loyal god of the Ramayana.

<aside class="note"><b>Where was he born?</b> Most books say Bhabhra. But a village called Badarka, in Unnao in Uttar Pradesh, also says he was born there. Even his birth date is sometimes given differently.</aside>

His mother, Jagrani Devi, had a dream for him. She wanted him to become a great scholar of Sanskrit. So he was sent far away, to study in the holy city of Banaras.

## A city on fire

In 1921, all of India was talking about one thing. Mahatma Gandhi had started the Non-Cooperation Movement. He asked Indians to stop helping the British government. Students left British schools. People burned foreign cloth in the streets.

Chandrashekhar was only fifteen. But he joined the protests too.

One day in December 1921, the police caught him. He was taken to court.

## Three questions

The magistrate looked down at the boy. Then he began to ask the usual questions.

"What is your name?"

"Azad," said the boy. It means "free".

"What is your father's name?"

"Swatantrata." That means "freedom".

"Where do you live?"

"In jail."

The courtroom must have gone very quiet.

<aside class="note"><b>Magistrate</b>: a judge who hears smaller cases in court. Books give this magistrate's name as Khareghat.</aside>

## The punishment

The magistrate was not amused. He ordered that the boy be whipped. Most books say he got fifteen lashes with a cane.

Each lash hurt badly. But the stories say the boy did not cry. With every stroke, he shouted a slogan for his country instead.

<aside class="note"><b>Is it all true?</b> This story comes from the books written about Azad after he died. The tellings differ a little. Some say his father's name was given as "Azadi", not "Swatantrata". Some give a bigger number of lashes. But almost every account agrees on the name he gave: Azad.</aside>

## A new name

People in Banaras heard what the boy had done. They were amazed by his courage. From that day on, everyone called him Chandrashekhar Azad.

He kept that name for the rest of his life.

A year later, Gandhi called off the movement. Azad was very disappointed. He wanted to keep fighting. So he joined a secret group of young revolutionaries.

The schoolboy who said he lived "in jail" made a new promise then. He would never go to jail again.

He never did.

Think about how young he was. Fifteen is only a few years older than a Class 5 student. Yet he stood before a judge and answered without fear. A single word was enough to make him famous.
