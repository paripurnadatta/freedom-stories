Allahabad has a big, green park in the middle of the city. In 1931 it was called Alfred Park. People also called it Company Bagh. It was built in 1870, when a British prince named Alfred came to visit.

On the morning of 27 February 1931, two young men sat talking in that park.

One of them was 24 years old. He had a strong face and a thick moustache. He was the most wanted man in North India.

His name was Chandrashekhar Azad.

## The man nobody could catch

For almost ten years, the British police had chased Azad. They chased him after a train robbery at Kakori in 1925. They chased him after a police officer was shot in Lahore in 1928. Many of his friends were caught. Some were hanged.

Azad was never caught.

He was a master of disguise. He moved from town to town. He used other names. For a while he lived as a holy man near Jhansi. The police did not even have a proper photograph of him. That made him very hard to find.

<aside class="note"><b>HSRA</b>: the Hindustan Socialist Republican Association. It was a secret group of young revolutionaries. Azad was its leader. Bhagat Singh was one of its members.</aside>

Azad had made a promise. He said the police would never take him alive. He would never go to jail again.

## The commander

After Kakori, Azad rebuilt the secret group almost from nothing. In 1928 it got a new name, the HSRA. Azad became its commander-in-chief. On notices, he signed himself with a secret name, Balraj.

He was older than most of the members, but not by much. Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev and Rajguru looked up to him. In December 1928, after a British officer was shot in Lahore, Azad helped them get away.

## A worried winter

The winter of 1931 was a sad time for Azad. His closest friends, Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev and Rajguru, were in Lahore jail. They had been sentenced to death.

Azad wanted to save them. He met leaders in Allahabad to talk about it. One of them was Jawaharlal Nehru.

Nehru later wrote about this meeting in his autobiography. Azad asked him a simple question. If the Congress made peace with the British, could his friends ever live in peace too? Or would they be hunted forever?

Nehru wrote that he had no answer.

## The tip-off

That February morning, Azad went to Alfred Park. With him was a young comrade named Sukhdev Raj. They sat and talked.

They did not know that someone had told the police.

<aside class="note"><b>Who told?</b> An informer gave Azad away. People have argued for almost a hundred years about who it was. Several names have been suggested. Nobody has ever proved it.</aside>

The man who received the message was an Englishman called Nott-Bower. He was a senior police officer. He rushed to the park with his men. The police surrounded it. There was no easy way out.

## The gunfight

Shots rang out across the park.

Azad was hit. But he did not run. He took cover behind a tree. Then he did something brave. He told Sukhdev Raj to escape. He kept firing so that the police would keep their heads down. Sukhdev Raj got away.

Now Azad was alone.

He fought for a long time. One of his bullets hit Nott-Bower in the wrist. Another hit an Indian police officer, Thakur Vishweshwar Singh, in the jaw.

Azad was badly hurt. And he was running out of bullets.

## The promise

What happened next is told in most books in the same way. Azad had one bullet left. He remembered his promise. He would not be taken alive. He used that last bullet on himself.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: almost every account says Azad kept his promise with his last bullet. Nehru, writing a few years later, only said he heard that Azad had been "shot down and killed by the police". So we cannot be fully sure of every detail of those last moments.</aside>

Azad had once said he would face the enemy's bullets and stay free. In Hindi the line goes: *"Azad hi rahe hain, aur Azad hi rahenge."* It means: "Free I have been, and free I will stay."

The park went silent. One account says the police were afraid to go near him, even after he had fallen. They could not quite believe that the man they had chased for years was really gone.

Chandrashekhar Azad was 24 years old.

## What happened next

News of his death raced through Allahabad. Crowds gathered at the park. They shouted slogans in his honour, the same way people had once cheered a brave schoolboy in Banaras.

Nehru was in Delhi that day. He heard the news there.

Azad's body was taken to Rasulabad Ghat by the river and cremated. Kamala Nehru, Jawaharlal's wife, was among those who came.

The tree Azad hid behind became a special place. People came to touch it and leave flowers. Many accounts say the British grew so worried that they cut it down.

Less than a month later, on 23 March 1931, Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev and Rajguru were hanged in Lahore. Azad never learned of it.

## The park today

Alfred Park does not carry a British prince's name any more. It is called Chandrashekhar Azad Park. It covers 133 acres. A tall statue of Azad stands there.

The Allahabad Museum is inside the park too. In its hall lies the pistol Azad held that morning.

Years later, relatives of Bhagat Singh came to the park. They brought soil from Bhagat Singh's village, Khatkar Kalan. They sprinkled it under a jamun tree beside Azad's statue.

The two friends could not meet again in life. But a little earth from one man's village now rests beside the other man's statue.

## Why does this story matter?

Azad started as a boy who shouted slogans in the street. He ended as the leader of a secret army. He was never caught, and he never broke his word.

When people in India want to describe someone who will not give in, they often say one word: Azad. It means "free". Azad kept his name, and his word, to the very end.
