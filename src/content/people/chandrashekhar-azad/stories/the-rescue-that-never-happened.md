In the spring of 1930, Bhagat Singh was in Lahore jail. He was on trial, along with many of his friends. Everyone feared the worst.

Outside the jail, Chandrashekhar Azad was not ready to give up on them.

## A daring plan

Azad's group made a secret plan. They would try to break Bhagat Singh and his comrades out.

A plan like that needed money, people and weapons. The group gathered all three.

One person who helped was a young woman named Durga Devi. The revolutionaries called her Durga Bhabhi. She sold her own jewellery, worth about 3,000 rupees, to help pay for the rescue.

<aside class="note"><b>Bhabhi</b>: a Hindi word for a brother's wife. The young revolutionaries used it as a warm, respectful name for her.</aside>

## The man who made the bombs

Durga Devi's husband was Bhagwati Charan Vohra. He was one of the cleverest members of the group. He wrote the group's papers. He also knew how to make bombs.

Azad trusted him completely. People said Bhagwati Charan was Azad's right hand.

For the rescue, the group needed bombs that would work every time. So Bhagwati Charan made one. Then he went to test it.

## By the river Ravi

On 28 May 1930, Bhagwati Charan and a few comrades walked to a quiet spot on the bank of the river Ravi, near Lahore.

Something went terribly wrong. The bomb went off too early, while it was still in his hand.

His friends ran for help. But they came back too late. Bhagwati Charan died there by the river. He was 28 years old.

A friend later remembered something he used to say. He wanted to die "in such a place and manner that no one may know of it and shed tears." In the end, that is almost what happened.

## Azad's loss

The news hit Azad very hard. He had lost his right hand.

For about a month after that, Durga Devi stayed in hiding in the same place as Azad. The police were looking for both of them.

Without Bhagwati Charan, the rescue plan could not go on. It was given up.

## What came after

On 7 October 1930, the court gave its verdict. Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev and Rajguru were sentenced to death.

Durga Devi did not stop fighting. She was later arrested and spent three years in prison. After India became free, she lived quietly and opened a school for poor children in Lucknow.

The rescue never happened. But it shows how far Azad and his friends were ready to go for each other.
