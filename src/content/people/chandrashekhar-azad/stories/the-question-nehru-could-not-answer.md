Early in 1931, Jawaharlal Nehru had just come out of prison. His family home was in Allahabad.

One day, a visitor came to see him. Nehru knew the name at once. Every policeman in North India knew it too.

It was Chandrashekhar Azad.

## Two kinds of freedom fighter

Nehru and Azad both wanted India to be free. But they had chosen very different paths.

Nehru was a leader of the Indian National Congress. Like Gandhi, he believed in fighting the British without weapons. People marched, went on strike and went to jail.

Azad led a secret group that used guns and bombs. He had been on the run for years.

So why would Azad come to Nehru?

## A time of talks

In those weeks, there was a lot of talk about peace. Gandhi had just been let out of prison. Soon he would hold talks with the British Viceroy, Lord Irwin. Many people hoped the two sides would reach an agreement.

<aside class="note"><b>Viceroy</b>: the most powerful British official in India. He ruled on behalf of the British king.</aside>

Azad had heard this too. And he was worried.

Three of his closest friends, Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev and Rajguru, were waiting to be hanged in Lahore. One writer who knew Azad says he went to Nehru to plead for them.

## The question

Nehru wrote about the visit a few years later, in a book about his life.

He said Azad wanted to know one thing. If the Congress made peace with the British, would people like him get any peace too?

Then Azad added something sadder. What was he supposed to do? The police were hunting him all the time. He was given no chance to settle down.

Nehru tried to explain his own ideas. He believed violence would not win India its freedom.

But then he wrote an honest line. He said he "had no answer" to Azad's basic question: what was he to do now?

## A few weeks later

Not long after that meeting, on 27 February 1931, Azad was surrounded by police in Alfred Park in Allahabad. He died there.

Nehru was in Delhi when he heard the news.

<aside class="note"><b>Different memories</b>: people who wrote about this meeting later tell different versions of what was said. Nehru's own book is the only account written by someone who was in the room.</aside>

## Why it matters

This story is not about who was right. It is about two men who loved the same country and could not agree on how to free it.

Azad asked a hard question. Nehru, to his credit, did not pretend to know the answer.
