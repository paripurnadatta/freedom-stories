On 9 August 1925, a train was chugging towards Lucknow. It was the Number 8 Down, coming from Shahjahanpur.

Near a village called Kakori, somebody pulled the emergency chain. The train screeched to a stop.

The man who pulled it was Rajendra Lahiri. And he was not alone.

## Why stop a train?

Lahiri belonged to a secret group called the Hindustan Republican Association, or HRA. Its leader was Ram Prasad Bismil. The group wanted to drive the British out of India by force.

To do that, they needed money. They needed it to buy weapons and print leaflets.

Bismil knew that this train carried money bags in the guard's van. The money had been collected by the British government. So, the group thought, it was really India's money. They decided to take it.

## The robbery

About ten young men took part. One of them was nineteen years old. His name was Chandrashekhar Azad.

The men went to the guard's van. They broke open the money box. By one count, they took about 8,000 rupees.

Then something went wrong. A passenger, a lawyer named Ahmad Ali, had stepped down from the train. A shot was fired by mistake, and he was killed. The revolutionaries had not planned to hurt anyone. But now a man was dead.

<aside class="note"><b>HRA</b>: the Hindustan Republican Association, a secret group set up in 1924. In 1928 it became the HSRA, with Azad as its leader.</aside>

## The great hunt

The British were furious. This was not just a robbery. It was a challenge to their rule.

The police began a huge search. On 26 September 1925, they arrested many members of the group in one sweep. Bismil was caught at Shahjahanpur. Ashfaqulla Khan escaped for more than a year, but he was caught in Delhi in December 1926.

The trial went on for a long time. In 1927, four men were sentenced to death: Ram Prasad Bismil, Ashfaqulla Khan, Rajendra Lahiri and Roshan Singh. All four were hanged in December 1927.

## The empty chair

But one name was missing from the courtroom.

Chandrashekhar Azad had slipped away. He was one of the very few in the group who were never arrested.

How did he do it? He moved quietly from place to place. He changed his name and his clothes. For a long time he hid near Jhansi, dressed as a holy man.

The police kept searching. Still nobody caught him.

## What came next

Azad was now one of the last free leaders of the group. He did not give up. In 1928 he helped to build the group again, with young new members. One of them was Bhagat Singh.

The friends who were hanged for Kakori never saw their dream come true. But the one who got away carried it forward.
