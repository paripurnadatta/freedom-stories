In the summer of 1925, a secret group of young revolutionaries had a problem. They wanted to fight the British. But they had almost no money.

Guns cost money. Printing leaflets cost money. Even travelling from town to town cost money.

The group was called the Hindustan Republican Association, or HRA. Its leader was Ram Prasad Bismil.

## A daring idea

Bismil had noticed something. Trains carried money collected by the British government from one town to another. The money was kept in a safe in the guard's van.

What if they stopped one of those trains, and took the money? It was India's money, they said, collected by the British from Indian people.

On 8 August 1925, the group met in Shahjahanpur to decide.

## The voice against

One member was not happy. It was Ashfaqulla Khan, Bismil's closest friend.

Ashfaq argued against the plan. We do not know all his reasons. But it is easy to see the danger. A train robbery would bring the whole might of the British police down on their small group.

## Standing by his friends

The group did not change its mind. The plan would go ahead.

Ashfaq now had to choose. He could walk away. Or he could stay with his friends, even though he had argued against the plan.

He chose to stay.

<aside class="note"><b>Loyalty</b>: being faithful to your friends, even when things are hard. It does not always mean agreeing with them.</aside>

## The night of 9 August

The very next evening, 9 August 1925, the plan was carried out. The train was stopped near a station called Kakori, not far from Lucknow. The group broke open the safe in the guard's van and took the money bags.

Ashfaq handed his pistol, a Mauser, to another member of the group, Manmath Nath Gupta.

Then something went terribly wrong. A passenger named Ahmad Ali, a lawyer, had stepped down from the train. A shot was fired, and he was killed. Most accounts say the shot came from Manmath Nath Gupta.

Nobody had planned for anyone to die. But it had happened.

<aside class="note"><b>How much money?</b> Old records do not agree. A court history gives a little over 4,553 rupees. Other accounts give about 4,600, or even 8,000.</aside>

## He had been right

Ashfaq's fears came true. The British police hunted the group down. Within two years, Ashfaq, Bismil and two others had been hanged. Many more went to prison for years.

Ashfaq had said no. Then he had gone along anyway, because his friends were going.

Was that right? People still argue about it. But nobody has ever doubted his courage, or his loyalty.
