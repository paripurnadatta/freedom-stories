Shahjahanpur is an old town in Uttar Pradesh. A hundred years ago, two young men lived there who would become one of the most famous pairs of friends in Indian history.

One was Ram Prasad. He wrote poems under the name Bismil. He was a Hindu, and a member of the Arya Samaj.

The other was Ashfaqulla Khan. He was a Muslim, from a Pathan family. He wrote poems too, in Urdu.

People often say that India's freedom was won by Hindus and Muslims together. The story of these two friends shows what that looked like.

## The youngest son

Ashfaqulla Khan was born on 22 October 1900, in Shahjahanpur. He was the youngest child in his family. His father, Shafiq Ullah, was a landowner. His family had a long tradition of military service.

As a boy, Ashfaq went to a school in the town, the Abbie Rich Mission High School.

## Stories from a big brother

Ashfaq had an elder brother named Riyasat. Riyasat had been in the same class as Ram Prasad Bismil.

By then, Bismil was already famous in Shahjahanpur. He had been part of a secret plot against the British, and had been on the run. Riyasat often told his little brother about Bismil's courage. He told him about Bismil's Urdu poems too.

Ashfaq was fascinated. He wanted to meet this man.

## How they met

At first, Bismil was not interested in the young boy who kept trying to meet him. He simply ignored him.

Then, during the Non-Cooperation Movement, Bismil spoke at public meetings in Shahjahanpur. At one of them, Ashfaq walked up and introduced himself. He said he was the younger brother of Bismil's old classmate. He said he wrote poems too.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: one book says they first met at school, when Ashfaq was in Class 8 and Bismil in Class 9. A memorial trust in Faizabad says they met at a public meeting in 1922. Both are later accounts, and we cannot be sure which is right.</aside>

## Poets together

Ashfaq wrote his poems under pen names. One account gives them as Warsi and Hasrat. Another spells them Varsi and Hazrat.

When Bismil heard Ashfaq's couplets, he was impressed. The two became close friends. Bismil often helped polish Ashfaq's poems. At poetry gatherings called mushairas, people loved hearing the two of them.

<aside class="note"><b>Mushaira</b>: a gathering where poets read out their poems, usually in Urdu, to a live audience.</aside>

## One plate, one name

One of their biographers tells a lovely story about them.

In those days, many Hindus and Muslims would not eat from the same plate. These two did. They ate their meals from the same dish. And Ashfaq gave his friend a special name. He called him simply "Ram".

<aside class="note"><b>A story told later</b>: this detail comes from a biography written many years afterwards. We have no letter or diary from the time that proves it. But it shows how people remember their friendship.</aside>

## A school and a choice

In 1920 and 1921, Gandhi's Non-Cooperation Movement swept across India. Ashfaq joined it. One account says his headmaster expelled him from school for it.

In 1921, both friends went to the Congress meeting in Ahmedabad.

But in February 1922, Gandhi called off the movement. Many young men felt let down. Bismil and Ashfaq decided to take a different, more dangerous path.

## The secret group

In 1924, a secret group was set up, the Hindustan Republican Association, or HRA. Bismil became one of its leaders. Ashfaq joined too. The group wanted to drive the British out of India by force.

To buy weapons, they needed money. So they planned to rob a train carrying government money.

At first, Ashfaq argued against the plan. He thought it was too risky. But once the group decided, he stood by his friends.

## Kakori

On the evening of 9 August 1925, near a village called Kakori close to Lucknow, the group stopped a train. They broke open the safe in the guard's van and took the money.

During the robbery, a passenger named Ahmad Ali was shot dead by mistake. The young men had not meant to kill anyone. But now the British would hunt them without mercy.

Ashfaq was there that night. He handed his own pistol, a Mauser, to another member of the group, Manmath Nath Gupta.

Within three months, Bismil was arrested. Ashfaq escaped. He stayed on the run for more than a year.

## Apart

For more than a year, the two friends were apart. Bismil was in jail in Lucknow, on trial with the rest of the group. Ashfaq was hiding. For a while, he even worked as a clerk in a faraway town, under a false name.

Then, in Delhi, a man he trusted gave him away to the police. Ashfaq was arrested.

The police wanted him to give evidence against Bismil and the others. If he did, he might save his own life. Ashfaq refused.

The main trial went on for more than a year. In April 1927, the judge sentenced Bismil and two others to death. By then, the main trial was over. So Ashfaq was tried separately, with one other member of the group, Sachindra Nath Bakshi. Bakshi was sentenced to prison for life. Ashfaq was given the same sentence as his friend Bismil. Death.

## Two prisons, one day

The friends appealed. In August 1927, judges in Lucknow heard the appeals. They did not change the sentences.

On 19 December 1927, Ram Prasad Bismil was hanged in Gorakhpur jail.

On the very same day, in another town, Ashfaqulla Khan was hanged in Faizabad jail.

Bismil was 30. Ashfaq was 27.

## Why they are remembered together

Today, schools, roads and parks are named after these two men. Their story has been told in films and television series too. In the 2006 film Rang De Basanti, the actor Kunal Kapoor played Ashfaqulla Khan. And very often, their names are said together: Bismil and Ashfaq.

They came from different religions. They prayed in different ways. But they shared their poems, their plates, their dreams, and in the end, their fate.

When people want to show what friendship across faiths can mean, they still tell the story of Ram and Ashfaq.
