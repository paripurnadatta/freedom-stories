In 1927, Ashfaqulla Khan sat in a small cell in Faizabad jail. He had been sentenced to death for his part in the Kakori train robbery.

His lawyers appealed. In August 1927, two judges of the Chief Court in Lucknow, Sir Louis Stuart and Justice Raza, heard the appeals in the Kakori case. They did not change his sentence. Four men would hang: Ashfaq, his friend Bismil, Rajendra Lahiri and Roshan Singh.

His fellow prisoner in the second trial, Sachindra Nath Bakshi, was luckier. He was sentenced to prison for life instead.

There was nothing more to be done.

## A diary in a cell

Ashfaq was alone in his cell. But he was not idle.

A memorial trust in Faizabad says that he kept a diary while he was in prison. One line from it is often quoted: "Only for the love of our country I suffer so much."

<aside class="note"><b>Is the diary real?</b> The memorial trust has printed parts of it. But historians have not yet been able to check the words against the original handwritten pages. So we call them words "said to be" his.</aside>

## Not a murderer

Ashfaq was a deeply religious man. He believed that God knew what was in his heart.

During the robbery at Kakori, a passenger had been killed by mistake. Ashfaq did not want people to think of him as a murderer. Before he died, he said that his hands were not stained with anyone's murder.

## The morning

The date was set: 19 December 1927.

That morning, one biography says, Ashfaq woke early. He bathed. He prayed. He read from the Quran, as he did every day.

Then the guards came for him.

He walked out of his cell carrying a bag. Inside was his copy of the Quran. He walked calmly.

<aside class="note"><b>His last words</b>: books tell this moment in different ways. One says he called out "Khuda Hafiz", which means "May God protect you", a way of saying goodbye. Others say he recited the Kalma, the Muslim declaration of faith. Nobody wrote his words down at the time, so we cannot know exactly.</aside>

## Two friends, one day

Ashfaq did not die alone in spirit. On that same morning, his closest friend, Ram Prasad Bismil, was hanged too, in Gorakhpur jail, far away.

Two days earlier, another comrade, Rajendra Lahiri, had been hanged in Gonda. Roshan Singh was hanged on 19 December too, in Allahabad.

Four young men gave their lives for the Kakori case.

## What he left behind

Ashfaq left behind a handful of poems, a few photographs, and a story.

His name is still said together with his friend's: Bismil and Ashfaq.

He was 27 years old. He had spent the last year of his life in a prison cell, writing.
