Ashfaqulla Khan was hanged on 19 December 1927. He was 27 years old. For him, the story ended there. For the people who remembered him, it did not.

This story is about what came after. It is about a family, a grave, a statue and a film. It is also about a friendship that people still talk about.

## The place where he died

Ashfaq was hanged in the district jail at Faizabad. The platform where it happened is still there, says the National Book Trust book on the Kakori prisoners. A life-size statue of Ashfaq now stands at the place.

Only one source says so, but a memorial website agrees about the jail. It calls the Faizabad jail Ashfaq's "final home", the place where he wrote his last verses.

## His grave

He is buried in his home town, Shahjahanpur. There is a memorial grave there, called the Ashfaqullah Khan Smarak, and people gather there every 19 December. ThePrint, a news website, also says a memorial grave was set up in Shahjahanpur.

the memorial website is run by admirers of Ashfaq and does not name its makers. It is trusted here for the grave and the gathering, because ThePrint says the same. It is not relied on for things no one else says.

No source describes the burial itself. Who was there, and what was said, is not on record.

## What happened to his family

Ashfaq's grandson, Shahdabullah, remembers what came after the hanging. The British government took away the family's property. His grandmother's family was well off, and that helped the family to get by.

He also said something sad. People seem to remember Ashfaq only twice a year, on Independence Day and on the day he died.

These are one man's words about his own family. They are not a record from a court. Still, they remind us that a hanging leaves a family behind.

His father's side was Pathan, and his father was a kotwal, a police officer in the old style. His eldest brother told him stories of Bismil's bravery and his Urdu poetry, long before the two men met. This too is the grandson's account.

## A friend's words

Ashfaq's best-known friend was Ram Prasad Bismil. In his own autobiography, written in prison, Bismil wrote a whole passage addressed to Ashfaq.

Ashfaq started as a friend and became like a brother, Bismil wrote. People were surprised that a devout Arya Samaj Hindu and a devout Muslim could be friends. When friends doubted Ashfaq, he walked straight ahead on his own path. When some Muslims scolded him for his friendship, he paid no attention.

The newspaper Counterview says Bismil's memoir uses this friendship as proof that Hindu-Muslim unity is possible. Ashfaq, before his execution, asked Hindus and Muslims to stop quarrelling and work together for the country.

Bismil made one more point in the same memoir. Men who took part in communal riots were given lighter punishments, he wrote, while freedom fighters who avoided needless violence were given death. Ashfaq was one of those freedom fighters. This is Bismil's opinion, not a court finding.

## His own words

Ashfaq wrote poems under pen names. The National Book Trust book gives them as Varsi and Hazrat. The memorial website and Wikipedia give the name Hasrat.

In one poem he says he lives only to serve his motherland. In another, he says that whether he gets a long sentence or the death sentence, he will sing her praises even with chained hands.

Three days before the hanging, he wrote a poem in Faizabad jail. One line in English reads: "Worn down by their tyranny, we depart toward nothingness from Faizabad's prison."

the original handwriting of these poems was not seen. They are given as the book and the website give them, and are not called certain.

## A new name for the group

After Ashfaq, Bismil, Lahiri and Roshan Singh were hanged, their group went on without them. It changed its name and took up socialist ideas. It became the Hindustan Socialist Republican Association. This was the group Bhagat Singh and Chandrashekhar Azad belonged to.

## The film

In 2006, a Hindi film called *Rang De Basanti* brought Ashfaqulla Khan onto the screen. An actor named Kunal Kapoor played him.

There are two other screen roles. One is in a television series called *Chandrashekhar*. The other is in *Mujahid-E-Azadi: Ashfaqullah Khan*, a programme shown on the channel DD Urdu in 2014.

A film is not history. It is a story told for a cinema audience. But it can make people curious, and curiosity is a good place to start. For some viewers, it may have been the first time they heard his name.

## What is known, and what is not

The time and place of the hanging are known. The family says where he is remembered. Some of his words have come down in books and websites.

Much is still missing. Nobody has written who attended his burial. His original letters have not been found. And there is no full account of what his family went through after 1927.

## Why it matters

A young man died for a cause when he was 27. Almost a hundred years later, he is still being talked about.

Part of the reason is his friendship with a man of another faith. Part is his poems. And part is that people keep telling his story, in jails, on screens, and in books for children. If you want to go further, ask a grown-up to take you to a library. There, you can look up Bismil's autobiography and read what he wrote about his friend.
