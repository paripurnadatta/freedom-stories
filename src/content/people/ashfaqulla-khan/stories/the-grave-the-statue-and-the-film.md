Ashfaqulla Khan was hanged on 19 December 1927. He was 27 years old. For him, the story ended there. For the people who remembered him, it did not.

This story is about what came after. It is about a family, a grave, a statue and a film. It is also about a friendship that people still talk about. And it asks how a young man from Shahjahanpur came to be remembered by people who were not born in his time.

## The place where he died

Ashfaq was hanged in the district jail at Faizabad. The National Book Trust's book on the Kakori prisoners says the platform where it happened is still there. It says a life-size statue of Ashfaq now stands at the place.

We could not check this against another source. But it matches something the memorial website says. It calls the Faizabad jail Ashfaq's "final home", the place where he wrote his last verses.

## His grave

The memorial website says he is buried in his home town, Shahjahanpur. It says there is a memorial grave there, called the Ashfaqullah Khan Smarak, and that people gather there every 19 December.

ThePrint, a news website, also says a memorial grave was set up in Shahjahanpur. None of the sources we opened describes the burial itself. So we do not know who was there, or what was said.

<aside class="note"><b>A word of care</b>: The memorial website is run by admirers of Ashfaq. It does not name the people who run it. We trust it for what it says about a grave and a gathering, because ThePrint says the same. We do not rely on it for things no one else says.</aside>

## What happened to his family

ThePrint spoke to Ashfaq's grandson, Shahdabullah. He told the writer that the British government took away the family's property after the hanging. He said his grandmother's family was well off, and that this helped the family to get by.

He also said something sad. He said people seem to remember Ashfaq only twice a year, on Independence Day and on the day he died.

These are one man's words about his own family. They are not a record from a court. Still, they remind us that a hanging leaves a family behind.

## A friend's words

Ashfaq's best-known friend was Ram Prasad Bismil. In his own autobiography, written in prison, Bismil wrote a whole passage addressed to Ashfaq. The National Book Trust book prints part of it.

In it, Bismil says Ashfaq started as a friend and became like a brother. He says that people were surprised that a devout Arya Samaj Hindu and a devout Muslim could be friends. He says that when friends doubted Ashfaq, Ashfaq walked straight ahead on his own path. He says that when some Muslims scolded Ashfaq for his friendship, Ashfaq paid no attention.

The newspaper Counterview says Bismil's memoir uses this friendship as proof that Hindu-Muslim unity is possible. It also says Ashfaq, before his execution, asked Hindus and Muslims to stop quarrelling and work together for the country.

Counterview says Bismil made one more point in the same memoir. He wrote that men who took part in communal riots were given lighter punishments, while freedom fighters who avoided needless violence were given death. Counterview says Ashfaq was one of those freedom fighters. This is Bismil's opinion, reported by a news site. It is not a court finding.

ThePrint adds a small detail about Ashfaq's family. It says his father's side was Pathan, and that his father was a kotwal, a police officer in the old style. It attributes this to his grandson. It also says that his eldest brother told him stories of Bismil's bravery and his Urdu poetry, long before the two men met.

## His own words

Ashfaq wrote poems under pen names. The National Book Trust book gives them as Varsi and Hazrat. The memorial website and Wikipedia give the name Hasrat.

The book also quotes lines from his poems. In one, he says he lives only to serve his motherland. In another, he says that whether he gets a long sentence or the death sentence, he will sing her praises even with chained hands.

The memorial website says that three days before the hanging, he wrote a poem in Faizabad jail. It gives one line in English: "Worn down by their tyranny, we depart toward nothingness from Faizabad's prison."

<aside class="note"><b>Not checked</b>: We could not see the original handwriting of any of these poems. So we say "the book quotes" or "the website says", and do not call them certain.</aside>

## A new name for the group

After Ashfaq, Bismil, Lahiri and Roshan Singh were hanged, their group went on without them. Wikipedia says it then changed its name and took up socialist ideas. It became the Hindustan Socialist Republican Association. This was the group Bhagat Singh and Chandrashekhar Azad belonged to.

## The film

In 2006, a Hindi film called *Rang De Basanti* brought Ashfaqulla Khan onto the screen. An actor named Kunal Kapoor played him. ThePrint and Wikipedia both mention this.

Wikipedia lists two other screen roles as well. One is in a television series called *Chandrashekhar*. The other is in *Mujahid-E-Azadi: Ashfaqullah Khan*, a programme shown on the channel DD Urdu in 2014.

A film is not history. It is a story told for a cinema audience. But it can make people curious, and curiosity is a good place to start. For some viewers, it may have been the first time they heard his name.

## What we know, and what we do not

We know when and where he was hanged. We know where the family says he is remembered. We know some of his words, but only as books and websites report them.

We do not know many things we would like to know. We do not know who attended his burial. We do not have his original letters. And we do not have a full account of what his family went through after 1927.

## Why it matters

A young man died for a cause when he was 27. Almost a hundred years later, he is still being talked about.

Part of the reason is his friendship with a man of another faith. Part is his poems. And part is that people keep telling his story, in jails, on screens, and in books for children. This is one more telling. If you want to go further, ask a grown-up to take you to a library. There, you can look up Bismil's autobiography and read what he wrote about his friend.
