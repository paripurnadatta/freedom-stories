## Research notes

Central leader of India's mass anti-colonial movement.

### A politics built for mass participation

Gandhi’s importance to the freedom struggle lay not only in individual campaigns but in his ability to turn political resistance into a form that could draw in peasants, workers, merchants, students and women across regions. His methods changed over time, but nonviolent mass action, disciplined non-cooperation and moral pressure remained central.

### From South Africa to Indian mass politics

After returning to India in 1915, Gandhi moved cautiously through local struggles before emerging as a national leader. Champaran and Kheda became early laboratories for methods that joined investigation, organization, negotiation and civil resistance.

### Salt and the nationalization of protest

The 1930 Salt March demonstrated how an apparently narrow colonial monopoly could be transformed into a national political symbol. The campaign linked everyday life to the legitimacy of imperial rule and drew international attention.

### The final mass confrontation

In 1942 Gandhi became the central public voice of the Quit India call. The colonial state arrested Congress leaders almost immediately, and the movement developed in highly regional forms beyond centralized direction.

### A figure with multiple historical readings

A mature profile should distinguish Gandhi’s enormous mobilizing role from debates over strategy, caste, communal politics, revolutionary violence and the limits of Congress nationalism. Those interpretive questions should be sourced rather than flattened into a single verdict.

### Chronology

- **1915**: Returns to India. Returns from South Africa and begins a new phase of public work in India
- **1917**: Champaran Satyagraha. Intervenes in the indigo cultivators’ struggle in Bihar
- **1918**: Kheda Satyagraha. Supports peasant resistance to revenue demands in Gujarat
- **1930-03**: Dandi March. Launches the Salt Satyagraha by marching to the coast and challenging the salt laws
- **1942-08**: Quit India. Congress adopts Quit India with Gandhi as the movement’s central leader
