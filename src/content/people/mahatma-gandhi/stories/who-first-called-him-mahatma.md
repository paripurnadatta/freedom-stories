His real name was Mohandas Karamchand Gandhi. But almost nobody calls him that.

People call him Mahatma Gandhi. Or Gandhiji. Or Bapu, which means "father".

"Mahatma" comes from two Sanskrit words. "Maha" means great. "Atma" means soul. A Mahatma is a "great soul".

So who gave him that name?

## The famous answer

Ask most people, and they will say: Rabindranath Tagore. Tagore was India's great poet. He was the first Indian to win a Nobel Prize.

The story goes that Tagore called Gandhi "Mahatma" when they met. Some writers say this happened on 6 March 1915, at Tagore's school in Santiniketan.

It is a lovely story. But is it the first time?

## Older clues

Historians have dug deeper, and found earlier clues.

In 1909, a friend of Gandhi's named Pranjivan Mehta wrote a private letter to the leader Gopal Krishna Gokhale. In it, he used the word Mahatma for Gandhi. This is the oldest written record of the title that we know of.

Some say the name was used for Gandhi in South Africa even earlier, around 1914.

<aside class="note"><b>Written record</b>: something written down at the time, like a letter or a newspaper. Historians trust these more than memories written many years later.</aside>

## The paper from Jetpur

There is another clue, in Gujarat.

On 21 January 1915, Gandhi visited the town of Jetpur. A local man named Nautamlal Bhagvanji Mehta presented him with a document honouring him. In that document, Gandhi is called "Mahatma".

That paper still exists. It is kept at the National Gandhi Museum in New Delhi.

## And at the Gurukul

In April 1915, Gandhi visited a school called the Gurukul at Kangri, near Haridwar. Some accounts say people there called him Mahatma too. And Gandhi, in return, called the school's founder, Munshiram, a Mahatma.

## So who was first?

The honest answer is: we are not completely sure.

Pranjivan Mehta's letter of 1909 is the oldest written use we know. The Jetpur paper of January 1915 is another early one. Tagore may have used the name in March 1915. By the end of that year, many people were saying it.

Perhaps the best answer is this. Nobody gave Gandhi the name all at once. Many people, in many places, started calling him the same thing, because they felt it fitted.

## Did Gandhi like it?

Not really. In the introduction to the story of his life, Gandhi wrote: "Often the title has deeply pained me." He said he could not remember a single moment when it had pleased him.

But the name stuck, and it has stayed with him ever since.
