In 1946, India was close to freedom. But it was also full of fear and anger.

Hindus and Muslims, who had lived side by side for centuries, were turning against each other. Riots broke out in city after city.

In October 1946, terrible violence hit Noakhali. That was a district in East Bengal, where most people were Muslim. Hindu families there were attacked. Homes were burned. Many people were killed. Many fled.

## "I am going"

Gandhi was in Delhi. He heard the news from Noakhali, and he could not rest.

He was 77 years old. Delhi was full of important meetings about India's future. Leaders were making big decisions. But Gandhi decided his place was somewhere else.

On 6 November 1946, he left for Noakhali. On the way, he said he was going to comfort the victims of the riots.

## Staying among the people

Gandhi did not stay in a grand house with guards. He refused police protection.

He asked the people who came with him to split up. Each one should go and live in a different village where trouble had happened. They should stay there, among the families, and help bring back peace. Anyone who was afraid could go home.

## The barefoot walk

On 2 January 1947, Gandhi began a walking tour. It started at a village called Srirampur.

He took off his sandals. He walked barefoot, carrying a bamboo stick. Some say he went barefoot out of respect for those who had died.

Day after day, he walked the narrow paths between the villages. He visited 47 villages in all, covering about 116 miles.

In each village, he talked to people. He held prayer meetings. He listened to Hindus and to Muslims. He asked them to forgive, and to live together again.

<aside class="note"><b>Noakhali today</b>: Noakhali is now in Bangladesh. In 1947, when India was divided, East Bengal became part of Pakistan. In 1971, it became the free country of Bangladesh.</aside>

## Walking alone

As he walked, Gandhi often sang a song by Rabindranath Tagore. It is called "Ekla Chalo Re". It means: "If no one answers your call, walk on alone."

At first, thousands of people came to his prayer meetings, Hindus and Muslims alike. But later, some people turned against him. On some days, rubbish and filth were put on the path in front of him, on purpose.

Gandhi did not get angry. He cleaned the path, and walked on.

## Leaving

On 1 March 1947, the walk ended, at a village called Hemchar. The next day, Gandhi left Noakhali. Now there was terrible violence in Bihar, where Muslims were being killed. He went there next, to do the same work.

## Did it work?

Gandhi did not bring perfect peace to Noakhali. His work there was left unfinished. He himself was sad about how much hatred he had seen.

But for many families, his visit meant something. An old man had walked to their door, without shoes, without guards, to say that their lives mattered.
