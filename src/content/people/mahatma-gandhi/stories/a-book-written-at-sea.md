In November 1909, a big steamship sailed out from England. It was called the Kildonan Castle. It was heading south, all the way to South Africa.

On board was a lawyer from South Africa, coming home. He was disappointed. He had spent months in London, asking the British government to treat Indians in South Africa fairly. It had not worked.

His name was Mohandas Gandhi. He was 40 years old.

## Ten days of writing

Gandhi did not waste the voyage. He sat down and began to write.

He wrote fast. He wrote every day. When his right hand grew tired, he switched the pen to his left hand and kept going. About forty pages of the book were written with his left hand.

In just ten days, from 13 to 22 November 1909, he finished the whole book.

He called it Hind Swaraj. That means "Indian Home Rule".

<aside class="note"><b>Swaraj</b>: "self-rule". Indians used this word for the idea of ruling their own country, instead of being ruled by Britain.</aside>

## A book in his own language

Gandhi wrote the book in Gujarati, his mother tongue. He wanted ordinary Indians to read it, not just scholars who knew English.

He wrote it as a conversation. There are two people in the book. One is called the Reader. He is an angry young Indian who wants to drive the British out. The other is called the Editor. That is Gandhi himself.

The Reader asks questions. The Editor answers them.

## What did the book say?

Gandhi had a surprising idea. The British did not hold India because they were so strong, he said. They held India because Indians helped them. Indians worked in their offices, bought their cloth and obeyed their laws.

If Indians stopped cooperating, British rule would fall.

He also said violence was the wrong way. Bombs and guns would not bring real freedom. Only truth and peaceful resistance could.

These were the ideas he would use, years later, in India.

## Banned!

The British did not like the book one bit. In 1910, they banned it in India. They said it was dangerous to the government.

So Gandhi translated it into English himself. It is the only one of his books that he translated with his own hand.

## A spinning wheel he had never seen

In Hind Swaraj, Gandhi wrote that the spinning wheel and the handloom could help cure India's poverty.

Here is a funny thing. He later admitted that, when he wrote that, he did not remember ever having seen a spinning wheel!

Finding one turned out to be harder than he expected. But that is another story.

## Why it matters

Hind Swaraj is a small book. You could read it in a day or two. But many people think it holds the seed of everything Gandhi did afterwards.

It was written on a rocking ship, with tired hands, by a man who was not yet famous. Today it is read all over the world.
