In January 1948, India had been free for five months. But the country was in pain. Partition had brought terrible violence. Millions of people had lost their homes.

Gandhi was 78. He was staying at Birla House, a big house in New Delhi. Every day, people came to see him. Every evening at five o'clock, he held a prayer meeting in the garden. Anyone could come.

He spent his last days still trying to bring Hindus and Muslims together. Not everyone liked that. Some people were very angry with him.

## Ten minutes late

On 30 January 1948, Gandhi was busy with a meeting. It ran late.

When he finally set out for the prayer ground, it was past five. He said, a little worried: "I am late by ten minutes."

He walked across the garden. Beside him were two young women who looked after him, Manu and Abha. People were waiting on the lawn.

## The shots

As he reached the crowd, a young man stepped forward. He seemed to bow to Gandhi, as many people did.

Then he fired three shots from a pistol, at close range.

Gandhi fell.

He was 78 years old. The man who had spent his whole life teaching that violence was wrong had been killed by violence.

<aside class="note"><b>The man who fired</b>: his name was Nathuram Godse. He was against Gandhi's efforts for Hindu–Muslim unity. He was caught at once, tried, and hanged in 1949.</aside>

## "He Ram"

Most accounts say that as Gandhi fell, he said two words: "He Ram". That means "O God". Ram was the name of God that Gandhi had prayed to all his life.

Not everyone agrees. One man who said he had been an aide of Gandhi's claimed many years later that Gandhi said nothing. The assassin's brother, who was not there, claimed he made only a faint sound.

The historian Vinay Lal has looked carefully at all the accounts. He says that most of Gandhi's biographers agree: Gandhi died with the name of Ram on his lips. It is exactly how Gandhi had always hoped to die.

## The broken watch

Gandhi always carried a small watch. When he fell, it broke.

The watch stopped with its minute hand at 13. It is kept today at the Gandhi museum at Rajghat in Delhi. One historian has called it the most reliable witness to what happened that evening.

## "The light has gone out"

That night, Prime Minister Jawaharlal Nehru spoke to the country on the radio. He said:

"The light has gone out of our lives, and there is darkness everywhere."

All over India, and all over the world, people wept. People who had never met Gandhi felt as if they had lost their own father. That was what they had called him: Bapu.

## The footsteps

Today Birla House is a museum, called Gandhi Smriti. In the garden, there is a line of footsteps made of stone. They mark the path Gandhi walked that evening.

The footsteps stop at a small memorial. That is where he fell.

Visitors walk beside those stone footsteps every day. They walk slowly, and quietly. It is the last walk of a man who walked thousands of miles for India.
