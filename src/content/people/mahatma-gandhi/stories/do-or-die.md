On the evening of 8 August 1942, Gandhi stood before the leaders of the Congress party in Bombay. He was 72 years old.

He said he could not wait any longer.

"I want freedom immediately," he told them, "this very night, before dawn, if it can be had."

Then he gave them one short sentence to remember.

"Here is a mantra, a short one, that I give you," he said. "The mantra is: Do or Die."

## Quit India

That day, the Congress made a demand: the British must leave India. The movement came to be called Quit India.

Gandhi explained what "Do or Die" meant. "We shall either free India or die in the attempt," he said. "We shall not live to see the perpetuation of our slavery."

<aside class="note"><b>Perpetuation</b>: keeping something going for ever.</aside>

But he also said something else, which people often forget. The fight must be without violence. "There is nothing but purest Ahimsa in all that I am saying and doing today," he said. And he told them to have no hatred for the British. "Speaking for myself, I can say that I have never felt any hatred."

<aside class="note"><b>Ahimsa</b>: non-violence. Not hurting any living being.</aside>

## Arrested before dawn

The British did not wait to see what would happen. Early the next morning, 9 August 1942, the police came to Birla House in Bombay, where Gandhi was staying. They arrested him.

Within hours, almost all the Congress leaders were in prison.

The meeting went on without them. On 9 August, a younger leader, Aruna Asaf Ali, presided over it at the Gowalia Tank Maidan in Bombay, and raised the flag.

Gandhi was not taken to an ordinary jail. He was taken to Poona, to a large palace that belonged to the Aga Khan. The British used it as a prison.

With him went his wife, Kasturba. So did his secretary, Mahadev Desai, a doctor named Sushila Nayar, and his helper Pyarelal. Later, the poet Sarojini Naidu was brought there too.

## India without its leaders

Outside, with every leader locked up, people protested in their own ways. Some protests were peaceful. Others turned violent.

The British answered with great force. More than a hundred thousand people were arrested.

<aside class="note"><b>How many died?</b> British figures said 1,028 people were killed. Congress estimates were much higher, between 4,000 and 10,000. Nobody knows the true number.</aside>

Not everyone in India supported Quit India. The Muslim League opposed it. So did the Hindu Mahasabha and the Communist Party of India. Each had its own reasons.

Far away, Subhas Chandra Bose saw it differently. He said that a new chapter in India's freedom struggle had begun.

Mahadev Desai had been Gandhi's secretary for 25 years. He had joined Gandhi in 1917. From then on, he kept a diary of his life with Gandhi. His last diary entry was dated 14 August 1942.

On the morning of 15 August 1942, only six days after the arrests, Mahadev Desai had a heart attack in the Aga Khan Palace. He died. He was 50.

Gandhi called out his name: "Mahadev! Mahadev!"

Mahadev was cremated in the grounds of the palace.

<aside class="note"><b>Cremated</b>: when a body is burned after death, as in Hindu funerals.</aside>

## Twenty-one days

The British government said that the Congress was to blame for the violence outside. Gandhi said this was not true.

So, in protest, he began a fast. From 10 February to 2 March 1943, he ate nothing for 21 days. He was 73.

At one point he came very close to death. The Gandhi Heritage Portal says the government even prepared for his funeral, and wrote a notice announcing his death. But he survived.

## Kasturba

Kasturba was 74. She had been at Gandhi's side through many struggles.

In the Aga Khan Palace, she became very ill. She had a lung disease called bronchitis, and then pneumonia. In January 1944, she had two heart attacks. For a while she got a little better, and could sit out on the veranda in a wheelchair. Then she grew worse again.

Gandhi wrote to the government, asking that she be treated by a doctor of his choice, Dinshaw Mehta, and nursed by Kanu Gandhi.

Her family and doctors argued about how to treat her. Her son Devdas wanted her to have a new medicine, penicillin. Her doctors said it could not help her any more.

On 21 February, Gandhi sent a message saying she was "slowly going". On 22 February 1944, at 7.35 in the evening, Kasturba Gandhi died.

The next day, her youngest son, Devdas, lit her funeral fire in the palace grounds, close to where Mahadev had been cremated.

## Free, but alone

Gandhi himself fell ill. On 6 May 1944, the British released him, because of his health. At eight in the morning, he was let out of the palace where he had spent almost two years.

As his car drove away, he spoke about Kasturba. "She had been so eager to get out of prison," he said, "yet I know she could not have had a better death."

Before he left, he asked the government to let the plot of land where Mahadev and Kasturba were cremated be kept safe.

## The palace today

In 1969, the Aga Khan gave the palace to the people of India, out of respect for Gandhi. Today it is a museum. In its grounds are two marble memorials, one for Kasturba and one for Mahadev Desai. Some of Gandhi's own ashes were placed there in 1992.

## Why it matters

"Do or Die" sounds like a call to fight. But Gandhi said it in the same speech in which he asked for no hatred and no violence. Both parts belong together.

The cost of Quit India was high, for the whole country and for Gandhi himself. He went into the Aga Khan Palace with his wife and his closest helper. He came out without either of them.
