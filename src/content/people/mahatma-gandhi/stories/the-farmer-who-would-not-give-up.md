Most stories about Champaran begin with Gandhi. This one begins with a farmer.

His name was Raj Kumar Shukla. He lived in a village in Champaran, in the north of Bihar, near the border with Nepal.

## The blue dye

Champaran was famous for one crop: indigo. Indigo plants make a deep blue dye. It was used to colour cloth all over the world.

The land in Champaran was controlled by British planters. They forced the farmers to grow indigo on part of their land. Out of every twenty parts of a farmer's land, three had to be indigo. The farmers were paid very little for it.

Raj Kumar Shukla was one of these farmers. He had suffered too.

<aside class="note"><b>Planter</b>: a person who owns or runs a large farm, called a plantation. In Champaran, most of the indigo planters were British.</aside>

## Lucknow, December 1916

In December 1916, the Indian National Congress held its big yearly meeting in Lucknow. Raj Kumar Shukla went there. He wanted the leaders to hear about Champaran.

Most of them were not very interested. Then he found Gandhi.

Gandhi listened. But he was cautious. He later admitted that he did not know even the name of Champaran before then. He did not want to promise anything about a place he had never seen.

## The shadow

Shukla did not give up.

When Gandhi went to Kanpur, Shukla caught up with him there. "Please come to Champaran soon," he said.

When Gandhi went home to his ashram in Ahmedabad, Shukla was already there, waiting for him. He would not leave until Gandhi named a date.

Then Gandhi went to Calcutta. Shukla followed him there too. He wrote letters. He sent messages.

At last, Gandhi agreed. The two of them got on a train together.

People later called Shukla the man who "shadowed" Gandhi.

## Into Champaran

On 10 April 1917, Gandhi and Shukla reached Patna. From there they went to Muzaffarpur, and then on to Motihari, in Champaran.

Gandhi began to ask questions. He met the farmers and listened to their stories.

The British officials did not like this. They ordered Gandhi to leave Champaran.

Gandhi refused. He said he would accept whatever punishment they gave. It was his first act of civil disobedience on Indian soil.

## A victory for the farmers

The government backed down. It set up an inquiry, and Gandhi was made a member of it.

In the end, the forced growing of indigo was stopped. The planters had to pay back part of the money they had taken from the farmers. Within ten years, most of them had left Champaran.

## Remembering Shukla

Raj Kumar Shukla died in 1929, in Motihari. For a long time, few people remembered his name.

Today he is not forgotten. In 2000, India Post issued a stamp in his honour. A statue of him stands in the Gandhi museum in Patna.

Gandhi's first great fight in India began because one farmer simply would not take no for an answer.
