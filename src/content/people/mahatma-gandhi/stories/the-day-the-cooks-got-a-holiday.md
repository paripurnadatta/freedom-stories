Who cooks the food at your school? Who sweeps the floors and cleans the toilets?

Now imagine a visitor comes and says: "From tomorrow, you will do it yourselves."

That is what happened at one famous school in Bengal, in 1915.

## A new arrival

Early in 1915, Gandhi had just come home to India from South Africa.

The group of people who had lived with him at his Phoenix farm in South Africa had come ahead of him. They were staying at Santiniketan, the school started by the poet Rabindranath Tagore.

In February 1915, Gandhi went to join them. The teachers and students gave him a warm welcome. On that visit he met two young teachers, Kaka Kalelkar and J. B. Kripalani. Both became his helpers for life.

## Gandhi's idea

Santiniketan had paid cooks who made food for the boys. Gandhi had an idea.

He suggested to the teachers that they stop using paid cooks. The teachers and the boys could cook their own food. That way, the teachers could take charge of the kitchen and keep it clean and healthy. And the boys would learn to help themselves.

Some teachers were not sure. Others liked the idea. The boys seemed keen.

Gandhi wrote that he asked "the Poet" for his opinion. Tagore said he did not mind, if the teachers agreed. Then he told the boys: "The experiment contains the key to Swaraj."

<aside class="note"><b>Swaraj</b>: self-rule. It was the word Indians used for freedom from British rule.</aside>

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: Gandhi's autobiography says he asked Tagore about the plan on this visit. But the Gandhi Heritage Portal says Tagore was away in February, and that the two men first met only on 6 March 1915, on Gandhi's next visit.</aside>

## Pots, pans and music

The work was split into groups. Some boys cut vegetables. Some cleaned the grain. Others did the cleaning.

A man named Pearson worked harder than anyone. He even scrubbed the big cooking pots. Cleaning the pots was boring, so a group of students played the sitar while the others worked.

The food was very simple. Rice, dal, vegetables and wheat flour were all cooked together in one big steam cooker, with no spices.

Not every boy enjoyed it. Some got tired of it quickly. Gandhi admitted that it was a lot to ask of 125 boys who were not used to hard work.

## Sad news

Gandhi had planned to stay for a while. But he had been there hardly a week when a telegram came from Poona. The great leader Gopal Krishna Gokhale had died.

Santiniketan was full of grief. Gandhi left for Poona that same day.

After some time, the school dropped the experiment. But Gandhi believed the school had learned something from it.

## A day to remember

Santiniketan did not forget. Every year, on 10 March, it marks a day called Gandhi Punyaha. On that day, the school's staff get a holiday. The students and teachers do the chores themselves.

<aside class="note"><b>Punyaha</b>: a holy or blessed day.</aside>

It still happens today. On 10 March 2026, one department of Visva-Bharati, the university at Santiniketan, held a cleaning drive. Students, teachers and staff were each given a place to clean, from the main building to the canteen.

## Why it matters

Gandhi stayed only a few days. The experiment lasted only a short while. But the idea stayed behind: cleaning up after yourself is not beneath anyone. A poet and a freedom fighter both thought it had something to do with being free.
