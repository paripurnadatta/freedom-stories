At midnight on 14 August 1947, India became free.

In Delhi, there were huge celebrations. Jawaharlal Nehru made a famous speech. The new flag went up. People danced in the streets. Fireworks lit up the sky.

But one man was missing.

Mahatma Gandhi had spent thirty years fighting for this moment. Yet he was not in Delhi at all.

## Freedom, and blood

India's freedom came with a terrible price. The country was being divided into two: India and Pakistan.

As the new borders were drawn, Hindus, Muslims and Sikhs turned on each other. Riots broke out in many places. Calcutta was one of the worst.

Gandhi felt he could not celebrate while people were killing each other. He said freedom had come "stained with blood".

## An unusual house guest

Bengal's leader was a Muslim League politician named Huseyn Shaheed Suhrawardy. He asked Gandhi to stay in Calcutta, to help stop the riots.

Gandhi agreed, but on one condition. Suhrawardy must live with him, under the same roof, and work with him for peace.

So they moved into an old, run-down house in Beliaghata. It belonged to an elderly Muslim woman. It was in a troubled area, and not easy to protect. Gandhi refused armed guards.

## Stones through the window

On 13 August 1947, Gandhi moved in. An angry crowd of young men was waiting. They shouted slogans. They threw stones, and broke the windows.

Gandhi did not hide. He went out and talked to them. He explained why he was there. Slowly, over the next day, their anger began to fade.

## Independence Day

On 15 August 1947, the first day of free India, Gandhi did not make a speech or wave a flag.

He fasted for twenty-four hours. He prayed. He went to visit areas hit by the riots and met the families who had suffered. At prayer meetings, readings were heard from the Quran, the Bhagavad Gita and the Bible.

He was asked to send a message to the nation, to be broadcast on the radio and by the BBC. He refused. He said: "My independence has not yet come."

## The miracle

Then something amazing happened. In Calcutta, on 14 and 15 August, Hindus and Muslims came out together, and celebrated side by side.

A poet who saw it, Sudhindranath Datta, called it "the only miracle I have seen".

<aside class="note"><b>One-man boundary force</b>: people said that in Bengal, one old man had done what a whole army might not have done. They called Gandhi a "one-man boundary force".</aside>

## It did not last

Gandhi was not sure the peace would hold. He was right. At the end of August, riots broke out again in Calcutta.

So Gandhi began another fast. He said it would end only when the violence ended. The violence stopped.

## Why it matters

On the happiest day in India's history, Gandhi chose to be where things were worst. He did not want to celebrate. He wanted to help.

Many people think that is exactly what made him the Mahatma.
