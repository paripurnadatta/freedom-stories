Look at the little boy in the old photograph. He is seven years old. His ears stick out. He looks a bit serious.

This is the only photograph we have of Mohandas Gandhi as a child.

## A very shy boy

Mohandas grew up in Rajkot, a town in Gujarat. He went to the local school.

He was not a star pupil. He was, he wrote later, a very shy boy. He did not like playing with others. His books and his lessons were his only friends.

When the school bell rang at the end of the day, he did not hang about. He ran straight home. He was scared that someone might talk to him, or tease him.

## The inspector's visit

One day, an important visitor came to the school. He was Mr Giles, the Educational Inspector. His job was to check how well the boys were learning.

Mr Giles gave the class a spelling test. There were five English words.

One of the words was "kettle".

Mohandas wrote it down. But he spelled it wrongly.

## The teacher's boot

Mohandas's teacher was walking around the classroom. He saw the mistake. He did not want his class to look bad in front of the inspector.

So he tried to help. He gave Mohandas a little nudge with the tip of his boot. He wanted him to look at the slate of the boy next to him, and copy the right spelling.

But Mohandas did not understand. He thought the teacher was walking around to stop the boys from copying! So he did not look.

## The result

When the slates were checked, every boy in the class had spelled all five words correctly.

Every boy except one.

Later, the teacher told Mohandas he had been stupid. But Mohandas did not feel sorry. He wrote that he "never could learn the art of copying".

<aside class="note"><b>How do we know?</b> Gandhi told this story himself, in the book about his life he wrote in the 1920s. No school record of the test survives. So it is his own memory of what happened.</aside>

## The truthful king

There was another thing young Mohandas never forgot.

Once, he saw a play about King Harishchandra. The king in the play always told the truth, even when it cost him everything he had.

The story moved Mohandas so much that he cried. He kept asking himself: why can't everyone be truthful, like Harishchandra?

## Why it matters

It is a small story about one spelling mistake. But it shows something about the boy who grew up to be the Mahatma.

He would rather be wrong and honest, than right and a cheat. All his life, he called his way of living "experiments with truth".
