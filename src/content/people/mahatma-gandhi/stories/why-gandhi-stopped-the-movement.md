In 1920, Gandhi started something huge. He called it Non-Cooperation.

The idea was simple. The British could rule India only because Indians helped them. So Indians would stop helping.

## A country says no

Across India, people joined in. Students walked out of British schools and colleges. Lawyers stopped going to British courts. People gave back medals and titles the British had given them.

Huge bonfires were lit in the streets. People threw in their foreign cloth and watched it burn. They began to wear khadi instead.

Thousands went to jail. Many people felt that freedom was very close.

## Chauri Chaura

Chauri Chaura was a small town in the United Provinces, near Gorakhpur. On 4 February 1922, a big crowd of protesters gathered there. There were about two or three thousand people.

There was trouble with the police. The police fired on the crowd. Three people in the crowd were killed.

Then the crowd lost control. The policemen were inside the police station. The crowd set it on fire. Twenty-two policemen died.

<aside class="note"><b>22 or 23?</b> Some books say 23 policemen died. One constable who was first counted among the dead was later found alive.</aside>

## Gandhi's shock

When Gandhi heard the news, he was shaken.

His whole movement was built on one rule: no violence. Whatever the British did, the protesters must never hit back. Now people marching in his name had killed.

Gandhi felt that India was not ready. If he carried on, there might be more Chauri Chauras.

## "Stop"

On 12 February 1922, Gandhi called off the Non-Cooperation Movement, all over India.

Then he went without food for five days. He said it was a penance, to make up for what had happened.

<aside class="note"><b>Penance</b>: something hard that a person does to show they are sorry for a wrong.</aside>

## Anger and disappointment

Many people were furious. Jawaharlal Nehru and many other Congress leaders were in jail at the time. They felt Gandhi had acted too quickly. Why should the whole country stop because of one town?

Even Gandhi himself later felt that he had decided too hastily.

A few weeks later, on 10 March 1922, the British arrested Gandhi. He was sentenced to six years in jail. He was let out in 1924 because he was ill.

## What happened at Chauri Chaura afterwards

The British punished the town harshly. 225 people were put on trial. In the end, nineteen men were hanged in 1923.

The story of Chauri Chaura is a sad one on every side. Policemen died. Protesters died. More died later.

## Why it matters

Was Gandhi right to stop? People still argue about it.

But the story shows how seriously Gandhi took his rule. To him, freedom won through violence was not the freedom he wanted. He was willing to give up a movement that was close to success, rather than break that rule.

Eight years later, he started again. This time, it began with a handful of salt.
