In Lahore in the early 1890s, a group of Indians wanted a bank of their own.

## The idea

A man named Rai Mool Raj, a member of the Arya Samaj, first suggested an Indian-owned national bank. Lala Lajpat Rai then sent a circular, a printed letter, to selected friends. He called for an Indian bank owned by shareholders.

<aside class="note"><b>Joint stock bank</b>: a bank owned by many people who each buy a share. The profit is divided among them.</aside>

Lala Harkrishan Lal had come back from England with ideas about trade and industry. He was eager to start it.

## The dates

The bank was registered on 19 May 1894 under the Indian Companies Act. The first meeting of the board was on 23 May 1894. The bank opened for business on 12 April 1895, the day before the festival of Baisakhi.

The office was in a rented house in Anarkali Bazar in Lahore.

the bank's own page says the rented house was "opposite the Arya Samaj Mandir" in one place, and "opposite the post office" in another.

## The board

The first board had seven directors by one count, though eight men are named. Sardar Dayal Singh Majithia was the first chairman, and Lala Harkishan Lal was the board's first secretary. Other names include Kali Prosanna Roy, E. C. Jessawala and Lala Lalchand. Bulaki Ram Shastri was the first manager.

The board came from different parts of India and from different faiths. The founders were leaders of the Swadeshi movement. Their aim was "a truly national bank that would further the economic interest of the country."

## Where was Lajpat Rai?

Was Lajpat Rai a founder of the bank? The bank's own page says he was the first person to open an account, and that he joined the board soon after. It names him as the man who called for the bank by circular.

The long article on the bank does not list him among the founders. It says only that he took an active part in managing it in the early years. The article on Rai says he was "linked" with the Punjab National Bank and with the Lakshmi Insurance Company.

All agree that he was part of it. The only question is the word for his part. "He helped start the bank" is safe. "Founder" depends on who counts as one.

## Why it matters

A bank owned by Indians, set up in Lahore in 1894, was one way of working for the Swadeshi aim. It shows that the freedom struggle was not only marches and speeches. People also set up banks, schools and companies.
