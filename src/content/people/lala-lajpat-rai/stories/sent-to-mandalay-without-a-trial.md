On 9 May 1907, Lala Lajpat Rai was arrested in Lahore. He was put on a journey to Mandalay, in Burma, far from home. No judge had heard his case.

## The trouble in Punjab

In 1907 many farmers in Punjab were angry. Wikipedia explains that a new law, the Punjab Colonisation Bill of 1906, made the anger worse. Under it, if a man who held land from the government died without sons, the land went back to the government. It did not go to his wider family. The first protests were in the Chenab Colony, a region where canals had opened new farmland.

Lajpat Rai and a leader named Ajit Singh opposed the Bill, says the AllAboutSikhs website.

## The arrest

The Tribune says Lajpat Rai was arrested on 9 May 1907. He was sent to Mandalay for six months under a law called Regulation III of 1818. The Tribune says he was freed on 11 November 1907.

<aside class="note"><b>Regulation III of 1818</b>: an old law that let the government hold a person in prison without a trial. The Swarajya article says Rai was sent away "without trial," with the consent of the Viceroy, Lord Minto.</aside>

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: The Tribune says Ajit Singh was arrested later, on 2 June 1907. Wikipedia says both men were sent to Mandalay "in May 1907." We could not tell which is right. All the sources agree that both were freed after six months.</aside>

## The government's reasons

The Swarajya article says that 1907 was fifty years after the great revolt of 1857, and the authorities feared another. A government report blamed the agitation in Punjab. A cipher telegram called Rai the "head and center" of it. The writer of that article admires Rai, so his reading should be weighed with care.

Wikipedia says something different about the evidence. It says the government did not have enough proof to hold Rai for working against it. So he was deported all the same.

According to Swarajya, Rai wrote to his father from prison. He said "whatever comes should be manfully borne."

## After the six months

The AllAboutSikhs page says the deportation "created a wave of indignation" across the country.

In December 1907, Congress met at Surat. Wikipedia says that Rai's supporters tried to make him the president of the Congress there, and did not succeed. The Tribune adds that Ajit Singh attended the same session, where Bal Gangadhar Tilak gave him a symbolic crown and called him the king of the Punjab peasants. Soon afterward, the Tribune says, Ajit Singh feared a false case against him and left India. He lived abroad for more than half his life.

## Why it matters

This story shows a way of governing that Indians came to hate. A person could be taken away by an order, and the question of his guilt was never put to a court. Rai's stay in Mandalay was short. In 1908 another leader, Bal Gangadhar Tilak, was sent to the same city for six years. His story is told in "Six years and a book in Mandalay."
