## Research notes

Accepted symbolic leadership of the 1857 uprising in Delhi.

### A sovereign made by rebellion

Bahadur Shah did not initiate the sepoy rising, but his name gave the rebellion a powerful language of legitimacy that could travel beyond Delhi.

### Authority without full control

The rebel court was politically important yet internally fragmented; his profile should not imply that he exercised unified operational command over every 1857 theatre.

### Chronology

- **1857-05**: Rebels enter Delhi. Sepoys from Meerut reach Delhi and recognize Bahadur Shah as the symbolic sovereign of the uprising
- **1857**: A rebel administration forms. Military and civil structures emerge around the Mughal court as the rebellion tries to govern Delhi
- **1857-09**: British reconquest of Delhi. The rebel capital falls and Bahadur Shah becomes a prisoner
- **1858**: Trial and exile. The former emperor is tried at the Red Fort and then transported out of India

### Evidence claims

- Government historical accounts identify Bahadur Shah II, or Bahadur Shah Zafar, as the last Mughal emperor and the sovereign proclaimed by the 1857 rebels in Delhi. (high confidence)
- His court became the political centre around which the Delhi rebels attempted to construct an administration during the uprising. (high confidence)
- The Dictionary of Martyrs records Bakht Khan's arrival in Delhi and appointment to senior military command under Bahadur Shah's rebel administration. (high confidence)
- After British forces retook Delhi in September 1857, Bahadur Shah was captured, tried at the Red Fort in 1858 and transported into exile. (high confidence)
