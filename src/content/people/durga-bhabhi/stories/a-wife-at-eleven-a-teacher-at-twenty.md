Most people know Durga Devi from one night in December 1928, when she pretended to be Bhagat Singh's wife. But she was twenty-one by then. She had been married for ten years. She had a small son. She had a job.

This story is about the years before that night. We know less about them, and the sources do not always agree. That is why it is worth looking carefully.

## A girl with no brothers or sisters

Durga Devi was the only child of a Gujarati Brahmin couple who had settled in Allahabad. This is the account given by the historian Kama Maclean, whose book is printed in part by Scroll.in.

Her mother died when she was young. Her father then took *sannyas*, which means he gave up family life to become a wandering holy man. An aunt brought her up.

<aside class="note"><b>Sannyas</b>: in Hindu tradition, a person who takes sannyas gives up home, family and property to follow a spiritual life.</aside>

She was born, Wikipedia says, on 7 October 1907, at Shahjadpur in the Allahabad district. This is in today's Kaushambi district of Uttar Pradesh.

The same Wikipedia article gives only "Allahabad" in its main text, so the birthplace is told in two ways. The date, 7 October 1907, comes from Wikipedia alone. Maclean does not give a birth date, and we found no birth record.

## School, and marriage

Durga Devi studied up to Class V. That is about the age of the readers of this site. She was married at eleven.

If the 1907 birth date is right, the wedding was around 1918 or 1919. Neither of our sources says more about it.

## The husband's family

Her husband was Bhagwati Charan Vohra, who lived from 1903 to 1930. His father was Shiv Charan Das, a rich Gujarati who worked for the railways and had the title Rai Sahib.

<aside class="note"><b>Rai Sahib</b>: a title the British government gave to Indians it wished to honour, usually for loyal service.</aside>

A son of such a man might have lived a quiet life. Instead, Bhagwati Charan studied at the National College in Lahore. There he met Bhagat Singh. His sister Sushila also became a well-known woman revolutionary.

## A son

Their son was born in 1925. His name was Sachinanda, but everyone called him Sachi.

Sachi was born in 1925, which would make him about three in December 1928.

Maclean gives the year 1925. Wikipedia only calls him "an infant" at that time. This story follows Maclean, but his birth record was not seen.

## Visitors in the house

Durga Devi first met revolutionaries through her husband. Young men from the National College came to their home in Lahore. Maclean names Bhagat Singh, Yashpal and Sukhdev among the visitors.

So the house was a meeting place. Maclean does not say what was talked about at the table, and we do not know either. It was in this house that a young mother heard the plans and opinions of men who would soon be famous.

## 16 November 1926

Durga Devi first became well known through the Naujawan Bharat Sabha, according to Wikipedia. This was a youth group in Lahore, started by young men including Bhagat Singh and Bhagwati Charan.

On 16 November 1926, the Sabha held a meeting in Lahore to mark the eleventh anniversary of the death of Kartar Singh Sarabha. He was a young revolutionary who was hanged in 1915.

This event is found in one Wikipedia article only. It does not say what she did at the meeting.

## A job and a name

Durga Devi worked as a teacher at a girls' college in Lahore. She kept this job until 1929, when she had to go into hiding.

The members of the revolutionary group called her *Bhabhi*, which is "brother's wife" in Hindi. That is how she became Durga Bhabhi.

She was not really Bhagat Singh's wife. She was Bhagwati Charan's wife. The name Bhabhi was a title that the group gave her.

## The group changes its name

By late 1928, Durga Devi and her husband had joined the Hindustan Socialist Republican Association, the HSRA. It was the new name of the older HRA. Her husband became the group's Propaganda Secretary. He was one of its main thinkers, and he wrote a paper called *The Philosophy of the Bomb*.

<aside class="note"><b>Propaganda</b>: in those days the word meant spreading a group's ideas by pamphlets and posters. It did not always mean lies.</aside>

## Two things about money

When her husband left for Calcutta in December 1928, he left four or five thousand rupees with her. Maclean says Durga Devi remembered this later. It is her own memory, told long afterwards.

She sold ornaments worth three thousand rupees to help pay for the defence of Bhagat Singh and his comrades. That would have been after their arrest in 1929.

<aside class="note"><b>One source only</b>: we found the sale of the ornaments in Wikipedia only.</aside>

## A bomb factory?

She, her husband and a man named Vimal Prasad Jain are said to have run a bomb factory in Delhi, on Qutub Road, in a shop disguised as "Himalayan Toilets".

Only one source gives the Qutub Road factory. Maclean says the group's bomb factory was at a place called Kashmir House, which was rented in Bhagwati Charan's name. These may be two different places, or one place described two ways.

## What we do not know

We do not know what her days were like in Lahore. No source we opened gives her words from those years. We do not know what she taught. And we do not know whether she was ever afraid.

## What the facts add up to

A girl from Allahabad who lost her mother early. A wife at eleven. A mother at about eighteen. A teacher. A friend of the men who came to her house.

We have no words of hers from those years. We do not know what she taught, or whether she was ever afraid.

## Why it matters

When she said yes in December 1928, it was not the choice of a stranger. She knew these men. She knew her husband's work. She knew the risk.

Understanding that makes the famous night clearer. She did not step out of an ordinary life by chance. She had been standing at its edge for years.
