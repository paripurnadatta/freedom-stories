Most people know Durga Devi from one night in December 1928, when she pretended to be Bhagat Singh's wife. But she was twenty-one by then. She had been married for ten years. She had a small son. She had a job.

This story is about the years before that night. We know less about them, and the sources do not always agree. That is why it is worth looking carefully.

## A girl with no brothers or sisters

The historian Kama Maclean has written the fullest account we could find. Scroll.in printed a part of her book. She says Durga Devi was the only child of a Gujarati Brahmin couple who had settled in Allahabad.

Her mother died when she was young. Her father then took *sannyas*, which means he gave up family life to become a wandering holy man. An aunt brought her up.

<aside class="note"><b>Sannyas</b>: In Hindu tradition, a person who takes sannyas gives up home, family and property to follow a spiritual life.</aside>

Wikipedia gives her birth date as 7 October 1907. It names the place as Shahjadpur, in the Allahabad district. This is in today's Kaushambi district of Uttar Pradesh. But the same article says only "Allahabad" in its text. Maclean does not give a birth date at all.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: The place of her birth is given as Allahabad in one line of Wikipedia and as Shahjadpur in another. The date, 7 October 1907, comes from Wikipedia alone. We found no birth record.</aside>

## School, and marriage

Maclean says she studied up to Class V. That is about the age of the readers of this site. She was married at eleven.

Wikipedia also says she was married at eleven, but it gives no year. If the 1907 birth date is right, the wedding was around 1918 or 1919.

We state it plainly, as the sources do. Neither of them says anything more about the wedding.

## The husband's family

Her husband was Bhagwati Charan Vohra. Maclean says he lived from 1903 to 1930. His father was Shiv Charan Das, a rich Gujarati who worked for the railways and had the title Rai Sahib.

<aside class="note"><b>Rai Sahib</b>: a title the British government gave to Indians it wished to honour, usually for loyal service.</aside>

A son of such a man might have lived a quiet life. Instead, Maclean says, Bhagwati Charan studied at the National College in Lahore. There he met Bhagat Singh.

Bhagwati Charan's sister was Sushila. Maclean says she also became a well-known woman revolutionary.

## A son

Their son was born in 1925. His name was Sachinanda, but everyone called him Sachi. Maclean gives the year. Wikipedia only calls him an infant in December 1928. 

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: Maclean says Sachi was born in 1925, which would make him about three in December 1928. Wikipedia calls him "an infant". We follow Maclean, but we have not seen his birth record.</aside>

## Visitors in the house

Maclean says Durga Devi first met revolutionaries through her husband. Young men from the National College came to their home in Lahore. She names Bhagat Singh, Yashpal and Sukhdev among the visitors.

So the house was a meeting place. Maclean does not say what was talked about at the table. We do not know either.

## 16 November 1926

Wikipedia says Durga Devi first became well known through the Naujawan Bharat Sabha. This was a youth group in Lahore, started by young men including Bhagat Singh and Bhagwati Charan.

The article says that on 16 November 1926, the Sabha held a meeting in Lahore to mark the eleventh anniversary of the death of Kartar Singh Sarabha. He was a young revolutionary who was hanged in 1915.

We found this event in the one Wikipedia article only. It does not say what she did there.

## A job and a name

Maclean says that she worked as a teacher at a girls' college in Lahore. She kept this job until 1929, when she had to go into hiding.

Wikipedia says that the members of the revolutionary group called her *Bhabhi*, which is "brother's wife" in Hindi. It says this is how she became Durga Bhabhi.

The Quint makes a point of saying that she was not really Bhagat Singh's wife. She was Bhagwati Charan's wife. The name Bhabhi was a title that the group gave her.

## The group changes its name

By late 1928, Maclean says, Durga Devi and her husband had joined the Hindustan Socialist Republican Association, the HSRA. It was the new name of the older HRA. Her husband became the group's Propaganda Secretary. He was one of its main thinkers, and he wrote a paper called *The Philosophy of the Bomb*.

<aside class="note"><b>Propaganda</b>: In those days the word meant spreading a group's ideas by pamphlets and posters. It did not always mean lies.</aside>

## Two things about money

Two sources speak about money, but in different ways.

Maclean says that when her husband left for Calcutta in December 1928, he left four or five thousand rupees with her. She remembered this later. It is her own memory, told long afterwards.

Wikipedia says she sold ornaments worth three thousand rupees to help pay for the defence of Bhagat Singh and his comrades. That would have been after their arrest in 1929. We found this claim only in Wikipedia.

## A bomb factory?

Wikipedia also says that she, her husband and a man named Vimal Prasad Jain ran a bomb factory in Delhi, on Qutub Road. It says the shop was disguised as "Himalayan Toilets".

We have no second source for this. Maclean, in the excerpt we read, says the group's bomb factory was at a place called Kashmir House, which was rented in Bhagwati Charan's name. These may be two different places, or one place described two ways. We cannot tell.

## What we do not know

We do not know what her days were like in Lahore. No source we opened gives her words from those years. We do not know what she taught. And we do not know whether she was ever afraid.

What we have is a list of facts. A girl from Allahabad who lost her mother early. A wife at eleven. A mother at about eighteen. A teacher. A friend of the men who came to her house.

## Why it matters

When she said yes in December 1928, it was not the choice of a stranger. She knew these men. She knew her husband's work. She knew the risk.

Understanding that makes the famous night clearer. She did not step out of an ordinary life by chance. She had been standing at its edge for years.
