What do you do after you have spent years hiding from the police? Durga Devi chose to teach very small children.

## A school for poor children

Wikipedia says that she later opened a school for poor children in Lucknow. It does not say when.

The Quint is a news site. It carries an opinion piece by Vivek Shukla, who writes about her life. He says she trained in the Montessori way in Madras in 1939. In 1940 he says she went to Lucknow and began the Lucknow Montessori School for children from poor families.

<aside class="note"><b>Montessori</b>: a way of teaching small children, started by an Italian doctor, Maria Montessori. Children learn by choosing activities and doing things with their hands.</aside>

Shukla says that she ran the school until 1975. Then she moved to Ghaziabad.

## Which year?

Kama Maclean is a historian. In an article about what Durga Devi did next, she writes: "After independence, she set up and dedicated all her energies to a Montessori school in Lucknow." She adds that Durga Devi was still working with the school when she was interviewed in 1972, at the age of 65.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: Shukla says 1940. Maclean says after independence in 1947. That is a gap of seven years. We could not open a school record. Two Times of India reports are also said to give 1940, but we cannot open that newspaper and have not used it.</aside>

## The years in between

Maclean's article fills the gap between the hiding and the school. It says Durga Devi gave herself up in Lahore on 14 September 1932. Police records, as Maclean quotes them, say she was held in jail for two months under a special law, the Special Powers Ordinance. Then she was kept to the city of Lahore for twelve months.

In 1936, Maclean says, she became a teacher at the P. L. Girls School in Ghaziabad. In 1937 and 1938 she was President of the Delhi Congress Committee. She helped to organise a welcome for the Kakori prisoners who had been freed. In 1938 she was arrested over a *hartal* and jailed for a week.

<aside class="note"><b>Hartal</b>: a strike in which shops and offices close as a protest.</aside>

So by the time of the Montessori training in 1939, she had already been a teacher and a Congress leader.

## What we can say

We can say that she opened a school in Lucknow, because Wikipedia, Maclean and Shukla all say so. We can say it was for children from poor homes. Beyond that, the year, the number of pupils, and the way she ran it all rest on one opinion piece.

## Why it matters

A woman who had carried messages for a secret group now spent her days with children who could hardly pay to learn. She did not stop working when the struggle ended. The work changed.

The school is a reminder that the freedom movement was made of people who had other lives too. Some were teachers. Some were mothers. After 1947, some went back to the classroom.
