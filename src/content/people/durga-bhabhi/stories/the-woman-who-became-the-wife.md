Durga Devi had a three-year-old son and a husband who was away. Then, one night in December 1928, two men came to her in Lahore.

They were Sukhdev and Bhagat Singh. They had brought a third man, Rajguru. They were asking for her help.

Durga Devi's answer was yes.

## Who she was

Durga Devi Vohra was born in 1907. Wikipedia says she was married at eleven to Bhagwati Charan Vohra. He was a member of the revolutionary group that Bhagat Singh belonged to. Wikipedia says the other members called her Durga Bhabhi.

<aside class="note"><b>Bhabhi</b>: a word for a brother's wife. It is a friendly, respectful way to address a woman.</aside>

In 1925 she had a son, Sachinanda, who was called Sachi. In December 1928 he was about three years old.

She first became well known through the Naujawan Bharat Sabha, a youth group. It planned a gathering in Lahore on 16 November 1926 to remember a freedom fighter named Kartar Singh Sarabha.

## The night they came

Only days earlier, a police officer named Saunders had been shot dead in Lahore. The police were searching everywhere for the men responsible.

Her husband was not at home. Historian Kama Maclean writes that Bhagwati Charan had left Lahore in early December. He had gone to the Congress meeting in Calcutta. Before he left, he gave Durga Devi a large amount of money. She later remembered it as "4000 or 5000 rupees".

<aside class="note"><b>Congress</b>: the Indian National Congress, the biggest political party working for India's freedom.</aside>

Maclean says that Sukhdev and Bhagat Singh came to her on the night of 18 December. She did not ask them questions.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: Maclean says the night was 18 December 1928. Wikipedia says Sukhdev asked for her help on 19 December. We do not know which date is right.</aside>

## The plan

Bhagat Singh had to change his looks. Wikipedia says he shaved and cut his hair.

Then came the part that Durga Devi played. She agreed to pretend to be his wife. Her small son would be their child. Rajguru would play their servant.

Maclean says Bhagat Singh was disguised "as a married man", with Durga Devi and her son as his "family".

## On the train

Maclean writes that the group got past a police cordon and boarded a train. They travelled first class.

The National Book Trust book tells the story with extra pictures. It describes a big American car pulling up at Lahore station, where the platform was full of police and plain-clothes detectives. In this version, an attendant opens the car door. Bhagat Singh steps out as a well-dressed "American". Durga Bhabhi follows as his "mem sahib". Rajguru goes to a third-class compartment.

The same book says that Chandrashekhar Azad boarded the train too, dressed as a holy man. Maclean and Wikipedia do not mention this. We have only one source for it.

<aside class="note"><b>Mem sahib</b>: a word Indians used at that time for a European or Western lady.</aside>

The group did not go straight to Calcutta. Wikipedia says they bought tickets to Cawnpore, the old name for Kanpur. At Lucknow the group split. Rajguru left for Benares. Bhagat Singh, Durga Devi and the child went on towards Calcutta.

## A telegram

From Lucknow, Maclean writes, Bhagat Singh sent a telegram to Bhagwati Charan. It said that he was coming and was bringing "Durgawati".

Bhagwati Charan was staying with his sister Sushila in Calcutta. He was surprised.

Maclean cites the recorded memories of Shiv Verma. In them, Bhagwati Charan says: "I have got a revolutionary wife." This is a memory told long afterwards, not a note written at the time.

Durga Devi came back to Lahore a few days later with her child.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: Wikipedia says she came back a few days later. Maclean says she stayed for some sessions of the Congress in Calcutta first. The two accounts differ a little in the order of things.</aside>

## After the escape

In April 1929, Maclean writes, her husband called her to Delhi. She met Bhagat Singh at Qudsia Park, shortly before he threw a bomb in the Assembly.

On 28 May 1930, Bhagwati Charan died.

In 1972, an interviewer recorded Durga Devi. Maclean says she was at first unwilling to be interviewed, and gave a short account. In it she said: "I told Azad that I wanted my full share of revolutionary work."

That sentence is one of the few that we have in her own voice. It tells us she did not see herself as a helper at the edge.

## Later years

Maclean writes that after independence she started a Montessori school in Lucknow and gave it all her energy. Wikipedia also says she opened a school for poor children there. She lived to the age of 92 and died in Ghaziabad in 1999.

We do not yet have a source we could open that gives the exact year the school began.

## Why it matters

Many tellings of this escape are about Bhagat Singh's clever disguise. But a disguise needs people who will play their parts.

Durga Devi did not have to say yes. She was a mother with a small boy. She agreed, and she travelled beside a wanted man while the police searched for him.

When we remember the escape, we should remember her name.
