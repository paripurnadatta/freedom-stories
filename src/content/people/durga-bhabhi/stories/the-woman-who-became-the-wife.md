How could a woman travel with a wanted man without revealing who he was?

The answer involved a family that was not quite what it seemed.

Her name was Durga Devi Vohra. People called her Durga Bhabhi. She helped Bhagat Singh leave Lahore after a police officer was killed in 1928.

Her part in this story was more than a clever disguise. She made a decision of her own.

## Before the train

The man who had died was John Saunders. Bhagat Singh and Shivaram Rajguru took part in the shooting.

They had meant to target another officer, Scott. The group believed he was responsible for the police action against Lala Lajpat Rai.

After the killing, members of the revolutionary network faced a police search.

Durga Devi knew some members through her husband, Bhagwati Charan Vohra. He worked with Bhagat Singh and other revolutionaries.

Historian Kama Maclean studied Durga Devi's life. She used newspapers, archives and later interviews to look beyond famous stories about men.

## An unfamiliar family

Durga Devi agreed to pretend that Bhagat Singh was her husband. She took her young son, Sachi, with her.

Rajguru travelled with them as a supposed servant.

<aside class="note"><b>Disguise</b>: pretending to look or act like someone else, to hide your identity.</aside>

The group boarded a train out of Lahore. Maclean describes their journey to Lucknow, with travel continuing towards Calcutta.

The National Book Trust book also names Durga Devi as the pretend wife. It identifies Rajguru as the attendant.

These accounts agree on the main arrangement. They do not prove every dramatic detail added to retellings.

## A woman who acted

Durga Devi was not a passenger who happened to be present. She agreed to help people whom the police were seeking.

Her son travelled with her. This made the false family appear ordinary, according to Maclean's study.

But carrying a child did not make the journey safe. The people taking part could have been arrested.

We cannot know exactly what she felt. No source gives us her thoughts word for word.

We should also avoid invented conversations at the station.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: most histories put the Saunders killing on 17 December 1928. Maclean prints 18 December. This story follows the accepted order of events without claiming that the disagreement has been solved.</aside>

## Why it matters

Many stories about this escape focus only on Bhagat Singh's new appearance.

Durga Devi made that appearance more believable. Rajguru and her son were also part of the plan.

When women worked inside underground groups, their decisions could change what happened. Remembering Durga Devi by name helps us see that wider history.
