Why would a railway passenger pretend to work for a family he knew?

In December 1928, Shivaram Rajguru helped Bhagat Singh leave Lahore. Rajguru took the part of a servant. The supposed family included a woman and her small son.

The woman was Durga Devi. Many people called her Durga Bhabhi. She was a participant in the revolutionary movement, not merely someone helping from the edge.

## After the shooting

Bhagat Singh and Rajguru were linked to the killing of police officer John Saunders. Saunders had been shot in Lahore.

The revolutionaries had meant to attack another officer, Scott. Their mistake did not change the danger they faced.

Bhagat Singh needed a way to leave the city. The police were searching for people connected to the shooting.

Historian Kama Maclean studied Durga Devi's life. Her research explains how the escape depended on more than one person.

## A family for the journey

Durga Devi agreed to travel as Bhagat Singh's wife. She brought her young son, Sachi.

Rajguru travelled with them, pretending to be their servant. The arrangement helped the group pass as ordinary travellers.

<aside class="note"><b>Disguise</b>: clothes or a role used to hide who someone really is.</aside>

They boarded a train and went as far as Lucknow together, Maclean writes. The account continues with their onward travel towards Calcutta.

R. K. Tandon's National Book Trust book also tells of the escape. It identifies Durga Devi and Rajguru in the invented family.

The two sources agree on the main arrangement. That does not make every colourful detail in their versions certain.

<aside class="note"><b>Later memories</b>: some detailed scenes come from accounts told long afterwards. We cannot prove every item of clothing or every exchange at the station.</aside>

## Durga Devi's choice

Durga Devi carried responsibility of her own. She agreed to travel with a wanted revolutionary. Her young son was with her.

Maclean's research tells us that women in these groups did many kinds of work. They moved people, handled money and kept networks connected.

When the escape is told only as Bhagat Singh's clever plan, those choices disappear. Rajguru's part also becomes easy to miss.

The three adults played different roles. No role alone explains the journey.

## A small difference between sources

Most accounts date the Saunders shooting to 17 December. Maclean prints 18 December. The train story follows the shooting, whatever that difference means.

We do not have tickets or a complete police travel record. So we should not pretend to know each hour of the journey.

## Why it matters

The story of a servant is really about several people working together. Durga Devi's courage and Rajguru's participation both belong in it.

A railway journey can reveal how an underground movement worked, without turning a dangerous escape into make-believe.
