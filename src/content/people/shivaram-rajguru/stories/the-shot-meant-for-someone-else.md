This is a hard story. A man was killed in Lahore on 17 December 1928. He was the wrong man. Then a second man died, trying to catch the killers.

Shivaram Rajguru was part of it. He was only 20 years old. A few years later, he was hanged for it. To tell this story fairly, we have to look at all of it: the anger, the plan, the mistake, and the people who died.

## The anger

In 1928, many Indians held protests against a British commission called the Simon Commission. Lahore was one of the places.

At a protest in Lahore, the police beat people with lathis. One of the people hurt was Lala Lajpat Rai, a famous leader. He died of his injuries on 17 November 1928.

<aside class="note"><b>Lathi</b>: a long wooden stick used by the police in those days.</aside>

Some young revolutionaries were furious. They belonged to a group called the Hindustan Socialist Republican Association, the HSRA. It had been formed in September 1928, at Feroz Shah Kotla in Delhi. They decided to take revenge.

which police officer ordered the baton charge? The Home Affairs ministry page and Wikipedia name Superintendent of Police James Scott. The National Book Trust book names Saunders. Here the two that agree are followed, but the police records were not seen.

## The plan

Bhagat Singh, Rajguru, Sukhdev and Chandrashekhar Azad planned to kill Scott. Rajguru's own place in the group was in its fighting wing, under Azad. His secret name in the group was "Raghunath". This comes from the website shivaramrajguru.com.

Another member, Jai Gopal, followed Scott's movements for several days. His job was to point Scott out.

## The mistake

On 17 December 1928, at about four in the afternoon, a young police officer came out of the District Police Headquarters in Lahore. He was walking towards his motorcycle. With him was a head constable named Chanan Singh.

Jai Gopal gave the signal. But the officer was not Scott. He was John Saunders, an Assistant Superintendent of Police. It was a case of mistaken identity. The National Book Trust book puts it in one short sentence: "Jaigopal signalled and Rajguru fired."

## Who was Saunders?

Saunders was 21 years old. He was born in Peterborough, in England, in 1907. He had joined the Indian Police in April 1927, and in December 1928 he was still on probation, which means he was still in training. A plaque in his memory hangs in Peterborough Cathedral.

He was not the officer the group had wanted. He was a young man doing his first job.

## The shots

Rajguru fired first. Then Bhagat Singh fired more shots. Saunders died.

Head Constable Chanan Singh ran after the attackers. He was shot dead too. No source says who fired the shot that killed him.

<aside class="note"><b>Head constable</b>: a senior kind of constable, a police officer of the lower ranks.</aside>

The attackers ran through the nearby grounds of a college called D.A.V. College, which is now the Government Islamia College. Then they were gone.

## The next day

The group had prepared posters in advance, claiming that Scott had been killed. After the shooting, the posters were changed to talk about Saunders instead. The notice, "Beware, Ye Bureaucracy", is dated 18 December 1928.

<aside class="note"><b>Two dates</b>: every source gives 17 December 1928 for the shooting. The notice the group put out is dated 18 December. Do not mix up the two.</aside>

The police did not know at first who had done it. The first police report did not name Bhagat Singh or Rajguru, because nobody had yet identified them.

## What happened to the attackers

The group scattered. Bhagat Singh left Lahore by train. Rajguru went away too. Later he used the story of the Saunders killing to win new members for the group. He was good at organising, and he did this in Uttar Pradesh, Delhi and Punjab, says the National Book Trust book.

On 8 April 1929, Bhagat Singh threw a bomb in the Central Legislative Assembly in Delhi. He and other members of the group were arrested. A poster in Bhagat Singh's handwriting then helped the police link the group to the killing of Saunders.

Rajguru was arrested in Pune on 30 September 1929. He was tried along with Bhagat Singh and Sukhdev. On 7 October 1930, a special tribunal sentenced the three to death. They were hanged at the Lahore Central Jail on 23 March 1931. Rajguru was 22.

## The case that followed

The case was called the Lahore Conspiracy Case. It opened on 10 July 1929, with 28 accused.

At first it was registered under sections about plotting against the government. The charge of murder, section 302, was added later, when the police linked Bhagat Singh to the killing of Saunders.

The government's list had about 607 witnesses. By 26 August 1930, the prosecution had questioned 457 of them and then closed its case. The case leaned on five men who had turned approvers, among them Jai Gopal. One legal writer says there was no direct evidence against Bhagat Singh and the others. That is the writer's view, and it is not a court finding.

<aside class="note"><b>Approver</b>: a person charged in a case who agrees to give evidence for the government, usually in return for a lighter punishment or a pardon.</aside>

## What nobody knows

Rajguru left no writings that are known. The website that collects material on him says none exist. So he is known only through other people, and what he thought that evening is not on record.

Jai Gopal later gave evidence for the government, and much of the case against the others rested on his word. Exactly what he saw is also not known.

## Why it matters

The group's anger came from the death of a leader they loved. But the shots killed two men who had nothing to do with that. One was a 21-year-old trainee officer. The other was a head constable doing his duty.

A fair history can say that the young revolutionaries loved their country, and also say that people died who should not have. Both are true. We do not need to praise a killing to remember the people who were part of it.
