Which police officer had the revolutionaries planned to attack?

The answer was James Scott, a senior officer in Lahore. Yet Scott was not the man who died. Another officer, John Saunders, was killed instead.

That difference matters. It is part of Shivaram Rajguru's story. We should not change the name to make the past easier to tell.

## Why Scott was the target

In October 1928, Indians protested against the Simon Commission. The commission was supposed to discuss India's future. Yet it had no Indian members.

One protest took place in Lahore. Lala Lajpat Rai, an Indian political leader, joined it. Police used lathis against the marchers.

<aside class="note"><b>Lathi</b>: a heavy stick used by police.</aside>

Lajpat Rai died on 17 November. Bhagat Singh and his companions blamed the police action. They wanted to punish Scott, the officer they held responsible.

The group belonged to the Hindustan Socialist Republican Association, or HSRA. Its members wanted to end British rule. Some believed they could do so using weapons.

Other Indian leaders chose a different path. Gandhi urged people to resist through nonviolence.

## The wrong identification

On 17 December, the group watched the police headquarters in Lahore. A member named Jai Gopal was watching for Scott.

According to R. K. Tandon's book, Jai Gopal gave a signal. Rajguru fired at the officer who appeared. Bhagat Singh fired too.

The officer was Saunders, not Scott. Saunders died.

The Ministry of Home Affairs also calls this a mistake in identity. A later legal history gives the same account.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: most accounts date the shooting to 17 December 1928. Historian Kama Maclean's study prints 18 December. That date has not been reconciled with the other accounts.</aside>

## Another death

The group then left the area. Tandon writes that an Indian police constable, Chanan Singh, chased them.

He was shot by Chandra Shekhar Azad and died. The story cannot end after naming only Saunders.

Rajguru's part is important. So are the deaths of both men. One cannot cancel the other.

## What the sources cannot tell us

Books written later give precise movements and positions. But we have not checked the original court testimony for every detail.

We cannot describe the words Rajguru spoke. We cannot know what each person thought. And we should not invent the sounds or sights of that afternoon.

## Why it matters

History needs names, dates and difficult questions. The group intended one killing. Another man died. An Indian constable died too.

Rajguru was later tried in the Lahore case. Remembering that entire chain helps us understand the event, rather than turning it into an exciting adventure.
