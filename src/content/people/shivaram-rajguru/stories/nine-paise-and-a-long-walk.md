How far can you go with nine paise in your pocket?

In 1924, a boy named Shivaram left his home. He told nobody. He had nine paise, a few small coins. He was about sixteen years old.

<aside class="note"><b>Paise</b>: small coins. Nine of them did not buy much, even in those days.</aside>

This boy would later be known to the whole country as Rajguru.

## A boy who lost his father

Shivaram Hari Rajguru was born on 24 August 1908. Rajguru was his family's title, a name passed down from older generations.

His father died when he was six. His older brother, Dinkar, looked after him.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: Wikipedia says Shivaram was born in Khed, near Pune, a town now called Rajgurunagar. The National Book Trust book says he was born in Varanasi. Wikipedia says only his father died when he was six. The book says both parents died. We have not seen his birth record.</aside>

Shivaram went to a Marathi school. The National Book Trust book says he liked sports much more than lessons.

He studied at the New English High School in Pune. He joined the Seva Dal while he was still young.

<aside class="note"><b>Seva Dal</b>: a volunteer group. The word means "service force".</aside>

## Leaving

In 1924, he walked out of the house. The book gives no reason. It only says he left without telling anyone.

He walked about 130 kilometres and reached Nasik. Then he went on, sometimes on foot and sometimes by train.

His route took him through Jhansi, Kanpur and Lucknow. At last he reached Kashi, the old city we now call Varanasi.

## A new life in Kashi

In Kashi, he stayed for some days at Ahilya Ghat, on the bank of the river Ganga.

<aside class="note"><b>Ghat</b>: a row of steps going down to a river.</aside>

He got a place in a school where Sanskrit was taught. Later he found work in a primary school.

There he met Muneshwar Awasthi, who worked on a newspaper. Through him, says the book, Shivaram joined a secret group that wanted to end British rule.

## What others said about him

The book describes him as a reliable and careful organiser. It says he worked for the group in Uttar Pradesh, Delhi and Punjab.

It also says he could go without food for many days. But he made sure his friends were never hungry.

These are the words of a later writer. We have not found a letter or a diary that tells us how Rajguru saw himself.

## Why it matters

We often remember Rajguru only for 1928 and 1931. But before that, he was a boy who walked a very long way from home.

We do not know why he went. We know only where he went, and who he met.
