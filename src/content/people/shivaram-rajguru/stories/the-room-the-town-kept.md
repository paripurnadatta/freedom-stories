How can a small room become a place that a whole town wants to protect?

In Rajgurunagar, Maharashtra, a room is linked to the birth of Shivaram Rajguru. He later joined the fight against British rule.

Rajguru died in Lahore in 1931. The room in his hometown remained. People kept remembering the man who had lived there.

## A room with a history

Rajgurunagar was earlier called Khed. It is near Pune.

The Government of Maharashtra calls the site the Shivaram Hari Rajguru Birth Room and Thorla Wada.

<aside class="note"><b>Wada</b>: a traditional home or building complex in Maharashtra.</aside>

A government notice in March 2024 tells us something important. The birth room and Thorla Wada were already protected monuments.

The notice refers to an earlier declaration from January 2000. That earlier step gave the buildings formal protection.

<aside class="note"><b>Protected monument</b>: an old place that the government recognises as worth preserving.</aside>

## A new problem

Buildings do not stay strong without repairs. Water, age and damaged wood can make them unsafe.

In March 2026, The Times of India reported on work at the birth room. Officials said a major crack had formed in a wall near the Bhima River.

They also said that wooden roof supports had weakened. Repair workers strengthened the damaged parts.

The newspaper reported that the team studied old photographs. That helped them understand the building's earlier appearance.

This is not the same as rebuilding something however a visitor might like. Careful repairs try to keep what makes a place historic.

## Protecting more than four walls

The 2024 government notice was also about land next to the monument.

It proposed controls on building work, changes to nearby structures, cutting trees and land use.

<aside class="note"><b>Two legal steps</b>: the notice says the room was already protected. It separately proposes rules for the land around it. It does not prove that every proposed rule became final.</aside>

The March 2026 newspaper report said the room was set to reopen to visitors on 23 March. We have not confirmed that reopening happened as planned.

## Why it matters

A historic room cannot explain every choice Rajguru made. But it can help children ask new questions.

Who protects old places? How should damaged buildings be repaired? And how do we separate a surviving object from stories told about it?

Preserving a building is one way to keep those questions open.
