## Method and reading key

I opened the sources listed below in this session. The [S#] identifiers link to those exact openable pages, not search snippets. A few sources are retrospective compilations; these are explicitly marked. For each angle, **THIN** means inadequate material or only one genuinely independent source for the essential claim, **SHORT** means there may be enough verified detail for a roughly 350-word treatment, and **FLAGSHIP** would require at least 900 words of independently sourced detail. **No flagship is recommended yet**. An attractive plot is not evidence. A quotation from a secondary source can be verified *as printed* without establishing that the historical person actually said it. No fabricated speech or atmosphere should be added. The story is for Class 5; mention killings or execution without graphic details or praise for the act.

Use **HRA** for the association before September 1928 and **HSRA** after its September 1928 reorganisation. Rajguru's presence at the Delhi reorganisation meeting is **not established** by the opened sources.

## 1. Subject, dates, family, links, core chronology

**Preferred name:** Shivaram Hari Rajguru (also spelled Shivram Hari Rajguru; Raj Guru in some British-era or popular accounts). The contemporary government announcement supplies the full name, birth date and place [S1]; the Ministry of Home Affairs records his joint execution [S3]. Birth: **24 August 1908, Khed, near Pune in the then Bombay Presidency**, per [S1] and [S4]; Khed is now Rajgurunagar. **Important conflict:** National Book Trust [S2, printed p.17] instead explicitly states birth in **Varanasi** on the same date; it also separately says he subsequently travelled to Kashi. Do not silently hide or harmonise that contradiction. Death: **23 March 1931, Lahore Central Jail**, executed following the Lahore Conspiracy Case [S1, S3, S10].

**Parents/family:** Wikipedia [S5] names parents Hari Narayan/Hari Narayan Rajguru and Parvati Devi, and an older brother Dinkar; [S2, printed p.17] names father Hari Rajguru and elder brother Dinkar Hari Rajguru. **Conflict:** [S2] says both parents died when he was six, whereas [S5] says only his father died then. No opened birth, municipal, or family-primary record establishes those details. Mark all such particulars provisional. Educational travel to Varanasi/Kashi for Sanskrit learning is supported by [S1, S2]. [S2] alone supplies an eventual teaching job and a connection to Muneshwar Awasthi; do not elevate these details without another source. His revolutionary association in the late 1920s is supported by [S1, S3, S8]; before September 1928 refer to **HRA**, and after that month **HSRA** [S6].

**Connected people and places:** Rajguru — Khed/Rajgurunagar and Pune (birthplace and arrest); Varanasi/Kashi (Sanskrit studies); Lahore (Saunders action, case and execution). Lala Lajpat Rai (protest and death), J. A. Scott (intended target), J. P. Saunders (person killed), Jai Gopal (reported mistaken signal), Bhagat Singh (fellow participant, co-accused), Chandra Shekhar Azad (associate), Chanan/Channan Singh (Indian police constable killed during pursuit), Sukhdev Thapar (co-accused), Durga Devi/“Durga Bhabhi” and her son (escape), Bhagwati Charan Vohra (revolutionary connected with Durga Devi), Batukeshwar Dutt (Bhagat Singh's 1929 Assembly collaborator, **not** Rajguru's collaborator in that specific act) [S2, S3, S7, S8, S15]. For younger readers, make clear that a police officer and an Indian constable died in the episode.

**Chronological spine, not independently verified in every small detail:**

1. **1908:** Birth at Khed according to [S1, S4], contradicted by the NBT statement [S2].
2. **Undated youth:** Move to Kashi and Sanskrit learning [S1, S2]; journey anecdotes are later narratives.
3. **8–9 September 1928:** HRA reorganisation as HSRA at Delhi [S6]; Rajguru's attendance is not proved.
4. **30 October / 17 November 1928:** Lajpat Rai injured during anti-Simon-Commission protest and later died [S3, S13]. The specific officer blamed for the baton charge is disputed [S2 versus S3].
5. **17 December 1928:** Saunders killed at Lahore by a group that included Rajguru, after Scott was reportedly mistaken for Saunders [S2, S3, S8, S9]. [S7] prints **18 December** instead.
6. **Soon afterwards:** Train escape aided by Durga Devi, with Rajguru presented as a servant [S2, S7]; precise route details come from later recollections.
7. **1929:** Arrest in Pune; [S13] says **30 September 1929**, whereas NBT [S2] prints **30 September 1928**, impossible in its own sequence because this precedes the December incident. No contemporary arrest register inspected.
8. **1930:** Ordinance-created special tribunal; **7 October 1930** death sentences [S8, S13].
9. **11 February 1931:** Privy Council appeal dismissed according to [S8, S13]; original judgment not inspected.
10. **23 March 1931:** Execution with Bhagat Singh and Sukhdev in Lahore [S1, S3, S10]; The Tribune printed its prominent report on **25 March 1931** [S10].

## 2. Proposed story angles (14)

### Angle 01 — A House in Khed, Two Birthplaces in Print
**Hook:** Why does a government account name one birthplace while a printed national-publisher book names another?  
**Size:** **THIN** — multiple sources exist but their directly conflicting claims cannot yet be resolved using independent birth records.  
**Key facts:** [S1] dates his birth to 24 Aug 1908 in Khed near Pune; [S4] gives Kheda/Pune; [S2, printed p.17] calls Varanasi the birth city. The house and its later protection can be treated separately [S11].  
**Disagreement:** Khed versus Varanasi. Never claim a historian verified the correct place through a birth register.  
**Not checked:** Birth certificate, ancestral records, original Hindi biography *Ajeya Krantikari—Rajguru*.  
**Story boundary:** A well-evidenced story about modern preservation could be written, but a dramatized birth-day scene could not.

### Angle 02 — The Sanskrit Student in Kashi
**Hook:** How did a student who travelled to learn Sanskrit become part of a revolutionary circle?  
**Size:** **SHORT**, if confined to documented journey, Sanskrit studies, and later association; no invented classmates or classroom scenes.  
**Key facts:** Government [S1] connects his Varanasi Sanskrit studies with meeting revolutionaries. National Book Trust [S2, printed p.18] recounts his arrival in Kashi and entry into a Sanskrit school; [S4] separately discusses his youthful departure.  
**Disagreement:** [S2] describes childhood family losses differently from [S5]; [S2] also erroneously says he was born in Varanasi. Its specific nine-paise journey budget disagrees with the three-anna account in [S4].  
**Not checked:** Exact departure date, travel itinerary, school admission, teacher's name, student register.  
**LEGEND / LATER MEMORY:** Travelling with a tiny coin sum and walking exact distances appears in later retellings; no named contemporary witness found. Omit colorful sums from published story.

### Angle 03 — The Name Raghunath
**Hook:** What can a second name tell us about people working secretly?  
**Size:** **THIN** — only the private commemorative compilation [S14] explicitly identifies the alias “Raghunath” in inspected material.  
**Key facts:** [S14] calls it a party alias; wider underground context is documented separately [S3, S8]. These do not provide an independent confirmation of the alias.  
**Disagreement:** None located, but a single later source is not confirmation.  
**Not checked:** Original party notes, seized correspondence, witness record proving Rajguru himself used it.  
**Story boundary:** Hold this angle until an independent archival record is found; do not invent a conversation about choosing a secret name.

### Angle 04 — When the Letters Changed: HRA to HSRA
**Hook:** What did adding one word to an organisation's name mean?  
**Size:** **SHORT** as a context story, **THIN if portrayed as a first-person Rajguru episode**.  
**Key facts:** The Tribune [S6] places the HRA-to-HSRA reorganisation on 8–9 Sep 1928 at Feroz Shah Kotla in Delhi, connecting the change to socialism; [S16] separately discusses the earlier HRA context; [S1] places Rajguru with the later organisation.  
**Disagreement:** [S1] uses the historical name “Army”; [S6] uses “Association”; explain variation without inventing a second organisation.  
**Not checked:** Minutes/attendance proving Rajguru attended the Delhi meeting.  
**Editorial guardrail:** Call it **HRA before September 1928**; **HSRA afterwards**. Never depict Rajguru at a meeting solely because he later belonged to the group.

### Angle 05 — The Protest and the Question of Who Gave the Order
**Hook:** What happened after the protest against the Simon Commission in Lahore?  
**Size:** **SHORT**, with a clear sources-disagree note.  
**Key facts:** [S3] says Lajpat Rai was beaten at the protest, died on 17 Nov 1928, and identifies police superintendent J. S. Scott; [S2, printed p.16–17] instead attributes the baton charge to J. P. Saunders. [S13] follows the Scott account.  
**Disagreement:** **Scott versus Saunders** as the officer directing the charge. The central mistaken-identity account in [S3, S8] must not be confused with the question of the lathi charge.  
**Not checked:** Contemporary police orders, contemporaneous medical opinion and the demonstration's original reports.  
**Tone:** Acknowledge opposition to colonial repression without stating disputed medical causation as settled.

### Angle 06 — Two Names, One Fatal Mistake
**Hook:** The group meant to target Scott, but a different police officer was killed.  
**Size:** **SHORT**; several sources support the sequence, but enough independent fine-grained evidence for a 900-word scene has not been demonstrated.  
**Key facts:** [S3, S8] identify Saunders as the man killed in a case of mistaken identity on 17 Dec 1928; [S2, printed p.15] places Jai Gopal's signal before Rajguru's first shot and Bhagat Singh's later firing. [S15] provides an additional state-broadcaster retelling, possibly derivative.  
**Disagreement:** [S7] says **18 Dec** instead of the 17 Dec found in [S2, S8, S9]; the date in Maclean's article must be called out rather than ignored.  
**Not checked:** Original 1928 depositions, ballistic proof, first-person account by Rajguru.  
**Class 5 note:** State clearly that Saunders was killed. Describe no wounds and glorify no shooting. Make clear Rajguru's involvement is not erased by questioning later embellishments.

### Angle 07 — The Indian Constable Left Out of the Picture
**Hook:** Why do retellings often forget the Indian officer who also died?  
**Size:** **THIN** — [S2, printed p.15] and [S15] name head constable Chanan/Channan Singh, but the state-broadcaster narrative appears potentially derived from common secondary summaries. The private compilation [S13] repeats it, not independent archival testimony.  
**Key facts:** [S2] reports that Chanan Singh pursued the fleeing revolutionaries and was shot by Azad; [S15] also mentions him.  
**Disagreement:** Transliteration Chanan/Channan; exact path and firing positions not directly independently checked.  
**Not checked:** Police death register, family record, original case testimony.  
**Story boundary:** A careful paragraph recognizing the second death is suitable inside Angle 06; a standalone 350-word narrative is not yet supported.

### Angle 08 — The Passenger Called a Servant
**Hook:** How did Rajguru travel out of Lahore with Durga Devi and a disguised Bhagat Singh?  
**Size:** **SHORT**; especially suitable to tell as a team effort, not to erase Durga Devi's choices.  
**Key facts:** Scholar Kama Maclean [S7] recounts Durga Devi travelling with her son and posing as Bhagat Singh's wife while Rajguru played the family's servant; [S2, printed p.17] independently recounts the false family arrangement and train.  
**Disagreement:** [S7] introduces the episode after dating the shooting 18 Dec, whereas [S2] dates it 17 Dec; details of dress, carriage and itinerary differ.  
**Not checked:** Original tickets, dated telegram image, eyewitness affidavits. Maclean cites later recollections; those are not direct recordings of the event.  
**LEGEND / LATER MEMORY:** Tandon's “American” disguise, cigar and stage-like railway detail should not be treated as independently observed facts. Do not ascribe private thoughts to Durga Devi.

### Angle 09 — The FIR Without Their Names
**Hook:** What does a first police report tell us—and what does it not prove?  
**Size:** **THIN** — a newspaper reported a lawyer's description of a recovered FIR, but the original Urdu FIR was not opened.  
**Key facts:** [S9] reports a 2019 Lahore court supplied an attested 1928 FIR, initially registered against unidentified shooters on 17 Dec 1928. [S8] independently describes the later prosecution and testimony; it does **not** verify the wording of the FIR.  
**Disagreement:** The lawyer's effort to reopen the case is a legal claim, not proof of innocence; the first report omitting names does not negate later identification or conviction.  
**Not checked:** Full scanned FIR, authentic Urdu transcription, case docket and final outcome of the later reopening petition.  
**Story boundary:** Useful to teach the difference between initial police information and later evidence, not to reverse a verdict without records.

### Angle 10 — Arrested in Pune: Which Year?
**Hook:** How can a printed arrest date appear to come before the event for which someone was wanted?  
**Size:** **THIN** — exact arrest date lacks directly inspected contemporary evidence.  
**Key facts:** [S13] gives Pune arrest **30 Sep 1929**; [S2, printed p.18] instead prints **30 Sep 1928** while placing it after the Dec 1928 shooting; [S15] confirms Pune arrest without exact date.  
**Disagreement:** This is an irreconcilable chronology in [S2]; the 1928 date is likely an error, but that conclusion is an inference, not a newly verified police document.  
**Not checked:** Arrest memo, police register, original warrant, alleged recovered pistol/cartridges.  
**Story boundary:** Make a small research-method note, not a dramatic story of a named informer whose role exists only in [S2].

### Angle 11 — A Court Created by an Ordinance
**Hook:** What was different about the court that tried Rajguru and his companions?  
**Size:** **SHORT**, possibly expandable only after reading the actual court record.  
**Key facts:** Ministry of Home Affairs [S3] says a British ordinance created a special tribunal and removed ordinary appeal rights; legal analyst [S8] identifies Ordinance III of 1930, tribunal hearings in some accused persons' absence, and the **7 Oct 1930** death verdict. Private compilation [S13] gives the same timetable.  
**Disagreement:** Wording about “no appeal” needs nuance: [S8] says ordinary High Court appeal was blocked but a *special leave* application reached the Privy Council. Do not say there was absolutely no legal challenge.  
**Not checked:** Complete special tribunal judgment, official text of Ordinance III, original record of arguments, claims about precise witness numbers.  
**Tone:** Explain a tribunal and appeal in simple language, without presenting lawyers' later interpretations as if they were the judgment text.

### Angle 12 — A Petition Reaches London
**Hook:** How did a legal request travel from the Lahore case to the Privy Council?  
**Size:** **SHORT** at most; more original law-report evidence needed before expansion.  
**Key facts:** [S8] recounts dismissal of a special-leave petition on **11 Feb 1931** with detailed reasons later in February; [S13] independently-in-form but retrospectively repeats that timeline; [S3] gives context of the reduced appeal rights.  
**Disagreement:** The restriction of ordinary appeals and the existence of this special leave proceeding are not contradictory once separated clearly.  
**Not checked:** Original Privy Council case report, correspondence, exact petitions, counsel submissions; private compilation and legal essay may draw on overlapping materials.  
**Story boundary:** Use as one legal-history piece or combine with Angle 11 rather than padding two near-identical stories.

### Angle 13 — A Headline Two Days Later
**Hook:** How did readers learn about the execution of the three young men?  
**Size:** **SHORT**, as a document-based look at how news circulated.  
**Key facts:** [S10] identifies a front page of *The Tribune* dated **25 Mar 1931** reporting the execution; the Commons scan [C1] allows the actual period image to be examined, and [S3] separately dates execution to **23 Mar**.  
**Disagreement:** The execution date and front-page date are two different things, not competing versions. Details of secret handling of the bodies and last personal visits must be checked against primary newspapers before inclusion.  
**Not checked:** Complete readable transcription of the small newspaper print and any independent account of the final hours.  
**Tone:** Do not invent Rajguru's last words, happy singing, or prison officers' emotions.

### Angle 14 — The Birth Room That Had to Be Saved
**Hook:** How does a community protect a building connected with someone who died long ago?  
**Size:** **SHORT**, well suited for young readers, with genuine freely reusable photographs.  
**Key facts:** Maharashtra's official gazette [S11] identifies the birth room and Thorla Wada at Rajgurunagar as a protected monument, proposing restrictions in the adjacent area; an independently opened *Times of India* report [S12] describes recent conservation and planned reopening for 23 Mar 2026. Commons photographs [C2–C3] show the room and gate in 2012.  
**Disagreement:** The gazette is a **notice of intention for adjacent controlled-area restrictions**, not proof those proposed restrictions were finally enacted. [S12] says reopening was planned, not definitively completed.  
**Not checked:** Completed final regulation, archaeological conservation report, on-site verification of the reopening or claimed preservation of ashes.  
**Tone:** This is the strongest nonviolent material-memory angle, and distinguishes original architecture from later remembrance.
