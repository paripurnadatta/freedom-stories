## Research notes

Major Congress leader and advocate of Hindu-Muslim unity.

### Print, faith and composite nationalism

Azad joined Islamic scholarship, Urdu journalism and a strong commitment to composite Indian nationalism.

### The newspaper as political institution

Al-Hilal and Al-Balagh show print becoming an organizing force powerful enough to attract colonial suppression.

### Congress leadership through crisis

His later Congress presidency should be tied to wartime politics without reducing the profile to negotiations alone.

### Chronology

- **1912-1916**: Al-Hilal and Al-Balagh. Uses Urdu journalism for anti-imperial politics and unity
- **1919-1920s**: Khilafat-era leadership. Becomes a major Muslim nationalist voice within wider anti-colonial politics
- **1923**: Congress presidency. Serves as Congress president
- **1940-1946**: Congress leadership in wartime. Leads Congress through wartime crisis and Quit India-era repression

### Evidence claims

- Born on 11 November 1888 in Mecca and later became an influential Urdu journalist. (high confidence)
- Published Al-Hilal and later Al-Balagh as vehicles of anti-imperial political writing. (high confidence)
- Was active in the Khilafat Movement and held leadership roles in the Khilafat organization. (high confidence)
- Served as president of the Indian National Congress in 1923 and again from 1940. (high confidence)
