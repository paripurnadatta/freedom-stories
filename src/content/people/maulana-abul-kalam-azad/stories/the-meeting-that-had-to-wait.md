Two men were in the same town, and they could not meet. One was a man held in internment. The other was a visitor who had come to help farmers.

Azad was the first. He had been kept in Ranchi since 1916. The second was Gandhi, who came to Ranchi in connection with his work among the farmers of Champaran. Azad writes that Gandhi said he wished to meet him. The Bihar Government did not give the permission. So they did not meet then.

## January 1920

Azad was let out on 1 January 1920. He says he met Gandhi for the first time in Delhi that month.

The question of the day was the Khilafat. This was the Muslim worry about Turkey and its Sultan, whom many Indian Muslims respected as their religious head. People wanted to send a group to the Viceroy.

<aside class="note"><b>Khilafat</b>: the Caliph was the religious head of many Muslims. After the First World War, Indian Muslims feared that the British would take away the Turkish Sultan's powers. Their movement to protect them was called the Khilafat movement.</aside>

On 20 January 1920 a meeting was held in Delhi. Azad says Gandhi was there, and Tilak and other Congress leaders supported the Muslim view. A group went to the Viceroy. Azad signed their letter but did not go with them. He thought, he says, that "matters had gone beyond memorials and deputations."

The Viceroy said he could do nothing himself.

## Gandhi's plan

At the next meeting, Gandhi laid out a different plan. He said it was time to stop asking. Indians should stop helping the government. He suggested that people give back their titles. They should boycott the courts and the government schools and colleges. They should leave government jobs, and keep out of the new legislatures.

He called it non-cooperation.

<aside class="note"><b>Non-cooperation</b>: refusing to help the government in any way, without using violence.</aside>

Azad tells us how the others in the room reacted. Hakim Ajmal Khan said he needed time to think. Maulvi Abdul Bari said it raised deep questions, and he wanted to pray before answering. Mohammed Ali and Shaukat Ali said they would wait for the Maulvi.

Then Gandhi turned to Azad. Azad says he answered "without a moment's hesitation" that he accepted the whole programme.

## An old idea

Why so fast? Azad says the plan reminded him of something. In 1901, an anarchist had attacked the King of Italy, and Tolstoy had written an open letter. Tolstoy said violence was wrong, and that a government could be stopped instead by refusing taxes, leaving its jobs and boycotting what it ran. Azad also remembered that he himself had put forward a similar idea in articles in Al-Hilal.

<aside class="note"><b>Tolstoy</b>: Leo Tolstoy, a famous Russian writer, who wrote about non-violence. Gandhi admired him too.</aside>

## Not everyone agreed

The Congress did not take it in at once. In September 1920 a special session was held in Calcutta. Azad says Lala Lajpat Rai, who led the session, and C. R. Das did not agree with Gandhi. Bipin Chandra Pal said boycotting British goods was the best weapon and that he did not have much faith in Gandhi's other items. The resolution still passed by a large majority. By the Nagpur session that December, Das had changed his mind, and Lajpat Rai joined too.

Azad says he travelled with Gandhi for most of that period.

## Jail again, and then the chair

The government arrested leaders across the country. In Bengal, Azad writes, C. R. Das and he were among the first. Subhas Chandra Bose was in prison with them in the Alipur Central Jail. Azad was tried for a long time. He was given one year. He says he was not freed until 1 January 1923.

When he came out, the Congress was split over whether to enter the councils. Azad tried to bring the two sides together. At a special session in September 1923 they reached an agreement. He was asked to preside. He was thirty-five. He says it was said that he was the youngest man ever elected President of the Congress. Wikipedia says the same.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: Wikipedia says Azad was sentenced to a year in prison in 1921, after a paper called Paigham was banned. Azad says he was held for a long time under trial and sentenced later. We do not know the exact date of his arrest.</aside>

## Why it matters

The two men could not meet in Ranchi because the government did not want them to. When they did meet, it shaped the rest of Azad's life. He worked beside Gandhi for many years, though they did not agree on everything. What we can say from his own book is simple. When Gandhi asked, he said yes at once.
