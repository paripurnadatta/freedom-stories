On 16 January 1948, a few months after India became free, Maulana Azad spoke to the All India Education Conference. Wikipedia reports one thing he said that day. Receiving basic education, he told them, is "a birthright of every individual."

At that time most Indian children never saw a school. Azad was the man now in charge of changing that.

## The job

Azad was the first Education Minister of independent India. Wikipedia says he held the post from 15 August 1947 until his death.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: The Wikipedia page on National Education Day gives his time as Education Minister as 15 August 1947 to 2 February 1958. The main Wikipedia page on Azad says he held it until his death, which was on 22 February 1958. We do not know the reason for the difference of twenty days.</aside>

Wikipedia lists the things he put first. These were adult literacy, free and compulsory education up to age 14, and the education of girls.

## Colleges and universities

The IIT Kharagpur's own news site says Azad was a force behind the founding of the first IIT, at Kharagpur. Wikipedia says the first IIT was set up in 1951. The same article dates the All India Council for Technical Education, which he also founded, to 1945.

<aside class="note"><b>IIT</b>: the Indian Institutes of Technology, a group of top colleges for engineering and science.</aside>

The University Grants Commission also has Azad's name. A body of the same name had been formed in 1945 to look after three universities: Aligarh, Banaras and Delhi. In 1947 it was made responsible for all Indian universities. On 28 December 1953 Azad, as Education Minister, inaugurated the UGC in its new form. In November 1956 a law of Parliament made it a permanent statutory body.

<aside class="note"><b>Statutory body</b>: an organisation created by a law passed in Parliament.</aside>

## Other work

Azad also stayed in politics. In 1952 he was elected to the Lok Sabha from Rampur. Wikipedia says that in 1957 he won from both Rampur and Gurgaon. In 1956 he presided over the UNESCO General Conference, held in Delhi.

## His last years and the day named for him

Azad died on 22 February 1958. He is buried near the Jama Masjid in Delhi. In 1992, after his death, he was given the Bharat Ratna, India's highest civilian honour.

Since 2008, India has marked 11 November as National Education Day. That is Azad's birthday. The government announced it on 11 September 2008 to honour his work in building the base of independent India's education system.

## Why it matters

Azad started out as a boy in a house in Calcutta who wanted to read English books. Decades later he was in charge of whether other children in India could go to school at all. Some of what he began, like the UGC, is still working today. Others, like free schooling for every child up to 14, took many more years.

We have not read Azad's own papers on these years, and we could not check government records. Much of the detail here comes from Wikipedia. A fuller story of what he did as minister needs more sources.
