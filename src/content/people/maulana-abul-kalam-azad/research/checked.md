# Azad: what was checked (22 October 2026)

Report filed: report-from-other-ai.pdf. Every claim below was tested against sources opened in this session:
Azad's own book *India Wins Freedom* (1959 edition, archive.org), Wikipedia (Abul Kalam Azad, Ghubar-e-Khatir,
National Education Day, University Grants Commission), the TPM journal article on Ghubar-e-Khatir, the IIT Kharagpur
KGP Chronicle, Janata Weekly (Jama Masjid speech) and Scroll.in.

## Held up
- Born in Mecca 1888 (11 November per Wikipedia); family came to Calcutta in 1890; mother died a year later.
- Home education, finished course at sixteen, learned English, read the Bible in three languages with a dictionary (Azad's own words).
- Revolutionary contacts (Shyam Sunder Chakravarty, Aurobindo), 1908 tour, al-Azhar denial: all from Azad's book.
- Al-Hilal (June 1912), deposits Rs 2,000 then Rs 10,000, press seized 1915, Al-Balagh, externed April 1916, Ranchi until 31 Dec 1919 / released 1 Jan 1920.
- Meeting Gandhi in January 1920, accepting non-cooperation at once, Congress president at 35 in 1923.
- Arrest 9 August 1942, ten Working Committee members at Ahmednagar, 24 unsent letters (Ghubar-e-Khatir), published 1946.
- Opposition to Partition, talks with Patel, Nehru and Gandhi (Azad's account only).
- Education minister, UGC (28 Dec 1953), National Education Day (from 2008), Bharat Ratna 1992.

## Disagreements found (told in the stories as "Two versions")
- Al-Hilal circulation: Azad says 26,000 a week; the report says over 25,000. Wikipedia gives no figure. We use Azad's own figure and say it is his.
- Al-Hilal ban: Wikipedia says 1914 (Press Act); Azad says deposits lost, press seized 1915.
- Date and place of writing Ghubar-e-Khatir (Wikipedia 1942-46; journal article 1942-45 in Ahmednagar and Bankura).
- Jama Masjid speech: Janata Weekly says 23 Oct 1947; Scroll says 1948.
- End of ministry: 2 Feb 1958 (National Education Day page) against until death 22 Feb 1958.

## Could not be checked, left out
- Sparrows mistaking him for a stone, hot tea used as ink at 3:30 am: no source we could open (Wikipedia only mentions a description of morning tea). Left out.
- Sitar lessons and the Taj at dawn (Times of India): site is blocked from here. Left out.
- Hali mistaking him for his father: reported as a legend; no source opened. Left out.
- Douglas's Oxford biography (later dates for the move to Calcutta, the 1906 Dhaka meeting): not opened. Left out; we use Azad's 1890.
- Dharasana 1930, Zulaikha Begum's year of death (report says 1943, Azad's book places the telegram in April after early 1944), Jamia Millia role beyond Wikipedia, Rs 48,000 Urdu grant, the sealed pages of India Wins Freedom and the 1988 Delhi High Court case: not verified. Left out.
- PIB, E-Pao and Constitution of India Archive pages from the report: not opened.

## Weak points
- Much of the Partition story rests on Azad's own book, published after his death. The story says so. We did not find Patel's or Nehru's side in their own words.
- The Jama Masjid text we found has spelling mistakes and no named translator; only short lines are used, labelled.
