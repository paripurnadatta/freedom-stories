## Research notes

Assassinated Michael O'Dwyer in London in 1940 in retaliation for the Amritsar massacre.

### Memory carried across borders

Singh’s profile should map a transnational political life rather than present a simple straight line of revenge.

### London, 1940

The killing transformed the memory of colonial violence in Punjab into an international act of political violence.

### Aliases and political identity

His multiple names should be preserved as part of underground identity, not treated as a data-cleaning anomaly.

### Chronology

- **1919**: Jallianwala Bagh context. Punjab’s postwar repression becomes central to his political memory
- **1920s-1930s**: Transnational revolutionary activity. Moves through overseas networks and aliases
- **1940**: Assassination of Michael O’Dwyer. Kills O’Dwyer in London and is arrested

### Evidence claims

- Was born in Punjab in December 1899 and was originally named Sher Singh. (high confidence)
- Jallianwala Bagh became a central part of his later political memory. (high confidence)
- Killed Michael O’Dwyer in London in 1940 and submitted to arrest. (high confidence)
- Used multiple names during his transnational life, including Ram Mohammad Singh Azad variants. (high confidence)
- The supplied record connects Udham Singh with Ghadar Movement as associated with. (medium confidence)
- The supplied record connects Udham Singh with Jallianwala Bagh Massacre as associated with. (high confidence)
