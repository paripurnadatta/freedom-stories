A prisoner waiting for trial has little to do. He can read, if he has books. He can write, if someone will carry the letters out.

Udham Singh wrote at least seven letters from Brixton Prison in 1940. The Tribune reported on them. They are handwritten and kept today in the Bhai Gurdas Library at Guru Nanak Dev University in Amritsar.

## What the letters ask for

Most of them were written to a friend and helper in London, Shiv Singh Jouhal. The Tribune says that most of the letters ask him for more books. One of them asks for the Heer of Waris Shah. Waris Shah was the writer of the Heer.

The books tell us something about him as a reader. We do not know which other books he asked for.

## The name and the number

The letters are signed "M Azad". That is the short form of the name he used in court, which we tell about in "A name on the arm".

The Tribune also says that all the letters carry the same prison number, 1010.

## The last letter

The last letter is dated 7 June 1940. That is three days after his trial began, the Tribune says. It is also two days after the death sentence, which Counsel gives as 5 June. In it he asks for a "Gutka Saheb". That is a small prayer book of Sikh hymns.

The Tribune notes that no one knows if his wish was met.

## A friend's name

The Tribune says that the letter of 30 March 1940 speaks of Bhagat Singh as his best friend. It says he also mentions someone "waiting for him on 23rd". According to the Tribune, he hoped to be hanged on the same date as Bhagat Singh.

The same letter describes death in a way that is hard to read. We do not copy the words here. A prisoner facing a trial may write many things that he feels in the moment. They are not a plan for anyone else to follow, and the story does not make them a lesson.

## How the letters came home

Jouhal, his friend and aide in London, kept the letters until 1972. The Tribune says that a man named M. S. Gill persuaded him to give them to the university in that year.

So the letters were in a friend's keeping for thirty-two years after Udham Singh died. We do not know what other papers there were.

## What the letters do not tell us

They do not say whether he thought the court was fair. They do not describe his hunger strike. They are short and have little about the shooting itself.

Counsel says that Singh also wrote letters saying that the police made up evidence. We did not see those letters in the Tribune's account.

## Why it matters

When a person's story is told mostly by courts and newspapers, his own letters are precious. They are small, and they ask for books and a prayer book. They also show that in the last weeks he reached out to friends and tried to read.

The Tribune says that his last request may never have been granted. This is the kind of small unanswered thing that a good story should not skip.
