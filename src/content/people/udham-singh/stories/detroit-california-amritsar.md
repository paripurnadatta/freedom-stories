Where was Udham Singh in the 1920s? Different writers give different roads. All the roads lead to the same place: a police station in Amritsar in 1927.

## The roads out of Punjab

After a three-month stay in Dover in 1921, he went to the United States. He worked in Detroit as a tool-maker for the Ford Motor Company and then moved to California. That is how a website of the University of Bristol, South Asian Britain, tells it.

Vinay Lal tells it differently. He went to Africa first, probably Uganda and possibly Nairobi, and then to America. In the middle of the 1920s he reportedly travelled through several European countries.

The National Book Trust book says he worked with a contractor, went to Africa and then America.

<aside class="note"><b>Several roads</b>: South Asian Britain says Dover in 1921 and then the United States. Vinay Lal says Africa and then the United States. The Tribune quotes Dr Navtej Singh as saying that Udham Singh worked for the North Western Railway from 1917 to 1922. The three do not fit together, and no source settles it.</aside>

## The Ghadar Party

In California, he made contact with the Ghadar Party, which was dedicated to Indian independence. He joined in 1924, says Wikipedia. Lal says that he supported the Ghadr movement and was influenced by Lala Lajpat Rai and especially Bhagat Singh.

<aside class="note"><b>Ghadar Party</b>: a party of Indians living abroad, mostly in North America, who wanted India to be free. It also had a newspaper.</aside>

## A police search in Amritsar

He came back to India in 1927, in July, says Lal. He carried arms and banned writings.

On 30 August 1927 he was arrested in Amritsar under the Arms Act. The police found revolvers, a pistol, ammunition and copies of a banned paper, Gadar-I-Goonj. A court gave him five years of rigorous imprisonment. He was released on 23 October 1931. Two sources agree on October 1931.

He served about four years, says Lal. That fits, since 1927 to 1931 is four years, and the sentence was longer.

## After prison

After his release he lived in Sunam and then in Amritsar, where he reportedly worked as a signboard painter. "Reportedly" is Lal's word, and he gives no source for it.

## Why it matters

A person's life can be a map with the middle missing. Here we have a start, in a factory town in America, and an end, in a police cell in Amritsar. The years between depend on the book.

Three things are firm: a 1927 arrest in Amritsar, banned papers and weapons, and a release in October 1931. The rest is told, but not proved.
