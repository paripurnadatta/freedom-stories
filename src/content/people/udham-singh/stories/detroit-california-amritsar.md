Where was Udham Singh in the 1920s? Different writers give different roads. All the roads lead to the same place: a police station in Amritsar in 1927.

## The roads out of Punjab

The South Asian Britain site, run by the University of Bristol, says that after a three-month stay in Dover in 1921 he went to the United States. It says he worked in Detroit as a tool-maker for the Ford Motor Company and then moved to California.

Vinay Lal's article gives a different route. It says he went to Africa first, probably Uganda and possibly Nairobi, and then to America. It says that in the middle of the 1920s he reportedly travelled through several European countries.

The NBT book says that he worked with a contractor, went to Africa and then America.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: South Asian Britain says he went to Dover in 1921 and then to the United States. Vinay Lal says Africa and then the United States. The Tribune quotes Dr Navtej Singh as saying that Udham Singh worked for the North Western Railway from 1917 to 1922. We cannot fit these together and we do not choose.</aside>

## The Ghadar Party

In California, says South Asian Britain, he made contact with the Ghadar Party. It describes the party as dedicated to Indian independence. Wikipedia says he joined in 1924. Lal says that he supported the Ghadr movement and was influenced by Lala Lajpat Rai and especially Bhagat Singh.

<aside class="note"><b>Ghadar Party</b>: a party of Indians living abroad, mostly in North America, who wanted India to be free. It also had a newspaper.</aside>

## A police search in Amritsar

He came back to India in 1927. Lal says it was in July. He says he carried arms and banned writings.

NBT is exact about the arrest. It says that on 30 August 1927 he was arrested in Amritsar under the Arms Act. It says the police found revolvers, a pistol, ammunition and copies of a banned paper, Gadar-I-Goonj. A court gave him five years of rigorous imprisonment. NBT says he was released on 23 October 1931. South Asian Britain says he was released in October 1931, too.

<aside class="note"><b>Arms Act</b>: a law that made it a crime to have weapons without permission.</aside>

Lal says he served about four years. That fits, since 1927 to 1931 is four years, and the sentence was longer.

## After prison

Lal says that after his release he lived in Sunam and then in Amritsar, where he reportedly worked as a signboard painter. "Reportedly" means that Lal does not give a source for it.

## Why it matters

A person's life can be a map with the middle missing. Here we have a start, in a factory town in America, and an end, in a police cell in Amritsar. The years between depend on the book.

The facts we can put down with confidence are these: a 1927 arrest in Amritsar, banned papers and weapons, and a release in October 1931. The rest is told, but not proved.
