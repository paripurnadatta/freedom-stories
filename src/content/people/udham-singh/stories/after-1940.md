The story of a person does not end on the day he dies. The sources for Udham Singh show this clearly. They disagree about his last day and they disagree about his journey home.

## The hanging

Most sources say that Udham Singh was hanged at Pentonville Prison in London on 31 July 1940. South Asian Britain says so. So does Wikipedia, Vinay Lal and the Tribune. Wikipedia says he was buried at the prison.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: The National Book Trust book says he was sentenced on 10 June 1940 and hanged on 12 June 1940. Four other sources say 31 July. We use 31 July.</aside>

## Coming home

Wikipedia says that his remains were dug up and sent to India in 1974. It says this was done at the request of a member of the Punjab assembly, Sadhu Singh Thind. It says the remains were cremated at Sunam on 2 August 1974, and that the ashes were divided into seven urns and sent to several places. One of them was Jallianwala Bagh. South Asian Britain also gives 1974.

The NBT book says that Udham Singh had asked for his ashes to be returned to India, but this was refused. It says the Government of India, under pressure from the Punjab government, brought them in 1975.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: 1974 in South Asian Britain and Wikipedia. 1975 in the NBT book. We do not know which is right.</aside>

Kim Wagner says that "some of Udham Singh's ashes are even kept at the Jallianwala Bagh memorial".

## Remembering him

Wagner says that after the killing Udham Singh "was instantaneously accorded the status of a true patriotic martyr". He adds that the memorial has two paintings of him. A statue of him was put up at the entrance of Jallianwala Bagh in 2018. Wikipedia says it was unveiled on 13 March 2018.

Wikipedia lists other honours. It says that Udham Singh Nagar, a district in Uttarakhand, was named after him in October 1995. It says that in 1999 he was given the Nishan-e-Khalsa, a Sikh honour, after his death. It says 31 July is a holiday in Punjab and Haryana.

## Different opinions

People did not all think alike. Wikipedia says that Jawaharlal Nehru called the killing senseless in March 1940, and that in 1962 he praised Singh as "Shaheed-i-Azam". We have not seen Nehru's words ourselves. The change is interesting. It shows how a person's reputation can change over twenty years.

Wagner makes another point. In the popular imagination, he says, there is no question about Udham Singh's connection to the massacre. Yet, as "Was Udham Singh at the Bagh?" shows, historians have asked about it.

## What we cannot say

We do not know what happened to every paper in his case. The British Library holds a file about it. Counsel's article says that the official transcript leaves out some of his last words. We found no copy of the document that Wikipedia says was published in 1996.

## Why it matters

Remembering is not the same as knowing. A statue, a holiday and a district name show that many people care about Udham Singh. They do not tell us what he thought. For that we have his letters, his name on his arm, and the court record. These are the places to begin.
