The man we call Udham Singh was born with another name. The National Book Trust's book on him says: "His real name was Sher Singh." Sher means lion.

## Two birthdays

Vinay Lal, writing for UCLA, says he was born in Sunam, in Punjab, on 26 December 1899. He adds that colonial records list his date of birth as 23 August 1901. Wikipedia also gives 26 December 1899.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: 26 December 1899 is the date used in most places. A government record cited by Vinay Lal says 23 August 1901. We cannot tell which is right. So we do not say exactly how old he was at any time in this story.</aside>

## Losing his parents

Lal says his father worked as a watchman at a railway crossing. The family farmed. The boy lost both parents when he was young.

Wikipedia says his mother died when he was about three. It says his father died in October 1907, in a hospital in Amritsar, while taking his two sons to the city. We found this only on Wikipedia.

## The orphanage

The NBT book says the brothers were admitted to the Central Khalsa Orphanage in Amritsar on 24 October 1907. Wikipedia says they were initiated into Sikhism on 28 October 1907. It adds that the orphanage called him "Ude".

NBT says that Sher Singh was "baptised as Udham Singh". It says his older brother, who was Mukta Singh, became Sadhu Singh. So they both got new names in the same year.

<aside class="note"><b>Initiated, baptised</b>: Sikh boys can take a vow and join the Khalsa, the community of initiated Sikhs. New names are often given at that time.</aside>

## Loss and school

The NBT book says his brother died in 1917. Wikipedia says the same and adds that the cause was an unknown illness.

NBT says Udham Singh passed the matriculation examination in 1918. That was a school-leaving test. We do not know what marks he got or what he planned to do next.

Wikipedia says that he joined the British Indian Army in the First World War, though he was too young. It says he served in Basra and Baghdad. It says he came back to the orphanage in early 1919. We did not find this in any other source, so we do not rely on it.

## What we do not know

We have no letters from the boy. We do not have a photograph of him at the orphanage. A child's life in 1907 left few papers. So most of these facts come from later books.

In 1919, when the massacre took place at Jallianwala Bagh, he was in his late teens or twenty, depending on which birthday is right. Where he was on that day is not clear. We look at that question in "Was Udham Singh at the Bagh?".

## Why it matters

Udham Singh's life ended in a London prison. It began with two brothers in an orphanage, taking new names. Some people gave him more names later, and we meet them in "A name on the arm".

When the records are thin, a story should be short. It should say what the books say, and where they differ.
