The man we call Udham Singh was born with another name. It was Sher Singh. Sher means lion. The National Book Trust's book on him says it plainly: "His real name was Sher Singh."

He was born in Sunam, a town in Punjab, on 26 December 1899. His father worked as a watchman at a railway crossing. The family also farmed.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: 26 December 1899 is the date used in most places, including Wikipedia. Vinay Lal of UCLA points out that colonial records give 23 August 1901. We cannot tell which is right, so we do not say exactly how old he was at any time in this story.</aside>

## Losing his parents

The boy lost both his parents while he was still young. His mother died when he was about three. His father died in October 1907, in a hospital in Amritsar. He had been taking his two sons to the city.

<aside class="note"><b>One source only</b>: the details of his mother's death and his father's last journey come from Wikipedia alone. We could not find them in another source.</aside>

So in October 1907, two brothers were alone in Amritsar. Sher Singh had an older brother, Mukta Singh.

## The orphanage

The brothers were taken to the Central Khalsa Orphanage in Amritsar. The National Book Trust says they were admitted on 24 October 1907. Four days later, on 28 October, they were initiated into Sikhism. At the orphanage, Wikipedia says, they called him "Ude".

<aside class="note"><b>Initiated, baptised</b>: Sikh boys can take a vow and join the Khalsa, the community of initiated Sikhs. New names are often given at that time.</aside>

Both boys got new names that year. Sher Singh was "baptised as Udham Singh". His brother Mukta Singh became Sadhu Singh.

## Loss, and school

His brother died in 1917 of an illness that nobody named. Udham Singh was left alone.

In 1918 he passed the matriculation examination. That was the school-leaving test. What he planned to do next, and what marks he got, no book tells us.

<aside class="note"><b>One source only</b>: Wikipedia says that during the First World War he joined the British Indian Army, though he was too young, that he served in Basra and Baghdad, and that he came back to the orphanage in early 1919. We found this in no other source, so the story does not rely on it.</aside>

## The year 1919

In April 1919, the massacre took place at Jallianwala Bagh in Amritsar. Udham Singh was then in his late teens or twenty, depending on which birthday is right. Where he was on that day is not clear. Another story on this site, "Was Udham Singh at the Bagh?", looks at that question.

## Why it matters

Sher Singh and Udham Singh were the same boy. Later he would be known by many more names.

Udham Singh's life ended in a London prison. It began with two brothers in an orphanage, taking new names.

A child's life in 1907 left very few papers. There are no letters from the boy and no photograph of him at the orphanage. When the records are thin, a story should be short. It should say what the books say, and where they differ.
