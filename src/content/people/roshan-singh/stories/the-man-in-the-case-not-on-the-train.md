Was Roshan Singh on the train that was stopped near Kakori?

His name appears beside the names of men involved in that robbery. He was sentenced to death in the case that followed.

Yet one published book says he was not at the train robbery itself.

How can both things be true?

## One name, a larger case

On 9 August 1925, members of the Hindustan Republican Association stopped a train near Kakori.

They took money being carried for the British government. A passenger named Ahmad Ali died during the robbery.

The organisation was called the HRA in 1925. Its members hoped to end British rule in India.

The court case was not just about one train. It covered other acts too.

A history published by the Allahabad High Court lists four robberies brought into the case.

The list includes Bamrauli in December 1924. It also includes Bichpuri and Dwarkapur in 1925.

<aside class="note"><b>Conspiracy</b>: a secret plan made by two or more people to do something unlawful.</aside>

## A very different question

R. K. Tandon's National Book Trust book discusses Roshan Singh's sentence.

It says he was not present at the main Kakori scene. It places the death sentence in the wider robberies case.

That is not the same as saying the court found him innocent.

The High Court's history identifies Roshan among people convicted after the trial. It does not say each convicted man rode that train.

<aside class="note"><b>Important difference</b>: being convicted in the Kakori conspiracy case does not, by itself, establish that Roshan Singh was physically present during the train robbery. The original Bamrauli evidence has not been independently checked.</aside>

## What happened at the trial?

The court heard charges involving both the HRA and several robberies.

Judge Hamilton delivered his decision on 6 April 1927.

Roshan Singh, Rajendra Lahiri and Ram Prasad Bismil received death sentences in the main case.

Ashfaqulla Khan was tried separately and also sentenced to death.

We cannot use the sentence alone to decide which person did exactly what at every place.

The original witness statements would help answer that question. We have not examined them.

## Four people, different prisons

Roshan Singh was executed at Allahabad jail on 19 December 1927.

Bismil and Ashfaqulla Khan were executed that day at other prisons. Lahiri had been executed on 17 December.

They are remembered together. Their personal histories were not identical.

## Why it matters

A court case may cover more events than its famous name suggests.

That is why we must ask two questions. What was a person charged with? And what can the records actually show?

Roshan Singh's story teaches us not to confuse those answers.
