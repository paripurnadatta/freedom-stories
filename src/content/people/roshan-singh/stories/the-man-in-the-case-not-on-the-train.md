Roshan Singh's name is tied to Kakori. Yet two of our sources say he was not at the train that night.

How can a man be sentenced to death in the Kakori case and not have been at Kakori?

## A case bigger than a train

Most of us know the Kakori case as the story of one train, stopped on 9 August 1925. But the court in Lucknow looked at more than that.

The Allahabad High Court's history lists four robberies that were brought into the case.

- **Bamrauli**, on 25 December 1924. The history says a wrestler who stood up to the robbers was shot dead.
- **Bichpuri**, on 9 March 1925. One person was killed.
- **Dwarkapur**, on 25 May 1925. A villager lost his life.
- **Kakori**, on 9 August 1925.

<aside class="note"><b>Robbery</b>: here, a group of men attacking a place or a train to steal money. The old word for it was "dacoity".</aside>

So the Kakori case was about all four. It was named for the last, because that one became the most famous.

## What the books say about Roshan Singh

The National Book Trust book says Roshan Singh was not even present at the "main scene" of the Kakori robbery. Wikipedia also says he did not take part in it.

Wikipedia says he was arrested and tried in January 1926 for a murder committed during the Bamrauli robbery.

The book prints words it says Judge Hamilton wrote about him. They say that at Bamrauli Roshan "was the leader, as he knew everything about that village". They also say that "two persons have revealed that he was carrying a pistol that day".

Because of this, the judge sentenced him to death under Section 396 of the law. Judge Hamilton gave his judgment on 6 April 1927.

<aside class="note"><b>Two things to keep apart</b>: Roshan Singh was sentenced for what the judge said happened at Bamrauli. That is different from being on the train at Kakori. We have not read the judgment or the witnesses' statements. We use the words the book prints.</aside>

## A death at Bamrauli

At Bamrauli, a man was killed. We should say it plainly. The sentence was for that death.

The book says many people felt Roshan Singh's sentence was too harsh. It says he was the oldest of the accused and that his part was the smallest. This is the opinion of the book's writer.

We do not have a way to check this. Courts, the writer and other people could see the same events differently.

## The end

Roshan Singh was hanged on 19 December 1927 in Allahabad jail. The National Book Trust book and Wikipedia agree on this.

Ram Prasad Bismil and Ashfaqulla Khan were hanged on the same day, in other jails.

## Why it matters

A name like "the Kakori case" is a short label. It can hide how many events lay inside.

When we read about a person, it is worth asking two questions. What was he charged with? And what do the records show?
