Thakur Roshan Singh is one of the four men hanged for the Kakori case. He is also the one we know least about. He left no famous poems. There is no autobiography.

But there is a trail. It begins in a village, goes through a police baton charge in Bareilly, and ends in a robbery in a small village in Pilibhit.

## A village and a family

Roshan Singh was born in Nawada, a village in the Shahjahanpur district of Uttar Pradesh. Some books spell the name Nabada. His father was Thakur Jangi Singh, and his mother was Kaushalya Devi. The family was Rajput. The names of his parents come from three Hindi websites, and only English Wikipedia names the family's community.

As a boy he was a brave wrestler and a sure shot. The ideas of the Arya Samaj shaped his life. He decided early on to stand against injustice.

## Four dates for one birthday

When was he born? Most sources say 22 January 1892.

Not every record agrees. English Wikipedia and two Hindi websites give 22 January 1892. Another Hindi news site gives 19 December 1892, which is also the day he was hanged in 1927, so it may be a slip. The National Book Trust book says 1894, and a newspaper, The Lucknow Observer, says 1890. No record from his own time was found, so 1892 is the date most pages share.

## A teacher

Roshan Singh studied only up to middle school. Even so, he became a teacher in a primary school.

When Mahatma Gandhi began the Non-Cooperation Movement, Roshan Singh worked for it in Shahjahanpur and Bareilly.

## The rifle in Bareilly

Then came the trouble in Bareilly, which English Wikipedia calls the Bareilly shooting case. The police were hitting a crowd with batons. Roshan Singh stood up to them. He took an officer's rifle and fired into the air to stop the attack. That is how two Hindi papers tell it.

He was arrested and sentenced to two years of hard labour. Every source agrees on the sentence. Later he was released from Bareilly Central Jail.

when did it happen? One Hindi site says the Non-Cooperation years, 1921 to 1922. Two others say 1922, during a protest after the violence at Chauri Chaura. And what happened? The National Book Trust book says only "firing". The Hindi papers say he fired into the air. None says anyone was hurt, and no court record was found.

## Meeting Bismil

After he came out of jail, he met Ram Prasad Bismil. This was a turning point in his life. In 1924 he joined the Hindustan Republican Association, the HRA.

The HRA was short of money and weapons. Roshan Singh made a suggestion. He said that wealth which grew under British protection, and was not used for the nation, could be taken as a "revolutionary tax". The Hindi words are *kranti kar*. Only one source tells this, and nobody wrote down who else was at that meeting.

## Bamrauli

The first of the four robberies in the Kakori case was at Bamrauli, in the Pilibhit district. It was on Christmas night, 1924. The house belonged to a rich moneylender and sugar-factory owner. A wrestler who stood up to the robbers was shot dead.

A murder case was filed against Roshan Singh. He escaped arrest at that time.

At the trial, the judge, Hamilton, said that at Bamrauli, "Roshan was the leader, as he knew everything about that village." Two people had told the court that he carried a pistol. These words come from the National Book Trust book. The judgment itself was not seen, so they are passed on as the book gives them.

A man died at Bamrauli. It is right to say so plainly. Roshan Singh was sentenced to death for that death, and not for the train at Kakori. The story is told with care, because a loss of life is at the heart of it.

## The arrest

On 26 September 1925, nine men were arrested on one day. Roshan Singh was one of them. He was tried in January 1926 for the murder at Bamrauli.

There may have been a mix-up. A Bengal revolutionary named Keshav Chakravarti, of the Anushilan Samiti, really did take part in the train robbery. One Hindi site says he looked like Roshan Singh, and the government took Roshan Singh to be that man. Another site says he was arrested "allegedly" because of a mistake about who he was.

was Roshan Singh arrested by mistake, or because of Bamrauli? One site says both: that the police took him for another man, and then held on to him because of Bamrauli. English Wikipedia lists a Keshab Chakravarty among the men who took part in Kakori and says he was never arrested. It does not say he looked like anyone. No record settles it.

At the Kakori trial he was the eldest of the accused. The National Book Trust book says many people in the court felt his crime was the smallest.

## What is not known

Nobody wrote down when he first met Bismil, or what the two of them said. There is no letter or diary from these years. Nothing says what he thought on the night of the Bamrauli robbery. His family in Nawada suffered hardship after 1927, but little is known beyond that.

## Why it matters

When only a few papers are left about a person, it is tempting to fill the gaps. It is better to leave them open.

Roshan Singh was a teacher who believed in Gandhi's movement. When it was ended, he went another way. That way led him to the HRA, and to Bamrauli. And in the end, it led to the gallows.
