Thakur Roshan Singh is one of the four men hanged for the Kakori case. He is also the one we know least about. He left no famous poems. There is no autobiography.

But there is a trail. It begins in a village, goes through a police baton charge in Bareilly, and ends in a robbery in a small village in Pilibhit. This story follows that trail. It also shows how many different things the sources say about a man's life.

## A village and a family

Roshan Singh was born in Nawada, a village in the Shahjahanpur district of Uttar Pradesh. Several sources spell the name Nabada. His father was Thakur Jangi Singh, and his mother was Kaushalya Devi. Three Hindi websites give these names. English Wikipedia says he belonged to a Rajput family.

A news site, Garima Times, says he was a brave wrestler and a sure shot from childhood. Another site, Satyarth Prakash Nyas, says that the ideas of the Arya Samaj shaped his life. It says he decided early on to stand against injustice.

## Four dates for one birthday

When was he born? Here is what we found.

- 22 January 1892: English Wikipedia, and two Hindi websites, Webdunia and Satyarth Prakash Nyas.
- 19 December 1892: Garima Times.
- 1894: the National Book Trust book.
- 1890: The Lucknow Observer, a newspaper.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: Four sources give four different answers. The 19 December date is also the day he was hanged, in 1927, so it may be a slip. Most sources lean towards 22 January 1892. But we have not seen a record from his own time.</aside>

## A teacher

The National Book Trust book says that Roshan Singh studied only up to middle school. Even so, he became a teacher in a primary school.

When Mahatma Gandhi began the Non-Cooperation Movement, the book says, Roshan Singh worked for it in Shahjahanpur and Bareilly.

## The rifle in Bareilly

Then came the trouble in Bareilly. The sources tell it in different ways.

The National Book Trust book says only that there was some firing in Bareilly, and that Roshan Singh was arrested and sentenced to two years in prison. English Wikipedia calls it the Bareilly shooting case.

Two Hindi sources say more. Satyarth Prakash Nyas says that during the Non-Cooperation Movement, he stood up to the police and took a police rifle. Garima Times, and a second paper, Panchjanya, say the same. They say the police were hitting the crowd with batons, and he took an officer's rifle and fired into the air to stop the attack.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: When was this? Satyarth Prakash Nyas says the Non-Cooperation years, 1921 to 1922. Garima Times and Panchjanya say 1922, during a protest after the violence at Chauri Chaura. And what happened? The book says "firing". The two Hindi papers say he fired into the air. None of them says anyone was hurt. We found no court record.</aside>

The sentence, in every source, was two years of hard labour. English Wikipedia says he was later released from Bareilly Central Jail.

## Meeting Bismil

After he came out of jail, Satyarth Prakash Nyas says, he met Ram Prasad Bismil. It calls this a turning point in his life. English Wikipedia says he joined the Hindustan Republican Association, the HRA, in 1924.

The HRA was short of money and weapons. Satyarth Prakash Nyas says Roshan Singh made a suggestion. He said that wealth which grew under British protection, and was not used for the nation, could be taken as a "revolutionary tax". The site uses the Hindi words *kranti kar*.

We found this idea in only one source. We do not know who else was at that meeting. We also do not know whether the group followed his suggestion.

## Bamrauli

The first of the four robberies in the Kakori case was at Bamrauli, in the Pilibhit district. The High Court's history gives the date as Christmas night, 1924. It says the house belonged to a rich moneylender and sugar-factory owner, and that a wrestler who stood up to the robbers was shot dead.

Garima Times says a man died in the shooting, and that a murder case was filed against Roshan Singh. It says he escaped arrest at that time.

The National Book Trust book prints words it says are from the judge, Hamilton. They say that at Bamrauli, "Roshan was the leader, as he knew everything about that village." And they say two people had told the court he carried a pistol.

We have not seen the evidence. We only know what the book says the judge said.

<aside class="note"><b>Handle with care</b>: A man died at Bamrauli. It is right to say so plainly. Roshan Singh was sentenced to death for that death, and not for the train at Kakori. We tell this part of his life with care, because a loss of life is at the heart of it.</aside>

## The arrest

Wikipedia's article on the Kakori robbery says that on 26 September 1925, nine men were arrested on one day. Roshan Singh was one of them. He was tried in January 1926, English Wikipedia says, for the murder at Bamrauli.

A Hindi site, Webdunia, says there was a mix-up. It says a man named Keshav Chakravarti, from a Bengal group called the Anushilan Samiti, really did take part in the train robbery, and that he looked like Roshan Singh. It says the government took Roshan Singh to be that man. Satyarth Prakash Nyas says he was arrested "allegedly" because of a mistake about who he was.

English Wikipedia's article on Kakori does list a Keshab Chakravarty among the men who took part. It says he was never arrested. It does not say he looked like anyone.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: Was Roshan Singh arrested by mistake, or because of Bamrauli? Webdunia says both: that the police took him for another man, and that they then held on to him because of Bamrauli. Neither site shows a record. We cannot say which part is true.</aside>

## What we do not know

We do not know when he first met Bismil, or what the two of them said. We do not have a letter or a diary from these years. And we do not know what he thought on the night of the Bamrauli robbery. Nor do we know what became of his family in Nawada in those years, apart from what Wikipedia says about their hardship after 1927.

## Why it matters

When we only have a few papers about a person, it is tempting to fill the gaps. It is better to leave them open.

What we can say is this. Roshan Singh was a teacher who believed in Gandhi's movement. When it was ended, he went another way. That way led him to the HRA, and to Bamrauli. And in the end, it led to the gallows.
