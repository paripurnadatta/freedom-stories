## Disagreements, shown in the stories as "Two versions" notes

- **Founding of the Khudai Khidmatgar**: September 1929 (his own words via Tendulkar); November 1929 (Wikipedia's page on him); March 1930 (Wikipedia, body of the group's page; the infobox says 1929).
- **Why red**: dirty white clothes (Tendulkar and Wikipedia); symbolic and socialist (Wikipedia).
- **Qissa Khwani deaths**: 20 (The Times via Wikipedia); 200 to 300 (Patel committee via Tendulkar); several hundred or 400 (Wikipedia); 200 to 250 (Wikipedia, page on him).
- **Length of firing**: more than three hours (Patel report); six hours (Wikipedia); 11 to 5 (newspaper quoted by Gene Sharp via Wikipedia).
- **Stones and the dispatch rider**: Wikipedia says stones were thrown and a rider was killed. The Patel report says the crowd was unarmed and an Englishman on a motorcycle crashed into an armoured car.
- **Garhwal Rifles**: Tendulkar says one platoon and 17 men sentenced (one for life, one 15 years, others 3 to 10). Wikipedia says two platoons and NCOs got up to eight years.
- **Congress presidency offer**: September 1934 (Tendulkar's statement); 1931 (Wikipedia and another passage in Tendulkar).
- **Partition**: the Congress leaders' own accounts of 3 June 1947 were not opened. We give his account and the reasons the leaders gave elsewhere (Nehru's remark, Gandhi's promise).

## Points from the report that could NOT be checked, and so were left out

- The dossier's claim of 100,000 members: only "citation needed" on Wikipedia. Left out. We use Tendulkar's "not more than 500" before April 1930.
- The dossier's mkgandhi.org version of the officer's remark ("insulting an Indian senior in age (parted hair)"). Tendulkar's version is used.
- The 1969 Ahmedabad anecdote: only on a memorial site, not opened.
- The dossier suggests "wolves" came before the last night with Gandhi. In Tendulkar the order is the opposite: the sad night in early May, then 3 June.
- Pashtunistan: Wikipedia says he later called it "just a name" for the Pashtun province. Used with attribution.
- The name "Frontier Gandhi" credited to a journalist: not opened.

## Left out on purpose

- Details of beatings at Utmanzai beyond a short summary.
- The first wife's death: Tendulkar says December 1915, Wikipedia says 1918. Left out.
- The funeral in 1988: Wikipedia has a ceasefire claim and two crowd sizes ("tens of thousands" and "over 200,000"); RFE/RL says thousands attended, 15 died in blasts, and does not mention a ceasefire. Not used yet. Birth date: 6 February 1890 (Wikipedia) versus only "1890".
- Last years in Pakistan and Afghanistan (arrests from 1948, Babrra firing of 12 August 1948, exile, Bharat Ratna 1987): not yet a story.
