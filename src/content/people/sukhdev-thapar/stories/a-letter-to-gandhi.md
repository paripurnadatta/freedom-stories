Can two people both want India to be free, and still disagree about almost everything else?

In March 1931, a young prisoner in Lahore and the most famous man in India found out.

## A deal with the British

On 5 March 1931, Gandhi signed an agreement with the Viceroy, Lord Irwin. It is called the Gandhi–Irwin Pact.

<aside class="note"><b>Viceroy</b>: the man who ruled India on behalf of the British king.</aside>

Because of the pact, thousands of people who had been jailed for peaceful protests were set free. But not everyone. Revolutionaries accused of violence were left out.

Among them were three young men in Lahore Central Jail, waiting to be hanged: Bhagat Singh, Rajguru and Sukhdev.

## Two ways to fight

Gandhi believed India should win freedom without violence. When Bhagat Singh and Batukeshwar Dutt threw bombs in the Delhi Assembly in 1929, Gandhi said it had set back the national movement. "Do not seek the gallows for political freedom," he said.

<aside class="note"><b>Gallows</b>: the wooden frame used for hanging people.</aside>

Sukhdev and his friends saw things differently. They believed that there were many ways to fight for freedom, and that theirs was one of them. They wanted more than the British leaving. They wanted a socialist republic, where wealth would be shared fairly.

## The letter

In mid-March 1931, Sukhdev's uncle, Chint Ram Thapar, visited him in jail. Afterwards, the uncle carried out a letter. It was addressed to Gandhi.

A writer in The Tribune says the letter asked uncomfortable questions. Why had Gandhi's movement stayed silent about revolutionaries who had spent years in prison? Was freedom to be won only for members of the Congress party?

Gandhi had called on the revolutionaries to make peace. In his reply, Sukhdev argued that "peace and compromise" was "but a temporary truce". His group, he said, stood for a socialist republic.

## Gandhi's answer

Gandhi did not hide the letter. He printed it in his own paper, Young India.

His reply was careful. He said he had done what he could. But, The Tribune writer notes, he did not publicly demand that the three men be spared.

## What Gandhi did in private

Another writer has looked at what Gandhi did behind closed doors. He says Gandhi asked Lord Irwin again and again to stop the hangings: on 18 February, and on 19, 21 and 22 March 1931. On the morning of 23 March, he wrote Irwin a personal letter asking him once more.

<aside class="note"><b>Two views</b>: some writers say Gandhi did not try hard enough to save Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev and Rajguru. Others point to his many meetings with Irwin and say he tried, but the British would not listen. Historians still argue about it.</aside>

It did not work. On the evening of 23 March 1931, Sukhdev, Bhagat Singh and Rajguru were hanged.

## Why it matters

Gandhi and Sukhdev both wanted India to be free. They disagreed about how to get there, and about what freedom should mean.

Sukhdev did not stay silent, even in a death cell. And Gandhi printed the words of a young man who was criticising him. Their argument is still going on today.
