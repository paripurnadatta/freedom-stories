When a British court sentenced three young men to death in October 1930, the judges wrote down something unusual. They described two of them almost like parts of one body.

Sukhdev, they said, was "the brain of the conspiracy". Bhagat Singh was "his right arm".

They meant it as an accusation. But it also tells us something true. These two young men had become so close that even their enemies saw them as working together, like a head and a hand.

How did that happen?

## Reading all night

It began at National College in Lahore, in the early 1920s. The college had been started by Lala Lajpat Rai for students who refused to study in British-run colleges.

Sukhdev came from Lyallpur. Bhagat Singh came to the same college. They became classmates, and then close friends.

The historian Chaman Lal writes that they read and discussed books together for hours, sometimes all through the night. Another friend from those days, Shiv Verma, later wrote that apart from Bhagat Singh, nobody in their group had studied socialism more seriously than Sukhdev.

<aside class="note"><b>Socialism</b>: the idea that land, factories and wealth should be shared fairly by everyone, not owned by a few.</aside>

Sukhdev's reading changed over time. At first he liked writers called anarchists, who wanted to get rid of governments altogether. Later, after talking with early Indian communists such as Sohan Singh Josh, he moved towards the ideas of Karl Marx.

## Two different jobs

As the years went on, the two friends began to do different jobs in the same cause.

They helped start a youth group in Lahore, the Naujawan Bharat Sabha. In 1928, their secret party was renamed the Hindustan Socialist Republican Association, or HSRA. Bhagat Singh became its coordinator for all the provinces. Sukhdev was put in charge of Punjab.

Shiv Verma described the difference well. Bhagat Singh, he said, was the party's guide in ideas. Sukhdev was the organiser who "built its edifice brick by brick".

<aside class="note"><b>Edifice</b>: a large building. Shiv Verma meant that Sukhdev built up the party slowly and carefully, piece by piece.</aside>

## An argument about love

Close friends often argue. Sukhdev and Bhagat Singh argued a lot.

In early April 1929, Bhagat Singh was in Delhi. A few days later, he was going to throw harmless bombs in the Central Legislative Assembly, and be arrested. Before that, on 5 April, he wrote a long letter to Sukhdev.

It was not about bombs. It was about love.

Sukhdev had asked him a question: has love ever helped any man? Chaman Lal writes that Sukhdev's ideas about love were "somewhat traditional and conservative". He had even accused Bhagat Singh of being "weak".

Bhagat Singh wrote back firmly. "I am not weak," he said, "not weaker than anyone amongst us, brother."

Then he answered the question about love. He told the story of Mazzini, an Italian freedom fighter. Mazzini had once fallen into deep despair. A letter from the woman he loved, Bhagat Singh wrote, made him "not only as strong as the others, but stronger than everybody else".

<aside class="note"><b>Mazzini</b>: Giuseppe Mazzini, a famous Italian freedom fighter.</aside>

Love, Bhagat Singh wrote, "always elevates the character of man". He asked his friend to be a little less strict with himself and others, and a little kinder.

## The letter the police found

A comrade, Shiv Verma, carried the letter to Lahore and gave it to Sukhdev. A few days later, the police arrested Sukhdev. They found the letter on him. It was used in court as evidence against them.

<aside class="note"><b>When was he arrested?</b> The notes to the letter say 13 April 1929. Wikipedia gives 15 April 1929, the day of the raid on the bomb workshop in Lahore. Another article says May 1929.</aside>

That is how a private letter between two friends, about love, ended up in a police file.

Chaman Lal writes that two letters from Bhagat Singh to Sukhdev have been published. But no letter from Sukhdev to Bhagat Singh has ever been found. We know Sukhdev's side of their arguments only through his friend's replies. His younger brother, Mathura Das Thapar, later wrote a book about him, Mere Bhai Sukhdev, "My Brother Sukhdev". It is still in print.

## The same case

Now both friends were in prison. Bhagat Singh had been arrested in Delhi. Sukhdev was arrested in Lahore.

The police brought one giant case against them and many others. It was called "Crown versus Sukhdev and others". Sukhdev was accused number one. Bhagat Singh was number twelve.

In jail, they joined the long hunger strike of the political prisoners, demanding better treatment.

Chaman Lal points out something important about Sukhdev. He did not fire a shot when the police officer John Saunders was killed in Lahore in 1928. But he did not try to escape the blame. He "owned every part of the assassination plan", and he chose to die with his comrades rather than save himself.

## Thinking for himself

Sukhdev also had his own ideas about what should come next.

In letters to his comrades from jail, he wrote that the revolutionaries had caught the public's imagination. Now, he said, they had to teach people what revolution really meant. He suggested a new mass party, and even gave it a name: the Red Revolutionary Party.

He also wrote that violence was not an end in itself. To him, building public support came first.

## Together to the end

On 7 October 1930, the court sentenced Sukhdev, Bhagat Singh and Rajguru to death.

On the evening of 23 March 1931, the three friends were hanged together in Lahore Central Jail. Sukhdev was 23.

## Why is one more famous?

Today, Bhagat Singh is far more famous than Sukhdev. Why? One historian, Kama Maclean, suggests that part of the reason is a single famous photograph: Bhagat Singh in his hat.

## Why it matters

The judges thought of Sukhdev as the brain and Bhagat Singh as the arm. But they were really two thinking people, who read together, argued, changed each other's minds, and stayed friends to the end.

Their friendship is remembered through a letter about love that the police kept as evidence. Sometimes history is saved in the strangest places.
