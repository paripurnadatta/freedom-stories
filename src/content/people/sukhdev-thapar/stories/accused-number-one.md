In April 1929, a senior police officer in Lahore wrote out a list. It was the first report in a big police case: a list of the people the police wanted to put on trial.

At number twelve was Bhagat Singh. At number twenty was Rajguru.

At number one was a young man named Sukhdev.

The case even took his name. Officially, it was called "Crown versus Sukhdev and others".

Today, when people remember the three friends who were hanged together in 1931, they usually say "Bhagat Singh, Rajguru and Sukhdev". His name often comes last. So why did the police put it first?

## A boy from Ludhiana

Sukhdev Thapar was born on 15 May 1907, in Ludhiana, in Punjab. His family lived in a lane in the old city called Naughara Mohalla. The name means something like "nine houses". It came from nine houses built close together by Thapar families.

His father, Ram Lal, died in 1910, when Sukhdev was about three. His mother, Ralli Devi, also died early.

He grew up in Lyallpur, a city in Punjab that is now in Pakistan and called Faisalabad. There he was raised by a relative, Lala Achint Ram Thapar. He was a freedom fighter himself, and one writer says he spent 19 years in British prisons.

<aside class="note"><b>Which uncle?</b> One writer in The Tribune says Achint Ram was a cousin of Sukhdev's father. Another, quoting a member of the family, calls him an uncle on his mother's side.</aside>

## The college in Lahore

In 1922, Sukhdev finished school in Lyallpur. Then he went to National College in Lahore, against the wishes of his guardian.

<aside class="note"><b>Which year?</b> One account says he joined National College in 1921. Another says he finished school in 1922 and then joined.</aside>

National College was no ordinary college. It had been started by the leader Lala Lajpat Rai, for students who were refusing to study in British-run colleges. There, Sukhdev met a student named Bhagat Singh. They became close friends.

<aside class="note"><b>Socialism</b>: the idea that land, factories and wealth should be shared fairly by everyone, not owned by a few. Sukhdev and Bhagat Singh read a lot about it.</aside>

Sukhdev read deeply. A fellow revolutionary, Shiv Verma, later wrote that apart from Bhagat Singh, nobody in their group had studied socialism more seriously than Sukhdev.

## The organiser

In March 1926, Bhagat Singh started a youth group in Lahore called the Naujawan Bharat Sabha. Sukhdev was one of its leading members.

On 8 and 9 September 1928, a secret meeting was held among the ruins of Feroz Shah Kotla, an old fort in Delhi. There, the revolutionaries gave their group a new name: the Hindustan Socialist Republican Association, or HSRA. Sukhdev was made the secretary of its Punjab branch.

People in a secret group cannot use their real names. Sukhdev's circle used many false names, among them "Grameen", "Kisan", "Gwar", "Swami" and "Dyal". Kisan means farmer. Years later, the police report described him as "Swami alias peasant".

<aside class="note"><b>Alias</b>: a false name, used to hide who someone really is.</aside>

Now his real work began. A secret group needs many things. It needs houses where members can hide. It needs clothes, food, train tickets and books. It needs money. Sukhdev arranged all of it, often with no money in his own pocket. He was the organiser.

## After the shooting in Lahore

On 17 December 1928, a British police officer named John Saunders was shot dead in Lahore. Bhagat Singh and Rajguru fired the shots. Sukhdev had been part of the plan.

<aside class="note"><b>Why?</b> A few weeks earlier, Lala Lajpat Rai had been beaten by police during a protest, and he died soon after. The revolutionaries wanted revenge. They meant to kill a different officer, James Scott. They shot Saunders by mistake.</aside>

Afterwards, the young men had to get out of Lahore. A writer in a Lahore newspaper, The Nation, says it was on Sukhdev's advice that they decided to leave. On the night of 19 December, Sukhdev took Bhagat Singh to the home of Durga Devi, the wife of their comrade Bhagwati Charan Vohra. Bhagat Singh had cut his hair and shaved, and wore Western clothes. Sukhdev introduced him as a "new friend". Durga Devi did not recognise him at all! The next morning, with her help, Bhagat Singh and Rajguru escaped by train.

## Room 69

Sukhdev set up a bomb workshop in Lahore, in a building called the Kashmir Building. One account says it was in Room No. 69.

In April 1929, Bhagat Singh and Batukeshwar Dutt threw two harmless bombs into the Central Legislative Assembly in Delhi, and were arrested.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: two articles in The Tribune say Sukhdev was the one who insisted that Bhagat Singh should go to the Assembly, because he would explain the group's ideas best at his trial. Another account says the first plan was for Sukhdev and Dutt to go.</aside>

A week later, on 15 April 1929, the police raided the Lahore workshop. They arrested Sukhdev and two others, Kishori Lal and Jai Gopal.

## The brain

Then came the long trial. Sukhdev and his friends went on hunger strike in prison in 1929, demanding better treatment.

On 7 October 1930, the special court gave its judgment. One writer in The Tribune says the judges called Sukhdev "the brain of the conspiracy", and Bhagat Singh "his right arm".

That is why his name came first. The British did not think of him as a follower. They thought of him as the planner.

Sukhdev, Bhagat Singh and Rajguru were all sentenced to death.

## 23 March 1931

The hanging was set for 24 March 1931. But the authorities were worried about public anger. They moved it eleven hours earlier.

At 7.30 in the evening on 23 March 1931, Sukhdev, Bhagat Singh and Rajguru were hanged in Lahore Central Jail. Sukhdev was 23 years old.

<aside class="note"><b>A later account</b>: in his book about Bhagat Singh, the journalist Kuldip Nayar wrote that when the hangman asked who would go first, Sukhdev said: "I will go first."</aside>

That night, the authorities took the bodies out through a hole broken in the back wall of the jail. They burned them in secret on the bank of the Sutlej river, at Hussainiwala, near Ferozepur.

## How he is remembered

Today, 23 March is remembered in India as Shaheed Diwas, Martyrs' Day.

A college of the University of Delhi, started in 1987, is named after Sukhdev. So is Ludhiana's big bus terminal. The house where he was born still stands in Naughara Mohalla, and it is now a protected monument.

## Why it matters

Some heroes are famous because of one brave moment. Sukhdev's work was mostly hidden. He found the rooms, the clothes, the money and the plans. Without people like him, the famous moments could not have happened.

The police knew that. That is why, on their list, he was number one.
