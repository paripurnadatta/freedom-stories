In the old part of Ludhiana, there is a lane called Naughara Mohalla. The name means something like "Nine-House Lane".

It got that name long ago, from nine houses built close together by families called Thapar. In one of those houses, on 15 May 1907, a baby boy was born. His name was Sukhdev.

## A hero, and a forgotten house

Sukhdev grew up to be a revolutionary. In 1931, he was hanged by the British in Lahore, together with Bhagat Singh and Rajguru. He was only 23.

After India became free, people remembered him. A college in Delhi was named after him. So was Ludhiana's big bus terminal. Every year on 23 March, India remembers the three friends on Martyrs' Day.

But the house where he was born was not looked after.

## A newspaper story

On 18 March 2007, The Tribune newspaper printed a report about the house. Its title was "Sukhdev's house picture of neglect".

<aside class="note"><b>Neglect</b>: not being looked after.</aside>

A lawyer named H. C. Arora read about it. That same year, he went to the Punjab and Haryana High Court. He filed a PIL, asking the court to make sure the house was protected.

<aside class="note"><b>PIL</b>: a Public Interest Litigation. It is a case that anyone can bring to court, not for themselves, but for the good of the public.</aside>

## Saved, slowly

It took time. At first, only the ground floor of the house was protected. Then, in November 2014, the whole building was declared a protected monument.

## The road that is not there yet

But there was still a problem. The house is hidden away in a crowded part of the old city. It has been hard for visitors to reach it.

For years, there have been plans to make a direct path to it from Chaura Bazaar, a nearby market. A writer in The Tribune, Harish Jain, wrote in early 2026 that "preservation without access remains largely symbolic". In other words, saving a house does not mean much if people cannot get to it.

By August 2026, the land for the path had been bought. But the road had still not been built. Members of a trust that keeps Sukhdev's memory alive, led by its president Ashok Thapar, went on a hunger strike in protest.

## Why it matters

Remembering a hero is not only about statues and speeches. It is also about looking after the places where they lived.

Sukhdev spent his short life finding safe houses for his friends. It took a newspaper, a lawyer, a court and many years of campaigning to keep his own house safe. The work is still not finished.
