Sukhdev Thapar was always asking his family for clothes. Not for himself. For his "friends".

And his friends, it seemed, got through clothes very fast.

Who were these friends? They were young revolutionaries, living in secret.

## A boy who would not salute

Sukhdev was born in Ludhiana in 1907. His father died when he was small, and he grew up in Lyallpur, a city in Punjab that is now in Pakistan. The family ran a grain business there. He was raised by a relative, Lala Achint Ram, and by his older cousin, Gauran Devi.

He went to the Sanatan Dharma school in Lyallpur. One day, an officer came to visit the school. Sukhdev refused to salute him.

<aside class="note"><b>Salute</b>: a sign of respect, made by raising the hand to the forehead.</aside>

We do not know exactly what happened to him afterwards. But the story was remembered, and it is still told about him today.

## A pocket with no money

When Sukhdev grew up, he went to college in Lahore. There he joined Bhagat Singh and his friends. Later, he became the organiser of their secret group in Punjab.

His job was to find whatever the group needed. Houses to hide in. Clothes. Food. Money for travel. Books. A writer in The Tribune says he arranged all of it, often with no money in his own pocket.

So he asked his family.

## What his cousin remembered

Years later, his cousin Gauran Devi remembered him fondly, and with some puzzlement. He was always short of money, she said. He was never shy about asking for help. And he was always collecting clothes for "friends" who had already used up the last lot.

<aside class="note"><b>A family memory</b>: these are Gauran Devi's memories, told many years later and reported by a writer in The Tribune.</aside>

## Others first

His comrades remembered the same thing.

Shiv Verma, who was in the group with him, said that Sukhdev clothed others before he clothed himself.

Later, Sukhdev was locked up in Lahore Central Jail. A fellow prisoner there remembered his laughter and his warmth. He remembered how much Sukhdev loved to feed and clothe his comrades.

## Why it matters

Sukhdev was hanged in 1931, at the age of 23, together with Bhagat Singh and Rajguru. People often remember the three of them for their courage at the end.

But Sukhdev's story is also about small, kind things. A borrowed shirt. A shared meal. A friend looked after before himself.
