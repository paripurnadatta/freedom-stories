What does an empty iron shell look like? To most people, like a bit of scrap metal.

But in April 1929, to the police in Lahore, it was a clue.

## The bombs in Delhi

On 8 April 1929, Bhagat Singh and Batukeshwar Dutt threw two small bombs onto empty benches in the Central Legislative Assembly in Delhi. They were arrested on the spot.

The police wanted to know where the bombs had come from. Who had made them?

## A workshop in Lahore

The answer was in Lahore. Sukhdev, the organiser of the revolutionaries in Punjab, had set up a secret workshop there to make bombs.

It was in a building on McLeod Road. The building was called the Kashmir Building. An article in The Nation, a Lahore newspaper, says the group used a flat there called Room No. 69.

The room had been rented in the name of Durga Devi. She was the wife of their comrade Bhagwati Charan Vohra. She lived there with her husband and their little son, Sachindra.

<aside class="note"><b>Durga Devi</b>: many people knew her as Durga Bhabhi. She was a revolutionary herself.</aside>

## The man with the shells

One of Sukhdev's helpers was a man named Kishori Lal.

A historian, Bakhshish Singh Nijjar, wrote that the police once noticed Kishori Lal carrying some empty shells. That was enough to make them suspicious.

<aside class="note"><b>Shell</b>: here, the hollow metal case of a bomb, before it is filled.</aside>

## The raid

On 15 April 1929, one week after the bombs in Delhi, the police raided the workshop. They arrested three men: Kishori Lal, Jai Gopal and Sukhdev himself.

It was a heavy blow to the revolutionaries. Sukhdev was their organiser in Punjab, and now he was in prison.

## What came next

The police built a huge case. It was officially called "Crown versus Sukhdev and others", and Sukhdev was accused number one. The trial began in July 1929.

On 7 October 1930, the court gave its judgment. A Lahore newspaper, the Daily Milap, printed a sheet with photographs of the accused and their sentences. Look at the second row. Under one photograph are the words "M. Sukh Dev. Sentenced to hanging." Next to him, "M. Kishori Lal. Transportation for life."

## Why it matters

A secret can be lost through one small mistake. A few empty shells, noticed by the police in Lahore, helped to end Sukhdev's freedom.

He never left prison again. On 23 March 1931, he was hanged, together with Bhagat Singh and Rajguru.
