Subramania Bharati died in Madras in 1921. He was thirty-eight years old. This story tells what the sources say about the last weeks, and it says where they do not agree.

## The elephant

Bharati visited the Parthasarathy temple in Triplicane, in Madras. A temple elephant lived there. DT Next says that one day in July 1921 the elephant was in musth and had been chained, and that people were kept away. The article says Bharati went up to it with fruit anyway, and the elephant knocked him down with its trunk.

<aside class="note"><b>Musth</b>: a time when a male elephant can become hard to control.</aside>

DT Next says he lay unconscious and bleeding. A man named Kuvalai Krishnan carried him out. Doctors said he was out of danger. The article says he went back to visit the elephant later, but he was weaker after the fall.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: DT Next says Bharati came with fruit. Wikipedia says he had offered the elephant a coconut. Wikipedia names the elephant Lavanya. DT Next does not name it. We cannot tell which details are exact.</aside>

DT Next also says that Bharati himself turned the episode into a short story, "Kali koil yaanai". It marks the story as a memory and not a record of the day.

## The illness

DT Next says that around August 1921 he had a stomachache. It says he "adamantly refused to take medicines". The other article from DT Next says he "died of illness". Nothing we opened gives a medical cause. The elephant was in July and the death in September, so we cannot say the fall caused it.

## One death, two dates

Bharati died shortly after midnight. Most places give the date as 11 September 1921. The Government of Puducherry's museum also gives 11 September.

But a second DT Next report says that his death certificate, issued by the Corporation of Madras, gives 12 September. The time on it is 1 a.m. That would make it the 12th. The report says that the founder of a memorial trust in Kovilpatti got the certificate through a right-to-information request. It says that a stone tablet at the memorial in Ettayapuram also says 12 September. Because he died before dawn, many people keep the 11th as his day.

We have not seen the certificate. So we give both dates.

## The funeral

DT Next's article says that only a dozen people attended. It says the relatives could not reach Madras in time, and the family house was surrounded by orthodox Brahmins, so the cremation was not put off. Friends pooled money for the rites and a new set of clothes. The article says that Surendra Nath Arya came late and gave a poem of grief in Telugu.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: Wikipedia says fourteen people attended the funeral. It says the figure is "recorded", and it points to a BBC report that we have not seen. DT Next says a dozen. Both say very few.</aside>

## His last speech

Wikipedia says his last speech was at the Karungalpalayam Library in Erode, on "Man is Immortal". It gives no date.

## Why it matters

A great poet's death is not always a grand event. A few friends came, and the sources do not agree on the exact number. We should not tidy it into a legend.
