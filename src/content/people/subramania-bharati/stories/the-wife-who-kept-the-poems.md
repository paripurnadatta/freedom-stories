Most stories about Bharati stop at 1921. But the poet's work did not stop. Someone had to look after his papers, and the sources say it was his wife.

## Chellamma

Her name appears as Chellamma, Chellammal and Chellama. The PIB says she was a child bride when they were married in June 1897. The museum in Puducherry lists her and their two daughters, Thangamma and Sagunthala, among the people it displays. It also lists his brother Viswanathan.

Their life was not easy. DT Next says that during his exile in Pondicherry she took the children to her parents' house, and that she gave him an ultimatum to come back to British India. The same article says that when they lived with a friend, Surendra Nath Arya, she was uneasy when she learned that his butler was from a Dalit community. It says that Bharati reportedly did not mind. This is only that one article's account.

Madras Musings says that the family knew intense poverty in Pondicherry. We cannot say more about what she thought or felt, and we should not guess.

## The papers

The Wire is an article by a modern writer, Mira T. Sundara Rajan. It says that after Bharati's death Chellamma "took it into her own hands to publish Bharati's works". It says she meant to "bequeath them to the public as a gift" when she died.

She also wrote his first biography. The Wire says that her Bharatiyar Charithiram was "dictated to her elder daughter Thangammal, who acted as her scribe".

So the first life of Bharati, as far as the article says, was a mother's memories written down by a daughter. That makes it precious. It also means we should treat it as a family's memory. A wife's recollection is not the same as a record from the day. The article does not give us the book's words, and we do not repeat any.

<aside class="note"><b>Biography</b>: the story of a person's life, written by someone else. <b>Scribe</b>: a person who writes down what another person says.</aside>

## Whose words?

The Wire says publication "proved to be unexpectedly problematic". It says that false attribution and keeping the integrity of his words were problems then, and that they remain.

<aside class="note"><b>Attribution</b>: saying who wrote something. False attribution means giving a poem or a line to the wrong writer.</aside>

That means that a line you see on a poster with Bharati's name may not be his. This is a warning for anyone who shares quotes. Look for the oldest printed edition.

## A gift to the public

Wikipedia says that his poems were the first in India to be "nationalized", in 1949. We do not know the details from that page. We also do not know whether this happened because of Chellamma's plans, so we do not link them. We can only say that the aim of giving the poems to the public, which The Wire says she held, and the nationalisation in 1949 point in the same direction.

## Why it matters

When we remember a poet, we usually remember a poem and a face. We forget the people who kept the papers, copied the pages and told the story. In Bharati's case, the sources say that one of them was his widow, and another was her daughter holding the pen.
