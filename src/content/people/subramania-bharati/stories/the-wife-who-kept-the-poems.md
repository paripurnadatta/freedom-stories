Most stories about Bharati stop at 1921. But the poet's work did not stop. Someone had to look after his papers, and that someone was his wife.

## Chellamma

Her name appears as Chellamma, Chellammal and Chellama. She was a child bride when they were married in June 1897. The museum in Puducherry lists her and their two daughters, Thangamma and Sagunthala, among the people it displays. It also lists his brother Viswanathan.

Their life was not easy. The family knew intense poverty in Pondicherry. During his exile there, she took the children to her parents' house and told him to come back to British India.

An article in DT Next adds one more scene. When they lived with a friend, Surendra Nath Arya, she was uneasy when she learned that his butler was from a Dalit community. Bharati reportedly did not mind. This is only that one article's account.

What she thought or felt cannot be known from these pages, and it would be wrong to guess.

## The papers

After Bharati's death, Chellamma "took it into her own hands to publish Bharati's works". This is from an article in The Wire by a modern writer, Mira T. Sundara Rajan. She meant to "bequeath them to the public as a gift" when she died.

She also wrote his first biography. Her *Bharatiyar Charithiram* was "dictated to her elder daughter Thangammal, who acted as her scribe".

<aside class="note"><b>Biography</b>: the story of a person's life, written by someone else. <b>Scribe</b>: a person who writes down what another person says.</aside>

So the first life of Bharati was a mother's memories written down by a daughter. That makes it precious. It also means it is a family's memory. A wife's recollection is not the same as a record from the day. The book's own words are not quoted here.

## Whose words?

Publication "proved to be unexpectedly problematic". False attribution and keeping the integrity of his words were problems then, and they remain.

<aside class="note"><b>Attribution</b>: saying who wrote something. False attribution means giving a poem or a line to the wrong writer.</aside>

That means that a line you see on a poster with Bharati's name may not be his. This is a warning for anyone who shares quotes. Look for the oldest printed edition.

## A gift to the public

In 1949, his poems were the first in India to be "nationalized". The details are not known here, and nothing says this happened because of Chellamma's plans. But the aim of giving the poems to the public, which she held, and the nationalisation in 1949 point in the same direction.

## Why it matters

When we remember a poet, we usually remember a poem and a face. We forget the people who kept the papers, copied the pages and told the story. In Bharati's case, one of them was his widow, and another was her daughter holding the pen.
