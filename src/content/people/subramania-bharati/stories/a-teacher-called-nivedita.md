Sister Nivedita was a disciple of Swami Vivekananda. Subramania Bharati was a Tamil journalist and poet. At a Congress session, the two of them met. Bharati later called her his teacher.

Everyone agrees on that. They do not agree on where.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: Madras Musings says they met at the Calcutta session of the Congress in 1906. The Vice-President's speech of 2021 says Bharati met her while returning from the Calcutta Congress. Sujata Nahar's chapter says the meeting was at the Benares session. Wikipedia says he went to a Congress session in Varanasi in December 1905 and "later" met her. We cannot tell which of these is right.</aside>

The place and the year are not settled. The sources agree about what happened next.

## What she asked of him

Nivedita urged him to overcome prejudices of caste and creed. She also urged him to work for the freedom of women. He agreed to do so, says Madras Musings.

He said that she was "certainly an emanation of Mother Shakti". The words come from an article about his poems. We do not have the place or the time where he said them.

After meeting her, Bharati became a stronger voice for the freedom and empowerment of women, the Vice-President said in his speech. We do not know how the speaker knows this. It is a view of a speaker, not a record.

## The dedications

Bharati dedicated his poems to her. He dedicated the first two volumes of Swadesa Gitangal to her, with the words: "I place this slim volume at the Teacher's Feet."

He called her his Gurumani. He wrote a line, given in the 2021 speech: "the guru has showed me the Sampurna Rupa of Bharata-Shakti."

<aside class="note"><b>Guru</b>: a teacher, especially a spiritual guide. <b>Gurumani</b>: a word that means something like "jewel of a teacher".</aside>

## A magazine for women

Bharati was the editor of a magazine called Chakravartini. He announced that its goal was women's empowerment. Under the title he printed a two-line verse. The speech renders it like this: "When knowledge of women is raised, womanhood becomes great; when this happens, the country becomes great."

"Bharati's commitment to women's freedom was without equal," The Wire says. It also quotes him: "it is sheer ignorance to suppose that freedom will lead women to disregard the virtue of chastity."

The Puducherry museum lists Chakravartini among the periodicals he edited, along with India, Vijaya and Karmayogi.

## Why it matters

When we are told that a great man was "inspired" by someone, we should ask how we know. Here the sources give different places for the meeting. Yet they agree that he thought of Nivedita as a teacher, that he put her name on his books, and that he spoke for women's freedom.

We have no record of her words. What we have is what Bharati did afterward.
