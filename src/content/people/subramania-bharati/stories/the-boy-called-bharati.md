Subramania Bharati was born on 11 December 1882 in Ettayapuram, a village in the Tirunelveli district. His family called him Subbiah. The PIB gives his parents' names as Chinnaswamy Iyer and Lakshmi Ammal.

Wikipedia says his mother died in 1887, when he was about five. His father and grandmother raised him.

## Poems at seven

The PIB says he began writing Tamil poems at the age of seven. By the time he was eleven, it says, learned men praised his writing.

## The debate

Here is a story told by a government biography. When he was in his eleventh year, the boy challenged the eminent scholars of the Ettayapuram court to a debate. He asked them to pick any subject, without warning. The contest was held at a special sitting of the court, with the Raja present. The subject was "Education", and the boy won.

After this, the PIB says, the boy who was called "Ettayapuram Subbiah" came to be known as "Bharathi". Wikipedia and the 2021 speech of the Vice-President also say he was given the title Bharati at about the age of eleven.

<aside class="note"><b>A later account</b>: this story comes from a biography published in 2016, more than a century later. We did not find a report from the day, or the words of the debate. So we say "the biography tells". We do not make up what anyone said.</aside>

## Married at fourteen

In June 1897, when he was nearly fifteen, he was married to Chellamma. The PIB calls her a child bride. Wikipedia says she was about seven. This was a custom among many families in those days. Today, in India and everywhere else, we would not let a child be married. Later in his life Bharati spoke against child marriage, says the PIB.

Wikipedia says his father died when he was sixteen. Sujata Nahar says it left him with no money.

## Banaras

After the wedding he went to Benares, now called Varanasi. The PIB says he spent about two years there, with his aunt Kuppammal and her husband Krishna Sivan.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: The PIB says about two years in Banaras. A caption on Wikimedia Commons says he lived there from 1898 to 1902. Wikipedia says that he came back to Ettayapuram in 1901. We do not know which is exact.</aside>

In Banaras, says the PIB, he learned Sanskrit, Hindi and English. He passed the Entrance Examination of the Allahabad University "with credit". This is not a university degree, only the test to get into one.

The stay changed him, according to the PIB. He grew a moustache, wore a Sikh turban and developed a bolder way of walking. We have not found a dated photograph from those years.

## Back home

Wikipedia says that in 1901 he returned to Ettayapuram and served as the Raja's chief court poet. In 1904 he taught Tamil at a school in Madurai, and that year he went to a newspaper. That story is in "A weekly with a cartoon".

## Why it matters

Many people first meet Bharati as a statue or a stamp. Behind them was a boy with a nickname, who lost his mother at five and was married as a child. He read Sanskrit, Hindi and English in a faraway city, and came back with a turban and a new way of walking.

The stories of his childhood are the least certain part of his life. We have enjoyed them, and we have said where they come from.
