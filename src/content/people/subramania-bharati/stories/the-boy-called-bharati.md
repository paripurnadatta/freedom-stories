Subramania Bharati was born on 11 December 1882 in Ettayapuram, a village in the Tirunelveli district. His family called him Subbiah. His parents were Chinnaswamy Iyer and Lakshmi Ammal.

His mother died in 1887, when he was about five. His father and grandmother raised him.

## Poems at seven

At seven, he began writing Tamil poems. By the time he was eleven, learned men were praising his writing.

## The debate

There is a story about the boy in his eleventh year. He challenged the eminent scholars of the Ettayapuram court to a debate. He asked them to pick any subject, without warning. The contest was held at a special sitting of the court, with the Raja present. The subject was "Education", and the boy won.

After this, the boy who was called "Ettayapuram Subbiah" came to be known as "Bharathi". At about eleven, he was given the title.

<aside class="note"><b>A later account</b>: the debate comes from a biography published in 2016, more than a century later. No report from the day, and none of the words spoken, survive. So it is told here as the biography tells it, and nothing is added.</aside>

## Married at fourteen

In June 1897, when he was nearly fifteen, he was married to Chellamma. She was a child bride, about seven years old. This was a custom among many families in those days. Today, in India and everywhere else, we would not let a child be married. Later in his life Bharati spoke against child marriage.

His father died when he was sixteen, and the family had no money.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: the age of the bride is given differently. The government biography only calls her a child bride. Wikipedia says she was about seven.</aside>

## Banaras

After the wedding he went to Benares, now called Varanasi. He stayed with his aunt Kuppammal and her husband Krishna Sivan, for about two years.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: a caption on Wikimedia Commons says he lived in Banaras from 1898 to 1902. Wikipedia says he came back to Ettayapuram in 1901.</aside>

In Banaras he learned Sanskrit, Hindi and English. He passed the Entrance Examination of the Allahabad University "with credit". This is not a university degree, only the test to get into one.

The stay changed him. He grew a moustache, wore a Sikh turban and developed a bolder way of walking.

## Back home

In 1901 he returned to Ettayapuram and became the Raja's chief court poet. In 1904 he taught Tamil at a school in Madurai, and that year he went to a newspaper. That story is in "A weekly with a cartoon".

## Why it matters

Many people first meet Bharati as a statue or a stamp. Behind them was a boy with a nickname, who lost his mother at five and was married as a child. He read Sanskrit, Hindi and English in a faraway city, and came back with a turban and a new way of walking.

The stories of his childhood are the least certain part of his life. We have enjoyed them, and said where they come from.
