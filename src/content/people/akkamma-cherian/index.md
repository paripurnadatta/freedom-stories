## Research notes

Led major demonstrations for responsible government in Travancore.

### Freedom politics inside a princely state

Akkamma Cherian’s career is a reminder that the freedom struggle did not unfold only against directly ruled British provinces. In Travancore, the demand for responsible government created its own institutions, repression and mass politics.

### The 1938 confrontation

When the Travancore State Congress faced suppression and arrests, Cherian emerged as a visible organizer of public protest. Her rally toward the palace should be represented as a specific event in the princely-state struggle.

### Organizing women as political volunteers

Her work with the Desasevika Sangh gives the project a strong example of a relationship that is more specific than a generic label such as ‘women’s participation.’

### Travancore is not Madras Presidency

The atlas and filters must preserve the political geography of the princely state. This pilot is a test of whether the product can keep modern Kerala and historical Travancore visible at the same time.

### Chronology

- **1938**: Leaves teaching for political work. Joins the expanding Travancore State Congress struggle
- **1938-08**: State repression and civil disobedience. Travancore authorities suppress the State Congress and imprison leaders
- **1938**: Mass rally toward the palace. Organizes a large demonstration pressing for release of leaders and responsible government
- **1938-10**: Desasevika Sangh. Takes responsibility for organizing the women’s volunteer corps

### Evidence claims

- Born in Kanjirapally, Travancore, in 1909 and worked as a teacher before entering the freedom movement. (high confidence)
- Joined the Travancore State Congress struggle for responsible government. (high confidence)
- Organized a major rally demanding release of jailed leaders and responsible government in Travancore. (high confidence)
- Was entrusted with organizing the Desasevika Sangh, the women’s volunteer corps, in October 1938. (high confidence)
