Imagine being the most wanted person in a whole district. Soldiers are camped in the villages. A reward is out for you. And every day, for almost three years, you have to find a new place to sleep.

That was Surya Sen's life after the Chittagong raid of April 1930.

## After Jalalabad

A few days after the raid, the group was attacked on a hill called Jalalabad. Kalpana Dutt says they met the next day and made a new decision. The plan to take the town was over. They would carry on in small groups, using guerrilla methods.

She says they told everyone plainly that this would be hard. Anyone who did not want the hardships of living in hiding could leave. Some young people who the police did not know about were told to go home, to keep the movement safe.

<aside class="note"><b>Guerrilla</b>: fighting in small groups that strike and then disappear, instead of in big armies.</aside>

Surya Sen stayed. Wikipedia says he moved from hiding place to hiding place. Sometimes he dressed as a labourer, a farmer, a priest, or a servant. Sometimes he dressed as a Muslim.

## The stories people told

Kalpana Dutt says the villagers invented stories about him. She heard these herself, when she came back to Chittagong for her summer holiday in 1930. Some said he had walked past police dressed as a gardener. Some said he had talked to a police officer for a long way, and the officer did not know who he was. Some said he knew magic words that made him vanish.

Later, she asked Surya Sen himself about them. She writes that he answered, "Not all the stories are true. But they show that our countrymen love us. That is why they don't want to hand us over to the police."

She also tells a funny story. Soldiers were camped at a village called Patiya. One night, she says, two or three hundred soldiers surrounded a house. In the morning they asked the owner his name. He said, "Surjya Sen." The soldiers were thrilled. They arrested him.

Then they sent for police officers who had photographs of the real man. It turned out that this Surjya Sen was the headmaster of the local school. Kalpana Dutt says people laughed at the soldiers.

<aside class="note"><b>Story or fact?</b>: We have only Kalpana Dutt's word for this story. She wrote it in a book published around 1945. It may have grown in the telling, like the others she mentions.</aside>

## The price on his head

The police offered money for news of him. The Daily Star mentions a wanted poster from 1932 that offered a reward of 10,000. A Reuters news report from 1933 says the reward was 10,000 rupees.

Kalpana Dutt says people only laughed. "Surya Sen cannot be caught for money," they told each other.

Reuters wrote in August 1933 that the reward had "failed to induce anyone to betray him" for the two years detectives had searched. Other sources say that in the end someone did tell the police. You will read about that below.

## "My place is right here"

The Daily Star says that Sarat Chandra Bose offered to arrange for Surya Sen to leave the country safely. Sarat Bose was the brother of Subhas Chandra Bose. The Daily Star says Surya Sen answered, "My place is right here in Chittagong. Here I will fight and die."

Kalpana Dutt gives a similar reason. She was in hiding with him by then. She says that by the end of 1932, the police thought he must have left the district, and watched less closely. Then came the Pahartali raid of September 1932, which Pritilata Waddedar led. After it, the police were sure he was still there, and soldiers came back to the villages. Everyone told him to leave. He said the whole point of the struggle was to build the group in Chittagong, and he did not see why he should go away and keep himself safe.

## Dhalghat, June 1932

In June 1932, the police found him. Kalpana Dutt and Wikipedia both date this to 13 June. He was in a house in the village of Dhalghat. Nirmal Sen, a comrade of many years, was with him. A young woman named Pritilata Waddedar had come to meet him.

Soldiers surrounded the house. There was a gunfight. Kalpana Dutt says a British officer, Captain Cameron, was killed. Reuters, in its 1933 report, says a Captain Cameron of the Gurkhas died during a round-up of the group. Kalpana Dutt says Nirmal Sen and another member, Apurva Sen, were killed too.

Surya Sen and Pritilata got away. Kalpana Dutt says it was hard for him to leave his friend Nirmal Sen behind. But she says he did not believe in throwing away his life, and he had more work to do.

## Gairala, February 1933

By early 1933, Kalpana Dutt was hiding with him. Here is how she remembers the end.

One morning in February, the two of them were walking to an appointment. Rifle shots went past them. They had walked into a ring of soldiers. Kalpana Dutt ran one way. She saw one of the young men fall. Then she met Surya Sen in the bushes. She says he said, "I know you don't know the ins and outs of this place, so I was waiting for you."

She slipped into a pond. Gurkha soldiers ran up from behind and caught Surya Sen by the waist. "I never saw him again," she writes.

The same village is named in a Reuters report dated 17 February 1933. It says a "sharp engagement" took place at Gairala, near Patiya, about twenty miles from Chittagong. It says the Government had offered 10,000 rupees for his capture.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: The date and place are not agreed. Wikipedia and Banglapedia give 16 February 1933. One article, in a magazine called Manushi, says 7 February. Banglapedia says he was in the house of a woman named Sabitri Devi at Gairala. Wikipedia names the house as Khirodprova Biswas's. Kalpana Dutt says only that they were walking toward an appointment. The Reuters report, dated the next day, matches 16 February best.</aside>

## Who gave him away

Wikipedia and a website called The Better India say a man named Netra Sen told the police where Surya Sen was hiding. Wikipedia says he was a relative. Kalpana Dutt also names Netra Sen. Banglapedia says only "a villager" did it. Kalpana Dutt says young members of the group killed Netra Sen on 2 January, ten days before Surya Sen was hanged. She also says nobody would give evidence against the killers, though everyone knew who they were.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: The Reuters report of August 1933 says no one betrayed Surya Sen for two years. Other sources say someone did tell the police. We note both. We also do not praise the killing of the informer. It is part of the history, and it is a sad part.</aside>

## What came next

Kalpana Dutt was caught three months later, on 19 May, with another comrade, Tarakeswar Dastidar. Surya Sen, Tarakeswar Dastidar and Kalpana Dutt were tried together. Kalpana Dutt says Surya Sen quietly expected to be hanged. In the court, she says, he explained to Tarakeswar what work should be done afterward.

On 14 August 1933, a special tribunal sentenced Surya Sen and Tarakeswar Dastidar to death. Reuters reported this on the same day. It says the charge was conspiracy against the King-Emperor. Kalpana Dutt was sentenced to "transportation for life," which meant being sent away to prison for the rest of her life. The court called Surya Sen the "master mind" of the plot.

## Why it matters

Almost three years of hiding is a long time. Kalpana Dutt says that for a long time, villagers told the police nothing, even when the police pressed them hard. She also tells of a widow in Dhalghat who gave shelter and paid for it. Whatever you think of what the group did, many ordinary people took great risks to look after them. That part of the story is easy to forget.
