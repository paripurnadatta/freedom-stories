After the night of the raid, the young raiders went up into the hills. They had taken guns, but they had not found the main store of bullets. Now they were hiding from the soldiers who were looking for them.

On 22 April 1930, the soldiers found them.

## A hill against a hill

The fight began at about half past four in the afternoon. Kalpana Dutt describes it in her book. She was not there, but she knew the people who were.

The young raiders were on Jalalabad Hill. The British soldiers were on the hill opposite. The two sides fired at each other across the gap between them. A pitched battle went on until dusk. Then the soldiers fell back.

<aside class="note"><b>Pitched battle</b>: an open fight where both sides stand and fire, not a surprise attack.</aside>

A Reuters news report from that day, printed in the Hong Kong Telegraph, tells it more briefly. Men of the Eastern Frontier Rifles and a troop of Light Horse "engaged thirty rebels a few miles from Chittagong." The paper of 24 April says the raiders were "in a very strong position in the hills."

## The count

This part of the story is sad. People were killed. And the numbers are not agreed.

Eleven of the young raiders fell in the first volley from the soldiers' machine guns, Kalpana Dutt says. Four others were badly hurt. One of them, Ardhendu Dastidar, was caught. He died a few days later in police custody. That makes twelve on the raiders' side. Wikipedia also says twelve young revolutionaries died. Banglapedia says fourteen.

On the soldiers' side, the numbers are much less certain.

<aside class="note"><b>Many numbers</b>: Wikipedia says "over 80" soldiers died. Other pages repeat this, and none of the ones we read shows any record for it. Kalpana Dutt says there were no clear figures. She says rumour talked of 150 soldiers. She also says that months later, a senior police officer, Sir Charles Tegart, told some captured raiders, "You have killed 64 of our men." The Hong Kong Telegraph's reports of those days do not give any number of dead soldiers. We could not check official records, so we do not give a number as fact.</aside>

## One of the wounded escaped

One of the wounded, Ambika Chakrabarty, got away. The Government even announced that he had died, Kalpana Dutt says. He had not. He escaped with two other wounded comrades. He was caught later, in November 1930, while very sick.

## What Surya Sen decided

Surya Sen did not stay on the hill. He moved the rest of the group into nearby villages, in small parties, says Wikipedia. Some escaped. Some went to Calcutta. Some were caught.

The plan to attack the town again was given up, Kalpana Dutt says. From then on, the group would fight by hiding and striking in small parties. That fight lasted almost three years.

## Why it matters

Jalalabad was where a very young group met a real army. Seven in every ten of the raiders were teenagers, the Daily Star says. For Chittagong's young people the hill became a "saga of unyielding resistance", writes Kalpana Dutt.

For readers today, it is also a reminder to be careful with numbers. Kalpana Dutt, Wikipedia, Banglapedia and the press of 1930 each tell a part of what happened. They do not add up to one story. When sources do not agree, the honest thing is to say so.
