History books name the leaders. They do not often name the people who gave the leaders a roof.

One of those people was a widow in the village of Dhalghat. Kalpana Dutt wrote about her in a chapter of her book. She calls her "Savitri Debi," and also "Mashima," which is a word for an aunt.

## The house at Dhalghat

On 13 June 1932, Kalpana Dutt says, there was a fight in the village between government forces and some of the people who had escaped after the raid. It happened in the house of a poor Brahmin family.

The Brahmin man had died long before. His widow lived there with her son, and a married daughter was with them too. Wikipedia says that Surya Sen and Nirmal Sen were at the Dhalghat camp that day. A young woman, Pritilata Waddedar, met them there.

Kalpana Dutt says two of the group were killed. A British officer, Captain Cameron, was also killed. A Reuters report of 1933 says a Captain Cameron of the Gurkhas died during a round-up of the group. Surya Sen and Pritilata got away.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: Banglapedia says that Surya Sen hid at the house of a woman named Sabitri Devi at Gairala, in 1933. Kalpana Dutt puts her at Dhalghat, in 1932. These may be two different women. Or one source may have the place wrong. We could not check.</aside>

## What the police wanted

After the fight, the police arrested the widow, her son and her daughter. Kalpana Dutt says the police tried hard to make her talk. They offered her gold. They offered her money. She also says the police treated the family roughly.

The daughter, Kalpana Dutt says, became a Crown approver.

<aside class="note"><b>Crown approver</b>: someone who agrees to give evidence for the Government against others, so that they will be treated more gently themselves.</aside>

But the widow and her son said nothing. Kalpana Dutt says they were poor and had no schooling. She says she wondered how they could bear it. She writes that the widow had a bad name in her village for quarrelling. Even so, she loved the young "Swadeshi" men and women, and she did not give them away.

## In jail

Kalpana Dutt was arrested a few months later. When she walked into the women's ward, a middle-aged widow ran up to her and asked, "Are you a Swadeshi?"

It was Mashima. She warned the new prisoner about the police. "Never tell them anything," she said. Kalpana Dutt says the widow had been hurt a great deal, and that this had left her a little confused. But her courage had not gone.

Mashima and her son Ramkrishna were each sentenced to four years in prison. Kalpana Dutt later found her again in the jail at Midnapore. She says Mashima still argued with the jail officers whenever she felt she was owed something. She asked for atap rice, which is the kind of rice widows eat in her community. She asked for fresh vegetables, a postcard, and a visit with her son.

## Her son

Her son was in the same jail. He was a Division III prisoner, which meant hard work and the poorest treatment. He had to work the oil press. After two months, Kalpana Dutt says, he fell very ill with tuberculosis.

For six months, his mother was not allowed to see him. Then she was told he could be released if a relative would stand surety. The two of them had eight annas between them. A telegram to his uncle cost twelve. The jail would not pay the difference. They sent a post-card. No reply came.

The day before he died, Kalpana Dutt says, he told his mother that nobody was likely to come. He asked her not to quarrel with people after he was gone.

## After jail

Mashima was released a few months later. Her relatives did not treat her well. She built a small hut and lived on charity. Kalpana Dutt says the comrades helped her now and then, and she felt ashamed that they did not do more.

When a women's group opened at Dhalghat, Mashima joined it. When a famine kitchen opened there, she took no food without working. She cooked for the hungry crowd every day. Later, she cooked at a children's canteen, and was fed in return.

## Why it matters

Surya Sen and his friends could not have hidden for three years without people like her. She was poor. She was not famous. She lost her son. Whatever we think about the raid, she is part of the story.
