Kalpana Dutt first met Surya Sen on a dark, drizzly night in June 1931. She was waiting under a mosquito net in a house deep in the countryside. Two men came in and spoke quietly. She could not tell which one was the famous leader.

Later she wrote what she saw. He was a "smallish, short man, very reserved." She says nobody would have guessed that this quiet man was the "King of Chittagong."

His first question was about her exams.

## A boy from Noapara

Surya Sen was born on 22 March 1894, in a village called Noapara, near Chittagong. Wikipedia says his father, Rama Niranjan Sen, was a teacher.

He passed his entrance examination in 1912, says Banglapedia. In 1916 he was studying for a B.A. at Behrampore College, far away in Murshidabad. A teacher there, Shatishchandra Chakrabarti, spoke to him about the freedom movement. Banglapedia says this teacher belonged to a secret group called Jugantar.

<aside class="note"><b>Jugantar</b>: a secret group in Bengal whose members believed in armed action against British rule.</aside>

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: The sources we read name the college in different ways. Wikipedia calls it Berhampore College in one place and Krishnath College in another. We use "Behrampore College," as Banglapedia does.</aside>

## The school

In 1918, Surya Sen came back to Chittagong. He taught at the National School there. Wikipedia says that there he became known as "Master-da." Banglapedia writes it as "Mastarda," and says it means "teacher."

The Daily Star says he was the most popular master at the "Swadesh schools," also called national schools. He also helped lead boycotts of government schools, colleges and courts during the Non-Cooperation Movement.

<aside class="note"><b>Boycott</b>: to refuse to use or join something, as a way of protest.</aside>

Banglapedia says he later gave up teaching so that he could work full time for the movement. He also became president of the Chittagong branch of the Indian National Congress. In 1929, Banglapedia says, Subhas Chandra Bose presided over a district Congress conference in Chittagong, and Surya Sen was chosen as its general secretary.

## Jail and a gym

Wikipedia and The Better India say he was jailed from 1926 to 1928. Wikipedia says Ambika Chakrabarti was jailed with him. Banglapedia says police arrested the two men after a raid on the Assam-Bengal Railway treasury. Wikipedia's first lines give the reason as "revolutionary activities," and its later text mentions the same railway raid.

After he came out, Banglapedia says, he set up a gymnasium for physical and weapons training. He also set up a branch of the Indian Republican Army.

## What he was like

Kalpana Dutt wrote about him from close up. She says he did not like slackness in studies. When he heard her exam had gone badly, she could tell from his tone that he minded.

She says he looked after his comrades very carefully. "My job is to save my cadre, not cash," he used to say. He did not let anyone go short of good food while hiding. He even arranged singing evenings in the hiding places. He believed that keeping cheerful was one of the biggest jobs of a revolutionary.

The Daily Star quotes something he said about the group. "It is not our aim to swell our numbers by indiscriminate recruiting," he said. He wanted quality, not numbers.

## The boy with the silver bangles

Before the raid, they needed about 15,000 rupees. Kalpana Dutt says Surya Sen did not believe in political robbery. So the young members brought money from their own homes. Some brought two or three thousand rupees. Some brought family jewellery.

One boy came to him crying. His mother was very poor. He had tried everywhere. In the end he broke open his mother's safe at night, and took a pair of silver bangles worth two or three rupees.

Kalpana Dutt says Surya Sen was touched. He put the boy first on the list of those who would go into action.

<aside class="note"><b>A word of care</b>: This story comes only from Kalpana Dutt. She wrote it years later, and her book is a friend's memory, not a government record.</aside>

## Why it matters

When we think of the Chittagong raid, we think of guns and flags. But the man behind it was a teacher who cared about studies, food and cheerfulness. That does not tell us whether the raid was right. It does help us see who the people were.
