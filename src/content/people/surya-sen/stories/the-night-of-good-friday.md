At ten o'clock on the night of 18 April 1930, three places in the port city of Chittagong were attacked at the same time. They were the telegraph office, the armoury and the police barracks.

It was Good Friday, a holiday. The people who planned the attack were mostly very young. Their leader was a schoolteacher.

## The plan

The leader was Surya Sen. His friends called him Masterda, which means "teacher-brother". Some write it as Master-da or Mastarda.

Surya Sen wanted to show one thing. Indians could fight the Government even without help from another country. That is how Kalpana Dutt, a young member of the group, remembered it later. She was in Calcutta on the night of the raid, so she wrote from what her comrades told her.

So the plan was bold. The group would take the weapons from the police armoury and from the armoury of the Auxiliary Force. They would cut the telegraph and telephone lines. They would pull up railway lines. Chittagong would be cut off from Calcutta, and help could not arrive quickly.

<aside class="note"><b>Armoury</b>: a building where guns and bullets are kept.</aside>

The group called itself the Chittagong Branch of the Indian Republican Army. Surya Sen was its president. The name was copied from the Irish Republican Army. An Irish fighter, Dan Breen, was Surya Sen's ideal, and the Easter Rising in Ireland in 1916 had inspired the group.

## The young raiders

About sixty-five people took part. Perhaps seven in every ten of them were teenagers.

Ganesh Ghosh led the party that went for the police armoury, with Ananta Singh. Lokenath Bal led ten young men to the armoury of the Auxiliary Force, with Nirmal Sen. Ambika Chakrabarti took the telephone and telegraph office.

Ananta Singh and Ganesh Ghosh had both been held in detention in the 1920s. Ananta Singh had worked on the plan of a raid ever since he came out in 1927.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: the names differ a little from book to book. Wikipedia says Ganesh Ghosh led the police armoury party and Lokenath Bal the other. Banglapedia pairs Ghosh with Ananta Singh at the police armoury and Bal with Nirmal Sen at the second one.</aside>

## Ten o'clock

At ten, the groups moved. Rails were pulled up at two places, Nangalkot and Dhum. Leaflets were scattered around the town. One said that Gandhiji had called on people to break the law, so the writers were breaking the law of treason. Another explained the aim of the raid and was signed by the President of the Indian Republican Army.

About sixteen raiders went to the club where Europeans met, at Pahartali. Few people were there, because it was a holiday.

Outside the police armoury, Surya Sen took a salute. He raised a flag and announced a "Provisional Revolutionary Government."

<aside class="note"><b>Provisional</b>: for a short time only, until something proper can take its place.</aside>

## The thing that went wrong

The raiders found guns. They did not find bullets. The ammunition was kept in a different place. Without it, the stolen guns were much less useful.

The raiders decided to burn the armoury, so that the Government could not use what was left. As they poured petrol, a young man named Himangshu Sen caught fire and was badly burned. His comrades took him into the town. He died there, in hiding, three days later.

## Who died that night

This is a sad part. People died.

Major Farrell, a British officer, was killed at the armoury. Two sentries died at the police barracks. That is how Kalpana Dutt counted.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: a Reuters report in the Hong Kong Telegraph of 21 April 1930, written within three days of the raid, gave a different count. One line said four were killed. Another listed six: a sergeant major, an Anglo-Indian man and four Indians. It said "a hundred insurgents" attacked and admitted that details were "still lacking." Kalpana Dutt wrote of three deaths.</aside>

The same newspaper carried one more piece of news. An ordinance against violence was renewed in Bengal. The Viceroy explained that, because of the latest disturbance, it had become "essential" to renew powers that had lapsed.

## Before dawn

The raiders did not stay in the city. Before dawn they left for the hills.

The District Magistrate was fired at, but he was not hit. He went to the jetty and sent word out by wireless. Soldiers were called in.

Ananta Singh, Ganesh Ghosh and two others took the burned Himangshu into the town. The rest went to the hills to plan their next move. Ananta Singh's party could not join them again.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: Wikipedia says Ganesh Ghosh, Ananta Singh and two teenagers were elsewhere during the raid, and nearly got caught at Feni railway station. Kalpana Dutt places Ananta Singh and Ganesh Ghosh at the armoury and says the Feni event came later.</aside>

## Brave, and violent

The raid was brave. It was also violent. Real people were killed that night. Many readers feel sad about that, and some feel proud. Both feelings belong to this story.

Surya Sen and his friends believed in armed action. The raid came in the weeks after Gandhi's Salt March, which was entirely peaceful. The two ways of fighting were very different.

The raiders thought again later. Kalpana Dutt wrote in her book that the group moved away from "individual heroism" towards rousing the whole people. In the preface, dated October 1945, P. C. Joshi of the Communist Party called terrorism the "infant" stage and Communism the "mature" stage of their lives. Not everyone agrees with this view of the raiders.

The Daily Star of Bangladesh gives another view. Surya Sen wanted to replace what it calls "individual terrorism" with a planned struggle by a "dedicated band of youths." For him, the raid was meant to begin something bigger.

## The weeks after

The raid did not free Chittagong for long. Within days, soldiers came to the hills. But for a short time, a small group had held the armouries of a big British port.

Officials in Chittagong were so afraid in the weeks afterward that British men and women spent their nights on steamers. There was a strict curfew, and police search parties were everywhere.

A British committee wrote about what came next: "Recruits poured into the various terrorist groups in a steady stream and these included women and young girls."

## Why it matters

The raid did not end British rule. But it made many young people in Bengal think about what they might do. How you feel about the way they chose is a question for you to think about.
