At ten o'clock on the night of 18 April 1930, three places in the port city of Chittagong were attacked at the same time. They were the telegraph office, the armoury and the police barracks.

It was Good Friday, a holiday. The people who planned the attack were mostly very young. Their leader was a schoolteacher.

This is the story of that night. It is also a story about how hard it is to know exactly what happened, even after almost a hundred years.

## The plan

The leader was Surya Sen. Banglapedia says people called him Mastarda, which means "teacher." Others write it as Master-da.

Kalpana Dutt wrote about him later. She was a young member of the group. She was away in Calcutta on the night of the raid, so she wrote from what her comrades told her. She says the group wanted to show one thing. They wanted to show that Indians could fight the Government even without help from another country.

So the plan was bold. They would take the weapons from the police armoury and from the armoury of the Auxiliary Force. They would cut the telegraph and telephone lines. They would pull up railway lines. Chittagong would be cut off from Calcutta, and help could not arrive quickly.

<aside class="note"><b>Armoury</b>: a building where guns and bullets are kept.</aside>

The group called itself the Chittagong Branch of the Indian Republican Army. Surya Sen was its president. Kalpana Dutt says the name was copied from the Irish Republican Army. Dan Breen, an Irish fighter, was Surya Sen's ideal. Wikipedia adds that the group was inspired by the Easter Rising in Ireland in 1916.

## Who took part

Several sources say about sixty-five people took part. The Daily Star of Bangladesh says that about seven in every ten of them were teenagers.

Names differ a little from source to source. Wikipedia says Ganesh Ghosh led the group that took the police armoury. It says Lokenath Bal led a group of ten that took the Auxiliary Force armoury. Banglapedia names Ananta Singh with Ganesh Ghosh at the police armoury. It names Nirmal Sen with Lokenath Bal at the Auxiliary Force armoury. It puts Ambika Chakrabarti at the telephone and telegraph office.

Ananta Singh and Ganesh Ghosh had both been held in detention in the 1920s. Kalpana Dutt says Ananta Singh had worked on the plan of a raid ever since he came out in 1927.

## Ten o'clock

Kalpana Dutt gives the time as ten at night. Wikipedia gives it as 22:00. That is the same time.

Rails were tampered with at two places, Nangalkot and Dhum. Leaflets were spread around the town. One leaflet said that Gandhiji had called on people to break the law, and so the writers were breaking the law of treason. Another leaflet explained the aim of the raid. It was signed by the President of the Indian Republican Army.

Wikipedia says about sixteen raiders took the club where Europeans met, at a place called Pahartali. Few people were there, because it was a holiday.

Outside the police armoury, Wikipedia says, Surya Sen took a salute. He raised a flag and announced a "Provisional Revolutionary Government."

<aside class="note"><b>Provisional</b>: for a short time only, until something proper can take its place.</aside>

## The thing that went wrong

The raiders took guns. They did not find bullets.

Wikipedia and the Daily Star both point to this problem. The ammunition was kept in a different place. Without it, the stolen guns were much less useful.

Kalpana Dutt says the group decided to burn the armoury, so that the Government could not use what was left. As they poured petrol, a young man named Himangshu Sen caught fire. Banglapedia also says he was badly burned. Kalpana Dutt says his comrades took him to the town. He died there, in hiding, three days later.

## Who died that night

This is a sad part. People died.

Kalpana Dutt says that a British officer, Major Farrell, was killed at the armoury. She says two sentries died at the police barracks.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: A Reuters news report in the Hong Kong Telegraph on 21 April 1930 gave a different count. One line of it says four people were killed. Another line lists six: one sergeant major, one Anglo-Indian man and four Indians. The same report says "a hundred insurgents" attacked, while later sources say about sixty-five. It was written within three days of the raid, and it says that details were "still lacking." Kalpana Dutt writes about three deaths. We do not know which count is right, and we have not seen the police records.</aside>

The same newspaper had one more piece of news. An ordinance against violence was renewed in Bengal. The Viceroy explained that, because of the latest disturbance, it had become "essential" to renew powers that had lapsed.

## Before dawn

The raiders did not stay in the city. Wikipedia says they left before dawn and went toward the hills. Kalpana Dutt says the District Magistrate was fired at, but he was not hit. He went to the jetty and sent word out by wireless. Soldiers were called in.

As Kalpana Dutt tells it, Ananta Singh, Ganesh Ghosh and two others took the burned Himangshu to town. The rest went to the hills to plan their next move. Ananta Singh's party could not join them again.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: Wikipedia says Ganesh Ghosh, Ananta Singh and two teenagers were elsewhere during the raid. It says they nearly got caught at Feni railway station. Kalpana Dutt places Ananta Singh and Ganesh Ghosh at the armoury and says the Feni event happened later. We do not know which is right.</aside>

## Why people think differently about it

The raid was brave. It was also violent. Real people were killed that night. Many readers feel sad about that, and some feel proud. Both feelings belong to this story.

Surya Sen and his friends believed in armed action. The raid came in the weeks after Gandhi's Salt March, which was entirely peaceful. We did not find a source that records what Gandhi himself said about the raid, so we do not say what he thought.

The raiders thought again later. In the preface to Kalpana Dutt's book, dated October 1945, P. C. Joshi of the Communist Party calls terrorism the "infant" stage and Communism the "mature" stage of their lives. Kalpana Dutt wrote in the same book that the group had moved away from "individual heroism" toward rousing the whole people. Not everyone agrees with this view of the raiders either.

The Daily Star gives another view. It says Surya Sen wanted to replace what it calls "individual terrorism" with a planned struggle by a "dedicated band of youths." For him, the raid was meant to begin something bigger.

## Why it matters

The raid did not free Chittagong for long. Within days, soldiers came to the hills. But for a short time, a small group had held the armouries of a big British port.

Kalpana Dutt says officials in Chittagong were so afraid in the weeks afterward that British men and women spent their nights on steamers. She says there was a strict curfew, and police search parties were everywhere.

A British committee wrote about what happened next. The Daily Star quotes it: "Recruits poured into the various terrorist groups in a steady stream and these included women and young girls."

The raid did not end British rule. But it made many young people in Bengal think about what they might do. How you feel about the way they chose is a question for you to think about.
