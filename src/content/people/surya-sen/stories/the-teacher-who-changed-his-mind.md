Kalpana Dutt was a college student when she joined Surya Sen's group. In her book she tells how she was asked to hide, and what her leader said when she asked him about it.

It is one of the clearest pictures we have of Surya Sen as a man who could think again.

## An iron rule

Kalpana Dutt says there was an "iron rule" among the revolutionaries of that time. Members should keep away from women.

Surya Sen had lived by that rule for many years. He had been a revolutionary since 1913, she says. But the rule was not working. Girls were already in the party before 1930, she writes. After the raid, they began to take a real part in the action, side by side with the men.

## "Their bravery made up my mind"

In 1932, Kalpana Dutt was arrested. She was let out on bail after two months. Then Surya Sen sent her a message. She should disappear into hiding.

Kalpana Dutt says this was not an easy decision for him. One day he told her: "I just could not make up my mind about letting girl revolutionaries abscond. But their bravery and steadiness made up my mind for me."

<aside class="note"><b>Abscond</b>: to run away and hide, usually to escape arrest.</aside>

She adds that Surya Sen "never hesitated to change his mind when he found he was wrong."

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: Kalpana Dutt says she was arrested in September 1932, a week before Pritilata Waddedar's raid on the Pahartali club, and let out on bail two months later. The Wikipedia page on her gives September 1931. Her own book gives 1932, and the club raid happened in September 1932. We use her own date.</aside>

## A second change of mind

Ananta Singh was another leader, and a close friend of Surya Sen. Kalpana Dutt says he too changed his mind. Long after she first got in touch with him, he sent a message. "I had a wrong idea about the worth of women in revolutionary work," he wrote. "They should forgive me for my mistake."

## Pritilata

Wikipedia tells how Pritilata Waddedar was accepted. On 13 June 1932, she met Surya Sen and Nirmal Sen at the Dhalghat camp. One leader, Binod Bihari Chowdhury, did not want a woman in the group. Wikipedia says she was accepted because women carrying weapons were less likely to attract suspicion.

Kalpana Dutt says that on 24 September 1932, Pritilata led a raid on a club at Pahartali, where Europeans met. A woman, Mrs. Sullivan, was killed. Pritilata herself died that night. She had carried poison, and she took it so that she would not be caught. A statement was found in her pocket. It said that everything since the 1930 raid was a part of a war that the group had declared on 18 April 1930.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: Kalpana Dutt says the official announcement counted thirteen people wounded. Wikipedia says the police reported eleven injured. We do not know which count is right. Pritilata's own story deserves a telling of its own.</aside>

## Why it matters

Surya Sen was not a perfect leader. He kept an old rule for many years. But he was willing to say he had been wrong. According to Kalpana Dutt, that mattered to the young women who worked with him.

The Daily Star writes that after the raid, a British committee saw "women and young girls" among the new recruits. Whether you agree with what they did or not, it is worth knowing that young women were in that story from the start.
