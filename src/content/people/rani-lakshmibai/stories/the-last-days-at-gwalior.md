After the fall of Jhansi, the Rani did not go into hiding. She went to find other rebel leaders and kept fighting.

## Kalpi

The Rani reached Kalpi at midnight on 5 April 1858, says Wikipedia. By 27 April, about ten thousand soldiers had gathered there. The leaders included the Rani, Tatya Tope, the Raja of Banpur, and Rao Sahib, a nephew of Nana Sahib.

She was not chosen as the main commander. Tatya Tope got that job.

Twice, her advice was not followed. At Kunch, she told Tatya Tope to guard his flanks. He did not, and lost six hundred men and his guns. At Kalpi on 22 May, she advised against attacking over difficult ground. The attack went ahead, and failed.

## A reward on her head

Around this time, the Governor-General, Lord Canning, named her a rebel leader. He offered a reward of twenty thousand rupees for her capture.

## Gwalior

The rebels then marched to Gwalior. On 31 May, soldiers of the Gwalior force turned against their own ruler, who fled to the British. The rebels walked into the great fort.

Rao Sahib held a grand court on 3 June, with feasting. The Rani refused to come. She felt that the rebels were celebrating when they should be preparing for battle. On 5 June she urged Rao Sahib to get ready.

## The battle

Hugh Rose reached Gwalior ten days after leaving Kalpi, and attacked on 17 June.

At half past seven in the morning, units of the 95th Regiment fought rebels led by the Rani on hilly ground between Kotah-ki-Serai and Gwalior. A squadron of the 8th Hussars then charged and scattered her guard. An eyewitness reported that the Rani, perhaps with a woman companion named Mundar, charged at them. This is from Wikipedia.

## How did she die?

All the accounts agree that she died fighting. After that, they part.

The exact way is unknown. India marks her death on 18 June 1858, though some accounts say 17 or 18 June. She "may have been shot or stabbed by a saber or both."

One report, in the papers of Lord Canning, says a trooper of the 8th Hussars shot her in the back. She then turned to fight and was killed with a sword.

The priest Vishnubhat Godse says in his book that she was hit by a bullet, then by a sword in the thigh, and fell from her horse. Tatya Tope rushed to her and held her. Live History India notes that he gives her death only three sentences.

<aside class="note"><b>Many versions</b>: was it 17 June or 18 June? Was it a bullet or a sword? Was Tatya Tope there? The sources do not agree, and none of them was written by a person we can prove was present. Wikipedia says her followers probably cremated her body. We do not know where.</aside>

## What the other side said

Hugh Rose's praise is often quoted. He called her "the best and bravest of the rebels", Wikipedia reports. The commander of the 8th Hussars said she "fought like bricks." But Wikipedia also records an officer who called her the "beast of Jhansi" and said she had "too easy a death."

Rose took Gwalior on 19 June. It was the last big battle of the revolt in central India.

## Her son

When she died, her adopted son Damodar Rao was a child of eight or nine. We tell his story in "The boy in the forest cave".

## Why it matters

The Rani was a young woman who led soldiers in a lost war. Her age is uncertain, because the sources give different birth years. Her enemies praised her courage. Her friends often ignored her advice.

We do not know the exact hour, the exact weapon or the exact place of her death. We do know that she chose to ride out on that morning.
