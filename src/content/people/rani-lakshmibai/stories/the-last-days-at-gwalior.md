After the fall of Jhansi, the Rani did not go into hiding. She went to find other rebel leaders and kept fighting.

## Kalpi

Wikipedia says she reached Kalpi at midnight on 5 April 1858. By 27 April, about ten thousand soldiers had gathered there. The leaders included the Rani, Tatya Tope, the Raja of Banpur, and Rao Sahib, a nephew of Nana Sahib.

She was not chosen as the main commander. Wikipedia says Tatya Tope got that job.

Twice, says Wikipedia, her advice was not followed. At Kunch, she told Tatya Tope to guard his flanks. He did not, and lost six hundred men and his guns. At Kalpi on 22 May, she advised against attacking over difficult ground. The attack went ahead, and failed.

## A reward on her head

Wikipedia says that around this time, the Governor-General, Lord Canning, named her a rebel leader. He offered a reward of twenty thousand rupees for her capture.

## Gwalior

The rebels then marched to Gwalior. On 31 May, Wikipedia says, soldiers of the Gwalior force turned against their own ruler, who fled to the British. The rebels walked into the great fort.

Rao Sahib held a grand court on 3 June, with feasting. The Rani refused to come. Wikipedia says she felt that the rebels were celebrating when they should be preparing for battle. On 5 June she urged Rao Sahib to get ready.

## The battle

Wikipedia says Hugh Rose reached Gwalior ten days after leaving Kalpi, and attacked on 17 June.

At half past seven in the morning, units of the 95th Regiment fought rebels led by the Rani on hilly ground between Kotah-ki-Serai and Gwalior. A squadron of the 8th Hussars then charged and scattered her guard. Wikipedia says an eyewitness reported that the Rani, perhaps with a woman companion named Mundar, charged at them.

## How did she die?

All our sources agree that she died fighting. After that, they part.

- **Wikipedia** says the exact way is unknown. It gives the date as 18 June 1858.
- **Britannica** says she was killed on 17 or 18 June. It says she "may have been shot or stabbed by a saber or both." It adds that India marks her death on 18 June.
- **National Geographic** points to a report in the papers of Lord Canning. It says a trooper of the 8th Hussars shot her in the back. She then turned to fight and was killed with a sword.
- **Vishnubhat Godse**, the priest, says in his book that she was hit by a bullet, then by a sword in the thigh, and fell from her horse. He says Tatya Tope rushed to her and held her. Live History India notes that he gives her death only three sentences.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: Was it 17 June or 18 June? Was it a bullet or a sword? Was Tatya Tope there? The sources do not agree, and none of them was written by a person we can prove was present. Wikipedia says her followers probably cremated her body. We do not know where.</aside>

## What the other side said

Hugh Rose's praise is often quoted. Wikipedia reports that he called her "the best and bravest of the rebels." The commander of the 8th Hussars said she "fought like bricks." But Wikipedia also records an officer who called her the "beast of Jhansi" and said she had "too easy a death."

Rose took Gwalior on 19 June. Wikipedia says it was the last big battle of the revolt in central India.

## Her son

When she died, her adopted son Damodar Rao was a child of eight or nine. We tell his story in "The boy in the forest cave".

## Why it matters

The Rani was a young woman who led soldiers in a lost war. Her age is uncertain, because the sources give different birth years. Her enemies praised her courage. Her friends often ignored her advice.

We do not know the exact hour, the exact weapon or the exact place of her death. We do know that she chose to ride out on that morning.
