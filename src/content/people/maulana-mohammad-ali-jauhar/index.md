## Research notes

Khilafat leader and Congress participant.

### A coalition with multiple goals

Jauhar's politics should not be reduced to either Congress nationalism or religious mobilisation alone; the Khilafat alliance brought distinct objectives into temporary alignment.

### Journalism and education as political tools

His press work and role in Jamia show how print and institutions carried anti-colonial politics beyond street protest.

### Chronology

- **1920**: Jamia Millia Islamia founded. Jauhar becomes one of the institution's founders and its first Vice Chancellor
- **1920-1922**: Khilafat and Non-Cooperation coalition. Jauhar operates at the centre of a mass political alliance combining anti-colonial and Khilafat demands
- **1930-1931**: Round Table Conference and final political phase. He participates in constitutional negotiations in London before his death in 1931

### Evidence claims

- Jamia Millia Islamia identifies Mohammad Ali Jauhar as a founder and its first Vice Chancellor. (high confidence)
- Jamia's official history describes him as a nationalist leader, freedom fighter, Khilafat leader and influential journalist. (high confidence)
- He was part of the political coalition from which Jamia emerged in 1920, alongside Hakim Ajmal Khan and other nationalist Muslim leaders. (high confidence)
- Jamia records that he later took part in the Round Table Conference and that political differences emerged after the withdrawal of Non-Cooperation and Khilafat. (high confidence)
