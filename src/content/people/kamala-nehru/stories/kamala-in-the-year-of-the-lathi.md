In 1930, Gandhi began the Salt March. Soon the Civil Disobedience Movement spread across India. Jawaharlal Nehru was sent to prison. In Allahabad, the work fell to his wife.

## More women than before

The National Herald says that by April 1930 Nehru was in prison again. It says many more women joined the struggle than in 1920 and 1921. They picketed shops that sold foreign cloth and liquor. They marched in processions. Some faced the police *lathi*, a long wooden stick.

<aside class="note"><b>Lathi charge</b>: when police hit a crowd with long sticks to break it up.</aside>

The Wire says the same. Both articles were written by the same author, Praveen Davar, so they are not two separate checks.

## A leader in her own city

The Herald says Kamala was president of the Allahabad District Congress Committee. It says she toured the district to bring in women and young people. The Wire adds that she led processions and picketed liquor and foreign-cloth shops. It says she dressed as a volunteer, in khadi pyjamas, a kurta and a Gandhi cap.

The Nehru Archive puts it another way. It says that in 1930 she was "for some time a member of the Congress Working Committee and the 'dictator' of Allahabad."

<aside class="note"><b>Dictator</b>: The page does not explain the word. We think it means a person put in charge of the local campaign, but we have not found a source that says so.</aside>

The Herald quotes Jawaharlal Nehru as writing: "Kamala surprised me, for her energy and enthusiasm overcame her physical ill health." We did not find the original book page.

## Hurt in the charge

Both articles say that Kamala's mother-in-law, Swarup Rani, was seriously hurt on the head in a lathi charge. The Herald says she continued to lead women afterwards.

We do not have a date or place for the injury.

## Arrest: three accounts

The Nehru Archive says Kamala was arrested on 1 January 1931. It says she was released at the end of that month with other Congress leaders.

The Herald says she was arrested in December 1930 for a protest march against her husband's arrest, and jailed on 1 January 1931 for two months. It quotes a letter in which Nehru told Indira that her mother's arrest was "a pleasant New Year gift", and that she was "thoroughly happy and contented".

The Wire says she was released after about two months because her health had got worse.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: Was she released at the end of January 1931, or after about two months? The Nehru Archive says January. The two articles say two months. The Archive's version fits with Nehru's own release at the end of January 1931. We lean toward it, but have not seen the jail record.</aside>

## Another loss

Motilal Nehru died on 6 February 1931, says the Herald. The Wire dates it February 1931 too. The Herald says that after this Kamala's health fell, and she could no longer take part in the movement.

## Why it matters

Kamala Nehru was sick and she was still out in the street. The stories do not make her a legend. They show a woman who did what was needed in her city, in a hard year, and paid a price in health.
