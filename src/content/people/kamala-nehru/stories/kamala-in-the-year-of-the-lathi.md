In 1930, Gandhi began the Salt March. Soon the Civil Disobedience Movement spread across India. Jawaharlal Nehru was sent to prison. In Allahabad, the work fell to his wife.

## More women than before

By April 1930 Nehru was in prison again, says the National Herald. Many more women joined the struggle than in 1920 and 1921. They picketed shops that sold foreign cloth and liquor. They marched in processions. Some faced the police *lathi*, a long wooden stick.

<aside class="note"><b>Lathi charge</b>: when police hit a crowd with long sticks to break it up.</aside>

<aside class="note"><b>One author</b>: The Wire carries the same account, but both it and the National Herald piece were written by Praveen Davar, so they are not two separate checks.</aside>

## A leader in her own city

Kamala was president of the Allahabad District Congress Committee. She toured the district to bring in women and young people. She led processions and picketed liquor and foreign-cloth shops. She dressed as a volunteer, in khadi pyjamas, a kurta and a Gandhi cap.

The Nehru Archive puts it another way. It says that in 1930 she was "for some time a member of the Congress Working Committee and the 'dictator' of Allahabad."

<aside class="note"><b>Dictator</b>: the page does not explain the word. We think it means a person put in charge of the local campaign, but we have not found a source that says so.</aside>

Jawaharlal Nehru wrote of her: "Kamala surprised me, for her energy and enthusiasm overcame her physical ill health." The Herald quotes this. We did not find the original book page.

## Hurt in the charge

Kamala's mother-in-law, Swarup Rani, was seriously hurt on the head in a lathi charge, both articles say. She continued to lead women afterwards, according to the Herald. We do not have a date or place for the injury.

## Arrest

Kamala was arrested on 1 January 1931, says the Nehru Archive. She was released at the end of that month with other Congress leaders.

The Herald tells it with more detail. She was arrested in December 1930 for a protest march against her husband's arrest, and jailed on 1 January 1931 for two months. In a letter, Nehru told Indira that her mother's arrest was "a pleasant New Year gift", and that she was "thoroughly happy and contented".

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: was she released at the end of January 1931, or after about two months? The Nehru Archive says January. The two articles say two months, and The Wire says she was let out because her health had got worse. The Archive's version fits with Nehru's own release at the end of January 1931. We lean toward it, but have not seen the jail record.</aside>

## Another loss

Motilal Nehru died on 6 February 1931, the Herald says. The Wire dates it February 1931 too. After this, Kamala's health fell, and she could no longer take part in the movement.

## Why it matters

Kamala Nehru was sick and she was still out in the street. The stories do not make her a legend. They show a woman who did what was needed in her city, in a hard year, and paid a price in health.
