When we hear about Jawaharlal Nehru, we hear about his speeches and his years in prison. But who was at home while he was away? And what did she do?

Her name was Kamala. She did much more than wait.

## A girl from Delhi

Kamala Kaul was born on 1 August 1899 in Delhi. Her family was a middle-class Kashmiri Pandit family. She was the eldest child of Jawaharmal and Rajpati Kaul.

On 8 February 1916, she married Jawaharlal Nehru. It was an arranged marriage. He was ten years older than she was. The wedding was on Vasant Panchami day, the stamp page adds.

<aside class="note"><b>Arranged marriage</b>: a marriage that the families plan, with the help of elders.</aside>

how old was she? Wikipedia says 16 and the stamp page says 17. By the birth and wedding dates she was 16. The Nehru Memorial Fund gives the wedding date.

Shortly after the marriage, Nehru went on a trip to the Himalayas. In his autobiography, he wrote that he "almost overlooked her."

On 19 November 1917, their daughter Indira was born.

## The year 1921

In 1921, the Non-Cooperation Movement was on. Gandhi had asked Indians not to help British rule.

<aside class="note"><b>Non-Cooperation Movement</b>: a campaign, begun in 1920, in which Indians stopped using British goods, courts and schools.</aside>

Kamala took part. She organised women in Allahabad. They picketed shops that sold foreign cloth and liquor.

Then something happened in her house. Nehru was arrested before a planned speech. Kamala read the speech to the crowd herself.

the Memorial Fund says Nehru was arrested on 6 December 1921. Wikipedia does not date the speech, so we cannot say whether it is the same occasion.

She was arrested on two more occasions, Wikipedia says. It does not give the dates.

## A dispensary at home

Kamala also looked after people. She started a Congress dispensary at her house in Allahabad, called Swaraj Bhavan. It treated wounded volunteers and local people.

<aside class="note"><b>Dispensary</b>: a small place where sick people get medicine and simple treatment.</aside>

After her death, Gandhi and other leaders turned the dispensary into the Kamla Nehru Memorial Hospital. The page we read gives no dates for any of this.

She also spent time at Gandhi's ashram. There she was with Kasturba Gandhi, and became close friends with a woman named Prabhavati Devi.

## 1930: the salt year

In 1930, Gandhi led the Dandi March and the Salt Satyagraha. The Civil Disobedience Movement began.

Kamala joined it. A stamp page, which summarises a booklet of India Post, says she led salt satyagraha work, picketing and protests in Allahabad. She also served on the Uttar Pradesh Congress Committee.

The Nehru Archive says that she was "for some time a member of the Congress Working Committee and the 'dictator' of Allahabad." The word "dictator" is in quotation marks in the page. We think it means a leader named to run the local campaign, but we have not found a source that says so.

She briefly became a member of the Working Committee when that body was declared unlawful and its members were arrested.

<aside class="note"><b>Working Committee</b>: the small group of top leaders that ran the Congress.</aside>

## Arrested, 1 January 1931

On 1 January 1931, Kamala was arrested. She was held in Lucknow Central Jail. She is quoted as saying, "I am happy beyond measure." That comes from a later summary, and we have not found the original.

She was released at the end of January 1931, along with other Congress leaders, says the Nehru Archive. Jawaharlal Nehru was also released on 26 January 1931. On that day a resolution honouring the part women played in the struggle was read at meetings across India.

## Illness

Kamala had tuberculosis, a serious lung disease. She was often ill. She went to sanatoria in Switzerland and returned to India when she was well.

<aside class="note"><b>Sanatorium</b>: a hospital for people with long illnesses, often in the mountains, where they rest and are treated.</aside>

In 1926, according to Wikipedia's page on Nehru, he went to Europe with his wife and daughter for her treatment. During that trip, he attended the Brussels congress.

the Memorial Fund says he attended the Congress of Oppressed Nationalities in Brussels on 10 February 1927. So the Nehru page and the Fund do not agree on whether the trip began in 1926 or 1927.

## The last years

In 1934, Nehru was in prison. He began his autobiography there in June, while Kamala was ill for a long time. The book is closely about their marriage.

In early 1935, Kamala's health got worse again. Subhas Chandra Bose took her to a sanatorium in Badenweiler, Germany. Nehru was in prison in India.

Nehru was released on 4 September 1935, from Almora jail. This was about five and a half months early, because Kamala's health was getting worse. He then went to Germany, where her health got better for a short time.

the page on Kamala says he was released in October 1935. The page on his autobiography gives a precise date, 4 September, so we use that.

## The end

Her illness had lasted for years. In all that time she kept working when she was well enough, and rested when she was not.

In 1936 she became worse again. Kamala died on 28 February 1936, in Lausanne, Switzerland. Her daughter and her mother-in-law were with her. She was 36.

Her treatment had been in Badenweiler, in Germany. She died in Lausanne. We do not know why she was moved.

Nehru wrote that he was devastated and mourned for months. We have only this short summary and not his own words, so we leave it there.

## Names and spellings

Her name is spelled both Kamala and Kamla. Both are used on the Wikipedia page, even for the hospital named after her.

## Why it matters

A freedom movement was made of many people. Some stood on platforms and gave speeches. Others picketed shops, ran a dispensary, or read someone else's speech to a crowd.

Kamala Nehru did all of these. She should be remembered for what she did, and not only for whom she married.
