When we hear about Jawaharlal Nehru, we hear about his speeches and his years in prison. But who was at home while he was away? And what did she do?

Her name was Kamala. She did much more than wait.

## A girl from Delhi

Kamala Kaul was born on 1 August 1899 in Delhi. Wikipedia says her family was a middle-class Kashmiri Pandit family. She was the eldest child of Jawaharmal and Rajpati Kaul.

On 8 February 1916, she married Jawaharlal Nehru. The Nehru Memorial Fund gives the date. Wikipedia says it was an arranged marriage, and that she was 16. The stamp page says she was 17. By the birth and wedding dates, she was 16. He was ten years older. The stamp page adds that the wedding was on Vasant Panchami day.

<aside class="note"><b>Arranged marriage</b>: a marriage that the families plan, with the help of elders.</aside>

Shortly after the marriage, Wikipedia says, Nehru went on a trip to the Himalayas. In his autobiography, he wrote that he "almost overlooked her."

On 19 November 1917, their daughter Indira was born. The Memorial Fund gives that date.

## The year 1921

In 1921, the Non-Cooperation Movement was on. Gandhi had asked Indians not to help British rule.

Kamala took part. Wikipedia says she organised women in Allahabad. They picketed shops that sold foreign cloth and liquor.

Then something happened in her house. Nehru was arrested before a planned speech. Wikipedia says Kamala read the speech to the crowd herself.

The Memorial Fund says Nehru was arrested on 6 December 1921. We do not know whether this is the same occasion. The Wikipedia page does not date it. So we cannot match the two.

<aside class="note"><b>Non-Cooperation Movement</b>: a campaign, begun in 1920, in which Indians stopped using British goods, courts and schools.</aside>

Wikipedia says she was arrested on two more occasions. It does not give the dates, so we cannot say when.

## A dispensary at home

Kamala also looked after people. Wikipedia says she started a Congress dispensary at her house in Allahabad, called Swaraj Bhavan. It treated wounded volunteers and local people.

<aside class="note"><b>Dispensary</b>: a small place where sick people get medicine and simple treatment.</aside>

After her death, the page says, Gandhi and other leaders turned the dispensary into the Kamla Nehru Memorial Hospital. There are no dates for any of this.

She also spent time at Gandhi's ashram. There she was with Kasturba Gandhi, and became close friends with a woman named Prabhavati Devi. The page gives no date.

## 1930: the salt year

In 1930, Gandhi led the Dandi March and the Salt Satyagraha. The Civil Disobedience Movement began.

The stamp page, which summarises a booklet of India Post, says Kamala joined it. It says she led salt satyagraha work, picketing and protests in Allahabad. It says she also served on the Uttar Pradesh Congress Committee.

The Nehru Archive says that she was "for some time a member of the Congress Working Committee and the 'dictator' of Allahabad." The word "dictator" is in quotation marks in the page, and it does not explain it. We think it means a leader named to run the local campaign, but we have not found a source that says so.

The stamp page says she briefly became a member of the Working Committee when that body was declared unlawful and its members were arrested. It gives no date.

<aside class="note"><b>Working Committee</b>: the small group of top leaders that ran the Congress.</aside>

## Arrested, 1 January 1931

Both pages say she was arrested on 1 January 1931. The stamp page says she was held in Lucknow Central Jail. It also quotes her as saying, "I am happy beyond measure." That is from a later summary, and we have not found the original.

The Nehru Archive says she was released at the end of January 1931, along with other Congress leaders. Jawaharlal Nehru was also released on 26 January 1931, according to the Memorial Fund. The stamp page says that on 26 January 1931, a resolution honouring the part women played in the struggle was read at meetings across India.

## Illness

Kamala had tuberculosis, a serious lung disease. Wikipedia says she was often ill. She went to sanatoria in Switzerland and returned to India when she was well.

<aside class="note"><b>Sanatorium</b>: a hospital for people with long illnesses, often in the mountains, where they rest and are treated.</aside>

Wikipedia's page on Nehru says that in 1926 he went to Europe with his wife and daughter for her treatment. During that trip, he attended the Brussels congress.

The Memorial Fund says he attended the Congress of Oppressed Nationalities in Brussels on 10 February 1927. So the Nehru page and the Fund do not agree on whether the trip began in 1926 or 1927.

## The last years

In 1934, Nehru was in prison. He began his autobiography there in June, and Wikipedia says he wrote it while Kamala was ill for a long time. The book is closely about their marriage.

In early 1935, Kamala's health got worse again. Wikipedia says Subhas Chandra Bose took her to a sanatorium in Badenweiler, Germany. Nehru was in prison in India.

Nehru was released on 4 September 1935, from Almora jail. The page on his autobiography says this was about five and a half months early, because Kamala's health was getting worse.

The page on Kamala says he was released in October 1935. The two pages disagree. The autobiography page gives a precise date, so we use it. He then went to Germany, where her health got better for a short time.

## The end

In 1936 she became worse again. Kamala died on 28 February 1936, in Lausanne, Switzerland. The Memorial Fund gives the date. Wikipedia says her daughter and her mother-in-law were with her. She was 36.

Her treatment had been in Badenweiler, in Germany. She died in Lausanne, in Switzerland. The page does not tell us why she was moved.

Wikipedia says Nehru wrote that he was devastated and mourned for months. We have only this short summary and not his own words, so we leave it there.

## Names and spellings

Her name is spelled both Kamala and Kamla. Both are used on the Wikipedia page, even for the hospital named after her.

## Why it matters

A freedom movement was made of many people. Some stood on platforms and gave speeches. Others picketed shops, ran a dispensary, or read someone else's speech to a crowd.

Kamala Nehru did all of these. She should be remembered for what she did, and not only for whom she married.
