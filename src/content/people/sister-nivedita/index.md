## Research notes

Supported Indian nationalist and educational causes; include as supporter, not fighter.

### Chronology

- **1898**: Arrived in Calcutta and was initiated as Nivedita
- **1898-11**: Opened a girls' school in Bagbazar
- **1899**: Participated in plague relief in Calcutta

### Evidence claims

- Sister Nivedita was born Margaret Elizabeth Noble in 1867 and died in 1911. (HIGH confidence)
- She came to Calcutta in 1898 after meeting Swami Vivekananda in London and received the name Nivedita. (HIGH confidence)
- In November 1898 she opened a girls' school in the Bagbazar area of Calcutta. (HIGH confidence)
- During the 1899 Calcutta plague epidemic, Nivedita participated in relief and nursing work. (HIGH confidence)
- Nivedita became actively associated with Indian nationalism and anti-imperial intellectual networks in Bengal. (HIGH confidence)

### Places

- Calcutta: principal field of educational and nationalist work
- Bagbazar: location of girls' school
