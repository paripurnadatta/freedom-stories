## Research notes

### A profile built from commemoration

Her inclusion is historically useful for gender and Northeast representation, but the evidence must remain explicitly bounded.

### Memory is evidence, but not the same as a battle report

The waterfall story can be presented as commemorative tradition rather than elevated into a precisely verified military incident.

### Chronology

- **1829-1830s**: Anglo-Khasi resistance. Ka Phan Nonglait is remembered as supporting the Khasi resistance associated with Tirot Sing
- **modern memory**: Government commemoration preserves her name in the landscape. The Ka Phan Nonglait Falls memorializes a woman whose surviving documentary record is much thinner than the public memory surrounding her

### Evidence claims

- Meghalaya government commemoration identifies Ka Phan Nonglait as a Khasi woman associated with resistance to British expansion in the Khasi Hills. (medium confidence)
- A Ministry of Culture factsheet links her to the fighters of Tirot Sing during the Anglo-Khasi conflict. (medium confidence)
- The commemorative account preserves a story in which she used deception near a waterfall to help disarm British soldiers. (low confidence)
- A waterfall in Meghalaya is officially named in her memory. (high confidence)
