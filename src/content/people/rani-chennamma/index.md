## Research notes

Led the Kittur resistance against Company annexation.

### A war over sovereignty before 1857

Kittur should be treated as a political conflict over succession and Company power in its own historical setting.

### The uprising of 1824

The resistance involved organized state and military action, not only symbolic defiance.

### Before the formal Doctrine of Lapse

Avoid anachronistically describing Kittur as a straightforward application of the later, formal Doctrine of Lapse.

### Chronology

- **1824**: Kittur succession crisis. Company refusal to accept the succession turns political dispute into armed conflict
- **1824-10**: Battle at Kittur. Chennamma's forces resist Company intervention
- **1824-1829**: Captivity at Bailhongal. The uprising is suppressed and Chennamma remains imprisoned until her death

### Evidence claims

- Was queen of Kittur and resisted East India Company intervention in the succession of the state. (high confidence)
- Led armed resistance during the Kittur uprising of 1824 after British authorities refused to recognize an adopted heir. (high confidence)
- Was captured and imprisoned at Bailhongal, where she died in 1829. (high confidence)
- The supplied record connects Rani Chennamma with Kittur uprising as leader of event. (high confidence)
- The supplied record connects Rani Chennamma with Kittur as associated with. (high confidence)
