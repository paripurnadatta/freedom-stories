Here is a story that was told about Dadabhai Naoroji. It has a lamp in it.

Before we tell it, we should say how we know it. Dinyar Patel found it in a Bombay newspaper called the Indu Prakash, dated 23 March 1885. The writer called himself "A Political Rishi". Patel thinks that the writer was probably Chandavarkar. That is more than thirty years after the events. So this is a later memory, and we label it so.

## The story

The writer tells of a rich Parsi, Sir Jamsetji Jejeebhoy, and an oil lamp. In the story Jejeebhoy turned a well-known saying about the lamp around. The writer says he then opened four schools for Parsi girls.

We do not have the words that were said, and we cannot tell you what the saying was. The newspaper is the only place the story comes from, as far as we found. So we do not tell more than that.

## What the records say

Other sources give the plain facts. Patel's timeline says Dadabhai helped found the Students' Literary and Scientific Society in 1848. It was a society of young men in Bombay. Janata Weekly says that girls' schools were helped to open in 1849.

<aside class="note"><b>Society</b>: a group of people who join together for a purpose, such as learning or reform.</aside>

Dadabhai was in his early twenties then.

## Papers and a society

He did not stop at schools. Patel gives 3 August 1851 for the Rahnumae Mazdayasnan Sabha. It was a Parsi society. The first issue of a newspaper, the Rast Goftar, came out on 15 November 1851.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: Patel's timeline dates the first Rast Goftar to 15 November 1851. Wikipedia says 1854. We have not seen the paper itself, so we cannot say which is right.</aside>

On 26 August 1852 he gave his first political speech, at the Bombay Association. He was twenty-six.

## The lamp, again

We cannot prove the lamp. The writer of 1885 remembered him as someone who worked for schools. The records agree that he was part of the early work for girls' schools.

## Why it matters

A story can be old, kind and still not be proved. The honest way to tell it is to say who wrote it down and when. Then we can enjoy it, and still know what we have and do not have. What we do know is plain: a young man in his twenties was linked with a society, with the first girls' schools and with a newspaper before he was thirty.
