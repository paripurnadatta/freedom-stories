Most people know Dadabhai Naoroji as a man of London and the Congress. For a short time he was something else: the chief minister of an Indian ruler.

## The job

Baroda was a princely state. Its ruler was called the Gaekwad. Dinyar Patel's timeline says Naoroji became its diwan in early December 1873. The ruler then was Malharrao Gaikwad.

<aside class="note"><b>Diwan</b>: the chief minister of a princely state. <b>Princely state</b>: a part of India ruled by an Indian prince, under the overall power of the British.</aside>

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: Patel's timeline names the ruler as Malharrao Gaikwad. Wikipedia says he served the Gaekwad Sayajirao III in 1874. We have not seen the state's own papers, so we do not choose.</aside>

## What he tried to change

Patel says that Naoroji twice offered to resign, in July and August 1874. The timeline does not say why.

On 31 July 1874 he put forward a list of reforms. One of them was to end "nazarana" in the courts. Nazarana was a payment made to the judges. Naoroji wanted that stopped.

We do not know how many of his changes were carried out. The sources are short on this. Patel's timeline gives the dates of the offers and the list, but not what the ruler said in reply. Wikipedia names Baroda only as a place where he served. So the details of daily work in the palace are missing from what we opened.

## The poison

Then came a frightening event. Manu Pillai writes that on 9 November 1874, the British Resident in Baroda, Colonel Phayre, was nearly poisoned. The Resident was the British officer who watched over the state. Pillai says the poison was arsenic mixed with crushed diamonds. Colonel Phayre did not die.

Patel's timeline says that evidence later linked the ruler himself to the poisoning. The British deposed Malharrao in late January 1875. The word means they removed him from the throne.

## Naoroji leaves

Naoroji resigned on 21 December 1874. He left Baroda on 11 January 1875.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: Pillai says Naoroji had already resigned before the scandal broke. Patel's dates show the poisoning on 9 November and the resignation on 21 December. The sources do not tell us what Naoroji felt, so we do not either.</aside>

We should be careful here. None of our sources says that Naoroji had any part in the poisoning. They say that he left, and that the ruler fell.

## After Baroda

Soon he was working in a different way. In 1876 he read the paper on the poverty of India in Bombay. You can read about it in "The forty shillings". He had left a post in a palace and gone back to writing and speaking.

## Why it matters

A diwan holds power. The records show that Naoroji listed the payments to judges as something to end, and that he offered to resign twice. They also show that he left before the year was out. Why he left is a question our sources do not answer.
