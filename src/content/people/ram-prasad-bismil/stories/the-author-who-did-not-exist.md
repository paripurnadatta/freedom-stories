Imagine writing a book that you know the government will hate. Would you put your own name on the cover?

A teenager in Shahjahanpur faced exactly that question. His answer was: no.

## A boy at the temple

Ram Prasad was a schoolboy in Shahjahanpur. He started going to the Arya Samaj temple in the town every day.

A holy man named Swami Somdev was staying there. He became Ram Prasad's teacher. He talked to him about religion, and about politics too.

## Angry news

When Ram Prasad was about 18 and in Class 9, he read some news that made him very angry. Bhai Parmanand, a friend of Swami Somdev, had been sentenced to death by the British in a case in Lahore.

Ram Prasad wrote a poem in Hindi. He called it Mera Janm, "My Birth". In it, he promised to work to end British rule in India. He took the poem to Swami Somdev.

He also made a vow to take revenge. According to the book Hanged for Patriotism, Swami Somdev answered with a warning: "It is easy to take a vow but hard to keep it."

## A book about another country

Then, with Swami Somdev's help, a Hindi book was published. Its name was America Ki Swatantrata Ka Itihas, "The History of America's Freedom".

Why a book about America? Because America, too, had once been ruled by Britain, and had fought to be free. On the surface it was only a history book about a faraway country. But it was a story about throwing off British rule.

## A made-up name

The book needed an author's name on it. So they invented one: "Babu Harivans Sahai, B.A." The letters B.A. made it look like the name of a man with a college degree.

The publisher's name was printed as Somdev Siddhgopal Shukla.

The government of the United Provinces banned it. It could no longer be sold in the province.

<aside class="note"><b>United Provinces</b>: the British name for the region that is now mostly Uttar Pradesh.</aside>

## A procession in Lucknow

In 1916, the Indian National Congress held its yearly meeting in Lucknow. Swami Somdev had told Ram Prasad to prepare well before he went.

Ram Prasad went with his friends. The great leader Bal Gangadhar Tilak came to the city. One account says that Ram Prasad and an older student led Tilak's procession through Lucknow, and that this made him known among young people in many places.

<aside class="note"><b>A later account</b>: the story of the book and the procession comes from later retellings of Bismil's life. The government's banning order itself has not been found online.</aside>

## Why it matters

Ram Prasad was still a teenager. He could not yet fight the British with weapons. But he had already found two tools he would use all his life: a pen, and a clever mind.

Later he would sign his poems with a name of his own choosing: Bismil.
