In 1918, a police team caught up with a group of young revolutionaries. Shots were fired on both sides.

One of the young men jumped into the river Yamuna.

The police believed he had drowned. They were wrong.

## Two groups join hands

The young man was Ram Prasad Bismil. He was about 20 years old.

He had started a small secret group of his own, called Matrivedi. His teacher, Swami Somdev, told him about a man in the town of Auraiya. His name was Genda Lal Dixit, and he was a schoolteacher.

Genda Lal had a secret group too, called the Shivaji Samiti. Its young members were armed. The two groups joined hands. Together they found young men in Etawah, Mainpuri, Agra and Shahjahanpur who wanted to fight British rule.

## Words first

On 28 January 1918, Bismil printed a leaflet. It was called Deshvasiyon Ke Nam Sandesh, "A Message to Countrymen". With it, the group handed out his poem Mainpuri Ki Pratigya, "The Vow of Mainpuri".

## Then robberies

In 1918, Bismil's group carried out robberies three times, to raise money for the group. Robbing people is a crime, and the police began to search the area around Mainpuri.

Then the group planned another robbery, somewhere between Delhi and Agra. A police team arrived. There was firing from both sides. And that was when Bismil leapt into the Yamuna.

According to one account, he swam away underwater. Both the police and his own companions believed he was dead.

<aside class="note"><b>Is it true?</b> This story comes from later retellings of Bismil's life. No police report of the river escape has been found. So we cannot be sure exactly how it happened.</aside>

## Caught and not caught

Genda Lal Dixit was not so lucky. He was arrested with some companions and locked up in the fort at Agra. Later, he managed to escape, and he lived in hiding in Delhi.

The case came to be called the Mainpuri Conspiracy. On 1 November 1919, the magistrate gave his judgment. He declared both Dixit and Bismil to be absconders.

<aside class="note"><b>Absconder</b>: a person who has run away and hidden to avoid being arrested or tried.</aside>

## Hiding

Bismil had to stay out of sight. Wikipedia says that one of the places where he lived in hiding was near a village called Rampur Jagir. Today, a park there is named after him.

In February 1920, the prisoners in the case were set free. Bismil came home to Shahjahanpur. He agreed not to take part in revolutionary work.

## Why it matters

For a while, Bismil kept that promise. But not for ever. A few years later, he helped to start a new secret party, the Hindustan Republican Association.

The river had not swallowed him after all. And the young man the police thought was dead would one day lead the robbery at Kakori.
