What day were you born? You know it. Your parents wrote it down.

Vallabhbhai Patel did not have that. The Congress website says plainly, "There is no record of his date of birth." The date we use today is 31 October 1875. It appears on his matriculation certificate, and he chose it himself, the Congress page says, "while filling in a form."

The Sardar Patel Trust says the same in fewer words: "The date was not registered." So 31 October is an adopted birthday. Patel picked it himself, and it is the one in every book now.

## One boy, two birthplaces

Where was he born? The Congress page and Wikipedia say Nadiad. He spent his childhood at Karamsad, a village in the Kheda district of Gujarat, where his family farmed. The Trust page says he was one of the six children of Ladba and Zaverbhai "of Karamsad."

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: Nadiad and Karamsad are both given as his birthplace. The two towns are close. Karamsad was his family's village and his childhood home. We do not know of an original record that settles it, so we say he grew up at Karamsad and leave the birthplace open.</aside>

His family belonged to the Lewa Patidar community of farmers. The Congress page says they were a lower middle-class family with no tradition of schooling. Vallabhbhai went to a school at Karamsad. The Trust says that in 1891 he went to Petlad, a nearby village, for English classes. He passed his matriculation at a high school in Nadiad in 1897.

## A strike, and a story about his father

A story from his school days says that, when he was in the top class, he organised a strike by his classmates that lasted three days. Their teacher was too fond of using the cane.

The same story adds that his father, "it is said, had fought in the Mutiny under the Rani of Jhansi." It adds that the father was later taken prisoner by Malhar Rao Holkar.

<aside class="note"><b>A word of care</b>: Both stories come from one biography on the Congress website. It writes "it is said" about the father. We did not find a second source for either one. Please take them as family and school tales, not as proven facts.</aside>

## Lawyer in the country

He could not afford a college. So he took a law course, and then became a country lawyer. The Trust says he passed the District Pleader's exam and began work at Godhra in 1900. In 1902 he moved to Borsad, in the Kheda district.

His wife Zaverba died in 1909, after an operation in Bombay. The Trust gives that. The Congress page tells a story about the moment. It says that when the news reached him, he was cross-examining a witness in a murder case at Anand. He "did not show grief but went on with the cross-examination in hand."

<aside class="note"><b>A word of care</b>: This is again one later biography. It is not a court record. We do not know what the message said, who brought it, or how Patel felt. We do not guess.</aside>

## London

In 1910, Patel sailed for England. The Trust says it was his long-time dream to become a barrister. He joined the Middle Temple. The Congress page says he topped his class in Roman law and was called to the bar after two years, instead of the usual three.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: Wikipedia says he finished the 36-month course in 30 months. The Congress page says two years. We do not know which is right, so we say only that he finished early.</aside>

He came back to India in 1913 and settled in Ahmedabad. The Congress page says he built a successful practice there. Within a few years, Gandhi would pull him away from it.

## Why it matters

Patel's early life was not one long story of courage. It was school, farm work, exams, a small town, and the money to study. Much of what we hear about it comes from later biographies, and we have marked where. What we know for certain is smaller. A boy from a farming family became a lawyer in London, and picked his own birthday.
