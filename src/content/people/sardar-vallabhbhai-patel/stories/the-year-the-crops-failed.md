A farmer's year depends on rain. In Kheda, in 1918, the crops had failed. Wikipedia says there was famine in the area, and cholera and plague as well.

The farmers asked the government not to take the year's tax. The government said no. This is how the Kheda satyagraha began.

## The four-anna rule

There was a rule about this. Gandhi writes about it. Under the Land Revenue Rules, if a harvest was four annas or less in the rupee, the farmer could ask for the whole year's tax to be put off.

<aside class="note"><b>Anna</b>: an old coin. There were sixteen annas in a rupee. "Four annas" meant a quarter of a normal harvest.</aside>

The officials said the crop was more than four annas. The farmers said it was less. The government refused a request for a neutral person to decide. Wikipedia adds a figure: it says the authorities had also raised the tax in Kheda by 23 per cent.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: The Wikipedia page says the problem was a tax rise of 23 per cent and also a famine. It does not describe the four-anna rule. Gandhi's account describes the four-anna rule but gives no percentage and no dates. We tell both, and we do not put dates on the story's days.</aside>

## The pledge

Gandhi says the farmers who joined the satyagraha signed a pledge. They would not voluntarily pay the full year's tax. They accepted that their lands could be taken. They would rather lose their land than their self-respect.

There was a kind rule too. The farmers who could afford to pay were asked to wait. They would pay only if the second instalment was put off for the whole district. That was so that poorer farmers would not have to sell their belongings or borrow money.

## The lawyer who came

Gandhi led the campaign. Wikipedia says he was its spiritual head. Several workers toured the villages. The list includes Vallabhbhai Patel, Indulal Yagnik, Shankarlal Banker, Mahadev Desai, Narhari Parikh and Mohanlal Pandya.

Gandhi writes that Patel was one of his chief comrades. He says Patel gave up a thriving legal practice for the struggle and never really went back to it. The Congress page quotes Gandhi saying that "this campaign would not have been carried through so successfully" without Patel's help. The campaign had its office in the Nadiad Anathashram.

The Press Information Bureau says Kheda gave two lasting results. It made the farmers a party in decisions about land tax. And it started the close teamwork between Gandhi and Patel.

## What the government did

Wikipedia says that the government's men took away farmers' property and cattle, and that police seized land. It also says the farmers did not fight back. They gave their cash and valuables to the Gujarat Sabha, which was running the protest. The Congress page says the campaign lasted about three months, with arrests and seizures, and with "official brutality" before the government gave way.

Wikipedia marks several of these details as lacking sources. So we say only what both pages agree on: goods were seized, the farmers stayed peaceful, and the campaign lasted some months.

## How it ended

Gandhi tells the ending himself, and he was not satisfied. An officer of Nadiad proposed that the well-off farmers pay, and the poorer ones would get the suspension. Gandhi got that in writing. Then the Collector said that suspension orders had already been issued, so the pledge was kept. Gandhi was unhappy. The Collector acted as if he had done nothing. And hardly any poor farmers got help.

Gandhi writes that a campaign should end by leaving people "stronger and more spirited." He felt Kheda did not completely do that.

## Why it matters

Kheda was a small campaign. It did not win everything. But it showed Patel what organised, peaceful refusal could do. Ten years later, in Bardoli, he would lead one himself. Gandhi's honest ending is worth remembering too. He did not call it a full victory.
