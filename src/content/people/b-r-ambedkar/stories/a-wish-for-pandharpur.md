Some promises are made in a crowd. This one was made after a funeral.

In May 1935, Ambedkar's wife, Ramabai, died after a long illness.

## The pilgrimage she never made

Ramabai had one great wish. She wanted to make a pilgrimage to Pandharpur, where there is a temple.

<aside class="note"><b>Pilgrimage</b>: a journey to a holy place.</aside>

Her husband had never allowed her to go. As an untouchable, she would not have been allowed into the temple.

<aside class="note"><b>Untouchable</b>: a word for people who were treated as unclean because of the caste they were born into.</aside>

Ambedkar told her he would create a new Pandharpur for her. Hinduism's own Pandharpur, he said, treated them as untouchables.

## The conference at Yeola

On 13 October 1935, Ambedkar was president of a meeting at Yeola, in Nashik district. It was called the Yeola Conversion Conference.

For years, many untouchables had fought for the right to enter temples. Ambedkar told the meeting to give up that fight. Instead, he said, they should leave Hinduism and join another religion.

He made a vow, as his biographer Dhananjay Keer recorded: "I solemnly assure you that I will not die as a Hindu."

He repeated this message at many public meetings across India. In April 1936 he said it again at a Sikh conference in Amritsar.

## A different path

Not everybody chose the same road. In 1936 the Maharaja of Travancore announced that the Depressed Classes could enter the temples of his state. A timeline by the scholar Frances Pritchett calls this "the first such event in modern India".

Ambedkar, meanwhile, had told his followers to stop campaigning for temple entry and to leave Hinduism instead. These were two answers to the same wrong. Those who disagreed with him had their own arguments, and this story only tells what he said and did.

## Twenty-one years later

In 1954 he told people that he was writing a book on Buddhism. When it was done, he said, he would formally convert. He finished the manuscript of *The Buddha and His Dhamma* in 1956.

He was ill then. From June to October he was bedridden in Delhi, with failing eyesight.

On 14 October 1956, at Nagpur, Ambedkar became a Buddhist. Many thousands of Mahars and other Dalits joined him. He explained his choice of Nagpur in a speech called "Why Was Nagpur Chosen?". He died in December 1956.

<aside class="note"><b>Ramabai and the vow</b>: no source says that Ramabai's death played a part in his decision. Her death was in May 1935 and the Yeola meeting was in October. Whether the two are linked is left to the reader.</aside>

## Why it matters

For years Ambedkar had argued with the Congress about how to win rights for his people. At Yeola he told them to look beyond the doors of the temples.

It took him twenty-one years to carry out the vow he made there. He had said "I will not die as a Hindu". On 14 October 1956 he showed what he meant.
