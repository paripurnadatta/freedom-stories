What do you do when you come home with high degrees, and many people of your community are still shut out of schools?

In 1924, Ambedkar's answer was three words.

## Home again

Pritchett's timeline says he came back to Bombay in 1924 after a "somewhat harrowing" sea voyage. His community gave him a warm welcome.

His family had had a hard time while he was away. They had little money.

Now he began to work as a barrister in Bombay. Wikipedia says that, while he practised law in the Bombay High Court, he tried to help untouchables get an education.

<aside class="note"><b>Untouchable</b>: a word for people who were treated as unclean because of the caste they were born into.</aside>

## A group with a motto

In 1924 he founded the Bahishkrit Hitakarini Sabha. Pritchett translates this as "Group for the Wellbeing of the Excluded". Wikipedia says it was meant to promote education and the welfare of "outcastes".

Its motto was three words: "Educate, Agitate, Organise."

<aside class="note"><b>Agitate</b>: here it means to speak up loudly and keep asking for change, by peaceful means.</aside>

It was the first group he organised himself. It was only the beginning.

## Busy years

In 1925 he began to teach part-time. His subject was business law, at a training institute in Bombay. He also published a book of his research on the history of India's provincial finances. He gave evidence before the Royal Commission on Indian Currency and Finance.

In 1926 the Governor of Bombay named him a member of the Bombay Legislative Council. He took his work seriously, and often spoke about money and the economy.

## A sorrow

These years were not only about work. Pritchett's timeline, quoting a biographer named Khairmode, says that between 1913 and 1920 Ambedkar and Ramabai had three children who died when they were a little over a year old.

In 1926 they lost another baby son, Rajaratna.

Ambedkar wrote to a friend that August. We have the words from Pritchett's page, which quotes Keer. "There is no use pretending that I and my wife have recovered from the shock of our son's death, and I do not think that we ever shall." He wrote that they had buried four children, "three sons and a daughter".

He ended: "With his passing away life to me is a garden full of weeds. But enough of this, I am too overcome to write any more."

They also had an older son, Yashwant, born in 1912.

## On to Mahad

The next year, 1927, he started a big campaign at Mahad. Thousands of people went to drink from a public tank. You can read about it in *The water of Chavdar Tale*.

## Why it matters

When we read about leaders, we often read only about speeches and laws. Ambedkar kept working through these years, and the biggest campaign of his early career came the year after he lost his son.

He gave his group a plain motto. Learn. Speak up. Join together. The three words are short enough for a child to remember.
