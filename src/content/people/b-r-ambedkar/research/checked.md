# Check of the owner's Ambedkar report (PDF, filed 22 October 2026)

The PDF is research only. No sentence from it is used in a story. Every story was written
from pages Claude opened in this session.

## What held up (opened and confirmed)
- Waiting for a Visa (full text, franpritchett.com): the Masur and Koregaon journey, the school sack and
  "no peon, no water", the barber and washerman, the Parsi inn in Baroda (11 days, rupee and a half
  a day, a dozen men with sticks, Kamathi Baug, 9 pm train), the Chalisgaon tonga, the Daulatabad tank.
  Quotes in the PDF from these parts are accurate.
- LSE archivist's blog (2016): Foxwell's "no more worlds here for him to conquer" (Nov 1920), thesis
  not recommended March 1923, resubmitted August 1923, accepted November 1923, no sign of a
  political reason in the student file.
- Pritchett timelines (1910s, 1920s, 1930s, 1950s): Columbia years, torpedoed books, Thomas Cook damages,
  1920 Mooknayak, Mangaon dinner, 1924 Sabha and its motto, Rajaratna's death, Yeola vow, conversion.
- Wikipedia: Keluskar's Buddha biography at the 1907 celebration; 50,000 books; Mooknayak; Kalaram (15,000).

## Errors or doubtful points in the PDF
1. The PDF says "Goregaon". Ambedkar's own text says Koregaon (in Khatav taluka, Satara district) and the
   station was Masur. Not the Koregaon near Poona (a different place).
2. The PDF says the boys drove the cart "in the dark" all night. In the text, they drove only the first
   4 or 5 miles. Then the cartman jumped in and took the reins.
3. The PDF says "Bhim and his brothers" in one place and "his older brother and nephew" in another. Ambedkar says he, his elder brother and one nephew set out,
   then writes "we four children". His text does not explain the fourth child. A note in the story says so.
4. The PDF says he "hid under a Parsi name" and "12 men ... cornered him". True to the text, but the text says
   it was a "dozen" and that nobody hit him.
5. The PDF gives the Baroda year as 1917 on the strength of an editor's note that "[Actually, he ... returned to India in
   July 1917...]". That wording is not in the version of the text we opened. The editor's note we saw says only
   "the dates here appear to be a bit confused". Other sources (Pritchett timeline, LSE, Wikipedia) say 1917, so
   the story uses 1917 and explains the confusion.
6. The PDF says the job in Baroda was "probationer in the Accountant General's Office" (Ambedkar's own
   words). Pritchett's timeline and the LSE article say Military Secretary. Both are shown in a note.
7. Thesis rejected "in March 1923" because "too revolutionary": the LSE archivist says the file does not show
   this. Pritchett and the 2023 LSE article differ. Shown as "Three versions".
8. The 1922 letter to Seligman ("lost my manuscript of the original thesis") is quoted on Pritchett's page. The
   exact date in the PDF (16 February 1922) is not on the page we opened, so we did not use it.
   We also cannot tell whether the "original thesis" was for Columbia or London.
9. Poona Pact seats (70 / 71 / 80): handled already in "Seventy-one seats or a life".
10. The Hindustan Times pieces (Devadas "Father is sinking"; the fountain pen swap) could not be opened
    (blocked). Not used.
11. Resignation date (27 Sept 1951 vs 6 Oct 1951 vs 1950): not written. After 1947.
12. "Indrani Jagjivan Ram's memoir" claim about the 1947 cabinet: seen only as Wikipedia's report of the
    memoir. Not used in a story.
13. "10 King Henry's Road, Chalk Farm": not checked. Not used.
14. The PDF links the Keluskar book to his later conversion ("changed the rest of his life"). No source we opened says that, so we did not use the claim.
