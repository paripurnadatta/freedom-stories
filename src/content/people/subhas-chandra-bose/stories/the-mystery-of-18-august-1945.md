In August 1945, the Second World War came to an end. Japan surrendered.

For Netaji, this was a disaster. Japan had been his only ally in Asia. Now his army had no one to help it.

He decided to try one more thing. He would go to Manchuria, in the far north of China, and try to reach the Soviet Union. Perhaps the Soviets would help.

## The flight

On 18 August 1945, Netaji was on a Japanese bomber plane at an airfield in Taiwan. The city was then called Taihoku. Today it is Taipei.

With him was one of his officers, Colonel Habibur Rahman. There were Japanese officers on board too.

The plane took off. Moments later, something went badly wrong. One of its engines and its propeller broke away. The plane crashed to the ground and caught fire.

## The hospital

Netaji was badly burned. He was rushed to a Japanese military hospital in the city. A doctor named Taneyoshi Yoshimi tried to save him.

That night, Netaji died.

Colonel Habibur Rahman survived, with burns.

Netaji's body was cremated two days later. His ashes were taken to Tokyo. They were placed in a small Buddhist temple called Renkō-ji. They are still there today.

<aside class="note"><b>What time did he die?</b> Even this is not certain. One report says between five and eight in the evening. Another says between nine and ten at night. The doctor remembered the time as 11:50 at night.</aside>

## "He is not dead!"

When the news reached India, many people simply did not believe it. Netaji had escaped before. He had vanished from his house in Calcutta. He had crossed the world in submarines. Surely he had escaped again?

Rumours spread. Some said he was hiding in the Soviet Union. Others said he had come back to India in disguise, as a holy man.

## The inquiries

Over the years, people kept asking the government to find out the truth. So there were several official inquiries.

- In 1946, a British intelligence report said he died in the hospital in Taihoku.
- In 1956, an Indian committee said the same. But one member disagreed. He was Netaji's own elder brother, Suresh Chandra Bose.
- From 1970 to 1974, another Indian inquiry agreed that he died after the crash.
- A Japanese government report from 1956, kept secret until 2016, also said he died in the crash.

<aside class="note"><b>Inquiry</b>: an official investigation to find out the truth about something.</aside>

## The inquiry that disagreed

Then came one more. In 2005, a commission led by Justice M. K. Mukherjee gave its report. It said there was no proof of the crash. It suggested the ashes in Tokyo belonged to someone else.

But the Government of India did not accept this report. And this commission also tested one of the famous rumours. A holy man in Uttar Pradesh, called Gumnami Baba, had been said to be Netaji. A DNA test showed he was not.

## What do we know?

Most historians today agree. Netaji died after the plane crash in Taiwan on 18 August 1945. Every inquiry except one came to that answer.

But some people still hope for more answers. And that is why, eighty years later, the question has not quite gone away.

The man who could vanish from a watched house never got a quiet ending. Perhaps that is why so many people wished he had escaped one last time.
