Subhas Chandra Bose was born on 23 January 1897, in Cuttack. His family was very big. He was the ninth of fourteen children. His father, Janakinath Bose, was a lawyer.

Look at the old family photograph. It was taken around 1905. The little boy standing at the far right is Subhas.

## A thinking boy

Subhas grew up to be a serious, thoughtful teenager. He thought a lot about religion. He wondered about big questions. What is the right way to live? What is the purpose of life?

He felt that he needed a guru. A guru is a spiritual teacher who guides you.

## Running away

In 1914, Subhas was seventeen. He was a student at a college in Calcutta.

That year, during the long holidays, he did something bold. He set off to find a guru. He did not tell his parents properly where he was going.

He did not go alone. His close friend, Hemanta Kumar Sarkar, went with him. The two had met a couple of years earlier, through a teacher they both admired.

## From one holy city to the next

The two friends travelled across North India. They visited the great holy places. They went to Haridwar on the Ganga. They went to Mathura and Vrindavan, where Lord Krishna is said to have grown up. They went to Varanasi too.

In each place they met holy men. They listened and asked questions. But none of them seemed like the right guru.

<aside class="note"><b>Which places?</b> Different books give slightly different lists. One adds Rishikesh and Gaya. Another adds Ayodhya. All agree the trip lasted a few months.</aside>

## The holy man's advice

At Vrindavan, they met a holy man called Baba Ramakrishnadas. He listened to the two young men. Then he gave them surprising advice.

He told them to stop searching for a guru. They argued too much, he said. They were not suited to a quiet spiritual life.

Perhaps he saw something in Subhas. This boy was not meant to sit still and pray. He was meant to act.

## Coming home

The trip did not end well. Subhas caught typhoid fever, a serious illness. He came home sick and disappointed. His worried parents were very upset.

The friendship lasted too. Years later, Subhas and Hemanta shared a prison cell. When Hemanta fell ill with smallpox, Subhas nursed him.

The boy who went looking for a guru never found one. Instead, he became a leader himself.
