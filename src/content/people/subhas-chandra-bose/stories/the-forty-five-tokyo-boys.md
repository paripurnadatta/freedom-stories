An army needs soldiers today. But a country needs leaders tomorrow.

Netaji was thinking about both.

## Looking to the future

In 1944, the Indian National Army was fighting alongside Japan. Most of its soldiers were older men. Many had been soldiers in the British Indian Army before.

But Netaji also wanted young Indians trained as officers and pilots. Japan had academies that could teach them.

## Forty-five chosen

Netaji decided to start right away. He personally chose forty-five young Indians. Some of them had joined the army from the Indian communities of Burma and Malaya.

In 1944, he sent them to Japan.

There, they joined Japan's military academies. Some went to the army academy. Others went to the air force academy, to train as fighter pilots.

People called them the Tokyo Boys.

<aside class="note"><b>Academy</b>: a special school. A military academy trains young people to become officers in an army, navy or air force.</aside>

## A long way from home

Think about what this meant. These were young men, sent to a faraway country, in the middle of a world war. Everything was new: the language, the food, the drill.

## The war ends

In August 1945, Japan surrendered. The war was over. The Tokyo Boys' training stopped suddenly.

The British held them as prisoners of war. In the winter of 1945, they were put on a ship, the Glenearn, and brought to Madras. They were not let go until 1946, after the trials of the Indian National Army's officers in Delhi.

The British did not treat them as harshly as the older soldiers. Officials kept an eye on them for a while. One wrote that nothing serious about them had come to notice.

## What became of them

The Tokyo Boys did not give up on flying, or on serving.

After they came home, many became officers. Some joined the forces of free India. Some served in Burma's navy. Some joined the forces of Pakistan, after the country was divided. A few became private pilots.

Some rose very high. One of them, Ramesh Sakharam Benegal, became an Air Commodore in the Indian Air Force. He was awarded the Maha Vir Chakra, one of India's highest awards for bravery in war.

## Why it matters

Netaji's army did not win its war. But he planted seeds for the future.

Forty-five boys went to Japan to learn to fly. Years later, some of them were flying for a free India.
