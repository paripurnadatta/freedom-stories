The war ended in 1945. Japan surrendered. Netaji's Indian National Army had lost.

Thousands of its soldiers were captured by the British. And the British had to decide: what should be done with them?

## Traitors or heroes?

To the British, these men were traitors. Many had once been soldiers in the British Indian Army. Then they had joined Netaji and fought against Britain.

So the British decided to hold trials. They would be held in public, at the Red Fort in Delhi. Everyone would see what happened to those who fought against the King.

That was the plan. It did not work out that way.

## Three officers

The first and most famous trial began in November 1945. Three officers stood in the dock together.

- Prem Kumar Sahgal, a Hindu.
- Gurbaksh Singh Dhillon, a Sikh.
- Shah Nawaz Khan, a Muslim.

All three were charged with "waging war against the King". That was one of the most serious crimes there was.

<aside class="note"><b>The Red Fort</b>: the great red sandstone fort in Delhi, built by the Mughal emperor Shah Jahan. Today the Prime Minister speaks from it every Independence Day.</aside>

## India takes their side

The British had not expected what happened next.

All over India, people took the side of the three officers. It did not matter that one was Hindu, one Muslim and one Sikh. They were India's sons. That was enough.

Crowds marched in the streets. A popular slogan wished long life to all three, by name. At protests, Congress and Muslim League flags flew side by side. That was rare in those days.

The Congress party formed a committee to defend them. Famous lawyers came forward. The defence was led by Sir Tej Bahadur Sapru. Jawaharlal Nehru was on the defence committee too.

## The verdict

The court found the three men guilty. They were sentenced to be sent away for life.

But the anger in the country was so great that the sentence was never carried out. The British Army chief in India, General Auchinleck, had to give way. All three were set free.

## The sailors rise

The trials lit a fire that the British could not put out.

In February 1946, sailors of the Royal Indian Navy went on strike. It became a mutiny that spread to ships and bases across India. The sailors used Netaji's name and the INA's. They demanded that the trials stop.

## Why it matters

Netaji's army had lost on the battlefield. But at the Red Fort, its story won the hearts of the whole country.

Less than two years later, in August 1947, India became free.
