By April 1945, the dream was falling apart.

The year before, the Indian National Army had marched with the Japanese towards India. They had fought at Imphal and Kohima, in India's north-east. They had been beaten back.

Now the British army was pushing hard through Burma. It was getting close to Rangoon, where Netaji had his headquarters.

It was time to leave.

## Leaving Rangoon

In the last week of April 1945, Netaji left Rangoon. With him went his senior officers, several hundred soldiers, and nearly a hundred women of the Rani of Jhansi Regiment.

Some women of the regiment came from Burma itself. They were allowed to stay behind and go home. The rest came along.

They were heading east, to a town called Moulmein. From there they hoped to reach Thailand.

They set off in trucks and cars.

## Trouble at the river

Soon they came to a wide river called the Sittang.

The sky above was not safe. American planes kept swooping down and firing at anything that moved on the roads. Because of these attacks, very few vehicles managed to get across the river.

The group was stuck. They had no trucks now. And Moulmein was still about eighty miles away. That is about 130 kilometres.

There was only one way to go. On foot.

## "I will walk with them"

The Japanese found a car for Netaji. He was the leader. He was important. He could ride to safety.

Netaji refused.

He would not leave his soldiers behind. If they had to walk, he would walk too. And so he did, with the men and the young women of the regiment.

<aside class="note"><b>Eighty miles</b>: that is about the distance from Delhi to Meerut and back again. Imagine walking it in the heat, with planes overhead.</aside>

## A week on the road

For about a week, the group trudged through the hot Burmese countryside.

At last, they reached Moulmein. From there, the group boarded Japanese trains heading on towards Thailand.

## Why it is remembered

Nothing was won on that walk. No battle. No town. The army was in retreat.

But the soldiers never forgot it. Their leader could have ridden away. Instead, he walked every mile beside them.

A few months later, the war ended. And not long after that, Netaji boarded a plane that would lead to the greatest mystery of his life.
