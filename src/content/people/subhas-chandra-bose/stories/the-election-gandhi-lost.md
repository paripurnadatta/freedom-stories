Imagine two of the most famous leaders in a country, on the same side, wanting the same thing. Now imagine them disagreeing so strongly that one of them has to leave.

That is what happened to Mahatma Gandhi and Subhas Chandra Bose in 1939.

## The young president

In January 1938, Subhas Chandra Bose became president of the Indian National Congress. He was only 40, and he was full of ideas.

In February 1938, the Congress held its yearly session at Haripura, a small village in Gujarat. Gandhi asked the artist Nandalal Bose to decorate it. Nandalal and his students painted dozens of posters on handmade paper, with natural earth colours, showing village life: hunters, musicians, carpenters, smiths, spinners and women husking rice. Bose arrived for the session in a chariot pulled by 51 pairs of bullocks, one for each session of the Congress so far.

<aside class="note"><b>How many posters?</b> One source says Nandalal painted 84 posters himself. Another says 83.</aside>

In his speech, Bose said that freedom from Britain was "merely the first step". After that, India must plan how to build its industries. Later that year he set up a National Planning Committee, with Jawaharlal Nehru as its chairman.

Bose wanted the Congress to become a broad front against British rule, uniting everyone who wanted the British out. He wanted India to plan its industries, and he wanted a fairer, socialist economy. He did not rule out using force against the British.

<aside class="note"><b>Socialist</b>: wanting wealth, land and industry to be shared fairly, often with the government planning the economy.</aside>

Gandhi did not agree with all of this. He believed in non-violence, always.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: Wikipedia says Gandhi opposed Bose's presidency from the start. Another source says Bose was chosen in 1938 with Gandhi's backing.</aside>

## A second term

Bose decided to stand for president again. He wanted to push for a bold plan. The Congress would give the British an ultimatum: six months to grant India complete independence.

<aside class="note"><b>Ultimatum</b>: a final demand, with a warning of what will happen if it is not met.</aside>

Gandhi and most of the senior leaders did not want Bose to have a second term. Maulana Abul Kalam Azad was first asked to stand against him, but he withdrew. So Gandhi's side chose another candidate, Dr Pattabhi Sitaramayya.

## The vote

The election was held on 29 January 1939. The delegates of the Congress voted.

Bose won. One account gives the result as 1,580 votes for Bose and 1,377 for Sitaramayya.

<aside class="note"><b>Two versions</b>: one article says Bose won by 95 votes. The vote counts given by another source make the gap 203. The sources do not explain the difference.</aside>

Gandhi saw the result as a vote against himself. He said: "Pattabhi's defeat is more mine than his."

## The walk-out

Then came a shock. In February 1939, twelve of the fifteen members of the Congress Working Committee resigned all at once. The Working Committee was the party's top team. Among those who resigned were Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel, Maulana Azad and Dr Rajendra Prasad.

<aside class="note"><b>Did Nehru resign?</b> One source says Nehru resigned separately. Another says he never formally resigned, but his statement made many people think he had.</aside>

Bose had won the election. But now most of the senior leaders would not work with him.

## Carried in on a stretcher

In March 1939, the Congress met for its yearly session at Tripuri, a village near Jabalpur in central India.

Bose was very ill, with a high fever. He was carried to the session on a stretcher, and he could not take part in most of it.

Some of his opponents said his illness was "only a political illness", meaning that he was pretending. His brother, Sarat Chandra Bose, was furious. In a letter to Gandhi, he called the campaign against his brother "thoroughly mean, malicious".

## The Pant resolution

While Bose lay ill, a senior leader, Govind Ballabh Pant, put forward a resolution. It said the Congress had full confidence in Gandhi. And it said the president must choose his Working Committee according to Gandhi's wishes.

<aside class="note"><b>Resolution</b>: a formal decision that a meeting votes on.</aside>

The resolution was passed. Bose was still president. But now he could not even choose his own team without Gandhi's approval.

## Two sides

Who was right? People still argue about it.

Bose's supporters say that he won a fair election, and the old leaders refused to work with him. Sarat Bose wrote that the senior leaders "used Mahatmaji as their cover".

On the other side, Gandhi believed in non-violence and did not support Bose's readiness to use force against the British. The two men also disagreed about smaller things. Gandhi preferred to give only moral support to people's movements in the princely states, while Bose wanted the Congress to back them actively. And the senior leaders who resigned felt they could not work under Bose.

<aside class="note"><b>Princely states</b>: parts of India ruled by Indian kings and nawabs, under British control.</aside>

## Resignation

Bose tried to find a way to work with Gandhi, but they could not agree. On 29 April 1939, at a meeting in Calcutta, Bose resigned as president. Dr Rajendra Prasad took his place.

A few days later, Bose started a new group inside the Congress, the Forward Bloc, to bring together the left wing of the party.

<aside class="note"><b>Which date?</b> One source says the Forward Bloc was formed on 3 May 1939. Wikipedia says 22 June 1939.</aside>

In August 1939, the Working Committee went further. It banned Bose from holding any elected post in the Congress for three years.

## What came after

Within weeks, the Second World War began. Bose wanted a mass protest at once, because the Viceroy had declared war on India's behalf without asking Indians. Gandhi did not agree. So Bose led protests in Calcutta on his own. The British jailed him. He was released only after a seven-day hunger strike. The two men's paths had now split completely. Bose escaped from India in 1941, and went on to lead the Indian National Army in South-East Asia.

But that is not the end of the story.

On 6 July 1944, Bose spoke on the radio from Singapore. He addressed Gandhi as the "Father of the Nation", and asked for his blessings. Wikipedia says this was the first time anyone called Gandhi by that name.

## Why it matters

Gandhi and Bose disagreed deeply, about methods, about timing and about how to run the party. Their quarrel split the Congress in 1939.

Yet the man who lost the battle inside the Congress gave Gandhi the name that India still uses for him. Two great leaders can disagree completely, and still respect each other.
