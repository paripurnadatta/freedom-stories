In 1943, Singapore was full of Indians. Many worked on rubber plantations in nearby Malaya. Their families had come from India long ago. Some of their children had never set foot in India at all.

On 2 July 1943, Netaji arrived in Singapore. He was there to lead the Indian National Army.

Within days, he announced something nobody had ever seen in Asia.

## An army regiment of women

On 12 July 1943, Netaji said the army would have a regiment made up entirely of women. They would not just cook or sew. They would train as fighters.

He named it after one of India's bravest women: Rani Lakshmibai of Jhansi. She had fought the British on horseback in 1857.

<aside class="note"><b>Regiment</b>: a large group of soldiers in an army, with its own name and commander.</aside>

## The doctor

Who would lead it?

In Singapore lived a young Indian doctor named Lakshmi Swaminathan. She had studied medicine in Madras. In 1940 she had moved to Singapore and opened her own clinic. She was busy and successful.

She gave it all up to command the new regiment. Everyone called her "Captain Lakshmi". She later became known as Lakshmi Sahgal.

## The recruits

Women and girls came forward. Most were teenagers. Most were from families who worked on the rubber estates of Malaya. Very few had ever been to India.

Yet they wanted to fight for it.

Training began in Singapore on 23 October 1943. The first group was small, perhaps around 170. By November there were more than 300. In the end, about a thousand women joined.

## Learning to be soldiers

The training was tough. The women did drills and long marches. They learned to use rifles and hand grenades. They practised bayonet charges.

Not everyone became a fighter. About 200 were chosen to train as nurses. They formed their own group, called the Chand Bibi Nursing Corps.

On 30 March 1944, 500 women marched in their first passing-out parade in Singapore.

## The hard road back

Later, some of the regiment went to Burma, where the war was being fought. But the war went badly for the Indian National Army and Japan.

In 1945, the women retreated on foot with the army, under attack from the air. Nearly a hundred of them left Rangoon with Netaji himself.

After the war, the regiment was broken up. Captain Lakshmi was arrested by the British in Burma in May 1945.

## After freedom

Lakshmi Sahgal lived a long life. She went back to work as a doctor, in Kanpur. In 2002, she even stood for election as President of India. She died in 2012, aged 97.

The Rani of Jhansi Regiment lasted less than two years. But it showed that women could stand in the front line of India's fight for freedom.
