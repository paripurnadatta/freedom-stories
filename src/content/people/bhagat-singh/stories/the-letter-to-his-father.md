Bhagat Singh's father, Kishan Singh, was a brave man. He had been in and out of British jails himself. When his son was born, he was in prison.

But in the autumn of 1930, Kishan Singh was a father who was about to lose his son.

## A secret request

Bhagat Singh was on trial for his life. He had decided not to defend himself. He wanted to use the court to speak about freedom, not to beg for his own life.

His father could not bear it. Without telling his son, he wrote to the special court. He said Bhagat Singh was innocent of the killing. He asked for a chance to prove it.

<aside class="note"><b>Tribunal</b>: a special court set up for one case. This one was set up by the Viceroy to try Bhagat Singh and his friends.</aside>

## The son finds out

When Bhagat Singh heard about it, he was furious. On 4 October 1930 he sat down and wrote his father a letter.

He said he felt "stabbed at the back". He called the request a weakness of the worst kind. He wrote that he had never agreed to it, and he still did not want any defence.

Then came the hardest line of all: "Let me say, father, you have failed."

## Why so angry?

To Bhagat Singh, asking the British for mercy meant admitting they had the right to judge him. He had decided not to ask them for anything. If his family begged, it might look as if he had begged too.

He asked his father to do one more thing. He asked him to publish the letter, so that the public would know the whole truth.

## A father and a son

It was not the first time Kishan Singh had stepped in. A year earlier, Bhagat Singh had been on a long hunger strike in jail. His father had asked him to stop, and that time, Bhagat Singh had listened.

This time, he would not.

Three days after the letter, on 7 October 1930, the court sentenced him to death.

The letter sounds harsh. But try reading it the other way round. A young man is so sure of what he is doing that not even his father's love will make him turn back. And a father loves his son so much that he tries anyway.
