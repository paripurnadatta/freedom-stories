On the night of 23 March 1931, the bodies of Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev and Rajguru were burned in secret beside the Sutlej river. The place was near a village called Hussainiwala, in Punjab.

For Indians, that riverbank became a sacred spot. But in 1947, something happened that nobody had planned for.

## A line on the map

In 1947, India became free. But Punjab was cut in two. One part went to the new country of Pakistan. The other stayed in India.

<aside class="note"><b>Partition</b>: the dividing of British India into two countries, India and Pakistan, in 1947.</aside>

When the new border was drawn, Hussainiwala ended up on the Pakistani side. The place where India's best-loved martyrs had been cremated was now in another country.

## An unusual trade

For fourteen years, it stayed that way. Then India's Prime Minister, Jawaharlal Nehru, arranged a deal with Pakistan.

On 17 January 1961, Hussainiwala was handed back to India. In return, India gave Pakistan twelve villages near the Sulemanki headworks, in the Fazilka area.

<aside class="note"><b>Headworks</b>: a big gate system on a river that sends water into canals.</aside>

Twelve villages for one riverbank. That shows how much the place meant.

## Building the memorial

Work on a memorial began in 1965, but a war with Pakistan held it up. It was finally opened in 1968.

That same year, 1965, a sick man made a last request. Batukeshwar Dutt, who had thrown the bombs in the Assembly with Bhagat Singh, was dying. He asked to be cremated at Hussainiwala, beside his friends. On 19 July 1965, he was.

Later, Bhagat Singh's mother, Vidyawati, was cremated there too.

## Stolen busts

In December 1971, India and Pakistan went to war again. On the night of 3 December, Pakistani soldiers attacked Hussainiwala. They damaged the memorial and carried away the busts of the three martyrs.

<aside class="note"><b>Bust</b>: a statue of a person's head and shoulders.</aside>

The busts were later brought back through talks between the two countries. Then, strangely, they were forgotten. In 2000, an official found them lying in an old trunk in a government office in Ferozepur.

## Today

The memorial was rebuilt, and the river has since changed its course. Every year on 23 March, people gather there for a fair called the Shaheedi Mela, to remember the three friends.

The border with Pakistan is only a short distance away. And beside the river, three young men who dreamed of a free, united country still rest in the place India traded twelve villages to get back.
