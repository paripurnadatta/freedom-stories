Bhagat Singh grew up in Punjab, went to college in Lahore, and was hanged in Lahore. When India was divided in 1947, Lahore became part of Pakistan.

So did a busy road crossing called Shadman Chowk.

<aside class="note"><b>Chowk</b>: a square or crossing where several roads meet.</aside>

## Why this square?

The old Lahore jail is long gone, and the city has grown over it. People who campaign for Bhagat Singh say this square was once part of the jail. They say it is where he, Sukhdev and Rajguru were hanged.

So they asked: why not name it after them?

## A long campaign

The campaign is led by a Pakistani lawyer called Imtiaz Rasheed Qureshi. He runs a group called the Bhagat Singh Memorial Foundation Pakistan.

He took the matter to court. In September 2018, a judge in the Lahore High Court told the city government to name the square after Bhagat Singh.

But years passed, and nothing happened. In April 2024, a government lawyer told the court the order would be issued "soon".

## A sudden stop

Then, in November 2024, a committee was looking at the plan. One of its members, a retired navy officer, objected. He said Bhagat Singh was a criminal, not a revolutionary.

The city government dropped the plan. In January 2025, the Lahore High Court dismissed Qureshi's case. He said he would take it to the Supreme Court of Pakistan.

As of 2025, the square still does not carry Bhagat Singh's name.

## Not the whole story

It would be easy to stop there. But something else happened in Lahore at almost the same time.

In December 2024, the Punjab government in Pakistan opened a Bhagat Singh Gallery. It is inside an old building called Poonch House. It shows photographs, letters and newspaper cuttings about his life.

Poonch House is the very building where his big trial, the Lahore Conspiracy Case, began on 5 May 1930.

## Why does this matter?

Bhagat Singh fought before India and Pakistan were separate countries. He fought for all the people of undivided India, Hindu, Muslim and Sikh. So people on both sides of the border can claim him.

Not everyone agrees about him, in India or in Pakistan. But it says something that, more than ninety years after his death, a lawyer in Lahore is still going to court for him.
