## Research notes

Killed while leading a procession during Quit India.

### A local life that became a national symbol

Matangini Hazra’s story shows how the freedom struggle depended on local activists whose political importance came from sustained participation rather than formal office. Her death during the 1942 Tamluk procession became a powerful symbol of women’s participation in anti-colonial mass action.

### Midnapore activism before 1942

Government accounts place Hazra in salt-law protest, Congress activity and repeated confrontation with colonial policing before the Quit India period.

### The procession at Tamluk

On 29 September 1942 she advanced with a procession toward the Tamluk Police Station. Police firing killed her while she continued carrying the flag.

### Normalize source labels, not history

One official profile describes a 1932 action as Non-Cooperation even though the year belongs to the Civil Disobedience phase. The enriched record should preserve the underlying action but avoid repeating an anachronistic movement label.

### Chronology

- **1932**: Salt-law protest and arrest. Participates in salt-law protest activity and is arrested; the source’s movement label should be normalized carefully
- **1933**: Congress conference and police repression. Attends a subdivisional Congress conference and is injured in a police baton charge
- **1942-09-29**: Tamluk procession. Leads a procession and is killed by police firing while carrying the national flag

### Evidence claims

- She participated in anti-colonial activism in Midnapore and was arrested during salt-law protest activity. (high confidence)
- After release from prison she became an active member of the Indian National Congress. (high confidence)
- She was shot dead in front of the Tamluk Police Station on 29 September 1942. (high confidence)
