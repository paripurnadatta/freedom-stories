### Evidence claims

- In 1941 Mohan Singh was a captain and second-in-command in a battalion of the 1/14 Punjab Regiment of the British Indian Army in Malaya. (HIGH confidence)
- Mohan Singh became the central military leader in the formation of the first Indian National Army from Indian prisoners of war in Japanese-occupied Southeast Asia in 1942. (HIGH confidence)
- He insisted that INA military strategy and decisions should remain autonomous rather than be subordinated to Japanese direction. (HIGH confidence)
- Disputes over Japanese intentions and INA autonomy culminated in the collapse/dissolution of the first INA in December 1942 and Mohan Singh’s removal from command. (HIGH confidence)
