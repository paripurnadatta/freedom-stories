## Research notes

Documented in the cited source in connection with Early nationalist press activity.

### Historical geography

Jessore district: Undivided Bengal (Origin of Amrita Bazar Patrika)

### Historical geography

Calcutta: Capital of Bengal Presidency (Nationalist press centre)

### Overview

Journalist and political publicist best known as a founder of Amrita Bazar Patrika. Ministry of Culture traces the paper to 1868 and its shift to Calcutta in 1871, where it became an influential bilingual nationalist periodical. Sisir used the paper to criticize colonial policy and broaden participation in political associations. His profile is especially useful for the 1868–1880 press-politics bridge preceding the formation of the Congress.

### Historical significance

This profile was selected for Batch 008 because it repairs one or more post-175 gaps while adding a historically distinct strand to the archive. The final implementation should privilege the verified evidence claims and chronology above over generic fame-based biography.

### Chronology

- **1868-02-20**: Amrita Bazar Patrika launched in Jessore district
- **1871**: Press shifted to Calcutta and expanded its political reach
- **1873-1874**: Sisir Kumar Ghosh used the paper to press for broader access to organized political life

### Evidence claims

- Sisir Kumar Ghosh, with Motilal Ghosh and other family collaborators, founded Amrita Bazar Patrika in 1868. (HIGH confidence)
- The paper shifted to Calcutta in 1871 and developed a strongly anti-colonial public voice. (HIGH confidence)
- Sisir used the paper to intervene in political debates and argue for broader access to political associations in the 1870s. (HIGH confidence)
- Amrita Bazar Patrika later became a major platform in nationalist politics and Swadeshi agitation. (HIGH confidence)
