## Research notes

Led the Munda movement against colonial rule and exploitative land systems.

### Land, faith and political authority

Birsa Munda cannot be reduced to a conventional nationalist biography. His leadership developed from the specific pressures facing Munda communities in Chotanagpur and combined religious renewal, social discipline, land grievance and opposition to colonial rule.

### A movement rooted in Chotanagpur

The movement emerged from conflicts over authority, land and the disruption of community life. Birsa’s leadership became religious as well as political, giving the resistance a social world larger than a single confrontation with the state.

### Ulgulan

The late-1890s uprising remembered as Ulgulan represented the most intense phase of Birsa’s movement. The enriched event structure should treat it as a specific regional movement rather than as a generic precursor to later nationalism.

### A political memory larger than a short life

Birsa’s historical memory remains central to Adivasi political identity and to the history of resistance in Chotanagpur. The public profile should distinguish contemporary evidence from later commemorative interpretations.

### Chronology

- **1894**: Mobilization over grievances. Leads Mundas seeking redress and is arrested
- **1895-1897**: Imprisonment. Serves a period of rigorous imprisonment after colonial arrest
- **1899-1900**: Ulgulan. Renewed resistance takes on a broader religious and anti-colonial character
- **1900-02**: Capture and death in custody. Captured in the forest and dies in colonial custody in 1900
