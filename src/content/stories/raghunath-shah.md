## Research notes

Documented in the cited source in connection with Awadh resistance in 1857.

### A father-son resistance remembered together

The strongest sources narrate Raghunath Shah alongside Shankar Shah; the profile should preserve that relationship rather than manufacture an artificially independent campaign.

### Poetry, memory and punishment

Later state memory emphasizes their verses and anti-colonial stance, but precise claims about individual poems should be separated from the securely documented fact of arrest and execution.

### Chronology

- **1857**: Jabalpur resistance. Raghunath Shah joins his father Shankar Shah in opposition to British rule in the Jabalpur-Garha sphere
- **1857-09-18**: Execution of father and son. Commemorative and archival sources remember Shankar Shah and Raghunath Shah as executed together by the colonial state

### Evidence claims

- Government sources consistently identify Kunwar Raghunath Shah as the son of Raja Shankar Shah and as a participant in the 1857 resistance at Jabalpur. (high confidence)
- The Dictionary of Martyrs records a Raghunath Shah of Jabalpur who fought British forces in the region and was executed by being blown from a cannon. (high confidence)
- Government commemorations place Shankar Shah and Raghunath Shah together as tribal figures of the 1857 uprising. (high confidence)
