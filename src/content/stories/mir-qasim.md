## Research notes

Fought the East India Company before the Battle of Buxar.

### The politics of commercial privilege

The core conflict was not an abstract rejection of all commerce: it concerned who could trade on privileged terms and who controlled fiscal authority inside Bengal.

### From nawab-company dispute to north Indian coalition

Buxar broadens Mir Qasim's story beyond Bengal by tying his cause to Awadh and the Mughal emperor.

### Chronology

- **1760s**: Capital shifted to Munger. Mir Qasim seeks greater administrative and military distance from Company influence
- **1762-1763**: Trade dispute becomes open conflict. The argument over inland duties and Company private trade escalates into military confrontation
- **1764-10**: Battle of Buxar. Mir Qasim joins Shuja-ud-Daula and Shah Alam II in a coalition defeated by Company forces

### Evidence claims

- Mir Qasim shifted his capital to Munger in an attempt to reduce British political interference. (high confidence)
- A central dispute concerned duty-free Company trade and the attempt by Company servants to extend that privilege to private commerce. (high confidence)
- After a compromise on private-trade duties was rejected by the Calcutta Council, Mir Qasim abolished inland duties altogether. (high confidence)
- After military defeats, Mir Qasim allied with Shuja-ud-Daula of Awadh and Mughal emperor Shah Alam II. (high confidence)
- The combined forces were decisively defeated by the East India Company at the Battle of Buxar in October 1764. (high confidence)
