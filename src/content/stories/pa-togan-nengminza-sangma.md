## Research notes

### Village autonomy against annexation

The conflict belongs to the history of British territorial expansion into the Northeast rather than to the better-known plains-based nationalist movements.

### A locally rooted memory

Pa Togan's commemoration in Meghalaya gives the timeline a durable Garo historical node without inventing direct links to unrelated all-India leaders.

### Chronology

- **1871**: Colonial survey pressure intensifies. British surveying and administrative expansion provoke resistance among independent Garo villages
- **1872**: Armed resistance organizes. Pa Togan and allied leaders prepare to oppose the advancing colonial force
- **1872-12**: Battle of Chisobibra. Pa Togan is killed while resisting British troops at Chisobibra

### Evidence claims

- The Department of Posts identifies Pa Togan Nengminza Sangma as a Garo leader from the Samanda area who resisted British expansion in the Garo Hills. (high confidence)
- The official brochure places his resistance in the context of British moves after 1869 to bring independent Garo villages under colonial control. (high confidence)
- Pa Togan and other Garo leaders organized resistance after survey and military parties advanced into the hills. (high confidence)
- He was killed fighting British troops at Chisobibra in December 1872. (high confidence)
- The Government of Meghalaya and Department of Posts continue to commemorate his resistance. (high confidence)
