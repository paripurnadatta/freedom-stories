## Research notes

Led rebel forces in Bihar at an advanced age.

### Bihar’s 1857 commander

Kunwar Singh’s importance lies in how long he sustained resistance and how widely his forces moved. His campaign makes clear that 1857 was not one event but a connected sequence of regional military theatres.

### A campaign of mobility

Government accounts emphasize his ability to harry British forces and use mobile or guerrilla-style tactics. The atlas should eventually visualize the campaign as a route rather than a single point at Jagdishpur.

### Regional leadership and collaborators

His brother Amar Singh and commander Hare Krishna Singh appear in official summaries as important collaborators, providing a useful pilot for evidence-bearing military relationships.

### From regional chief to national memory

Later commemoration has elevated Kunwar Singh into a symbol of the 1857 struggle. The profile should retain the regional specificity of his Bihar campaign while showing how that memory became national.

### Chronology

- **1857-07**: Rebellion reaches the Danapur–Arrah region. The Bihar rebellion develops into an armed campaign centered on Jagdishpur and Arrah
- **1857-1858**: Mobile campaign. Kunwar Singh’s forces operate across Bihar and into adjoining regions while pursued by British forces
- **1858-04**: Return toward Jagdishpur. Returns toward his home territory during the final phase of his campaign
- **1858-04-26**: Death. Dies shortly after the last phase of fighting around Jagdishpur
