## Research notes

Led local resistance in the Baraut area.

### Agrarian rebellion inside 1857

The Baraut rising connects land-revenue grievances, village networks and the larger military revolt.

### Alternative authority from below

The 'hall of justice' and intelligence network show how rebel power could become administrative as well as destructive.

### Chronology

- **1857-05 to 1857-07**: Baraut countryside in rebellion. Shah Mal mobilizes cultivators and village headmen, attacks colonial infrastructure and links the countryside to the Delhi rebellion
- **1857-07**: Death in battle. Shah Mal is killed during British counter-insurgency operations near Baraut

### Evidence claims

- Shah Mal was a peasant leader from Bijrol/Baraut whose support base extended across the 'chaurasee des' network of villages. (high confidence)
- His forces attacked government and commercial targets, disrupted the Yamuna bridge and lines of communication, and supplied rebels connected to Delhi. (high confidence)
- He created an intelligence network and used an English officer's bungalow as a local 'hall of justice'. (high confidence)
- Shah Mal was killed in battle in July 1857. (high confidence)
