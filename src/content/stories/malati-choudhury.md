## Research notes

Gandhian organizer and activist in Odisha.

### Coastal satyagraha to princely-state politics

Malati Choudhury's career helps connect different political geographies within Odisha rather than treating the province as one uniform movement space.

### Women, peasants and socialism

Her profile brings together constituencies that can disappear when freedom history is told mainly through party leadership.

### Chronology

- **1930**: Salt Satyagraha in Odisha. Malati joins coastal civil disobedience and helps mobilize women against colonial law and foreign goods
- **late 1930s**: Peasant and Prajamandal politics. Her work broadens into agrarian organization and struggles inside Odisha's princely states
- **1942**: Quit India. She joins the wartime movement and is imprisoned during the repression

### Evidence claims

- Government sources identify Malati Choudhury as a freedom activist who joined the Salt Satyagraha in Odisha and mobilized women around boycott and civil-disobedience activity. (high confidence)
- She worked among peasants and became involved in Prajamandal agitation in Odisha's princely states. (high confidence)
- Odisha government historical material places her in peasant conferences and Congress Socialist activity during the late 1930s. (high confidence)
- She participated in Quit India, was arrested and spent years in prison. (high confidence)
- After release she continued educational and social work, while the package keeps post-1947 institution-building separate from the colonial chronology. (high confidence)
