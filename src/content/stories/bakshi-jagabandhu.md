## Research notes

Commander of the 1817 Paika rebellion in Odisha.

### Historical geography

Khurda/Khordha: Khurda kingdom / Company-ruled Odisha (Core theatre of Paika uprising)

### Historical geography

Banpur: Khurda region (Early target of insurgent attack)

### Historical geography

Puri: Company-ruled Odisha (Uprising spread into the coastal sacred centre)

### Overview

Hereditary Bakshi (military commander) of Khurda and the most prominent leader of the Paika uprising of 1817. His dispossession from the Rodanga/Rorang estate was one grievance within a broader crisis involving service-land resumptions, revenue pressure, salt restrictions and administrative abuse. Kandha insurgents from Ghumsur entered Khurda in March 1817 and the Paikas rallied under Jagabandhu. Resistance and British pursuit continued for years before his negotiated surrender in 1825; he died at Cuttack in 1829.

### Chronology

- **1814**: Company orders contributed to Jagabandhu's dispossession from the Rorang/Rodanga estate
- **1817-03**: Ghumsur Kandhas entered Khurda; Paikas rallied under Jagabandhu and the uprising spread
- **1825-05-27**: Negotiated settlement/surrender brought Jagabandhu out of armed resistance
- **1829-01-24**: Jagabandhu died at Cuttack

### Evidence claims

- Jagabandhu held the hereditary office of Bakshi/commander under the Raja of Khurda and possessed the Rorang/Rodanga estate before Company intervention. (HIGH confidence)
- In March 1817, Kandhas from Ghumsur entered Khurda and Paikas rallied under Jagabandhu, attacking Company institutions in Banpur and Khurda. (HIGH confidence)
- The uprising grew from more than a private estate dispute; official Odisha narratives also identify service-land resumption, revenue burdens, salt duties and official oppression as major grievances. (HIGH confidence)
- Jagabandhu surrendered under negotiated terms in 1825 and died in Cuttack in January 1829. (HIGH confidence)
