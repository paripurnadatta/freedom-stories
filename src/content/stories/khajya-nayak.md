## Research notes

Documented in the cited source in connection with Sannyasi–Fakir resistance in Bengal.

### 1857 from the Nimar hills

Khajya Nayak's profile broadens the uprising beyond sepoy cantonments and princely courts by foregrounding an armed Bhil force operating through difficult hill country.

### A revolt that outlasted the headline year

His importance lies partly in persistence: resistance continued into 1858 rather than ending with the first collapse of major rebel centres.

### Chronology

- **1857**: Bhil armed rising in Central India. Khajya Nayak emerges as one of the prominent Adivasi leaders confronting Company authority in the Nimar-Central India zone
- **1857-1858**: Resistance continues through the ghats. His force keeps attacking colonial movement and locally powerful allies after the main northern centres begin to fall
- **1858**: Death during continued resistance. The Dictionary of Martyrs records that he was killed through a British-side conspiracy in 1858

### Evidence claims

- The Ministry of Culture's Dictionary of Martyrs identifies Khajya Nayak as a Bhil leader of armed resistance in the Central India Agency during 1857. (high confidence)
- The archival reference describes his force as several hundred strong and overwhelmingly drawn from Bhil communities. (high confidence)
- His resistance continued after 1857, including attacks that obstructed British movement through the Nimar ghats. (high confidence)
- The Government of India later commemorated Khajya Nayak among the revolutionary warriors of Madhya Pradesh. (high confidence)
