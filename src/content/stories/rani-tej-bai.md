## Research notes

Documented in the cited source in connection with Ramgarh resistance in 1857.

### A deliberately cautious 1857 profile

Tej Bai is important precisely because participation in the uprising did not always take the form of battlefield command.

### Where the record is thin

The package preserves what the cited source explicitly says and does not manufacture a fuller military biography from later patriotic retellings.

### Chronology

- **1840s**: Jalaun loses autonomous statehood. The political setting of Tej Bai's later resistance is the Company's annexation of the Jalaun state
- **1857**: Indirect support for the uprising. Tej Bai is described as encouraging participation rather than leading a large independent field force
- **1858**: Imprisonment after the rebellion. The supplied sources associate her with a long sentence and harsh confinement

### Evidence claims

- The supplied biographical account connects Rani Tej Bai to the former Jalaun state and names Gopal Rao as her husband. (medium confidence)
- After annexation of Jalaun, the account depicts her as lacking the force for an independent rising but encouraging others to join the 1857 rebellion. (medium confidence)
- The same account states that she was sentenced to twelve years' imprisonment for her involvement. (medium confidence)
- A later research article places Tej Bai in confinement connected with the 1857 revolt and describes unhealthy prison conditions reported in 1858. (low confidence)
