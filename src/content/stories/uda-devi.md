## Research notes

Associated with resistance in the Lucknow area.

### A fighter and a memory tradition

The profile separates the securely supported fact of armed participation from later memorial claims that grew around her final battle.

### Caste and remembrance

Her place in Pasi public memory is historically significant in its own right and should be presented as memory history rather than used to inflate uncertain battlefield numbers.

### Chronology

- **1857**: Awadh uprising. Uda Devi joins armed resistance in the Lucknow theatre associated with Begum Hazrat Mahal
- **1857-11**: Death in the Lucknow fighting. Tradition locates her death in the Sikandar Bagh fighting; later commemoration preserves the episode in increasingly detailed forms

### Evidence claims

- Government sources identify Uda Devi as a Pasi woman who fought in the Lucknow/Awadh theatre of the 1857 uprising. (high confidence)
- The official profile associates her with the resistance under Begum Hazrat Mahal. (high confidence)
- A Ministry of Culture historical essay preserves later community tradition describing her fighting from a tree during the Sikandar Bagh battle. (medium confidence)
- The same essay distinguishes later claims of thirty-two British casualties from a nineteenth-century reminiscence that attributed more than half a dozen to the female combatant. (medium confidence)
- She was killed during the 1857 fighting and subsequently became a major figure in Pasi commemorative memory. (high confidence)
