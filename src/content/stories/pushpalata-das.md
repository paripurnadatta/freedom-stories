## Research notes

Organized women in Assam and was imprisoned during the freedom struggle.

### Youth activism becomes women's leadership

Her profile traces political formation from school protest into adult organizational responsibility.

### Quit India from Assam

The episode helps correct an archive in which the 1942 movement can otherwise appear overwhelmingly centred on western and northern India.

### Chronology

- **1930**: Mukti Sangha and school expulsion. Teenage political organizing brings direct disciplinary retaliation
- **1940**: Individual Satyagraha. Pushpalata enters a new phase of direct Gandhian protest and incarceration
- **1942**: Quit India in Assam. Women's mobilization in Darrang becomes part of dangerous flag-hoisting demonstrations and severe police repression

### Evidence claims

- Pushpalata Das participated in nationalist activity from childhood and joined a local girls' volunteer network promoting swadeshi and khadi. (high confidence)
- In 1930 she and friends formed the Mukti Sangha within the Kamrup Mahila Samity and organized protest around Bhagat Singh's death sentence. (high confidence)
- She later served in Congress women's organization in Assam and participated in Individual Satyagraha. (high confidence)
- During Quit India she and her husband were tasked with organizing women in Darrang district; she was imprisoned for years under wartime regulations. (high confidence)
