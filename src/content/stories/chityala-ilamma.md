## Research notes

Her resistance to landlord encroachment became a symbol of peasant and anti-feudal mobilization in Telangana.

### A peasant struggle inside a princely state

Ilamma's resistance took place under the Nizam's Hyderabad rather than direct British provincial rule, so the political context must remain distinct.

### Land, caste and gender

The historical power of the episode lies in how a dispute over cultivation became inseparable from caste status, women's agency and feudal authority.

### Chronology

- **1940s**: Land conflict with the Visnoor Deshmukh. Ilamma refuses to relinquish cultivated land, turning a local agrarian confrontation into a political symbol
- **1940s**: Telangana mobilisation expands. Her activism becomes part of the Andhra Mahasabha and communist-led peasant struggle against feudal authority

### Evidence claims

- The government profile identifies Chityala Ilamma as a woman from a Rajaka family in the Warangal region who became associated with the Telangana peasant struggle. (high confidence)
- Her best-known confrontation involved resisting efforts by the Visnoor Deshmukh's establishment to seize land she cultivated. (high confidence)
- She became associated with the Andhra Mahasabha and communist-led anti-feudal mobilization. (high confidence)
- Her home functioned as a centre of local anti-feudal activity and she is remembered for challenging both landlord authority and caste-gender hierarchy. (high confidence)
