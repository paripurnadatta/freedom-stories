Every famous person collects stories. Some are true. Some grow a little each time they are told. Some might never have happened at all.

Here are two of the best-loved stories about Bhagat Singh as a boy.

## Story one: the guns in the field

It is 1910, in the village of Banga in Punjab. A little boy of three is digging in a field, getting mud all over his clothes.

His father, Kishan Singh, walks up with a friend. "What are you doing?" he asks.

"I am sowing guns," says the boy. "When they grow, we will drive the British out."

## Story two: the bottle of earth

It is April 1919. In the city of Amritsar, British soldiers have fired on a peaceful crowd in a walled garden called Jallianwala Bagh. Hundreds of people have been killed.

Bhagat Singh is eleven. He insists on going to Amritsar to see the place for himself.

He comes home carrying a little earth from the garden, soaked with the blood of the people who died there. With his sister, Amar Kaur, he puts flowers on it, to honour them.

## So, did they happen?

Here is the honest answer. We don't know for sure.

Both stories come from the family's memories. We do not know of any letter, diary or newspaper from the time that records them. Nobody writes down what a three-year-old says in a field.

<aside class="note"><b>Historian</b>: someone who studies the past, using letters, newspapers, photographs and other records from the time.</aside>

Historians have a few ways to check a story like this:

- Was it written down at the time, in a letter or a diary?
- Do people who did not know each other tell it the same way?
- Does it fit with things we know for certain?

The massacre at Jallianwala Bagh certainly happened, on 13 April 1919. We also know that Bhagat Singh's family was deeply against British rule. His uncle Ajit Singh had been sent away to Burma for leading protests. So the stories fit the family he grew up in.

But "it fits" is not the same as "it is proven".

## Why keep telling them?

So should we throw these stories away? Not at all. They tell us something true, even if the details are uncertain. They show how his family saw him: as a boy who could not stand by and watch unfairness.

The important thing is to tell them honestly. When you share a story about the past, it is good to say how sure you are. "This really happened" and "This is what his family remembered" are both worth saying. They are just not the same thing.
