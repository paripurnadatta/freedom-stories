On 30 October 1928, Lala Lajpat Rai led a silent march through Lahore. He was one of the most loved leaders in Punjab, and he was in his sixties.

The marchers were protesting against the Simon Commission. It was a group of British men sent to decide India's future, and not one of them was Indian.

The police charged the march with lathis. Lajpat Rai was struck again and again. Less than three weeks later, on 17 November, he died.

<aside class="note"><b>Lathi</b>: a long, heavy bamboo stick carried by the police.</aside>

## A plan for revenge

The British said the beating had nothing to do with his death. Bhagat Singh and his friends did not believe them. They blamed the police chief who had ordered the charge, a superintendent called James Scott.

Their secret group, the Hindustan Socialist Republican Association, decided to kill him.

For several days, a member called Jai Gopal watched the police headquarters. His job was to learn what Scott looked like and to give a signal when he came out.

## Four o'clock

On 17 December, at about four in the afternoon, a young British officer walked out of the building. Jai Gopal gave the signal.

Rajguru fired first. Bhagat Singh fired too.

But the man on the ground was not Scott. He was John Saunders, an assistant superintendent. He was twenty-one years old, and he had joined the police just the year before. He had not given the order to charge the march.

Jai Gopal had pointed at the wrong man.

## The man who ran after them

As the young men ran, an Indian head constable called Chanan Singh chased after them. Azad, who was guarding their escape, shot him. He died too.

So two men who had nothing to do with Lajpat Rai's beating lost their lives that day.

## The notice

The group had already printed posters saying that Scott was dead. Now they had to change them.

The next day, notices appeared on walls in Lahore. They said Lajpat Rai's death had been avenged. They were signed "Balraj", a secret name used by Azad.

Look closely at the notice, though. The group also wrote that they were sorry they had killed a man. They said they wanted a revolution that would end one person's power over another.

It is one of the hardest parts of Bhagat Singh's story. He was brave and he believed in justice. But on this day, people died who should not have died. Both things are true.
