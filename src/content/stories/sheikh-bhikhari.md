## Research notes

Documented in the cited source in connection with Awadh resistance in 1857.

### A commander inside a regional coalition

Bhikhari's importance is best understood through the Chotanagpur network rather than as an isolated local rebel.

### The geography of resistance

Passes and road approaches mattered strategically: delaying British movement was part of sustaining the Ranchi-centred rising.

### Chronology

- **1857**: Chotanagpur joins the uprising. Bhikhari becomes part of the rebel command network associated with Vishwanath Shahdeo and other local leaders
- **1857-1858**: Resistance around Chutupalu. Rebel forces attempt to obstruct British movement through the Ranchi approaches
- **1858-01**: Capture and execution. Bhikhari is captured after the collapse of the Chutupalu position and executed by the colonial state

### Evidence claims

- Government sources place Sheikh Bhikhari among the principal figures of the 1857 uprising in the Chotanagpur-Ranchi region. (high confidence)
- He is described as a close military associate of Vishwanath Shahdeo and part of the wider rebel coalition in the region. (high confidence)
- The Chutupalu episode formed a major part of the effort to block or delay advancing British forces. (high confidence)
- Government commemorative accounts record his capture and execution in January 1858. (high confidence)
