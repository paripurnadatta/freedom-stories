## Research notes

Editor of Pratap whose anti-colonial reporting led to imprisonment during the Non-Cooperation period.

### Journalism as political action

Vidyarthi’s profile demonstrates that the nationalist press could connect agrarian violence, censorship, imprisonment and revolutionary education.

### Pratap

The newspaper was both a reporting institution and a political network.

### A bridge to Bhagat Singh

The documented work relationship gives the archive a strong connection between revolutionary and journalistic histories.

### Chronology

- **1921-10-25**: Imprisonment over Pratap reporting. Refuses to retreat from reporting that challenged colonial authorities
- **1921-1922**: Glimpses of Jail Life. Turns incarceration into published political testimony
- **early 1920s**: Bhagat Singh at Pratap. Provides a journalistic workplace and platform for the young revolutionary

### Evidence claims

- Edited Pratap and was prosecuted and imprisoned for anti-British reporting on events including Raebareli and Fyzabad. (high confidence)
- Was imprisoned from 25 October 1921 to 22 May 1922 and wrote about prison life. (high confidence)
- Bhagat Singh worked under Vidyarthi in Kanpur and published writing in Pratap. (high confidence)
- The supplied record connects Ganesh Shankar Vidyarthi with Bhagat Singh as worked with. (high confidence)
