On 12 September 1929, the jail officials in Lahore handed Bhagat Singh a notebook. It was about the size of a school exercise book. On it were the words: "404 pages for Bhagat Singh".

Bhagat Singh had a lot of time in jail. He spent it reading.

## What went into it

Every time he read something that made him think, he copied it into the notebook. Sometimes a line, sometimes a whole page.

He copied from more than a hundred writers. Some wrote about politics, like Karl Marx and Lenin. Some were poets, like Wordsworth, Byron and Walt Whitman. There were storytellers too, like Victor Hugo, Charles Dickens and Dostoevsky. Rabindranath Tagore is in there, and so are the verses of Omar Khayyam.

The very first page has two lines. One is from Shakespeare. The other is from the Urdu poet Ghalib.

<aside class="note"><b>Ghalib</b>: one of the greatest Urdu poets. He lived in Delhi in the 1800s.</aside>

He did not fill all 404 pages. About 140 have writing on them. The rest are still blank.

## Getting it out

On 16 September 1930, Bhagat Singh wrote to his younger brother, Kulbir. He asked him to collect his books, papers and belongings from the jail.

Before 23 March 1931, the notebook left Lahore Central Jail with those things. Kulbir kept it safe.

## A long silence

For a long time, hardly anyone knew it existed. In 1968, a writer saw it with Kulbir and wrote about it. Copies quietly found their way to other places. In 1981, one turned up in the basement of a school near Delhi. Another was found in an archive in Moscow. The two copies matched word for word.

It was finally printed as a book in 1994, more than sixty years after Bhagat Singh died. The historian Bhupendra Hooja published it.

## Where is it now?

The original notebook still belongs to the family. In 2023, Kulbir's grandson brought it to the World Book Fair in Delhi. It sat in a glass case, and crowds came to see it. It had been shown once before, in 2008, at an exhibition at the Supreme Court of India.

Most of what we know about Bhagat Singh comes from what he did. This notebook shows us something different: what he was thinking about, in his last months, with the end coming closer.

He was thinking about books.
