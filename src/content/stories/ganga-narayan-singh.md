## Research notes

Documented in the cited source in connection with Ganga Narayan's Bhumij resistance.

### From succession dispute to popular revolt

The movement's significance lies in its expansion beyond a ruling-family quarrel into a larger resistance to colonial revenue and political structures.

### An early eastern uprising

The Bhumij Revolt gives the timeline a major pre-1857 episode connecting tribal, agrarian and regional elite grievances.

### Chronology

- **1832**: The Bhumij Revolt begins. A dynastic grievance around Barabhum expands into armed resistance against colonial and intermediary power
- **1832-1833**: The rising spreads across Jungle Mahal. Ganga Narayan's coalition challenges police stations, revenue authority and local collaborators
- **1833**: Death and suppression. Ganga Narayan is killed and the rebellion is eventually suppressed, followed by administrative reorganization

### Evidence claims

- Ministry of Culture material identifies Ganga Narayan Singh as a leading figure in the Bhumij Revolt of 1832–33 in Jungle Mahal. (high confidence)
- The revolt grew from a Barabhum succession dispute but expanded into wider opposition to colonial revenue arrangements and local intermediaries. (high confidence)
- Ganga Narayan killed the unpopular diwan Madhab Singh and mobilized support from Bhumij communities, Ghatwals and sections of the zamindari order. (high confidence)
- The uprising spread widely enough that the colonial government later reorganized administration in the affected region. (high confidence)
