## Research notes

Documented in the cited source in connection with Khond resistance under Chakra Bisoi.

### Resistance and a morally difficult institution

The conflict cannot be reduced either to heroic anti-colonialism or to a British humanitarian campaign: sovereignty, taxation, local power and the colonial suppression of Meriah sacrifice were entangled.

### Why the hills mattered

Khond terrain and decentralized political organization allowed resistance to survive long after colonial officers expected the region to be pacified.

### Chronology

- **1830s-1840s**: British power expands into Ghumsur and the Khond hills. Annexation and political intervention create the setting for prolonged Khond resistance
- **1846**: Resistance intensifies. Chakra Bisoi uses deep resentment against colonial intrusion to sustain armed opposition
- **1850s**: A prolonged hill campaign. British forces continue operations against Bisoi and his allies well into the decade

### Evidence claims

- Odisha government historical material identifies Chakra Bisoi as a leading figure of Khond resistance in the Ghumsur-Khondmals region during the 1840s and 1850s. (high confidence)
- He mobilized Khond support amid hostility to expanding British intervention after the annexation of Ghumsur. (high confidence)
- Colonial efforts to suppress Meriah human sacrifice became one major arena of confrontation, alongside broader questions of political autonomy and control. (high confidence)
- The government account shows British forces still pursuing Chakra Bisoi in the mid-1850s and records his ability to sustain resistance through difficult hill terrain and regional alliances. (high confidence)
