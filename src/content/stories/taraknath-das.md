## Research notes

Transnational activist and organizer.

### Nationalism across the Pacific

Das's profile connects Indian independence politics to racial exclusion, migration and political organizing in Canada and the United States.

### A newspaper as a node in the graph

Free Hindusthan was both evidence of his politics and infrastructure linking diaspora readers to anti-colonial ideas.

### Chronology

- **1906-1908**: North American organizing and Free Hindusthan. Das settles in North America and develops print as a vehicle for anti-colonial and immigrant politics
- **1913**: Ghadar connection. Das becomes associated with Har Dayal and the emerging Ghadar network
- **1917**: Indo-German conspiracy case. His transnational revolutionary work brings prosecution and imprisonment in the United States

### Evidence claims

- Taraknath Das was drawn to the Anushilan Samiti before leaving India for Japan and then North America. (high confidence)
- After anti-Indian violence in North America, Das published Free Hindusthan as an anti-British and immigrant-rights newspaper. (high confidence)
- The April 1908 issue of Free Hindusthan survives in the South Asian American Digital Archive and identifies Das as editor/publisher. (high confidence)
- Das became associated with Har Dayal and the Ghadar movement in 1913. (high confidence)
- He was implicated in the Indo-German conspiracy and imprisoned in the United States. (high confidence)
